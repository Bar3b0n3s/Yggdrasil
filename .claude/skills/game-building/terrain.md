# Terrain

A reference page of the `game-building` skill (`skills/game-building`), for its steps "3. Scenes, assets and prefabs" and "4. Scripts": heightfield terrain, its heightmap import, sculpting and painting through `terrain_edit`, its collider, the script queries, and what a builder checks.

- **The heightfield.** A `Heightfield` asset (`.heightfield`) is a square of 3 to 1025 16-bit samples a side, each a fraction of the Terrain's `HeightScale` (65535 is the full scale). `asset_create {type: "Heightfield", path}` makes a flat 129 × 129 one, which takes no values. `terrain_import_heightmap {source, path}` converts a heightmap into one at `path` (below `Assets/`, ending in `.heightfield`; one already there is replaced and keeps its handle, so every Terrain naming it follows): `source` is a project path or an absolute one, a 16-bit PNG (its first channel; an 8-bit PNG is refused, since 256 levels terrace a terrain) or a `.raw` or `.r16` file of square little-endian 16-bit samples, row 0 first. The terrain inspector's Import Heightmap button runs the same method.
- **The Terrain** (`component_schema {name: "Terrain"}`) lays its `Heightfield`'s samples over `Size` metres along the entity's local X and Z **from the entity's origin** (the terrain spans [0, Size.x] × [0, Size.y], so put the entity at `-Size / 2` to centre it), heights up to `HeightScale` metres along local +Y. Up to four `Layers`, `{BaseColorMap, NormalMap, Tint, Tiling, Roughness, Metallic}`, each map repeating every `Tiling` metres, are weighed per point by the `SplatMap`'s red, green, blue and alpha channels (with no splat map, or all four weights 0, the first layer alone; with no layer, white). It is lit like a mesh and receives shadows when `ReceiveShadows`, but casts none. It draws in chunks of 32 cells, each at a level of detail picked by its distance from the camera (`LodBias` above 1 keeps detail farther, below 1 drops it nearer), stitched so no crack opens between levels.
- **Sculpt and paint** with `terrain_edit {entity, tool, points, radius?, strength?, height?, layer?}`, the editor's terrain brush's one action, so a stroke through it writes exactly the bytes the same stroke made in the scene view writes. `points` (1 to 4096) are world positions, their Y ignored (a point picked on the surface works as is), each one dab of `radius` metres (default 2) weighted by the falloff (1 − (d / radius)²)² and by `strength` (0 to 1, default 0.5), in order: `Raise` and `Lower` move the samples by `strength` × falloff metres, `Smooth` towards the mean of their 3 × 3 neighbours, `Flatten` towards `height` metres above the entity, and `Paint` moves the weight of splat layer `layer` (0 to 3) towards full while keeping the four weights summing to 255. Repeat a point for a stronger dab: a sample never leaves 0 to `HeightScale`. Each call rewrites and reimports the heightfield's or the splat map's file and records one undo step that restores its bytes. The first paint of a terrain without a `SplatMap` makes `<heightfield's folder>/<heightfield's stem>.<entity id>.splat.png` (the heightfield's resolution − 1 texels a side, layer 0 everywhere), imports it as a data texture (`Usage` Linear, `Compression` None) and assigns it in the same step. Edit scenes only: there are no run-time edits and no holes.
- **Collision.** A `TerrainCollider` (it needs the Terrain) makes exactly the drawn surface a height-field collider, which rays, shape casts, overlaps and moving bodies meet. When its own entity holds the body (no RigidBody on it or above it, and no collider-only ancestor gathering it), its `Layer`, `Friction` and `Restitution` are that static body's; otherwise they are unused, and a RigidBody holding it must be `Static` or `Kinematic` (`PHYSICS_NONCONVEX_DYNAMIC` otherwise). Its body is part of the physics state, so a replay of a ball rolling over sculpted ground is as deterministic as any other.
- **Scripts.** `Terrain:SampleHeight(position)` and `Terrain:SampleNormal(position)` (on `entity.Terrain`) return the world height and the world unit normal of the surface under the world point `position` (its Y ignored), or nil outside the terrain or without a loaded heightfield: place props on the ground, or check in a suite that a body rests on it (its centre a radius from the surface along the normal). Every field reads and writes through the proxy, `Layers` as a whole array.
- **Checks.** `project_validate` warns `TERRAIN_NO_HEIGHTFIELD` (nothing drawn, no body) and `TERRAIN_SPLAT_NOT_RAW` (a splat map imported as colour or compressed; `skills/game-debugging/validation-codes`). A heightfield edited after a replay was recorded changes the bodies, so record replays after the last stroke (`skills/game-building/replays`).
- **Limits.** A moving terrain's velocity is the camera's alone, so it ghosts under TAA; a terrain casts no shadows; the Overdraw view, the wireframe's edges and the selection outline leave terrains out (their depth still hides what is behind them); Jolt pads an odd sample count, so a terrain body's bounds reach one cell past its far X and Z edges.
- **Picking.** A terrain is hit where it is drawn: the editor's click selects it, and the CPU visual queries `viewport.pick` and `scene.raycast` (through `engine_call`) report it, submesh 0 and its cell's triangle, on the surface its collider shapes, so a mesh buried below the ground is never what a pixel shows.
- **Stats.** `stats_get` lists `TerrainDepthNormal` and `TerrainForward`, one draw per chunk in view, only while a terrain is drawn.
