# 2D games

A reference page of the `game-building` skill (`skills/game-building`), for its steps "2. Project settings" and "3. Scenes, assets and prefabs": sprites, sprite sheets and their clips, sorting, the pixel-perfect camera, 2D lights and normal maps, tilemaps and their painting, 2D physics and the editor's 2D mode. Scripts that drive them: `skills/luau-gameplay/sprites-2d`; when something does not show or move: `skills/game-debugging/sprites-2d`. Every field's range and default is in `reference/Components`.

- **A sprite** is an entity with a `SpriteRenderer` (it needs a Transform): `{Sheet, Frame, Texture, Color, FlipX, FlipY, PixelsPerUnit, SortingLayer, OrderInLayer, Lit}`. It draws frame `Frame` of the sprite sheet `Sheet`; with no `Sheet`, the whole `Texture` pivoted at its centre; with neither, the built-in 16-texel white square. Its size is texels over `PixelsPerUnit` (default 16, so a 16-texel image is one unit), in the entity's XY plane, times the entity's scale; `Color` tints it (linear, alpha in the fourth component). Sprites are unlit unless `Lit` is set (2D lights, below); under a `PixelPerfectCamera` (the 2D colour path, below) an unlit sprite's texel reaches the screen as its texture holds it, while an ordinary camera's tonemapping and post-processing still apply.
- **Built-in shapes:** `engine://Textures/SpriteSquare`, `SpriteRounded`, `SpriteBevel` and `SpriteCircle` are 16 x 16 white shapes to tint (`reference/BuiltinAssets`): a whole game can be drawn without importing a texture.
- **Sprite sheets** cut frames from one texture: `asset_create {type: "SpriteSheet", path: "Assets/Sheets/Hero.spritesheet", values: {Texture, Frames: [{Name, Rect: {X, Y, Width, Height}, Pivot, Borders}], Clips: [{Name, Frames: [{Frame, Ticks}], Loop}]}}`. `Texture` takes any asset reference: its id, its path (`"Assets/Textures/Hero.png"`) or an engine path (`"engine://Textures/SpriteSquare"`), stored as the id. Rectangles are texels from the top-left corner; a frame is identified by its index, a clip by its name, and a clip step shows its frame for `Ticks` fixed steps. `asset_get_properties` reads a sheet and `asset_set_properties {asset, values}` patches it; arrays replace whole, so restate every frame or clip. A frame outside its texture, or a step naming a frame the sheet lacks, is refused. A pixel-art pivot is a multiple of 1 / Width and 1 / Height, so the frame's texels stay on the pixel grid.
- **Animation:** a `SpriteAnimator {Clip, Playing, Step, StepTicks}` beside the `SpriteRenderer` plays a clip of its sheet by ticks: each step writes its frame to `SpriteRenderer.Frame` for its `Ticks` fixed steps; a looping clip starts again, a held one stops on its last frame and clears `Playing`. Its state is component data, so the state hash and replays cover it. Scripts start clips with `SpriteAnimator:Play(name)`.
- **Draw order:** sprites draw back to front in (sorting layer, `OrderInLayer`, canonical entity order), never by distance. Declare the layers back to front in the project: `project_set_settings {patch: {SortingLayers: {Layers: ["Default", "Background", "Characters", "Foreground"]}}}` (1 to 32 unique names, the first `Default`; the array replaces whole). A sprite's `SortingLayer` names one; an undeclared name draws in the first layer and is `SPRITE_UNKNOWN_SORTING_LAYER`. For y-sorting, set `OrderInLayer` from the position in a script. Sprites draw over the opaque meshes' depth without writing their own, so a mesh in front of a sprite hides it.
- **The pixel-perfect camera:** a `PixelPerfectCamera {ReferenceWidth, ReferenceHeight, PixelsPerUnit, LetterboxColor}` on the primary camera's entity renders the scene at the reference resolution (for example 320 x 180) in the 2D colour path (a linear tonemap without bloom, dither or FXAA), orthographic with a half height of `ReferenceHeight / (2 * PixelsPerUnit)` units whatever the `Camera` says, and upscales it by the largest whole factor that fits the window, with point sampling and `LetterboxColor` bars. Its camera position snaps to the pixel grid. Give sprites and camera the same `PixelsPerUnit`, so one texel is one scene pixel. Check it on game-view screenshots at the window's size (`viewport_screenshot {view: "game", width, height}`): a texel edge falls on a multiple of the scale.
- **2D lights:** a `Light2D {Type, Color, Intensity, Radius, Falloff, Height}` lights the lit sprites (`SpriteRenderer.Lit`) and lit tilemaps (`TilemapRenderer.Lit`) of the view and nothing else: unlit sprites, meshes and texts keep their colours. `Type` `Point` is a circle of light around the entity, full within `Radius * (1 - Falloff)` and fading smoothly to nothing at `Radius` (world units; `Falloff` 0 is a hard edge); `Global` gives every lit pixel the same light, wherever its entity is. `Color` is linear and multiplied by `Intensity` (0 to 1000: above 1 overbrights). Lights add up, and a lit pixel without any light is black: give a lit scene a dim `Global` light as its ambient. There are no 2D shadows, and a view adds the first 1,024 lights that reach it, in canonical entity order (the rest are skipped with one warning). Under a `PixelPerfectCamera` the lights accumulate at the reference resolution. Lights are presentation only: no simulation state, so adding or moving one never changes a replay's hash. The scene view shows each light as the point-light icon in its colour.
- **Normal maps:** `SpriteRenderer.NormalMap` takes a texture imported as a normal map, `asset_import {source, destDir, settings: {Usage: "NormalMap"}}` (tangent space, +X to the image's right and +Y to its top), laid like the sprite's texture: it is sampled at the same normalized coordinates, so a sheet's normal map has the sheet texture's layout. A lit point light then lights each pixel by the cosine between its normal and the direction to the light raised `Height` above the plane; global lights ignore normals, and a sprite without a normal map takes every light whole. Where lit sprites overlap, those underneath take the topmost one's normals.
- **Tilemaps:** a `.tilemap` asset is a grid of cells, each empty or a palette entry `{Sheet, Frame, Solid}` (a sprite sheet frame, and whether a `TilemapCollider` collides with it): `asset_create {type: "Tilemap", path: "Assets/Tilemaps/Level.tilemap", values: {CellSize: [1, 1], ChunkSize: 16, Palette: [{Sheet: "Assets/Sheets/Tiles.spritesheet", Frame: 0, Solid: true}]}}` (absent values make an empty map of unit cells in chunks of 16). Cell (x, y) spans `[x, x + 1] * CellSize.x` by `[y, y + 1] * CellSize.y` in the entity's local units, x right and y up. `asset_get_properties` reads the map and `asset_set_properties` changes its palette, cell size or chunk size (power of two from 1 to 64; arrays replace whole; a new `ChunkSize` sent without `Chunks` keeps every painted cell, laid in chunks of the new size). An entity with a `TilemapRenderer {Tilemap, Color, PixelsPerUnit, SortingLayer, OrderInLayer, Lit}` draws every tile exactly as a sprite of its entry's frame centred on its cell (its frame's pivot on the cell's centre), so match `PixelsPerUnit` to the frames' texels over `CellSize`. The tilemap sorts with the sprites as one item; within it, rows draw from the top down, each left to right, so a tile taller than its cell overlaps the row above. Only the chunks on screen are drawn. Lit tilemaps take the flat normal; tiles have no flips or rotations.
- **Painting tiles:** `tilemap_paint {tilemap, cells: [{x, y, tile}]}` (1 to 65,536 cells a call) sets each cell to `tile`, the 1-based palette index, or empties it with 0; a later entry for a cell wins; it reports `{changed, chunks, undoIndex}` and is one undo step (`undoIndex` 0 when nothing changed). In the editor, select one entity with a `TilemapRenderer` whose `Tilemap` is set: the scene view's toolbar shows a palette entry picker and an Erase toggle after the 2D toggle; left-drag paints the chosen entry into every cell the cursor crosses and right-drag erases, each stroke one `tilemap.paint`. While a lockstep, recording, replay or test session runs, a paint reaches the play session only after it ends.
- **Tilemap collision:** a `TilemapCollider {Tilemap, IsTrigger}` beside the `TilemapRenderer` (a null `Tilemap` takes the renderer's) makes the solid cells 2D colliders: each chunk's solid cells merged greedily into boxes, never across a chunk's edge, in one static body (or the compound of a `RigidBody2D` on the entity or above it). A ball rolling across the boxes' seams sets `RigidBody2D.EnhancedInternalEdgeRemoval`. A paint or reimport rebuilds the shape in place at the next step. A map without a solid cell makes no body; more than 65,536 boxes is `PHYSICS_INVALID_SHAPE`.
- **2D physics** is a second, plane-locked world: `RigidBody2D` (RigidBody's fields in the plane: `InitialLinearVelocity` is a vec2, angles are degrees about +Z, angular velocities radians per second) with `BoxCollider2D {HalfExtents, Offset, Angle, IsTrigger}`, `CircleCollider2D {Radius, Offset, IsTrigger}` or `PolygonCollider2D {Points (3 to 64, their convex hull), Offset, Angle, IsTrigger}`; a collider without a body is static. Bodies move in X and Y and turn about Z only; the entity's world Z is free for draw depth: a Transform write that changes only Z moves and wakes no body. Gravity is the project's `Physics2D.Gravity` (`[0, -9.81]` by default); layers and collisions are the 3D world's (`Physics.Layers`, `Physics.Collisions`). An entity holds 2D or 3D physics components, never both (`PHYSICS2D_MIXED_WORLDS`: it keeps its 3D body only); a 2D body's entity rotates about Z only (`PHYSICS2D_OFF_PLANE`). The 2D world joins the state hash only once a 2D body exists, so a 3D game's hashes never move.
- **Queries:** in a play session (`play_start {mode: "simulate", lockstep: true}` for a check without scripts), `engine_call {method: "physics2d.raycast", params: {origin: [x, y], direction: [x, y], maxDistance, all?}}` lists the hits by distance and `physics2d.overlap {shape: "Circle" | "Box", center, radius | halfExtents, angle?}` the entities a shape touches.
- **The editor's 2D mode:** `engine_call {method: "viewport.setOptions", params: {mode2D: true}}` (the scene view's 2D toggle) turns the scene camera into an orthographic one looking down -Z at its target's X and Y; `viewport.camera` then sets its `position` (`[x, y, 500]`), `target` (`[x, y, 0]`) and `orthographicSize` (half the height shown), and `viewport.frame` keeps it 2D (both through `engine_call`). `viewport.pick` and a click report the sprite drawn last under the pixel, before any mesh. Dropping a sheet or a texture on the scene view creates a sprite at z = 0 through `entity_create`: from automation, create it yourself, `entity_create {name, components: {Transform: {Translation: [x, y, 0]}, SpriteRenderer: {Sheet: "Assets/Sheets/Hero.spritesheet"}}}`.
- **Replays** of a 2D game record and verify as any other (`skills/game-building/replays`): sprite animation, sorting, tilemap fields and 2D bodies are simulation state, so record after the sheets, tilemaps, layers and physics settings are final; repaint a tilemap a replay plays on and its colliders change, so record it again. Lights are not simulation state.
