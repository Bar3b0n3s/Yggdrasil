# Prefabs and levels

A reference page of the `game-building` skill (`skills/game-building`), for its step "3. Scenes, assets and prefabs".

## Prefabs

- **A prefab takes no values.** `asset_create {type: "Prefab", path}` writes a prefab of one empty root entity named after the file and refuses `values` (at `/values`), as it does for a Scene, a Heightfield and a Folder. A prefab with components is saved from an entity: build it in a scene (`entity_create` with its `components`, its children below it), then `prefab_create {entity, path}`.
- Prefabs: build an entity, `prefab_create {entity, path, replaceWithInstance?}`, then `prefab_instantiate {prefab, parent?, index?, transform?, name?, components?}`; `prefab_apply` writes an instance's overrides into the prefab and `prefab.revert` (through `engine_call`) drops them. `prefab_create` returns the replacing instance as `instance`, `prefab_instantiate` the new root as `entity` (`{"$ref": "2.entity.id"}` in a batch). Deleting an instance's member (an entity below its root that the prefab made) records a `RemoveEntity` override, so it stays deleted through prefab updates until `prefab.revert`. Prefabs nest: `prefab_create` keeps the instances inside the subtree live, and only the outermost instance records overrides. `prefab_apply` writes an edit inside a nested instance to the outermost prefab, or with `level` to the prefab of an instance on the way down (`{instance: "/Room/Lamp", level: "/Room/Lamp"}` writes Lamp.prefab), then rewrites the prefabs nesting it (`updatedPrefabs`) and rebuilds their instances; `prefab.unpack` unpacks one level, from the outermost.
- **Adding a child to a prefab.** `prefab_apply` also writes every entity you added below the instance (a child with its subtree, at its place among its siblings, or an instance you placed there, which the prefab then nests) into the prefab, so every instance in every open scene gains it and scenes opened later rebuild with it: build the new part under one instance (`entity_create {parent: "/HUD", index: 1}`), then `prefab_apply {instance: "/HUD"}`. The added entities become members of the instance with new ids, listed in the result's `addedEntities` (`{"$ref": "3.addedEntities.0.id"}` in a batch), so take their ids from there, or address them by path, after the apply. The instance's own root and members may reference an added entity (their references follow it); any other reference to one makes the apply refuse with InvalidState naming both, so set such references after the apply. At a nested `level`, only the added entities may reference each other. An added instance of the prefab itself, or of a prefab nesting it, is refused as `PREFAB_CYCLE`.
- **A scene opened after its prefab changed opens unsaved.** `scene_open` rebuilds a scene's prefab instances from their prefabs' current versions, so a level whose `HUD.prefab` gained a child through another level's `prefab_apply` opens with `scene.dirty: true`, and the result's `rebuiltInstances` names each instance the rebuild changed with its prefab (`[{instance: {id, name, path: "/HUD"}, prefab: {id, path: "Assets/Prefabs/HUD.prefab", type: "Prefab"}}]`; empty when nothing changed). A scene whose prefabs are unchanged since its save opens clean with `rebuiltInstances` empty, whatever its instances override (a model's Animator layer at `Speed` 0.8 included: an override's value is recorded as the file holds it); one listed although neither its prefab nor a prefab it nests changed had a saved record that no longer matched it (a hand-edited file), and once saved it opens clean too. Check those instances (`scene_tree`, `entity_get`), then `scene_save` the scene: recording, replay verification, test runs and export play the saved file, and `test_run` refuses while a scene it plays has unsaved changes. A rebuild writes an instance's overrides of one component together, so fields valid only together (`UiRect`'s `AnchorMin` and `AnchorMax`) survive; an override the new version makes invalid is dropped alone, its valid siblings kept, and every dropped one is a `PREFAB_STALE_OVERRIDE` entry of `scene_open`'s `diagnostics` and of the `rebuildDiagnostics` of `prefab_apply`, `prefab.revert`, `asset_import`, `asset.reimport` and `asset.setImportSettings`, and of `edit_undo` and `edit_redo` when the step they undo or redo wrote a prefab that other open scenes follow (the scene, the instance root and the override's place in its record): set the value again on the instance if it still matters.
- **Per-instance values.** `prefab_instantiate`'s `components` sets instance overrides in the same command, as the inspector would: `{<Component>: {<Field>: value}}`, script fields through `Script: {Scripts: [{Fields: {...}}]}`, one entry per root slot, replacing the root's list: an entry without `Script` patches that slot, keeping what it omits (`{}` leaves the slot as it is; its script, `ExecutionOrder` and other fields kept; `null` removes a field). It works inside `edit_batch` and with `dryRun`, so a grid of variants is one op per cell:

```json
{"label": "Brick grid", "ops": [
	{"method": "prefab.instantiate", "params": {"prefab": "Assets/Prefabs/Brick.prefab", "parent": "/Bricks", "name": "Brick_1_01",
		"transform": {"Translation": [-7.2, 5.5, 0]},
		"components": {"MeshRenderer": {"Materials": ["Assets/Materials/BrickRed.material"]}, "Script": {"Scripts": [{"Fields": {"Points": 5}}]}}}}]}
```

## Replacing imported art

- **New bytes, same asset.** Art made outside the editor (a Blender kit piece, a texture, a sound) is refined by importing each new version over the old one: `asset_import {source, destDir, replace: true}`, with `destDir` the folder that holds the asset under the same file name, or the asset's own path (`"Assets/Kit/Wall.gltf"`) for a new file of another name. The asset keeps its `.meta`: its id, its import settings (unless you pass `settings`, which then replace them as for a first import) and the id of every sub-asset whose key the new file still has (`mesh:<i>:<name>`, `material:<i>:<name>`, `texture:<i>`, `skin:<i>`, `animation:<i>:<name>`). So kit prefabs, scenes, materials and Animator clips that reference them keep working and show the new version, and the glTF's own instances and the prefabs nesting it follow at once (an undo step of their own). A glTF's buffers and images are copied with it; those the old version used and the new one does not stay in place.
- **What it reports.** `keptSubAssets`, `addedSubAssets` and `removedSubAssets` (`{key, id, type}`), the files written (`copiedFiles`) and the files left unreferenced (`unreferencedFiles`). A glTF key is an index and a name, so renaming or reordering meshes in the exporter changes keys: a reference to a removed sub-asset draws the placeholder and `project_validate` reports it as `ASSET_MISSING`; assign the new sub-asset to it. Preview with `dryRun: true`, which writes nothing; one `edit_undo` restores the previous bytes.
- **Refusals.** No imported asset at the destination (`NotFound`), a file another importer takes (`InvalidArgument`: a `.png` cannot replace a `.gltf`), a new file that does not import (`ImportFailed` at `/source`: the replace imports it in memory first, so a broken export changes nothing), and a buffer or image the new glTF names where a different file of another asset lies (`AlreadyExists`: give each glTF its own copy). Each writes nothing. Without `replace`, an existing file is still `AlreadyExists`.

## Tracks, levels and collisions

- **Tracks and levels.** Give each level a root entity with a `Static` `RigidBody` and instantiate every track piece under it: the pieces join one static compound body, so a rolling ball crosses seams without bumps. Separate static bodies that touch are reported as `PHYSICS_ADJACENT_STATIC_BODIES`, and `project_validate {fix: ["PHYSICS_ADJACENT_STATIC_BODIES"]}` adds that root body for you. Lay out a level as one `edit_batch` of `prefab.instantiate` ops under the root (`"parent": "/Level"`), plus ordered waypoint entities along the main route for the autopilot.
- **A track kit** (as Rolling Ball's is built): every visible part of a piece is `engine://Meshes/Cube` with a default `BoxCollider` (`HalfExtents` 0.5), so the collider is exactly the drawn box at any scale, and no piece holds a `RigidBody`, so each one instantiated under the level root joins its compound. Single-box pieces (a straight deck, a ramp, a narrow beam) are pivoted at the box's centre and sized per instance by `prefab_instantiate`'s `transform` (`Scale`), which sets only the instance's root. Composite pieces (a curve of fixed deck and rail boxes, a jump's kicker) keep an unscaled root pivoted at the centre of the entry edge on the deck's top, heading local −Z, with fixed-size children, so one op places each piece where the previous one ends. Moving platforms (a `Kinematic` `RigidBody` moved by `MoveKinematic`) and triggers (below) keep bodies of their own. Waypoints (`PathNode001`, `PathNode002`, ..., sorted by name) and gap markers (a box tagged `Gap` over each void) are tagged entities with only a `Transform`: nothing draws them, and `entity_bounds` measures only what draws, so check them with `scene_query` and the suite's support check.
- **Mixed floors and belts** (M36, World Tree's briefs 15 and 16): keep one `Static` `/Level` and give a floor piece a `ColliderSurface` of its own, in its prefab or per instance: polished ice at `Friction` 0.02 beside marble that keeps the level's 0.5, a bouncing pad (`Restitution`), a belt (`SurfaceVelocity`, m/s along the piece's own axes, so a turned belt carries along its length). Contacts with that piece take its surface, so debris slides on the ice and rests on the marble with no seam and no `PHYSICS_ADJACENT_STATIC_BODIES`, and a belt carries crates, shards and characters while its piece stays where it is. A surface belongs to the colliders on its own entity: put it on the entity that holds the piece's `BoxCollider`, not on a parent or a trigger (`PHYSICS_SURFACE_UNUSED`). A behaviour that writes the belt's `SurfaceVelocity` stops or reverses it (`luau-gameplay`'s `characters-and-input` page), and characters read their footing with `Physics.GetSurface(controller:GetGroundCollider())` (nil in the air).
- **Triggers on a track** (checkpoints, a goal) carry a sensor `RigidBody` of their own beside their `IsTrigger` collider, on a layer of their own that collides with the ball's and that the track's queries leave out (`{Type: "Static", Layer: "Trigger"}`), which the engine creates as a Kinematic sensor that follows its entity. A trigger collider without one gets an implicit sensor body on the layer of its nearest ancestor `RigidBody`, under a level root the track's layer, and every query sees sensors (`Physics.Raycast` included), so every track-masked ray, such as a ground probe or a main-path support check, would hit the trigger volumes.
- **Entities that report their own collisions** (bricks, targets, pickups): a scripted collider merged into a root's compound body gets its own enter and exit, but at a seam only one piece may be notified (`luau-gameplay`, "Per-entity collisions"). So pieces that must each report a hit at a shared seam, such as Breakout bricks, keep their own `Static` `RigidBody` (in the prefab) under a root without a body, with a 0.1 m gap between neighbours so they never touch and `PHYSICS_ADJACENT_STATIC_BODIES` stays quiet; scripted pieces never hit on a shared seam (targets, pickups apart from each other) may share one static root. Walls the ball only bounces off can share one compound body under a root. `luau-gameplay` has the body settings of a constant-speed ball and a player-driven paddle.
