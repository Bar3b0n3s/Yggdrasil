# Look

A reference page of the `game-building` skill (`skills/game-building`), for its step "3. Scenes, assets and prefabs".

- **Defaults.** `PostProcess` has GTAO (`SsaoEnabled`), bloom (`BloomEnabled`, `BloomIntensity` 0.04) and FXAA on, with AgX tonemapping; a scene without one renders with these defaults. Exposure changes only through a `PostProcess`: without one, tune light and `Environment` intensities and material colours. A `DirectionalLight` casts PCSS soft shadows (`CastShadows`), whose penumbra radius is (receiver depth − blocker depth) × tan(`LightAngle` / 2), clamped: the default `LightAngle` 1 gives near-hard edges, about 10 looks soft. Its up to four cascades spread over `ShadowDistance` (default 100 m); about twice the camera's distance to the board keeps them sharp.
- **Point and spot lights.** A `PointLight` lights a sphere of `Range` metres, a `SpotLight` a cone down its local -Z; both cast soft shadows with `CastShadows`, the penumbra growing with `SourceRadius`. A view shadows at most 16 point lights and 16 spot lights, the most important it sees (`Intensity` × the brightest `Color` channel over the squared distance from the camera to the light's sphere): the four most important point lights at full shadow resolution, the other twelve at half. The rest light without shadows, counted by `stats.get`'s `droppedPointShadows` and `droppedSpotShadows`, and `project.validate` warns `RENDER_POINT_SHADOW_BUDGET` and `RENDER_SPOT_SHADOW_BUDGET` past 16 enabled in a scene. A light keeps its shadow until a newcomer is a quarter more important, so shadows do not flicker as the camera moves. Static lights cost little: a shadow whose light and casters did not move is reused from the last frame (`stats.get`'s `cachedPointShadows` and `cachedSpotShadows`), so braziers and lanterns that nothing passes draw no shadow per frame (only a cheap check of the casters in their range); a moving character redraws only the shadows whose range it is in (the spot shadows all together). A point light whose `Range` is not above twice its `SourceRadius` (at least 0.05 m) never casts. Dozens of small lights are cheap: each pixel shades only the lights of its 64-pixel tile and depth slice (`debugView: "LightCount"`, `game-debugging` item 7), so keep each `Range` as small as the look allows.
- **Light budgets.** A view shades at most `Rendering.MaxShadedLights` lights: 256 by default, or 512, 1024 or 2048 (`project_set_settings {patch: {Rendering: {MaxShadedLights: 1024}}}`); beyond it the least important are dropped (`RENDER_LIGHT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, and `stats.get`'s `maxShadedLights` shows the cap in force). It is a project setting, so every scene and view of the project uses it: raise it for a project whose scenes need it (a fire realm full of embers), not by habit. A larger cap costs every view a little more cluster memory and light-culling dispatch (about 1.6 MB at 1,024 lights and 1920 × 1080), whatever its lights; only the shading cost follows the lights actually there.
- **Repeated meshes.** Entities that draw the same mesh with the same `Opaque` or `Mask` material draw as one instanced draw per pass, whatever their transforms, `CastShadows` and `ReceiveShadows` (`Blend` ones only where the back-to-front order puts them next to each other): build a field of bricks or tiles from one mesh and a few shared materials rather than a material per piece. `stats.get`'s pass rows show it (`drawCalls` counts the instanced draws, `triangles` every instance's).
- **Static scenery's draws** (M43): under a `Static` root (`prefabs-and-levels`), each view draws one indexed draw per key (mesh, submesh, material and variant) of the static items it sees, and each shadow face one per key of the static casters it sees, so a static island's `DirectionalShadows` draws are of the order of its `ForwardOpaque` draws (`stats.get`'s pass rows). A mesh with morph targets whose default weights are not 0 draws undeformed as static scenery, and the renderer warns once per entity: give that entity `Static {Scope: NotStatic}` to draw it deformed.
- **Per-entity material parameters.** A `MaterialParameters` component beside a `MeshRenderer` overrides every material of that one entity without a material per state (M31): `Tint` multiplies the base colour (an elite's variant), `Emissive` × `EmissiveStrength` adds a glow (a hit flash: animate `EmissiveStrength` alone), `UVOffset` shifts and `UVScroll` scrolls the textures (flowing lava: texture units per second of the world's clock, `Time.GetTime()`'s; it moves in play only, so the scene view shows the offset alone, and holds while the world is paused), `DissolveAmount` 0 to 1 dissolves it by a built-in noise over its first texture coordinates with a glowing edge (`DissolveEdgeColor` × `DissolveEdgeStrength`, `DissolveEdgeWidth`; `DissolveNoiseScale` tiles the noise), its shadow with it, and `Opacity` fades an opaque entity by a dither that TAA resolves into a smooth fade over its 8-frame cycle (a wall that hides the player: it keeps its shadow and depth; without TAA the dither pattern shows). Skinned meshes take them too, and entities that differ only in them still draw as one instanced draw; a dissolving or fading entity draws with the Mask pipelines, one more draw. Its fields are hashed like any component's: drive them from scripts in the fixed step (`entity.MaterialParameters.DissolveAmount`), never from frame time.
- **Receivers.** Shadows and GTAO show only on surfaces the camera sees: a board viewed straight on needs a backboard behind it to receive them. A `Blend` material casts no shadow and takes no GTAO.
- **Glow.** Bloom has no threshold: it blends `BloomIntensity` of a blurred copy into the image, so at the default 0.04 the halo is barely visible, and emission alone pales the colours towards white before it glows. A tested pair, from M17's 3D Tetris (history: Tetris is rebuilt as pixel art under a pixel-perfect camera, whose 2D colour path has no bloom), under ACES at `ExposureEV` -0.8: `Emissive` the piece colour with `EmissiveStrength` 4 (6 for an accent line) and `BloomIntensity` 0.3; a high intensity also lifts the shadows. `BloomIntensity` is a scene component, hashed (set it before recording replays); `EmissiveStrength` is a material value, not hashed. Prove the glow with two screenshots in play at one tick, `BloomEnabled` true then false (`entity_update {target: "Play"}`).
- **Environments.** An `Environment` lights the scene with an imported equirectangular HDR image, twice as wide as high: `asset_import` takes Radiance `.hdr` and OpenEXR `.exr` (single-part scanline or tiled, its R, G and B channels; NONE, RLE, ZIPS, ZIP, PIZ or PXR24 compression). Its `Compression` setting `"Auto"`, the default, stores the skybox and reflection cubes as BC6H blocks, an eighth of RGBA16F's memory; `"None"` keeps them RGBA16F. The built-in Sky and Studio are BC6H.
- **Background.** An orthographic camera's skybox is one flat colour: set `Camera.Clear` to `"Color"` with a dark `ClearColor` and keep the environment for lighting.
- **Fog.** A scene's `Fog` (distance and height fog, an ambient tint and a sun glow) hides distance, depth and the world's edge; with `FogSkybox` it fogs the sky, and the clear colour of a camera that clears to one, so a void below a bridge fades into it. The [post-processing page](post-processing.md)'s Fog section has the fields and the recipes.
- **Mirrors.** A `PlanarReflection` makes a flat glass a true mirror: put it on the glass's own entity, the one whose `MeshRenderer` draws it, and set `Normal` to the axis the glass faces in that entity's local axes (the built-in Quad faces +Z, its default; the Plane +Y; for one face of a thin box, the box's thin axis with `Offset` 0.5 for its face), with a polished metal material (`Metallic` 1, `Roughness` 0.03 to 0.1; rougher glass blurs it, and from `PostProcess.SsrMaxRoughness` up it reflects only the probes). `RENDER_REFLECTION_INVALID` warns when the plane misses the glass and names the right axis and `Offset`; set the new `Normal` axis before zeroing the old one, since a zero `Normal` is refused. Each view draws at most 4 mirrors, the largest on screen first, each a second render of the scene at `ResolutionScale` (0.5 by default, a quarter of the pixels) from the camera mirrored in the glass. Three at 1080p must stay within the 2 ms budget (Perf.py's room of three mirrors measured a median of 0.188 ms on the development machine), and the cost grows with what each mirror redraws, so keep mirrors few and small on screen, and lower `ResolutionScale` for a large one. A mirror past the cap, out of view or seen from behind shows its probes and SSR; `stats.get`'s `planarReflections`, `droppedPlanarReflections` and `PlanarReflection<k>` rows say which drew and what each cost.
- **Screen text.** A `Screen` `Text` sits at `Anchor` (viewport-normalized: (0, 0) top-left, Y down), its block's `Pivot` on that point, moved by `Offset`; `Size` and `Offset` are pixels at the 1080p reference, scaled by viewport height / 1080 (× 0.83 at 900 px). The default font's digits are proportional, so a number centred on its anchor jitters as it changes: pin its left or right edge (`Pivot` x 0 or 1). A `World` text's em is `Size` / 100 metres before the entity's scale.
- **GTAO.** An occluder further than `SsaoRadius` in front of a surface casts little occlusion on it: set it above the depth gap between the pieces and the surface behind them. GTAO darkens the environment's image-based light only, never direct lights, so it shows mostly in `debugView: "AO"` and where ambient light dominates.
- **Framing.** Iterate the camera on game-view screenshots at the window's size (`width` and `height`), taken in play (`target: "Play"`) once the runtime entities exist, and with `debugView: "AO"` or `"ShadowCascades"`, which colours each surface by its cascade (`game-debugging` item 7): one colour over an orthographic board means it lies in one cascade, which `CascadeCount` 1 suits.
- **Iterate in play, then record.** Try values with `entity_update {target: "Play"}` in the lockstep session, which leaves the scene file and the replays alone, then write the chosen ones to the scene. `asset_get_properties {asset}` reads a material's or sound effect's values. Tune a `.material` against the running game: `asset_set_properties` of a material shows in the lockstep session's next frame (`play_step` once, then `viewport_screenshot {view: "game"}`), without a restart and without changing the state hash; any other asset's change (a texture, a mesh, a sound effect) waits until the session ends, so stop and restart play to see it. Finish the look before recording replays: every look component is hashed (the skill's "5. Verify deterministically"), so changing one later means re-recording every replay ("Re-recording replays" in `skills/game-building/replays`).
