# Effects

A reference page of the `game-building` skill (`skills/game-building`), for its steps "3. Scenes, assets and prefabs" and "5. Verify deterministically": particles and baked lighting probes, and what a builder checks.

## Particles

- **The emitter** (`component_schema {name: "ParticleEmitter"}`). While `Playing`, it emits `Rate` particles a second and `Burst` at the start of each cycle of `Duration` seconds; without `Loop`, one cycle. A new particle starts from its `Shape`: `Point`, `Sphere` (inside `ShapeRadius`), `Cone` (on a disc of `ShapeRadius`, within `ConeAngle` of the entity's local -Z: turn the entity to aim it, `EulerAngles` [90, 0, 0] points it up) or `Box` (`BoxHalfExtents`, moving down local -Z). It draws its speed, lifetime and size from the `Speed`, `Lifetime` and `Size` ranges (minimum, maximum), then accelerates by `Acceleration` (add (0, -9.81, 0) for gravity), slows by `Drag` and dies at its lifetime. `ColorOverLife` and `SizeOverLife` hold at most eight keys in ascending `Time` (the particle's age over its lifetime, 0 to 1). `SimulationSpace` `World` leaves a trail behind a moving emitter; `Local` moves the live particles with it. At most `MaxParticles` (up to 4096) live at once; beyond them nothing is emitted.
- **Drawing.** Each particle is a square of its size facing the camera (unless a `ParticleLook` lays it otherwise, below), its `Texture` (null: the built-in soft disc, `engine://Textures/Particle`) times its colour. `Blend` `Alpha` draws an emitter's particles back to front; `Additive` adds them (sparks, fire, glows). Particles are tested against the opaque scene's depth without writing it, after the meshes and sprites and before bloom, so they glow with bloom and are hidden by walls; under TAA they write their motion, so they stay sharp. Emitters draw one after another in scene order, so two overlapping emitters never interleave by depth. Particles are unlit, and data views, the overdraw view and the wireframe show none.
- **The look** (`component_schema {name: "ParticleLook"}`, on the emitter's entity). Every default draws as an emitter without one. A flipbook: `SpriteSheet` (its frames replace the emitter's `Texture`) with the range `FirstFrame` and `FrameCount` (0: to the sheet's last frame); `FrameMode` `OverLife` plays the range `FrameCycles` times over each particle's life (`BlendFrames` cross-fades each frame into the next, smooth for short ranges), `Random` picks one frame per particle (debris, varied puffs); a range past the sheet's frames draws nothing, and `project_validate` warns `PARTICLE_FRAME_OUT_OF_RANGE`. Cut square frames with a transparent texel between them: a frame fills the particle's square, and Linear sampling reads half a texel past a frame's edge. Rotation: `StartRotation` and `Spin` are ranges in degrees and degrees per second (counter-clockwise as seen). `Alignment` `Camera` faces the camera, `Velocity` stretches each particle along its motion across the view (sparks, embers, rain: length size plus speed times `VelocityStretch` seconds), `Flat` lays it in the entity's local XZ plane (ground rings, ripples: keep the entity level, its -Z the texture's top). `SoftDistance` (metres) fades particles out as they near the surface behind them instead of cutting them along a hard line (0.2 to 1 for flames and smoke on a floor). `Intensity` above 1 makes colours brighter than white, so with `PostProcess.BloomEnabled` embers and sparks glow. The look's angles and frames come from the emitter's second random stream: adding a look never changes where the particles go, and a digest with a look is the same in every build too.
- **Presentation only.** Particles advance in the frame phase, one fixed step per tick since the last frame, after the scripts' `OnUpdate` and `OnLateUpdate` and before the frame is drawn; a paused session freezes them. They never collide, never enter the state hash and never change a replay. Edit scenes simulate none: see them in play, `viewport_screenshot {view: "Game", target: "Play"}` after `play_step`. Colour and size change at the tick rate (positions are interpolated between ticks).
- **Seeded.** Each emitter's stream is seeded by its `Seed` and its entity's id, so a replay shows the same particles in every build, and two emitters with one `Seed` still differ. A suite pins an emitter with `Test.GetParticleDigest(entity)` after a fixed number of ticks, the same in the Editor, Release and Dist runs; bind such a suite with `Isolation: "Case"` so each case starts from the scene's first tick.
- **Scripts.** `ParticleEmitter:Emit(count)` (1 to 4096) queues particles for the next frame whatever `Playing` says (a stopped emitter is a one-shot effect a script fires), and `Clear()` kills them and drops what is queued. `GetParticleCount()` reads them in `OnUpdate`, `OnLateUpdate` or a test case and raises in fixed-step code: never let particles decide gameplay (`skills/luau-gameplay`).

## Baked lighting probes

- **Irradiance volumes** (`IrradianceVolume`) bake the light bouncing around a region: a grid of `ProbeCountX` x `ProbeCountY` x `ProbeCountZ` probes (2 to 16 a side, the outer ones on the faces) over a box of `Size` metres centred on the entity. Inside the box, surfaces take their diffuse ambient light from the probes instead of the scene's `Environment`, fading back to it over `BlendDistance` metres, times `Intensity`: a red wall tints the white floor beside it, and a closed room is no longer lit by the sky.
- **Reflection probes** (`ReflectionProbe`) bake what glossy surfaces inside their box reflect, a cube of `Resolution` texels a face (a power of two from 32 to 512) captured from the entity's position. Keep `BoxProjection` on in a room: it corrects the reflection for the box, so it lines up with the walls; turn it off for an open area, which reflects as if far away. A probe's data is an `Environment` asset, so a hand-made environment works too.
- **Baking.** Save the scene, then `lighting_bake` (the editor's Tools > Bake lighting) in an editor launched with `renderer: "vulkan"` and not playing. It captures every enabled probe of the scene (`irradianceVolumes` or `reflectionProbes` false skips that kind), writes `Assets/Lighting/<the scene's path below Assets/, without .scene>/<entity id>.irradiance` or `.hdr` (`Assets/Scenes/Room.scene` into `Assets/Lighting/Scenes/Room/`), imports them and sets each probe's `Data` in one undo step; then `scene_save`. A probe out of its ranges refuses the whole bake before anything renders. The captures see the scene's lights, environment and meshes once (one bounce: no earlier bake, particles, sprites or UI, and no SSR or TAA, which are a view's), so re-bake after moving a probe or changing the room. A bake frees what each capture and import used before the next, so its GPU allocations stay the same whatever the probe count, and baking again, as often as tuning takes, adds none (`stats.get`'s `memoryAllocationCount`). Two bakes of an unchanged scene on one machine write the same bytes, so re-baking changes nothing there; another graphics device or driver may bake other bytes, so commit the bake with the scene.
- **Limits.** Where boxes overlap, the smaller wins; a view uses at most eight volumes and eight reflection probes, the first in scene order among those in view. Each reflection probe costs an environment's memory whatever its `Resolution`.
- **Checks.** `project_validate` warns `PROBE_NOT_BAKED` for a probe without `Data` or whose `Data` does not load; it lights or reflects nothing until baked. A `viewport_screenshot` of the edit scene shows the baked light; compare it with the probe disabled to see what the bake adds.
