# 2D games

A reference page of the `game-debugging` skill (`skills/game-debugging`): why a sprite or tilemap does not show, sort, animate, light or look sharp, and why a 2D body or a tilemap collider does not move or collide as expected. How 2D games are built: `skills/game-building/sprites-2d`; the script surface: `skills/luau-gameplay/sprites-2d`.

- **A sprite draws nothing.** Its sheet is missing or fails to load, or its `Frame` is not an index of the sheet's frames (`SPRITE_FRAME_OUT_OF_RANGE`): such a sprite draws, picks and bounds nothing. A missing or failing texture (the sheet's or `Texture`) is not invisible: it shows the Missing checker over the sprite's quad. `entity.get` shows the `SpriteRenderer`, `asset.getProperties {asset: <sheet>}` its frames, and `project.validate` names both codes. Check also that the entity and its parents are active, and that the sprite lies inside the camera's clip range (z between the camera's near and far planes).
- **A sprite is too small, too large or misplaced.** Its size is its texels over its `PixelsPerUnit` (16 by default) times the entity's scale, and its pivot (the frame's `Pivot`, or the texture's centre) sits on the entity's position; `entity.bounds` reports the quad the sprite draws, picks and bounds with.
- **The draw order is wrong.** Sprites draw by (sorting layer, `OrderInLayer`, canonical entity order). A `SortingLayer` that `SortingLayers.Layers` does not declare draws in the first layer (`SPRITE_UNKNOWN_SORTING_LAYER`, a validation warning). Distance from the camera never orders sprites. A mesh in front of a sprite hides it, but `viewport.pick` still reports the sprite (sprites write no pick id: an accepted limit of scenes that mix them).
- **Data views, overdraw and the wireframe show no sprites:** sprites draw in the lit view only, so a `debugView` other than `Lit` leaves them out of screenshots, and so does `viewport.setOptions {wireframe: true}`, whose mesh edges replace the shaded image.
- **A pixel-art image is blurry or uneven.** Without a `PixelPerfectCamera` on the primary camera's entity, a texel spans no whole number of pixels, so texels come out uneven, and the image is tonemapped. With one, compare its `PixelsPerUnit` with the sprites' (equal values map one texel to one scene pixel), keep frame pivots on multiples of 1 / Width and 1 / Height, and take the game-view screenshot at the window's size: the reference resolution is upscaled by a whole factor, so a smaller view letterboxes it.
- **An animation does not advance.** `SpriteAnimator.Playing` is false (a held clip clears it after its last step), `Clip` names no clip of the sheet (`SPRITE_UNKNOWN_CLIP`, logged once per entity and clip at run time, and a validation warning), or the session is paused: clips advance one fixed step at a time (`play.step {ticks}` steps them in lockstep). A clip played from a contact callback or `OnDestroy` shows its first step from the next tick.
- **A lit sprite or tilemap is black or wrongly lit.** A lit item (`SpriteRenderer.Lit`, `TilemapRenderer.Lit`) shows only the light its 2D lights give it: without a `Light2D` reaching it, it is black. A `Point` light reaches `Radius` world units from its entity and fades over the outer `Falloff` of that radius, whatever the entity's scale; a `Global` light lights every lit pixel; lights add. Unlit sprites ignore every light, so an item that does not react is not lit. A normal-mapped sprite whose shading looks inverted or noisy has a normal map imported as a colour texture: import it with `settings: {Usage: "NormalMap"}` (`asset.getImportSettings` shows it); its +Y is the image's top. A point light with a small `Height` grazes a normal-mapped sprite, leaving its far side dark. A view adds at most 1,024 lights that reach it (logged once when more do). Lights draw in the lit view only, like sprites.
- **A tilemap draws nothing, or some tiles are missing.** Its `Tilemap` is null, missing or fails to load (tilemaps have no placeholder: the log names it), a palette entry's sheet is missing, or its `Frame` is not one the sheet has (`TILEMAP_FRAME_OUT_OF_RANGE`, a validation warning on the `.tilemap` naming `Palette[<i>].Frame`; its cells draw nothing). Tiles too small, too large or with gaps: a tile is its frame's texels over the renderer's `PixelsPerUnit`, centred on its cell, whatever `CellSize` says. `asset.getProperties {asset: <tilemap>}` lists the palette and the chunks; `entity.bounds` reports the drawn tiles' quads. A `SortingLayer` the project lacks is `SPRITE_UNKNOWN_SORTING_LAYER`, as for a sprite.
- **A body falls through a tilemap, or hops on it.** Only cells whose palette entry is `Solid` collide; the `TilemapCollider` needs its own `Tilemap` or the one of its entity's `TilemapRenderer`, and `IsTrigger` makes it a sensor. A collider without a tilemap, with one that is missing or fails to load, or with more than 65,536 merged boxes is `PHYSICS_INVALID_SHAPE` (`Component` TilemapCollider, `Field` Tilemap) and makes no body; a map without a solid cell makes none, quietly. A ball that hops at the seams between the chunks' boxes needs `RigidBody2D.EnhancedInternalEdgeRemoval`. A `tilemap.paint` during a lockstep, recording, replay or test session reaches the play session only after it ends.
- **A 2D body does not move.** It needs a `RigidBody2D` of `Type` `Dynamic` and a 2D collider; an entity that also holds a 3D physics component keeps only its 3D body (`PHYSICS2D_MIXED_WORLDS`, a validation error). A body moves in X and Y and turns about Z only; a parent or entity rotated about another axis is `PHYSICS2D_OFF_PLANE` and the body takes its rotation about Z. Gravity is `Physics2D.Gravity` times the body's `GravityFactor`; layers and collision pairs are `Physics.Layers` and `Physics.Collisions`. The 3D codes (`PHYSICS_INVALID_SHAPE`, `PHYSICS_UNKNOWN_LAYER`, `PHYSICS_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, ...) report a 2D collider's problems too, naming the 2D component.
- **What the 2D world holds:** in a play session, `physics2d.raycast {origin, direction, maxDistance, all: true}` and `physics2d.overlap {shape, center, radius | halfExtents}` report the bodies a ray or shape meets, with the tick they read; both need a session (`InvalidState` before `play.start`). A polygon's corners are rounded by up to 5 cm for contacts, so a square `PolygonCollider2D` lets a box slide off it a little earlier than a `BoxCollider2D`.
- **See the 2D colliders:** `viewport.setOptions {colliders: true}` (the scene view's Colliders option) and `viewport.screenshot {annotate: {colliders: true}}` draw every 2D collider in the plane of its entity: a box's rectangle, a circle, a polygon's convex hull and a tilemap's merged boxes, coloured by category as the 3D colliders are (static, kinematic, dynamic, sleeping, trigger, and invalid for a collider the world refused). In a play scene they show the session's bodies, awake or asleep.
- **What a 2D body is:** in a play session, `engine_call {method: "physics2d.bodyInfo", params: {entity}}` reports the body the entity's 2D colliders belong to: `body`, `origin` (its own `RigidBody2D`, `ImplicitStatic` for colliders without one, `ImplicitSensor` for an entity's trigger colliders), `type`, `isSensor`, `layer`, `colliders` (each collider entity once, a tilemap's entity for all its boxes), `position` and `angle` (degrees about +Z), the velocities, `sleeping`, the bounds rectangle and the `contacts`, sorted by partner, with the `tick`. `NotFound` at `/entity` means the entity has no 2D body (no 2D collider, or a validation error kept its body from being created); `InvalidState` means no play session runs.
- **2D callbacks do not fire.** They are `OnCollisionEnter2D`, `OnCollisionExit2D`, `OnTriggerEnter2D` and `OnTriggerExit2D`, never the 3D names; a trigger pair needs one `IsTrigger` collider, and both bodies' layers must collide. Events arrive sorted by entity, so their order is the same in every run.
- **A replay of a 2D game diverges.** The 2D world's block of the state hash, after the native block, covers every 2D body's position, angle, velocities and sleep state; animation and sorting are component data, which the scene's serialization covers. Diagnose with `play.state`, `entity.get {target: "Play"}` and the checkpoints `input.replay` compares, as for any replay.
