### 2. Physics: the layers, the level root, the track kit, the ball, the triggers and the platforms

- **Layers.** `Ball` holds the ball, `Track` every deck, rail, wall and moving platform, `Trigger` every checkpoint and goal. Only Ball–Track and Ball–Trigger collide: kinematic platforms never meet the static track, and no query on `Track` sees a trigger.
- **The level root.** Each level's `/Level` entity holds `RigidBody {Type: Static, Layer: "Track", Friction: 0.8, Restitution: 0}` at the origin with an identity transform. Every track piece, rail and wall is a collider-only descendant, gathered into one `StaticCompoundShape`, so the ball crosses seams without bumps (§5.3, §9.2). Moving platforms, checkpoints and the goal carry their own `RigidBody` and stay separate bodies. Friction and restitution are the body's, so the level root sets the whole track's.
- **The track kit** (`Assets/Prefabs/`). Every visible part is `engine://Meshes/Cube` with a `BoxCollider` of the default `HalfExtents` 0.5, so the collider is exactly the drawn box at any scale. Decks are 0.5 m thick; a piece's top surface is its deck. Two pivots, chosen so that one `prefab.instantiate` op places a piece:
  - **Single-box pieces,** pivot at the box's centre, sized per instance by the instance's `Scale` (`prefab.instantiate` sets the root's transform and overrides only, P07): `Straight` (default scale (4, 0.5, 8), `Track` material), `Ramp` (the same box pitched by `EulerAngles` x, `Ramp` material) and `Narrow` (default (1.2, 0.5, 8), `Narrow` material). Decision 3's tables give each instance's `Translation`, `EulerAngles` and `Scale`.
  - **Composite pieces,** an unscaled root whose pivot is the centre of the entry edge on the deck's top surface, heading local −Z, with fixed-size children:
    - `CurveLeft`: a 90° left turn of centreline radius 6 m and width 4 m, as six deck boxes of 15° each and six outer rail boxes (`Rail` material). Deck *k* (1 to 6) at mid angle φ = 15*k* − 7.5°: local `Translation` (−6 + 6 cos φ, −0.25, −6 sin φ), `EulerAngles` (0, φ, 0), `Scale` (4, 0.5, 2.106) (the outer chord, so the outer corners meet and the inner ends overlap inside the compound). Rail *k*: (−6 + 8.1 cos φ, −0.05, −8.1 sin φ), (0, φ, 0), (0.2, 0.9, 2.159): 0.4 m above the deck, reaching down to its underside. Deck 1 is at (−0.051, −0.25, −0.783), deck 6 at (−5.217, −0.25, −5.949). Exit: (−6, 0, −6), heading +90°.
    - `CurveRight`: `CurveLeft` mirrored, each child's x and yaw negated. Exit (6, 0, −6), heading −90°.
    - `Gap` (a jump): a `Kicker` box at local (0, −0.045, −1.049), `EulerAngles` (11.31, 0, 0), `Scale` (4, 0.5, 2.04) (`Kicker` material: a run of 2 m rising 0.4 m), then a 3 m void, and the `GapMarker` child (decision 3) at (0, 0, −3.5) with `Scale` (4.4, 6, 3.2) and the tag `Gap`. Exit: (0, 0, −5), the landing deck being the next piece.
  - **`MovingPlatform`:** one box at its root, sized per instance (4 × 0.5 × 4 in both levels), `RigidBody {Type: Kinematic, Layer: "Track", Friction: 1}`, `MovingPlatform.luau` (decision 4), `Platform` material.
  - **`Checkpoint` and `Goal`** below. With `Ball` and `HUD` that is eleven prefabs, each built in a scratch entity and saved with `prefab_create` (`asset_create {type: "Prefab"}` takes no values).
- **The ball** (`Ball.prefab`, tag `Ball`): `MeshRenderer` `engine://Meshes/Sphere` (radius 0.5) with the `Ball` material, `SphereCollider {Radius: 0.5}`, and `RigidBody {Type: Dynamic, Mass: 1, Friction: 0.8, Restitution: 0, LinearDamping: 0.1, AngularDamping: 0.4, MotionQuality: "LinearCast", AllowSleeping: false, Layer: "Ball", MaxAngularVelocity: 120, EnhancedInternalEdgeRemoval: true}`, plus the roll's `AudioSource` (decision 4) and `Ball.luau`.
  - `LinearCast`, so a fast ball never tunnels through a 0.5 m deck; `EnhancedInternalEdgeRemoval` with the compound, so seams never bump; `MaxAngularVelocity` 120 rad/s, so the speed cap is the script's, not Jolt's default 47 rad/s (23 m/s for this ball, §9.6).
  - `AllowSleeping` false: a ball under the player's control is never left asleep, and one active body costs nothing.
  - Friction 0.8 against the track's 0.8 (their geometric mean): with the starting torque (below) a rolling ball never slips (it needs about 3.4 N of the 7.8 N available).
- **Control** (`Ball.luau`, `OnFixedUpdate`; starting values the builder tunes and records in `AGENTS.md`, P10):
  - **The heading** is the ball's own fixed-step state, `Heading` (degrees, 0 facing −Z, positive turning left, `Transform.EulerAngles` y's convention): while the ball is grounded and its horizontal speed exceeds `HeadingMinSpeed` (2.5 m/s), it turns towards the direction of the horizontal velocity by at most `HeadingRate` (120°/s) a tick (`Math.DeltaAngle`), and at a spawn it takes the checkpoint's yaw. The follow camera reads it; the simulation never reads the camera (P05: a heading taken from the camera's frame-phase pose would make live play depend on the frame rate, while every lockstep run stays deterministic, so no suite would catch it).
  - **Steering.** `move` = right(Heading) × `MoveX` + forward(Heading) × `MoveZ`, its length clamped to 1. Grounded, the ball takes `AddTorque(vector.cross(up, move) × Torque)` (`Torque` 6 N·m: about 8.6 m/s² on the flat), the torque along the velocity fading to 0 between 80 % and 100 % of `MaxSpeed` (11 m/s) so that jumps stay calibrated, while braking against the velocity always applies. In the air, `AddForce(move × AirForce)` (`AirForce` 3 N).
  - **Grounded** is a `Physics.Raycast` from the ball's centre straight down over 0.65 m on `Physics.LayerMask("Track")`, which sees the decks and the platforms, never the ball itself (`Ball`) or a trigger (`Trigger`).
- **Checkpoints and the goal are explicit sensor bodies.** `Checkpoint.prefab`'s root (tag `Checkpoint`), at the deck's centreline on its top surface and facing along the track, holds `BoxCollider {HalfExtents: (2, 1.5, 0.5), Offset: (0, 1.5, 0), IsTrigger: true}` and `RigidBody {Type: Static, Layer: "Trigger"}`, which the engine creates as a Kinematic sensor that follows its entity (§9.2), plus `Checkpoint.graph` with its `Order` field. Its children only draw: two posts (`engine://Meshes/Cylinder`, (±2.3, 1.25, 0), scale (0.2, 2.5, 0.2), `Post` material), a `Bar` (Cube, (0, 2.6, 0), scale (4.8, 0.2, 0.2), `CheckpointIdle` material) and a `Burst` emitter (decision 5). `Goal.prefab` (tag `Goal`) is the same with `HalfExtents` (2, 1.5, 1), the `Goal` material on posts and bar, a flat `Pad` (Cube, (0, 0.01, 0), scale (4, 0.02, 2), no collider) and the `Sparkle` and `WinBurst` emitters.
  - **Why not implicit sensor bodies,** as §13.11 and §9.6 said until the lead's decision f: an implicit sensor body takes the layer of the nearest `RigidBody` above it, which under the level root is `Track`. A trigger there would be on `Track`, the `Trigger` layer would hold nothing, and every `Track`-masked ray, the support check's and the ground probe's, would hit the trigger volumes, since every query sees sensors and `Physics.Raycast` cannot leave them out. A sensor `RigidBody` of Type Static is the engine's documented way to give a trigger its own layer; nothing is worked around. P04 records it, and the documentation checkpoint before session 1 (the lead's decision f) corrected §13.11 and §9.6, with §5.3's sentence that checkpoints under a level root "just work" and game-building's `prefabs-and-levels` page.
- **Kill plane and respawn.** When the ball's `WorldPosition.y` is below its level's `KillHeight` (a field of the Ball instance, decision 3), `Ball.luau` calls `body:Teleport(spawn, Quat.Identity())`, then `SetLinearVelocity(vector.zero)` and `SetAngularVelocity(vector.zero)` (`Teleport` keeps the velocity), resets `Heading` to the spawn's yaw, counts `Respawns` and plays the fall sound. The spawn is the furthest checkpoint the ball has touched (the highest `Order`, so rolling back never moves it backwards), at its root plus (0, 0.6, 0), or the level's start until then. The timer keeps running.
- **Moving platforms** (`MovingPlatform.luau`, fields `Travel` (vector, metres), `MoveTicks`, `DwellTicks`, `PhaseTicks`): in `OnStart` it keeps its start pose; each `OnFixedUpdate` it counts a tick and calls `body:MoveKinematic(start + Travel × s, startRotation)`, where `s` (`Motion.Ease`, decision 4) dwells at 0 for `DwellTicks`, rises to 1 along (1 − cos(π t / `MoveTicks`)) / 2, dwells at 1 and returns the same way: a period of 2 × (`DwellTicks` + `MoveTicks`) ticks, counted in ticks and computed with the engine's deterministic `math.cos`. It exposes `AtStart`, `AtEnd` and `DwellLeft` (ticks) for the autopilot. Platforms have no rails; their tops are flush with the decks they serve at each end, 5 cm from them.

### 3. The three levels

- **Coordinates.** +Y up, metres. Each level starts at the origin heading −Z. A heading ψ (degrees) faces (−sin ψ, 0, −cos ψ); positive ψ turns left. The tables write negative numbers with a typographic minus: JSON takes them with an ASCII `-`. Every number below was computed by one script from the kit of decision 2 (the plan's scratch `levels.py`, not committed). The builder places pieces with these values as they stand, may adjust a value it can justify (it records each change and its reason in `AGENTS.md` and the friction log), and checks every level with `entity_bounds {entities: ["/Level"]}` against the extents given.
- **One batch per level.** A level is laid out as about one `edit_batch` (§13.11): an op named `level` creating `/Level` with its body, then a `prefab.instantiate` per piece with `parent: {"$ref": "level.entity.id"}` (or `"/Level"`), its `name` (`P01`, ...), `transform` (`Translation`, `EulerAngles`, `Scale`) and, where a field differs, `components`; then the checkpoints, the goal, the platform, the markers and the path nodes. 60 to 90 ops; run it with `dryRun: true` first, and read an offloaded result with `result_read` (P30).
- **Path nodes.** Each level holds an ordered polyline of path nodes: entities named `PathNode001`, `PathNode002`, ... (zero-padded, so their names sort in route order), each `entity.create {parent: "/Level/Path", tags: [...]}` with only a `Transform`, at the ball's centre height (deck top + 0.5 m): the spawn, each piece's entry, every 15° along a curve, a jump's lip, a drop's edge, a platform's boarding point and the goal. A node's tags are `PathNode` plus at most one hint for the autopilot about the segment that starts at it: `Fast` (a jump's run-up, at least 8 m/s), `Slow` (a narrow beam, at most 4.5 m/s) or `Board` (wait here for the nearest moving platform, ride it, then go on; decision 10). Nothing draws a node, and `entity_bounds` reports none (P08).
- **Gap markers.** An entity tagged `Gap` with only a `Transform` whose box (its `Translation`, `EulerAngles` and `Scale`: a sample inside it has every coordinate of `marker.Transform:InverseTransformPoint(sample)` within ±0.5) covers a void the path crosses: each `Gap` piece's own `GapMarker` child, a drop's void and a platform's void. Each spans 0.1 m past the void's edges and at least 3 m above and below the path.
- **Kill heights, pars and spawns.** The ball spawns at (0, 0.6, −3), heading 0. `KillHeight` is the level's lowest deck top less 8 m. The par is the level's design target for the level's own time (not the carried total), shown on the HUD and used as its autopilot case's `TimeoutTicks`: an autopilot run that misses par fails, which keeps the levels' pacing honest.
- **Checkpoints, the goal and the backstop** are placed by their roots (deck top, centreline). The lift's `Backstop` is a `Straight` instance used as a wall under the upper deck's first metre, so a ball that rolls onto the lowered lift stops against it instead of rolling off its far edge.

### 5. Visual scripting and M26's features

- **One behaviour is a graph: `Checkpoint.graph`** (the `graph_*` tools, M24; `skills/game-building/graphs`). On `Event.OnTriggerEnter`: when `other` has the tag `Ball` and `Activated` is false, set `Activated`, set the `Bar` child's material slot 1 to `ActiveMaterial` (the script API's material slots are 1-based: `MeshRenderer:SetMaterial` and `GetMaterial` take index 1 for the first submesh, as the game's graph uses it; this record said slot 0 until the lead's review of the game), `Emit(60)` on the `Burst` child's `ParticleEmitter`, and `Audio.PlayOneShot(Chime, <the checkpoint's position>)`. About twelve nodes in one `edit_batch` of named ops.
  - **Why a graph.** No game has used one; the program built graphs (ADR 0035) for exactly this kind of behaviour, and a game is where their gaps show.
  - **Why the checkpoint.** It is small and event-driven (one trigger event, one guard, three effects, one flag), and it is a prefab's behaviour, so it exercises a graph's per-instance field (`Order`), a graph reacting to a physics event, `graph_live_values` in play, and a Luau behaviour reading a graph's member (the ball reads `Order`, typed `any`).
  - **Why not more.** The gameplay the replays prove stays in Luau: the ball decides its spawn from the trigger event it receives itself, and the goal drives the timer and `Scene.Load`, which belong with `Game.luau`. So a graph limit (the one-signature node library, `any`-typed access; Backlog §6) can cost an effect, never the acceptance.
- **M26's features**, each used only where it serves the look without touching what the replays hash. A component's fields are hashed like any other component's, so each choice is set before the replays are recorded; particles, the adapted exposure and TAA's history are never hashed.

  | Feature | Used | Reason |
  |---|---|---|
  | Particles | yes: each checkpoint's `Burst` (Additive, cyan, `Sphere` of 0.6 m, `Speed` 2 to 4, `Lifetime` 0.5 to 0.9 s, `Size` 0.08 to 0.16, `Acceleration` (0, −4, 0), `Playing` false, emitted by the graph); the goal's `Sparkle` (looping, `Rate` 24, Additive, gold, a `Cone` pointed up) and `WinBurst` (as `Burst`, gold, 150 emitted by `Game.luau`); each with its own `Seed` | Presentation only and seeded, so a replay shows the same particles in every build; feedback for the two triggers; a game's first use of M26's emitters in play |

### 14. Start order and physics bodies (the review's findings)

Taken on 2026-11-15, after the S3 friction checkpoint (`6278a300`), on its independent review, which found two gaps in when an entity created during a session first runs and gets its physics body. Both are fixed in the engine (Session, Scripting and Scene: §5.7's start flush), a departure from §5.7's step order as Architecture stated it, which Architecture now states (§5.6, §5.7, §9.2, §9.3, §9.9).

- **Frame grouping (the review's notes).** A start flush started only the instances that existed when it began, so an instance created by an `OnStart` the flush ran started at the next flush. When the tick of that `OnStart` shared its frame with the next tick (live play, or a suite with a `Clock`), the next flush was a tick later than in lockstep: an entity whose `OnStart` spawned another (a spawner's level, a projectile's trail) gave the spawned one its first `OnFixedUpdate` a tick later on a ScriptedClock than in lockstep, so simulation depended on how ticks were grouped into frames, which §1.3 rules out and which no lockstep run (every replay, every suite without a `Clock`) could see. Reproduced before the fix by "PlaySession: nested spawns from OnStart first run at the same tick however the ticks are grouped into frames" (two and three levels deep, one, two and three fixed steps a frame: Level2's first `OnFixedUpdate` at tick 2 at two or three steps a frame and at tick 1 in lockstep, and the state hashes at tick 6 differing), with its variants for a startup `OnStart` and for one after `Scene.Load`.
- **The body window (friction F19 and the review's finding 1).** A body was created only at a pre-step (§5.7 steps 5 and 7a). An entity created after the tick's pre-step (a physics callback, an `OnDestroy`, `OnUpdate`, `OnLateUpdate`, an evaluation between ticks), or by a scene's startup `OnCreate` or `OnStart` (at the session's start and after `Scene.Load` and `Scene.Reload`, where no pre-step runs before the first tick's), ran its `OnStart` and first `OnFixedUpdate` without one, so a projectile's `SetLinearVelocity` in its `OnStart`, or a platform's `MoveKinematic` in its first `OnFixedUpdate`, raised and disabled the instance. The S3 checkpoint documented the window as behaviour; the review judged it a gap every game that spawns bodies meets (a pickup dropped from `OnCollisionEnter`, a level built by a spawner's `OnStart`), whose only game-side remedies (`Task.WaitTicks(1)`, polling `Physics.GetBodyBounds`) are workarounds this program forbids (decision 9).
- **The rule.** A start flush (§5.7 step 2, and the startup flush of the session and of a scene transition, after `OnCreate`) runs in rounds until no instance waits. Each round synchronizes the script instances (`ScriptEngine::SynchronizeInstances`), then the physics bodies of both worlds (`IScriptHost::SynchronizePhysicsBodies`: the session updates the transforms when the scene changed, then runs `PhysicsSystem::SynchronizeBodies` and `Physics2DSystem::SynchronizeBodies`, the pre-steps' rebuilds of the components EnTT's signals marked, without their kinematic moves, teleports and character updates, outside any physics dispatch, the 2D world created at its first body as its pre-step creates it), then runs `OnStart` for every waiting instance in canonical order; what the round's own callbacks create waits for the next round of the same flush. So an entity has its body from its `OnStart` on, wherever it was created, and first runs `OnStart` and `OnFixedUpdate` at the same tick however the ticks are grouped into frames. A flush runs at most `MaxScriptStartRounds` (16, the depth §5.7 allows nested creation) rounds: an instance still waiting then belongs to a runaway chain (each `OnStart` creating the next) and is published as a script error of its `OnStart` and disabled, the same in every grouping, instead of hanging the flush.
- **Where an entity starts (the lead's follow-up decision, 2026-11-15).** The first commit of this decision (`2608f467`) kept the frame phase's start flush and recorded what that left as a tracked follow-up; the lead decided to fix it at once, since the program ends with M27 and the simulation's frame-rate independence is one of the engine's guarantees. An entity created during a session therefore starts only in a fixed step's start flush (§5.7 step 2, before that tick's `OnFixedUpdate`), or in the startup flush of the session or of a `Scene.Load` or `Scene.Reload`, never in the frame phase: the frame phase runs no start flush (its `FrameStartFlush` phase is gone from the execution order ADR 0028's ledgers fixed), so an entity created in a fixed step, in the frame phase or between ticks starts at the next fixed step's flush, and `OnUpdate` and `OnLateUpdate` pass it by until then, as they pass every unstarted instance. A scene transition's startup callbacks, which run at the end of the frame that requested it, run as the session's own do, in the step view of the tick the frame ended with and with the fixed delta. So an `OnStart` reads the tick's input view and the fixed delta and its destroy and disable requests flush at the tick's step 8, however the ticks are grouped into frames. A startup flush is followed at once by a destroy and disable flush in the same step view (the start-order review's finding 1): what a scene's startup `OnCreate` and `OnStart` destroy or disable, with its `OnDisable`, `OnDestroy` and synthesized exits, is gone before the first tick, where before it waited for the next flush, tick 0's step 8 after its physics step in lockstep, or a frame-phase flush in the frame view before it when the first frame ran no step (live play above the tick rate, a short suite `Clock`). An entity created while no tick runs (time scale 0) starts at the first tick after the scale returns, and gets no `OnStart` or `OnUpdate` until then (finding 2; an accepted limit in `Docs/Backlog.md` §6 beside the UI's). Reproduced before this change by "PlaySession: what a spawned entity's OnStart reads and destroys is the same however the ticks are grouped into frames" (in lockstep an entity created in tick 0 read the frame view, `Move` still 0, where it read the next tick's 1 on two or three steps a frame; one created in the tick that ended a frame of two read a delta of two fixed steps; the box an `OnStart` destroyed still held a ball in the next tick's physics step when that tick shared the frame, and the state hashes differed) and "PlaySession: a scene's startup callbacks after Scene.Load read the same input and delta however the ticks are grouped into frames" (the new scene's `OnStart` read the delta of the frame that requested the load).
- **Why this rule.** It is the smallest one that gives both guarantees. The rounds change only what the flush's own callbacks create or enable (it starts in the same flush, earlier than before), and the fixed-step start point (the bullet above) changes only where an entity created during a session starts, not when it first runs `OnFixedUpdate`. The synchronization applies every physics component and `Active` change a pre-step would have applied a few phases later, from the same components, before any script can read it: a new entity's body, and as well every `RigidBody`, collider, `CharacterController`, `MeshRenderer` or `Terrain` change and every `Active` change made after a tick's pre-step (a physics callback, an `OnDestroy`, the frame phase, between ticks), which is in force at the next tick's `OnFixedUpdate` instead of from its pre-step (the start-order review's finding 4: a `RigidBody` type a collision callback changes, a pooled entity `OnUpdate` re-enables). So a spawned entity's first pre-step treats its body as it treats a scene-authored one at session start (a Transform write teleports a Dynamic body, a `MoveKinematic` sweeps a Kinematic one), and a body whose components the pre-step would have refused is refused, with its diagnostic, before its `OnStart` instead of after it, as a scene-authored one is. A frame-phase start flush could not have given the second guarantee for the `OnStart` it runs: the frame view, the frame's delta and the frame's destroy flush exist only once a frame, wherever its ticks fall.
- **What it leaves.** Nothing of the start order: an entity's `OnStart`, its first `OnFixedUpdate`, its body and what its `OnStart` reads and destroys no longer depend on how ticks are grouped into frames. What still does is what frame-phase code does itself, which §11.3 makes presentation (an `OnUpdate` that spawns spawns at a frame, and its spawn starts at the next fixed step); when a requested scene transition applies no longer does (decision 15).
- **Hashes (decision 9, item 2).** The rounds and the synchronization change the simulation only of a session that creates a bodied entity outside a fixed step's steps 1 to 4c, or in a startup callback, or that spawns from an `OnStart`, or that changes a physics component or an entity's `Active` state after a tick's pre-step (in force at the next tick's step 2 instead of its step 5); the startup destroy flush changes that of a session whose startup callbacks destroy or disable something (gone before tick 0's physics step instead of after it); the fixed-step start point also moves the `OnStart` of every entity created in a fixed step, the frame phase or between ticks to the next fixed step's start flush, in lockstep too, and changes what a scene transition's startup callbacks read on a frame of several fixed steps. ### 15. When a scene transition applies (frame-grouping path 1)

Taken on 2026-11-15 by the product owner, on the start-order review's frame-grouping path 1 and its minor finding 3. Until then a `Scene.Load` or `Scene.Reload` applied at the end of the frame that requested it, whatever the request's origin (friction F17 documented it as behaviour). So in a frame of several fixed steps (live play, a suite with a `Clock`), the frame's later steps still ran in the old scene and VM. The new scene's first tick, and every state hash after the request, then depended on how the ticks were grouped into frames, against Architecture §1.1 G3. A replay recorded in live play across a mid-frame load failed its lockstep verification. A startup callback's request applied at the end of the first frame: before tick 0 when that frame ran no step, after it in lockstep. The engine now applies a transition at a point that every grouping shares. Lockstep is unchanged in every case.

- **The rule** (`PlaySession::EndsFrameSteps`; Architecture §5.6, §5.7 and §11.3):
  - **(a) In a fixed step.** A transition requested in a fixed step belongs to that tick: `OnFixedUpdate`, an `OnStart` of a fixed step's start flush, a physics, animation or UI callback, a task, a test case, native code. Once it is pending, the frame runs no further fixed step: `FixedStep` called anyway runs nothing, as after a quit request. The frame runs its frame phase in the current scene and applies the transition at its end, as lockstep's frame phase applies it after every tick. The steps the scheduler gave the frame and it did not run go back to the scheduler (`FixedStepScheduler::EndFrameAfter`), and the following frames run them in the new scene, within the per-frame cap, beyond which they are dropped as any late frame's are. Every loop that runs a frame's fixed steps follows this: `FrameLoop`, through `IFrameLoopClient::EndsFrameSteps`, so the editor's play loop (`EditorPlayController`) and the Runtime's (`RuntimeApp`) follow it too, and the test runner's ScriptedClock frames, which used to step a frame's remaining steps after a case had returned.
  - **(b) In a scene's startup callbacks.** A transition requested by the startup callbacks of the session, or of the scene a `Scene.Load` or `Scene.Reload` made current (`OnCreate`, the startup flush's `OnStart`), belongs to the next tick. It waits for the frame that runs that tick, which stops after it as in (a). So the requesting scene runs that one tick, as lockstep does, and a frame that runs no fixed step leaves the request pending.
  - **(c) In the frame phase or between frames.** A transition requested in `OnUpdate`, `OnLateUpdate` or an evaluation applies at that frame's end, after its steps, unchanged. That code is presentation (§11.3).
  - **(d) A second request.** It is refused with `InvalidState` while one is pending, unchanged. So the game's flag still guards two requests made in one tick, by two behaviours for example.
- **Why this rule.** It is the smallest one that makes the new scene's first tick and the state hashes the same in every grouping while keeping lockstep as it was. Lockstep already ran the frame phase and applied the transition after every tick; now a frame of several steps does the same after the requesting tick. The startup rule keeps the requesting scene's one tick, which lockstep has always run before applying a startup request. Keeping the unrun steps in the scheduler keeps the simulation's pace, so no tick is lost within the cap. Applying the transition in the middle of the frame, between two of its fixed steps, would also give equal hashes, but it would skip the old scene's frame phase, which lockstep runs. A frame-phase request has no tick of its own, so it keeps the frame end.
- **What it leaves.** Only frame-phase code itself depends on the frame rate: what `OnUpdate` and `OnLateUpdate` do and request, which §11.3 makes presentation.
- **Hashes (decision 9, item 2).** The rule moves no lockstep run. The suites checked every committed hash, and none moved: FeatureTest's 14 replays verified at their committed `FinalStateHash` in the feature suite (Debug and Release), the export suite (Release and Dist) and the determinism suite (the Debug and Release editors and the exported Release and Dist runs agreeing), and `Scaffold.py --check` regenerated FeatureTest byte-identically, the new `Timing` cases included.
