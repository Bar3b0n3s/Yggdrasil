## Context

World Tree's brief 6 ("Particle look", medium, needed for the fire realm): particles are camera-facing squares with one texture, unrotated, cut by a hard depth line, with colours capped at 1 by the schema, and they ghost under TAA. The brief asks for flipbooks from sprite sheets (over the life or picked at random, ideally blended), a random start angle and spin, soft edges against the surface behind over a configurable distance, alignment stretched along the velocity and flat on a plane besides facing the camera, HDR colour (an intensity above 1), and, optionally, lit smoke and motion vectors for TAA; particles stay presentation only. "How the game will check it": golden images of a rotating flipbook flame meeting the floor softly, stretched sparks, a flat ring and a bright ember blooming, and the fire realm's screenshots before and after. ADR 0042 keeps motion vectors (answer 2e) and leaves lit smoke out (answer 2f, `Docs/Backlog.md` §2).

The facts this record relies on, each checked in the code at `bb51e110`:

1. **The emitter.** `ParticleEmitterComponent` (`Scene/Components/ParticleEmitterComponent.h`) is version 1, registered by `RegisterParticlesComponents` (`Scene/Registration/ParticlesRegistration.cpp`), alone in the Particles category, requiring Transform; every field is written with its default, so a new field would change the canonical form and the state hash of every scene and prefab that holds an emitter (FeatureTest's `Particles.scene` and `GoldenParticles/Particles.scene`, Rolling Ball's three levels and its `Checkpoint` and `Goal` prefabs). Its `ColorOverLife` keys are `ColorField`s ranged 0 to 1.
2. **The simulation.** `StepParticleEmitter` (`Scene/ParticleSimulation.h`) draws a fixed seven values per new particle from the state's one `Random` (`ParticleEmitterState::Generator`, seeded by `MakeParticleEmitterSeed`); `ComputeParticleDigest` hashes the particle count, every particle's `Position`, `PreviousPosition`, `Velocity`, `Age`, `Lifetime` and `StartSize`, the `Random` state, `CycleTime`, `Cycles`, `EmitAccumulator`, `PendingEmits` and `Steps`. Survivors keep emission order (`std::erase_if`). `ParticleSystem::Step` (`Scene/ParticleSystem.h`) steps each effectively enabled emitter in canonical order in the Particles frame phase.
3. **The committed digests.** "ParticleSimulation: a seeded emitter's particles hash to their committed digest in every configuration" (`Tests/Source/Engine/Scene/ParticleSimulationTests.cpp`) and FeatureTest's `Api.ParticleEmitter` suite (`Assets/Tests/Api/ParticleEmitter.test.luau`, `FOUNTAIN_DIGEST` `3b5d89a880fa484b` and `SPARKS_DIGEST` `f05b301294ea48f1` after 64 ticks, in the Editor, Release and Dist runs) commit digest values.
4. **The extraction.** `RenderExtraction.cpp`'s `AppendParticleItems` writes one `ParticleItem` per live particle (position at the request's alpha between `PreviousPosition` and `Position`, through the emitter's rendered world matrix for a Local emitter; size and colour from the curves at the age of the last step, since extraction does not know `FixedDelta`: Backlog §6) and one `ParticleEmitterItem` per emitter (texture, blend, range, entity). Extraction never touches assets; the renderer resolves sprite sheets through its `AssetManager` (`SceneRenderer.cpp`'s sprite stage, `Utils::ReportSpriteSheetFailure`).
5. **The pass.** `Renderer/ParticlePass.{h,cpp}` plans camera-facing squares on the CPU (`PlanParticlePass`: corners `Position ∓ right·h ± up·h` from the view matrix's rows, culling, Alpha emitters sorted back to front) and records them through the QuadBatcher's `Hdr` and `HdrAdditive` pipelines into `ForwardFramebuffer` (SceneColor with SceneDepth, tested `GreaterOrEqual`, unwritten), with the view's unjittered `Projection * View` (`SceneRenderer::State::RecordParticles`). The Particles declaration (`RenderPassDeclaration.h`) has no pipeline; 73 startup pipelines, 32 programs in `Shaders.json` ("Particles" sorts between "MatrixConvention" and "Quad").
6. **Depth as an input.** §8.3 keeps SceneDepth in `DepthWrite` from the prepass to the overlays and attaches it read-write with writes disabled, never as a read-only attachment (NVRHI stores one with `STORE`, which synchronization validation counts as a write). SceneDepth is created shader-readable (`CreateTarget`'s `isShaderResource`), and GTAO and the depth pyramid already bind it as a texture. At the Particles pass it holds the mesh and terrain prepasses' depth (the forward, transparent, sprite and 2D passes write none).
7. **The motion vectors.** `SceneVelocity` (RG16_FLOAT) is created for every view, cleared to zero (`RecordViewSetup`) and written only by the mesh and terrain prepasses (`Common/Motion.slang`'s `ComputeMotionVector`: current pixel minus previous, both through jittered matrices so the jitter cancels); TAA reads it (`TaaPass`). `MakeViewConstants` jitters `ViewProjection` and `PreviousViewProjection` (the camera's previous one, else `ViewProjection` itself) only when a jitter is set; without TAA `ViewConstants::ViewProjection` is exactly `Projection * View`. `RenderViewHistory::Apply` (`Renderer/TemporalHistory.h`) fills `CameraData::PreviousViewProjection` and each `MeshDrawItem::PreviousWorld` by entity, and captures reset it and render the warm-up with a delta time of 0.
8. **The golden.** "Golden: Particles" (`Tests/Source/Golden/ParticlesGoldenTests.cpp`) renders `GoldenParticles/Particles.scene` (scaffold `GoldenParticles.jsonl`, IdSeed 2402) after 64 ticks with FXAA, bloom off, an Alpha emitter "Smoke" and an Additive emitter "Embers", and repeats it byte for byte; no golden scene holds a particle under TAA.
9. **Sprite sheets.** `SpriteSheetData` (`Asset/SpriteSheetData.h`) holds a texture (null: frames sample opaque white), frames as texel rectangles in texel space (x right, y down) and named clips of tick durations; there is no placeholder sheet. `BuiltinAssetHandles::WhiteTexture` (0x0181) is an opaque white texel.

## Decisions

### 1. The component: `ParticleLook`

The ledger's ParticleEmitter row puts every setting of decision 3's M33 section in a component of its own beside the emitter, appended to the Particles category; C33.1 names it **`ParticleLook`** (`Scene/Components/ParticleLookComponent.h`), version 1, registered by `RegisterParticlesComponents` after `ParticleEmitter`, requiring `ParticleEmitter` (so a look on an entity without an emitter is `COMPONENT_MISSING_REQUIREMENT`), with the default flags (serialized, script-visible, editor-visible, removable). `ParticleEmitter` stays version 1 and no scene, prefab, digest or state hash moves: no existing file holds a `ParticleLook`. Its fields, in file order, with their defaults (each default reproduces today's look, so an emitter with a default `ParticleLook` draws byte-identically to one without):

| Field | Type, range, unit | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `SpriteSheet` | `SpriteSheet` asset | null | The flipbook's sheet. Null: the emitter's `Texture` is drawn whole, as without a look. Set: the sheet's frames replace the emitter's `Texture` (the sheet's texture, opaque white when it has none) |
| `FirstFrame` | uint32, 0 to 65,535 | 0 | The first frame of the flipbook's range, an index into the sheet's frames |
| `FrameCount` | uint32, 0 to 65,536 | 0 | The range's length; 0: every frame from `FirstFrame` to the sheet's last |
| `FrameMode` | enum `ParticleFrameMode`: `OverLife`, `Random` | `OverLife` | `OverLife`: the range played over each particle's life, `FrameCycles` times; `Random`: one frame of the range picked per particle at its birth and held |
| `FrameCycles` | float, 0.01 to 1,000 | 1 | How many times `OverLife` plays the range over a particle's life |
| `BlendFrames` | bool | false | `OverLife` only: cross-fade each frame into the next (two texture coordinates per vertex), so a short range plays smoothly |
| `StartRotation` | vec2 (minimum, maximum), −360 to 360, degrees | (0, 0) | The range a new particle's angle is drawn from |
| `Spin` | vec2 (minimum, maximum), −3,600 to 3,600, degrees per second | (0, 0) | The range a new particle's spin is drawn from; positive turns counter-clockwise as the camera (or, for `Flat`, a viewer above the plane) sees it |
| `Alignment` | enum `ParticleAlignment`: `Camera`, `Velocity`, `Flat` | `Camera` | `Camera`: a square facing the camera, turned by its angle; `Velocity`: stretched along the particle's velocity across the view (sparks, embers, rain), its angle unused; `Flat`: a square in the emitter's local XZ plane (its normal the emitter's +Y: ground rings, ripples), turned about that normal by its angle |
| `VelocityStretch` | float, 0 to 100, seconds | 0.1 | `Velocity` only: the quad's length is its size plus its speed across the view times this |
| `SoftDistance` | float, 0 to 1,000, metres | 0 | The view-depth distance over which a particle fades out as it nears the surface behind it; 0: a hard cut, as today |
| `Intensity` | float, 0 to 1,000 | 1 | Multiplies the colour's RGB (not its alpha), so a particle can be brighter than white and bloom |

- **Validation** (registry `Validate` and `Generate` hooks): `StartRotation` and `Spin` are ranges whose minimum must not exceed their maximum, as the emitter's ranges. A frame range outside the sheet is not a registry error (the sheet is an asset the component cannot see): the renderer draws nothing for it (decision 5) and `project.validate` reports it (decision 9).
- **Why a frame range and not a clip** (ADR 0042 decision 3 offers "a clip or frame range"): a flipbook's frame is a function of the particle's age, not of ticks, so a clip's per-step tick durations would have no meaning; a range keeps names out of the render snapshot, and a sheet cut as a grid (the usual flipbook) is a range already.
- **Frames are drawn square.** A particle's quad keeps the size its emitter gives it whatever its frame's aspect; a frame is stretched to fill it (sprites keep their frame's pixel size, particles their metre size).

### 2. The simulation: a second random stream, rotation and an emission serial

- **Per particle** (`Scene/ParticleSimulation.h`'s `Particle`, appended): `Rotation` and `PreviousRotation` (radians, the angle at the last two steps, for the drawing's interpolation), `Spin` (radians per second), `FrameDraw` (in [0, 1): the `Random` frame mode's pick) and `Serial` (the particle's ordinal among its emitter's emissions, for the motion vectors' matching, decision 6).
- **Per state** (`ParticleEmitterState`, appended): `LookGenerator`, a second `Random` seeded from the state's seed (`MakeParticleLookSeed(seed)`: XXH64, seed 0, over the seed and the tag `0x6b6f6f4c` "Look", both little-endian 64-bit), so it exists for every state and the first stream is untouched; `HasLook`, set by the first step taken with a `ParticleLook`; and `Emitted`, the emissions so far.
- **The step** (`StepParticleEmitter`, which takes the entity's `ParticleLook` or null as a new last parameter defaulting to null, so every existing call keeps its meaning): step 1 also sets `PreviousRotation = Rotation` and adds `Spin × dt` to `Rotation` for every live particle; a new particle takes its seven draws from the first stream exactly as before, then, only with a look, three draws from `LookGenerator`, in this order: its start angle (uniform in `StartRotation`, converted to radians), its spin (uniform in `Spin`, radians per second) and `FrameDraw`; without a look all three are 0 and `LookGenerator` is not drawn. Every new particle takes `Serial = Emitted` and increments `Emitted`. DetMath is not needed: the step uses only additions, multiplications and the conversion constant.
- **The digest keeps its committed values.** `ComputeParticleDigest` hashes exactly M26's bytes and, only when `HasLook`, appends every particle's `Rotation`, `PreviousRotation`, `Spin` and `FrameDraw` (as the little-endian bits of their floats, in order) and `LookGenerator`'s four state words. An emitter that never had a look hashes as before (fact 3), so FeatureTest's `Api.ParticleEmitter` digests and the unit case's committed digest keep their values; an emitter with a look is covered by the digest too, and FeatureTest commits one (decision 8). `Serial` and `Emitted` are bookkeeping for the drawing, not hashed. "ParticleSimulation: a seeded emitter's particles hash to their committed digest in every configuration" (M26's, unchanged) and "ParticleSimulation: a ParticleLook draws angle, spin and frame from a second stream, so the first stream's particles are unchanged" pin it.
- **The Particles phase** (`ParticleSystem::Step`) passes each emitter's `ParticleLook` (or null) to every step. The phase, its place, its stepping and the state hash are otherwise unchanged (ADR 0042's execution-order row: "M33 takes none"); particles stay presentation only.

### 3. The snapshot and the extraction

`Renderer/RenderSnapshot.h` (the ledger: M33 changes `ParticleEmitterItem` and `ParticleItem` only; no new snapshot member or header):
- **`ParticleItem`**, appended: `Rotation` (radians, at the request's alpha between the particle's `PreviousRotation` and `Rotation`), `Velocity` (world space, metres per second: a Local emitter's through its rendered world matrix as a direction), `FramePhase` (`OverLife`: the particle's age over its lifetime as of its last step, at most the largest float below 1, times `FrameCycles`; `Random`: its `FrameDraw`), `Serial`, and `Previous` (`std::optional<ParticleDrawState>`: the position, size, angle and velocity the view's previous render drew the particle with, filled by `RenderViewHistory`, decision 6). `Color`'s RGB is multiplied by the look's `Intensity`. `ParticleEmitterItem` also takes the look's `FrameCycles` and its state's `Incarnation` (`ParticleStateComponent::Incarnation`, which the Particles phase sets from a counter of the system's when it anchors a state, so a state made again after a disable or a re-added `ParticleEmitter` is told apart; not part of the simulation's state or digest).
- **`ParticleEmitterItem`**, appended: `Sheet` (the look's `SpriteSheet`, null without a look), `FirstFrame`, `FrameCount`, `BlendFrames` (the look's flag in `OverLife` mode, false in `Random`), `Alignment` (`RenderParticleAlignment`: `Camera`, `Velocity`, `Flat`), `VelocityStretch`, `SoftDistance`, and `PlaneRight` and `PlaneUp` (`Flat`: the emitter's rendered local +X and −Z axes, normalized, so a viewer above the plane looking down with the emitter's −Z up sees the texture upright).
- **Extraction** fills them from the emitter's `ParticleLook` (an emitter without one gets the defaults: no sheet, `Camera`, no softness, intensity 1) and computes the rendered world matrix also for a `Flat` emitter. Like the curves (Backlog §6), `OverLife`'s frame steps at the tick rate; the angle interpolates. Pure and deterministic, on the simulation path (Scene): no transcendental function.

### 4. The Particles program and pass

The ledger's render-pass row: the Particles declaration gets pipelines of its own, of a new program named after its pass.
- **The program** `Particles` (`Resources/Shaders/Passes/Particles.slang`, sorted into `Shaders.json`: 33 programs), entries `VSMain`, `PSColor` and `PSVelocity`, over a vertex of its own (`Shared/ParticleVertex.h`, 64 bytes: the corner's world position and its previous-render position, the colour, two texture coordinates and the blend toward the second, and the inverse soft distance). Set 0: the view's `ViewConstants` (b0), the particle texture (t0), its sampler (s0) and SceneDepth as a texture (t1).
- **Three pipelines** (one binding layout), none with a depth attachment: `ParticlesAlpha` (`PSColor`) and `ParticlesAdditive` (`PSColor` on the branch; `PSAdditive` since the merge, which fades rather than tints in fog: decision 10) into SceneColor alone, blending exactly as the QuadBatcher's `Hdr` (straight alpha; alpha `One`/`InvSrcAlpha`) and `HdrAdditive` (`SrcAlpha`/`One`, the target's alpha kept) did, and `ParticlesVelocity` (`PSVelocity`) into SceneVelocity alone, without blending. The motion vectors take a draw of their own because a pipeline of two targets needs one blend state for both unless the device enables `independentBlend`, which it does not (`GraphicsDevice.cpp`'s features), and an additive colour cannot share a blend state with a replaced motion. The Particles declaration declares 3 pipelines and the program: 76 startup pipelines on the branch (77 on the merged tree with M32's SSR classification, then 76 again once decision 10 removed `HdrAdditive`). `ParticlePass` becomes a class created by `SceneRendererPipelines::CreatePipelines` (place 1 of ADR 0029 decision 27) that owns its pipelines, its two samplers (Linear and Point, clamp, no mips, as the QuadBatcher's) and its vertex and index buffers (chunks of `ParticlePass::MaxQuadsPerBatch` quads, as the QuadBatcher's), so the recreation protocol covers it with the pipeline set; its binding sets come from the view's `ParticleBindings` (place 2) and its textures are cache mirrors (place 3).
- **The depth test moves into the shader.** The fragment loads SceneDepth at its pixel and covers nothing (`SV_Coverage` 0, as `Scene.slang`'s Mask path: a discard needs a device feature the device does not enable) where its depth is below the scene's (reverse-Z `GreaterOrEqual`, the test the pipelines did in hardware). `SV_Position.z` is the depth the fixed-function test compared, so a hard particle covers exactly the pixels it covered before.
- **Soft particles:** with a `SoftDistance` d above 0 the fragment's alpha is multiplied by `saturate((sceneViewDepth − particleViewDepth) / d)`, both linear view depths from §8.3's mappings (perspective `Near / depth`, orthographic `Far − depth (Far − Near)`, so an isometric camera fades by view depth too).
- **The vertices follow the 3D passes' camera.** The vertex shader transforms by `ViewConstants::ViewProjection`, which equals the old `Projection * View` exactly without TAA and carries the jitter with TAA, so particles rasterize on the jittered grid the depth they test against was drawn on and TAA anti-aliases them.
- **The plan** (`PlanParticlePass`, pure, CPU) gains each emitter's alignment, the particles' angles and velocities and the flipbook's frames: `Camera` turns the camera-facing square by its angle (no turn at an angle of exactly 0, so an unturned square's corners are computed exactly as before); `Velocity` lays the quad's long axis along the velocity's projection on the view plane, its width the size and its length the size plus that projected speed times `VelocityStretch`, falling back to the camera-facing square when the projected speed is not above 1e-6 m/s; `Flat` lays the square in `PlaneRight` and `PlaneUp`, turned by its angle (a degenerate plane axis faces the camera instead). The texture coordinates are the frame's rectangle (decision 5), with the next frame's for `BlendFrames`. Culling, the back-to-front sort of Alpha emitters and the skipping of non-finite particles are unchanged. The pass keeps its stats row, `Particles`, and its rule of one draw per run of one texture.
- **What stays the same** (the ledger: "their fields' defaults render the Particles golden byte-identically"): an emitter without a look, or with a default one, gives the same corners, texture coordinates and colour, the same depth test and the same blending, so "Golden: Particles" and the M26 GPU cases keep their bytes. The QuadBatcher, `Quad.slang` and `QuadVertex.h` are M32's and unchanged on this branch; the QuadBatcher's `HdrAdditive` pipeline loses its only user, and decision 10 removes it at M33's merge.

### 5. Flipbooks in the renderer

- **Resolution** (`SceneRenderer::State::RecordParticles`, before the plan): each emitter with a `Sheet` resolves it through the `AssetManager` as sprites do (`GetOrPlaceholder`; a missing or failed sheet is reported once through `ReportSpriteSheetFailure` and its particles draw nothing, as a sprite's), then its texture through the `GpuResourceCache` (null: `BuiltinAssetHandles::WhiteTexture`; a missing or failed texture draws its placeholder whole over each quad, as a sprite's). The range is `[FirstFrame, FirstFrame + n)` with `n` the `FrameCount`, or the frames from `FirstFrame` to the sheet's last when it is 0; a range that is empty or ends past the sheet's frames draws nothing (`project.validate` names it, decision 9).
- **A frame's texture coordinates** are its rectangle's texel edges over the texture's size, without a half-texel inset (`ExpandNineSlice`'s convention): (X / W, Y / H) at the quad's top-left corner and ((X + Width) / W, (Y + Height) / H) at its bottom-right one. A linear sampler may read the neighbouring frame's edge texels (a kept limit for sheets packed without padding: Backlog §6).
- **Which frame:** with the range's n frames, a particle shows frame `floor(FramePhase × n) mod n` of it and, with `BlendFrames`, blends toward the next (`+ 1 mod n`) by `fract(FramePhase × n)`, the fragment mixing the two texels before it multiplies by the colour, while the next frame is still to play: the range plays `FrameCycles` times over a life, so the last frame of the last play (`floor(FramePhase × n) + 1 ≥ FrameCycles × n`) is held rather than blended into the first ("ParticlePass: the last frame of a blended range's last play is held, not blended into the first").

### 6. Motion vectors (answer 2e)

- **The history matches particles.** `RenderViewHistory::Apply` keeps, besides the mesh items' worlds, how it drew every particle (its position, size, angle and velocity) by (emitter entity, the emitter's `Incarnation`, `Serial`), in that order (each emitter's block already ascends by serial, so only the blocks are sorted), and fills each `ParticleItem::Previous` with the previous Apply's draw of the same particle; a particle the previous render did not draw, one of a state made again (a new incarnation, its serials restarting at 0), a reset and a non-finite value leave it empty. So the motion is the drawn motion since the last render whatever moved it (its velocity, a Local emitter's own motion, a paused or slowed session, which a velocity times the frame time would get wrong), and a capture's warm-up (the same snapshot, delta time 0) draws zero particle motion.
- **The pass writes SceneVelocity when the view runs TAA** (`frame.Taa`, the only reader of SceneVelocity): after the colour draws of each chunk, the same quads again with `ParticlesVelocity`. Each vertex carries its corner as the previous render laid it, from the particle's `Previous` draw with the same layout (so a spin, a `SizeOverLife` growth and a `Velocity` quad's changing stretch are in the motion, not only the centre's translation; without a `Previous`, the corner itself), which the vertex shader projects with `ViewConstants::PreviousViewProjection`, and the fragment writes `ComputeMotionVector` (`Common/Motion.slang`) of the two clip positions where its alpha (the texel's times the colour's times the soft fade) is at least `ParticleVelocityAlpha` (0.5) and the scene's depth does not hide it, covering nothing elsewhere (the motion already there stays), so TAA reprojects a particle that covers its pixel by the particle's own motion. The rule is the same for Alpha and Additive emitters, and the particle drawn last wins, as the colour draws order them (an Alpha emitter's nearest).
- **What it removes:** Backlog §6's "Particles write no velocity" row. Particles that cover less than half a pixel's alpha still take the motion of what is behind them, and the previous corners are laid along the current view's axes, so a camera-facing quad's turn toward a turning camera is not in its motion, only the camera's own (the new, narrower limits, recorded in §6). "ParticlePass: a seen particle's previous corners are laid from its previous angle, size and stretch" and "TemporalHistory: a particle state made again never takes its predecessor's positions, its serials restarting" pin the fixes of the lead's review.

### 7. Script surface and automation

- **No new namespace, method or callback.** `ParticleLook` is read and written through the generic component proxy (`entity.ParticleLook.<Field>`, `Scripting/ScriptProxy`), as M31's and M32's components are (ADR 0042's script-API row), so its fields are animatable from scripts; `Engine.d.luau` gains the proxy type from the registration (`GenerateDocs.py`).
- **No new automation method.** `entity.create`, `entity.update`, `component.schema` and `component.list` cover the component, and `scene.open` and `project.validate` its scene; a Python test (`Tests/Automation/test_particle_look.py`) drives it through them (parity, decision 8).

## Risks

- **The motion vectors' second draw.** With TAA every particle quad is rasterized and fully shaded twice (colour, then motion: one or two texture samples, the SceneDepth load and the soft fade each time), so the particles' fill cost, their draws and the stats row's triangles double in a view with TAA; FXAA and no anti-aliasing, the defaults, draw once.
- **Byte identity through a new program.** The old image came from the QuadBatcher's pipelines with a hardware depth test; the new one computes the same corners, coordinates, colour and depth comparison in another program. The M26 GPU cases (exact pixels in pixel-art mode) and "Golden: Particles" are the check; if a driver compiled the transform differently, the golden would show it.
- **SceneDepth's state.** The pass reads SceneDepth as a texture between passes that attach it; NVRHI's automatic barriers move it between `DepthWrite` and `ShaderResource`, as GTAO's binding already does, and synchronization validation in the gpu suite guards it.
- **The motion vectors' memory.** The history keeps one record per drawn particle per view: its key (emitter entity, incarnation, serial, 24 bytes) and its draw (position, size, angle, velocity, 32 bytes), 56 bytes a particle, for every view with particles whether or not it runs TAA (4,096 particles an emitter at most). A reset empties it; its capacity is kept up to four times the last frame's count or 4,096 records (`MaxRetainedParticleRecords`), and an outsized frame's beyond that is released.
- **The merge with M32.** Fog's binding enters the particle program, and the `HdrAdditive` removal touches M32's QuadBatcher after it lands; the golden "particles in fog" is rendered on the merged tree.
