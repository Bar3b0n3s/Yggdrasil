### 5. The particle simulation (C-PART; stream P-A)

- **Presentation only, seeded, capped** (plan §6 "M26"): `Scene/ParticleSimulation.h` states one emitter's step as pure functions over its fields, its world matrix and its state (`ParticleEmitterState`: the live particles in emission order, the emitter's `Random`, its cycle, its fractional emit carry, its queued emits and its step count). On the simulation path (Scene), so DetMath only: directions from the seeded Random and DetMath's Sin, Cos and ACos.
- **The step** (the header's numbered order): integrate and age every live particle (PreviousPosition kept for the drawing's interpolation), kill those past their lifetime, then emit, each particle while fewer than MaxParticles live (the rest dropped and counted): while Playing and in the first cycle or looping, the cycle's Burst at the cycle's first step (CycleTime exactly 0) and Rate times the step with the remainder carried; then the queued emits, whatever Playing, Loop and the cycle say (so `Emit` works on a stopped or finished emitter); then, while Playing, advance the cycle: at Duration a looping emitter's CycleTime restarts at exactly 0 (the remainder dropped, so a cycle lasts a whole number of steps and every cycle bursts, where a float remainder would almost never land on 0 again) and a one-shot emitter's stays at Duration (the review's finding 10). A new particle draws its position and direction, speed, lifetime and size in that order, a fixed count of draws, so one emitter's stream repeats exactly.
- **The seed** (`MakeParticleEmitterSeed`): XXH64 over the entity's UUID and the emitter's Seed, so two emitters with one Seed differ and an emitter is the same in every configuration and run.
- **The digest** (`ComputeParticleDigest`), the acceptance's "seeded particle hash": XXH64 of the particles' floats as their bits and the stream's state; `Test.GetParticleDigest` returns it (decision 26). Stream P-A commits the digest of "ParticleSimulation: a seeded emitter's particles hash to their committed digest in every configuration" from Debug and confirms it in Release; FeatureTest's Particles suite checks digests through `Test.GetParticleDigest` in the Editor, Release and Dist runs (decision 25), the Dist proof.
- **Kept limits** (Backlog §6): no gameplay collision; no simulation in edit scenes (an edit scene's views draw no particles, so its captures stay deterministic; play or simulate to see them; the ledger mentions a "particle preview" among possible editor tools, and C26.1 builds none, so no method and no editor-only surface is needed for it).
- **The runtime state** is `Scene/Components/ParticleStateComponent.h` (RuntimeComponents.h's rules: never registered, serialized or hashed), created and stepped by the Particles phase and written by the script entries.

### 6. The Particles phase and the live guard (stream P-A)

- **The place** (the ledger's execution order): `PlaySessionPhase::Particles`, a frame phase after AudioUpdate (and so after Update, LateUpdate and the frame's transform update, which move emitters and queue emits) and right before RenderExtraction, which copies the particles. "PlaySessionPhase: Particles has its enumerator name and follows AudioUpdate, right before RenderExtraction" is live.
- **Its modes and stepping** (the ledger left them to C26.1): it runs in Play and Simulate alike (a system, not a script), **whether or not extraction is enabled**, and steps each emitter **one fixed step per tick the session ran since its last Particles phase** (`ParticleSystem::Step(scene, tick)` with the session's FixedDelta and the entity's current world matrix), never by the frame's delta time: a paused session freezes its particles, a lockstep frame steps once, a frame after three ticks steps three times, so a particle digest is a function of the ticks and the frames' emitter poses alone, equal across configurations and independent of rendering. **A state starts at its first phase, whoever made it** (the review's finding 1): a state no phase has stepped yet (`ParticleStateComponent::LastTick` empty: one the phase just created, or one `Emit` made since the last phase, at whatever tick) is anchored at that phase's tick minus one (0 at tick 0), so it steps once there (none at tick 0) and never catches up the ticks before it existed; an emitter spawned and emitted at tick 600 shows its particles at the next frame, newly born. A phase steps a state at most `MaxParticleStepsPerPhase` (60: one second at 60 Hz, twelve times the default MaxStepsPerFrame) times, dropping the rest of a longer gap, which only a MaxStepsPerFrame above 60 or fixed steps run without frames make. `Clear` on an emitter without a state makes none, and `FindParticleDigest` (`Test.GetParticleDigest`) is nil until a phase anchors the state. "ParticleSystem: an emitter spawned and emitted at tick 600 starts there, its particles shown in the next frame" (skipped, P-A) pins it. An entity that lost its ParticleEmitter or was disabled loses its state.
- **Scripts** queue emits and clear particles through `QueueParticleEmit` and `ClearParticles` (decision 26), which write only the runtime state, so the scene's revision and the state hash stay.
- **Particles never change the state hash** (the ledger: they enter neither the scene's serialization nor a block). **The live guard** "PlaySession: particles never change the state hash" pins the hashes of 60 lockstep ticks of a busy emitter riding a falling body over a ground (and of the same scene without the emitter), in Play and Simulate, at their values on the contract tree, where no Particles phase runs: `777f0d69345840c8` with the emitter and `492ca3cbba8a1f07` without. P-A's phase, state and stepping must keep both; the determinism and games suites prove the rest at the integration.
- **The session** creates the ParticleSystem with itself (it keeps it across a scene transition, since the state lives in the scene's runtime components) and returns it from `PlaySession::GetParticles`; P-A enters the phase in `PlaySession.cpp` and adds it to `PlaySessionTests.cpp`'s phase lists at its place (the ledger).

### 7. The particles in the snapshot and the Particles pass (stream P-B)

- **The snapshot** (after Ui, the ledger's order): `ParticleEmitters` (one `ParticleEmitterItem` per emitter: texture, blend, its range of `Particles`, entity) and `Particles` (`ParticleItem`: world position at the extraction's alpha between the particle's last two steps, Local emitters through the emitter's rendered world matrix; size; colour from the curves). Render extraction fills them from the runtime state, in canonical entity order; an edit scene has none (decision 5).
- **The pass:** the declaration `Particles` in the Sprites stage after Lights2D, its stats row `Particles`, no pipelines of its own: each emitter's particles are camera-facing QuadBatcher quads into SceneColor, depth-tested against SceneDepth without writing it, after the 3D forward passes and the sprites, before the HDR post chain, so TAA, bloom and exposure see them; in a Lit view only (not the data views, the Overdraw view or the wireframe). Alpha emitters with the QuadBatcher's Hdr pipeline, their particles sorted back to front in the view (view depth, ties by index); Additive emitters with **`QuadTarget::HdrAdditive`**, which P-B appends to M18's frozen `QuadBatcher.h` after M22's LdrDepth (the ledger's one permitted change: one pipeline, the QuadBatcher's count 6), changing none of the earlier targets. Emitters draw in snapshot order, so overlapping emitters do not interleave by depth (a kept limit, Backlog §6).
- **The built-in texture** `engine://Textures/Particle` (0x018b, `BuiltinTexture::Particle`, `GenerateBuiltinParticleTexture`): 32 x 32 RGBA8Srgb, one level, a white soft disc defined texel by texel in the enumerator's comment, integer arithmetic only, never compressed (the ledger). P-B adds its catalogue entries.
- **Recovery:** the pass keeps its binding sets and vertex data in the renderer's state (place 2) and its textures are cache mirrors (place 3); "Particles: a renderer drawing particles recovers from a device loss with the same image" goes through `Test::RunDeviceLossTest`.

### 8. Probe baking and `lighting.bake` (R-GI-PROBE; stream Q-A)

- **The irradiance volume format** (version 1; "Data formats"): a grid of 2 to 16 probes per axis over the box, each the SH9 of the irradiance it receives in EnvironmentData's convention (E(n)/pi, windowed), so the forward pass evaluates a probe exactly as it evaluates the environment's SH9. Its source, `.irradiance`, is an 8-byte header ("EIRV", version 1) and the payload, which the importer (IrradianceVolumeImporter version 1, claiming `.irradiance`, no settings, no dependencies) cooks unchanged.
- **The capture** (`Renderer/ProbeCapture.h`): a SceneRenderer renders the scene's snapshot six times from a point, 90-degree faces in Vulkan's cube order, with `RenderQualitySettings::Probes` false (one bounce: a bake never sees an earlier bake), no particles, sprites, UI or overlays and every temporal input reset, TAA and (since W26.2's review) SSR off, GTAO as the scene sets it, and reads SceneColor (linear HDR radiance, before the post chain) back through `SceneRenderer::GetSceneColorTexture` (a frozen addition to `SceneRenderer.h`). `ProjectCubeToIrradianceSH9` projects a capture with each texel's exact solid angle; `ResampleCubeToEquirect` and `WriteRadianceHdr` turn a reflection probe's capture into the `.hdr` the EnvironmentImporter imports (the vendored `stb_image_write`'s writer or a first-party one, as long as the bytes are deterministic). Pure CPU steps with IEEE-exact operations, sqrt and DetMath only.
- **The bake** (`EditorCore/Lighting/LightingBake.h`, `BakeLighting`): a saved edit scene's probes in canonical order, each checked before anything renders (a probe out of its ranges refuses the bake with Validation), then written as `Assets/Lighting/<the scene's path below Assets/, without its extension>/<entity id>.irradiance` or `.hdr` (`Assets/Scenes/Room.scene` into `Assets/Lighting/Scenes/Room/`; named by id, so a renamed entity keeps its data; the folder keyed on the scene's path since W26.1's review, decision 31, where the contract said `<scene stem>`), imported, and assigned to the probes' Data in one undoable edit. **Two bakes are byte-identical** (the acceptance): the captures are deterministic on one device (no temporal noise, no atomic deciding a sum or an order) and the CPU steps are pure, so a second bake of the unchanged scene writes the same bytes and changes no component. Bakes are compared on one device class, never across devices, as environment bakes are (EnvironmentBaker.h).
- **`lighting.bake {irradianceVolumes?, reflectionProbes?}`** (`EditorCore/Automation/LightingMethods.h`, the wire format frozen): the editor's Bake Lighting action (`Editor/Panels/LightingBakeAction`, a file of its own that `EditorLayer`, M24's file, only calls) as a method; Mutates, no dry run, not a batch op, the scene target (the inventory's relation "the target scene's probes"), an MCP tool with a 600 s timeout, editor-only.
- **The oracles' CPU halves:** "ProbeCapture: a uniform cube projects to its radiance in every direction (the furnace)" and "ProbeCapture: a cube lit red on one side projects to the reference irradiance of the red hemisphere (colour bleed)", against the test support's double-precision reference (fact 9); the GPU halves are Q-B's (decision 16).

### 10. The temporal-state section (R-TAA-VEL, R-TAA, R-AEX; streams X-C and X-A)

The plan's "one temporal-state section, in M26's ADR" (§7 item 9) and the golden premise it changes (§8 risk 19: "reproducible after a fixed warm-up from a reset history"):

- **What a view remembers.** A `RenderViewHistory` (`Renderer/TemporalHistory.h`) per view that renders over time: each editor view (scene and game), the Runtime's window, the FeatureTest runner's view, and every capture. `Apply(snapshot, deltaTime)` right before each Render fills `CameraData::PreviousViewProjection` (the previous Apply's unjittered Projection times View), every `MeshDrawItem::PreviousWorld` by entity (decision 11), `CameraData::DeltaTime`, and `CameraData::Jitter` (`GetTaaJitter` of its frame count when TAA runs, else zero), then records the snapshot. The renderer keeps only its per-view GPU history (TAA's history image, the adapted exposure) and starts it afresh whenever the snapshot says the history was reset (`PreviousViewProjection` empty).
- **A reset** happens at a view's first frame, at `Reset()` (a scene opened, closed or swapped, play started or stopped, every capture), and at a camera cut (another camera entity, projection kind or viewport size). After a reset: zero motion, the current image as TAA's history, and an exposure that **converges at once** (the target, `AdaptExposure` with reset).
- **Captures reset history and render a fixed warm-up:** a capture whose snapshot needs temporal state (`NeedsTemporalWarmup`: TAA or auto-exposure, in a Lit view outside pixel-art mode) resets its history and renders the same snapshot `CaptureWarmupFrames` (8, the jitter sequence's length) times with delta time 0, keeping the last image; a capture without temporal state renders once, as before. So two captures of one scene are byte-identical (the acceptance), at a cost of eight renders for temporal captures only (risk 2).
- **Pixel-art mode keeps M18's 2D colour path** (the Linear tonemapper at exposure 1, so a sprite texel reaches the output unchanged; the review's finding 4): `AntiAliasing` Taa acts as None there (no jitter, no resolve), as FXAA already does; `ExposureMode` Auto acts as Manual at exposure 1 (no Exposure pass); and no LUT grades the image, since a LUT would change the texels the path delivers unchanged. So a pixel-art capture never renders the warm-up (`RenderOutputSettings::PixelArt`'s comment; "TemporalHistory: captures warm up only with TAA or auto-exposure in a Lit view" checks auto-exposure there).
- **The hosts' calls** are X-C's: `ViewportCapture` (the warm-up), `ScreenshotMethods`, the Runtime, the FeatureTest runner, and `EditorHostViewports` (M24's file in phase 3: calls only, adapted at M26's merge).

### 15. Auto-exposure and LUTs (R-AEX, R-LUT; streams X-A and X-B)

- **Auto-exposure** (`Renderer/AutoExposure.h`, the CPU statement the GPU must equal): the histogram of the exposed image's Rec. 709 luminance in 64 bins over log2 luminance [-12, 20), binned by comparing with a shared table of bin edges, never by a log2, the luminance computed with NoContraction products on the GPU, so **the histogram equals the CPU bins** exactly (integer atomic adds into bins: no float sum or order depends on them); the target maps the mean log2 luminance between the 50th and 95th percentiles to middle grey (0.18), clamped to [AutoExposureMinEV, AutoExposureMaxEV], ExposureEV added as compensation; adaptation moves at SpeedUp or SpeedDown EV per second over `CameraData::DeltaTime`, converging at once after a reset. The Exposure pass (X-A's `Renderer/ExposurePass.h`, program Exposure, `Shared/ExposureConstants.h`) writes the histogram and the adapted exposure into the renderer's per-view buffers (place 2), which Tonemap reads (`TonemapFlagAutoExposure`, t8). Captures with auto-exposure are byte-identical (decision 10).
- **The tonemap order:** exposure, curve, LUT, then the encode and the dither (the ledger). The LUT's domain is the display-encoded value, as grading LUTs are authored: the lookup encodes the tonemapped display-linear value with the sRGB OETF, samples the 3D texture trilinearly, decodes the output with the EOTF, and `LutIntensity` blends it with the ungraded value before the encode. With no LUT nothing changes (the flag is clear), and in pixel-art mode neither auto-exposure nor the LUT applies (decision 10); an identity LUT changes an image only by the sampling's and the 16-bit float storage's rounding. `TonemapConstants` gains `TonemapFlagAutoExposure` (8), `TonemapFlagLut` (16), `LutIntensity` in place of its padding and, since the review (finding 3), `LutScale` and `LutOffset` appended (48 bytes; "Frozen interfaces"): the LUT coordinate of the display-encoded value e is e * LutScale + LutOffset, the CPU folding the domain and the texel centres into them (LutScale = (N - 1) / (N (DomainMax - DomainMin)), LutOffset = 0.5 / N - DomainMin * LutScale for a LUT of size N), so the domain needs no other binding (the contract's change, so X-A and X-B collide neither in the struct nor in the layout); `TonemapPassInputs` gains `AdaptedExposure` (X-A) and `Lut`, `LutDomainMin`, `LutDomainMax`, `LutIntensity` (X-B); the shader's steps are shared by function.
- **The LUT asset** (`Asset/LutData.h`, format version 1): Size^3 entries (2 to 64), red fastest, with a domain; `.cube` files (Adobe/IRIDAS; 1D tables refused) parsed first-party by `ParseCubeLut`, cooked by LutImporter (version 1, claiming `.cube`, no settings, no dependencies); never block-compressed (the ledger). **Built-in LUTs** in the block 0x0281 to 0x02c0, the identity LUT first: `engine://Luts/Identity` (0x0281, `BuiltinLut::Identity`, 33^3, procedural); X-B adds its catalogue entries and the procedural Lut branch. The cache's mirror is a Size^3 RGBA16_FLOAT 3D texture (`GpuResourceCache::GetLut`, place 3).

### 17. TAA (R-TAA; stream X-C)

Opt-in through `AntiAliasing` Taa (FXAA stays the default): the projection jittered by `CameraData::Jitter`, the Taa pass resolving the jittered HDR SceneColor against `TaaHistory` (reprojected by SceneVelocity, neighbourhood-clamped in YCoCg, a fixed feedback, no random term: plan §7 item 9), first in HdrPost so bloom and exposure read the resolved image; the history is per view (place 2) and starts afresh on a reset (decision 10). ### 18. SSR (R-SSR-IN, R-SSR; stream X-D)

Glossy screen-space reflections, only with `SsrEnabled` (**SSR off records nothing, so the image is byte-identical to the render without the pass**, the acceptance): after Skybox and before ForwardTransparent, each pixel whose roughness (SceneRoughness's word 0 a) is at most `SsrMaxRoughness` traces its reflection over the opaque SceneColor with the depth pyramid (hierarchical, at most `SsrMaxDistance` metres, a hit within `SsrThickness` behind a depth), writes `SsrColor` (rgb the traced radiance, alpha the confidence, fading with roughness, distance and the screen's edge), and the composite replaces, in SceneColor, the pixel's probe or environment specular by the traced radiance in proportion to the confidence and `SsrIntensity`: it removes exactly the forward pass's term, the prefiltered radiance at SceneRoughness's roughness (the reflection from SceneNormals, `Common/Probes.slang`) times its multiplier and the specular occlusion it evaluates with GTAO (decision 14), and adds the traced radiance times the same factor: where a ray misses, the probes' reflection stays (the probe fallback). X-D builds on W26.1's integrated probes and on X-C's targets (it reads SceneRoughness; its renderer-level cases run on a scratch merge with X-C, as M23's stream D did).

### 21. Terrain physics, queries and the rest height (C-TERR; streams T-A and T-B)

- **The surface rule** (`Asset/TerrainGeometry.h`, T-A): sample (x, z) at (x Size.x / (N - 1), sample / 65535 HeightScale, z Size.y / (N - 1)) from the entity's origin, each cell split along its diagonal from (x, z) to (x + 1, z + 1), as Jolt splits it, so what is drawn (T-C), what collides (T-B) and what is queried agree; `SampleTerrainHeight` and `SampleTerrainNormal` give the triangle's plane.
- **The HeightField shape** (T-B; the ledger's frozen-header change of M11's `PhysicsShape.h`, recorded here and reviewed at C26.1): `HeightFieldShapeGeometry {Heights (metres), SampleCount, CellSize}` appended last to `PhysicsShapeGeometry`, so the six existing alternatives keep their indices ("PhysicsShape: the height field is the variant's last alternative, after the six of M11" is live); Static and Kinematic only (`PHYSICS_NONCONVEX_DYNAMIC`, as a mesh; the contract's branch marks it so); never against a mesh or another height field; Jolt's quantization set fine enough for the rest-height tolerance.
- **The TerrainCollider in §5.3's composition** (the review's finding 16, recorded here and in Architecture §5.3): a solid collider like any other: it joins the nearest RigidBody at or above its entity (Static or Kinematic, `PHYSICS_NONCONVEX_DYNAMIC` otherwise), or else the implicit static body that gathers it (a collider-only ancestor's, or its own entity's, which then gathers its collider-only descendants). **The one departure** from §5.3 (collider components carry no layer; friction and restitution live on the body) and ADR 0014 decision 10 (implicit bodies take the nearest ancestor RigidBody's layer, else layer 0): when its own entity's implicit static body holds it, that body takes the TerrainCollider's `Layer`, `Friction` and `Restitution` instead of layer 0 and the default material, since a terrain is nearly always a root-level static body whose ground layer and material matter and would otherwise need a Static RigidBody only to carry them; under a RigidBody or an ancestor's implicit body the three are unused. `TerrainColliderComponent.h` and the fields' registry texts say so.
- **The TerrainCollider's body** (T-B, `PhysicsComposition` and `PhysicsSystem`): an implicit static body of the terrain's entity (or a part of the compound of the body that gathers it, above), rebuilt when the heightfield's asset version changes (an edit, a reimport), in canonical order; an ordinary implicit-static or rigid body of the physics block of the state hash, with no new body origin (`PhysicsBodyOrigin` and the registry enum of `PhysicsMethods.cpp` unchanged; reworded at W26.1's integration, decision 31, where the contract said "its body origin appended last"), so existing scenes' entries and every existing hash are unchanged (the ledger). Its contacts use M20's sort key.
- **The rest height** (the acceptance): "PhysicsShape: a sphere comes to rest on a height field at the terrain's analytic surface height" drops a sphere onto a sloped bump of a height field built from a heightfield through `GetTerrainSamplePosition` and checks its rest height against `SampleTerrainHeight` and the normal (the penetration slop along the normal, §9.1), within 1 %.
- **Queries:** `Physics.Raycast` and the overlap queries hit the terrain's body as any body; scripts sample the surface with `Terrain:SampleHeight` and `Terrain:SampleNormal` (decision 26, T-B).
- **The terrain replay** (E): `Assets/Tests/Replays/Terrain.replay`, input that rolls a ball over a sculpted terrain, identical in all four runs and failing without its input (the acceptance).

### 22. Terrain rendering and LOD (C-TERR; stream T-C)

- **The snapshot:** `Terrains` (after the probes, the ledger's order), each with its rendered world matrix, heightfield, extent, layers, splat map, ReceiveShadows, LodBias and a PickId after the meshes'.
- **LOD without cracks** (`Renderer/TerrainLod.h`): chunks of 32 cells, levels 0 to 5 by the camera's distance scaled by LodBias, neighbouring levels limited to one apart, and a chunk next to a coarser neighbour using only the neighbour's vertices along that edge, so both sides of every edge use one vertex sequence: "TerrainLod: neighbouring chunks at different levels share every edge vertex" (the acceptance's CPU statement) and T-C's GPU case "SceneRenderer: a terrain whose chunks change level shows no gap" (no background pixel inside the terrain's silhouette at a level boundary).
- **The passes:** TerrainDepthNormal (the prepass and picking prepass into DepthNormal's final targets, velocity and roughness included, so GTAO, SSR and picking see the terrain) and TerrainForward (M23's light loop, the probes, the splatted layers: each layer's maps tiled every Tiling metres in local XZ, weighted by the splat map's channels over their sum), program `Terrain` (`Passes/Terrain.slang`, `Shared/TerrainConstants.h`); vertices placed from the heightfield's R16_UNORM texture (`GpuResourceCache::GetHeightfield`, place 3); the chunks' index buffers per level and stitch in the pipeline set or the renderer's state (place 1 or 2). **A terrain casts no shadows** (the lead's note 3 left it to C26.1: the ledger's "if C26.1 makes the terrain cast shadows"): it receives them (ReceiveShadows) but draws into no cascade, spot tile or point face, a kept limit (Backlog §6), so the Shadows rows and pipelines are unchanged.
- **Velocity:** `TerrainItem` has no PreviousWorld (the view's history keeps the mesh items' only), so TerrainDepthNormal writes the camera's motion alone and a moving terrain ghosts under TAA, a kept limit (Backlog §6; the review's finding 23).
- **Recovery:** "SceneRenderer: a device loss recreates the terrain's chunks and textures and renders the same image" through `Test::RunDeviceLossTest`. T-C's renderer cases run on a scratch merge with X-C (decision 14).

### 23. Sculpting and painting through `terrain.edit` (C-TERR-EDIT; stream T-E)

- **One implementation** (`EditorCore/Terrain/TerrainEditing.h`): `EditTerrain(editor, scene, entity, stroke)` applies a `TerrainStroke` (Raise, Lower, Smooth, Flatten, Paint; points in the terrain's local XZ, radius, strength, height, layer) with the brush rules stated in the header (falloff (1 - (d/r)^2)^2, IEEE-exact operations, every sample rounded to nearest and clamped; paint keeping the four weights summing to 255), rewrites the heightfield's `.heightfield` or the splat map's PNG (Graphics/Image's deterministic encoder), reimports it, and records one undo step that restores the previous bytes (and, when the paint made the splat map, the Terrain's SplatMap). ### 28. Determinism and GPU-image determinism

- **Simulation:** the particles are presentation (decision 6's live guard); the terrain's body is the physics block's and its contacts sort by M20's key; no other M26 component reaches the simulation; PostProcess version 2 moves only the hashes of scenes that hold a PostProcess (decision 19).   A `ParticleColorKey` is `{"Time": t, "Color": [r, g, b, a]}`, a `ParticleSizeKey` `{"Time": t, "Size": s}`, a `TerrainLayer` `{"BaseColorMap", "NormalMap", "Tint", "Tiling", "Roughness", "Metallic"}`.

- **`IrradianceVolumeData` version 1** (the cooked payload; the `.irradiance` source is "EIRV", uint32 1, then the same bytes), little-endian:

  ```
  uint32 CountX; uint32 CountY; uint32 CountZ;   (each 2 to 16)
  f32x3 Size;                                     (each in (0, 10000])
  CountX * CountY * CountZ x { 9 x f32x3 SH9 }    (x fastest, then y, then z; EnvironmentData's SH convention)
  ```

- **`LutData` version 1** (the cooked payload): `uint32 Size (2 to 64); f32x3 DomainMin; f32x3 DomainMax; Size^3 x f32x3 Values` (red fastest). The source is a `.cube` text file (decision 15).
- **`HeightfieldData` version 1** (the cooked payload; the `.heightfield` source is "EHFD", uint32 1, then the same bytes): `uint32 Resolution (3 to 1025); Resolution^2 x uint16 Heights` (row-major from row 0).
- **The built-in particle texture** (0x018b): 32 x 32 RGBA8Srgb, one level, colour (255, 255, 255), alpha round(255 max(0, 1 - d^2)^2) with d the texel centre's distance from (16, 16) over 16 (`BuiltinTexture::Particle`'s comment states the integer rule). **The identity LUT** (0x0281): 33^3 entries (r, g, b) / 32, domain [0, 1].
