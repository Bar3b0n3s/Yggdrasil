- **The injections.** `--gpu-inject-fault=device-lost` sets the flag at the first submission, which is a startup submission (the DFG LUT that `SceneRendererPipelines::Create` generates in the editor's and the Runtime's initialization); the test hook `debug.deviceLost` sets it after the editor publishes its autosave snapshot (ADR 0021), which `test_device_loss_autosaves_current_dirty_scene` uses to prove the fatal autosave through the real editor.
- **A `SystemErrorHandler` that returns** makes the Dispatcher assert in Debug and answer Internal otherwise (`Automation/Protocol/Dispatcher.h`).
- **Events and statistics.** `EngineEventType` ends with `AutomationClientDisconnected` = 10 and is the automation enum `EngineEventType` that `events.read {types}` takes (`RegisterMethodsTests.cpp` expects 11 entries); `stats.get`'s `StatsGetResult` is filled by the host (`AutomationServerSpecification::ReadHostStatistics` in the editor) and is in the Runtime subset.

C18.4 freezes the hangs and out-of-memory, W18.4's contract, after W18.2's stream D2 and W18.3 were integrated (stream A starts from it, since W18.3 is integrated; stream B from the contract; plan §3.4: "W18.3 integrated for A; C18.4 for B"). The facts it relies on, checked at `64e40a6`:

- **One bounded wait, then the end.** `WaitForSubmission` (`Graphics/FramePacer.cpp`) runs `RunBoundedGpuWait` over `vkWaitSemaphores` slices, 100 of 100 ms, counted and never timed; a Hang is `FatalError(GpuHang)`. Its callers are `FramePacer::BeginFrame`, `Readback::ReadTexture` (captures, thumbnails, golden tests) and the editor's `EnvironmentBaker`; `WaitForIdle` (`vkDeviceWaitIdle`) is unbounded and serves shutdown, a swapchain's recreation and the recovery. `GraphicsSpecification::ResetWaitMilliseconds` (15,000 ms) is only the recovery's retry wait (decision 29), and nothing sets it.
- **Out-of-memory after startup ends the process.** Every site that meets a Gpu error on a healthy device turns it into `FatalError(OutOfMemory)`: the Application's `EndRenderingObjectFailure` (the swapchain's recreation, the headless offscreen target's and the forced UI target's resize) and `ImGuiLayer::Render`'s error; the editor's `CaptureView`, thumbnails, `CaptureEditorUi`, `OnRender` and `UpdateHostServices` (`EditorApp.cpp`, `Utils::IsGpuOutOfMemory`); the Runtime's game-view resize and blit; `SceneRenderer::Render` for a debug view's first pipelines (M9-F39). Only asset uploads have a fallback, a placeholder and a diagnostic (§8.14 item 7).
- **Nothing frees what can be rebuilt on demand.** `RenderTargetPool` releases a free target only after `keepFrames` frames and once its submissions completed (`EndFrame`, `ReleaseFree`); `GpuResourceCache::CollectStale` releases replaced versions, and unused mirrors only at a scene change; `Readback` creates and drops its staging texture within each copy, `AsyncPicker` keeps at most eight 1 x 1 staging copies, and the cache's upload command lists are executed and dropped at once. No error code names out-of-memory: `ErrorCode` has none, `Gpu` also means the loss on a lost device (decision 28), and `ToRpcErrorCode` answers Gpu and Io with Internal.
- **The faults.** `oom-texture` (sampled-only textures), `oom-specialization` (the debug views' specializations) and `hang` (every slice times out) strike from the start; `device-lost` and `device-lost-unrecoverable` arm at the device's first garbage collection (decision 30). No fault reaches a render target, a pipeline or a swapchain.
- **The command line.** `--gpu-inject-fault` and the GPU options are non-Dist; Dist honours `--headless`, `--frames`, `--expect-no-errors`, `--feature-test`, `--filter` and `--log-level` (§13.9). `CurrentProtocolVersion` is {1,0}, and phase 1's ledger names no protocol bump.
- **The Roadmap names the injections**: `hang-reset` and `oom-rendertarget`, `oom-pipeline`, `oom-swapchain`, each also `-persistent` (Roadmap M18's deliverables).

## Decisions

### 1. Stages and stage functions (R-PASS)

`SceneRenderer::Render` becomes one stage function per `RenderStage` (`Renderer/RenderStage.h`), called in this order between the GPU-zone hook's `BeginStage` and `EndStage` (decision 4):

| Stage | Passes (stats rows) | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| Prepare | Prepare (CPU) | — |
| Deform | reserved for M23's Skinning compute pass (records nothing in M18) | vertices |
| Shadows | DirectionalShadows, SpotShadows (M23's PointShadows after them) | shadow maps |
| DepthNormal | DepthNormal (prepass or picking prepass) | scene |
| Ambient | DepthPyramid, GTAO, GTAODenoiseHorizontal, GTAODenoiseVertical (M23's LightClusters after them) | scene |
| Forward | ForwardOpaque, Skybox, ForwardTransparent, or Overdraw | scene |
| Sprites | reserved for M21's Sprites and Lights2D (records nothing in M18) | scene |
| HdrPost | Bloom | scene |
| Tonemap | Tonemap, or the data views' composite under the same row | scene |
| LdrPost | FXAA | scene |
| SceneOverlay | Text (game texts, world then screen), then M22's Ui slot (reserved) | scene |
| Output | Upscale (only when the scene resolution differs from the view) | view |
| EditorOverlay | SelectionMask, SelectionDilateHorizontal, SelectionDilateVertical, SelectionComposite, Wireframe, Overlays, Labels, Letterbox (after an upscale with a letterbox; decision 6, added by the W18.1 integration) | view |

- **The stage functions** are members of `SceneRenderer::State` (`Renderer/Private/SceneRendererState.h`): `RecordPrepareStage` returns a `Status` (an error is one of `Render`'s errors before recording: nothing is recorded), the others are `void` and keep partial errors in `Detail::SceneRendererFrame::FirstError`, which replaces the `keepFirstError` lambda. `Detail::SceneRendererFrame` is the frozen hand-over between the stages: the snapshot, the open command list, the recording context, the output mapping, the view's flags (`IsLit`, `IsDataView`, `IsOverdraw`, `Wireframe`, `Picking`, `Selection`, `PixelArt`), the camera at the scene resolution, the scene LDR target's index, and the Output stage's `ViewColor`, `ViewOverlayFramebuffer` and `ViewOverlayConstants`. Stream A may add members only its own stages read.
- **Order changes against M17.** Game Text draws in SceneOverlay, before the editor overlays rather than after them, and the debug and annotation labels draw last, in EditorOverlay, under a new stats row, `Labels` (they drew in the Text row). The wireframe keeps being recorded before the selection composite and listed after it. The Overdraw data view replaces passes 7 to 9 in the Forward stage, and the data views' composite replaces Tonemap, as now.
- **Zero diff.** With the default scene resolution (decision 5) the Output stage records nothing and every stage draws at the view's size, so the split changes no image: of the 25 goldens only `Text` draws game text, with no editor overlay (Context), and every other order is kept. The proof is the golden and GPU suites against the base (ADR 0028 decision 3; plan §8 risk 1), run by stream A for its split (decision 12: its Render keeps M17's inline Text and overlay recording until the integration) and again at the wave's integration for the whole split, F's three stages and the new order included.
- **No render graph** (ADR 0028 decision 2): a fixed list of stages; a later milestone appends passes to a stage through decision 2's declarations and adds a stage only by a frozen-header change. Renderer still never includes Scene.
- **Phase 2's reserved passes have their stages now**, so none needs a new stage: M23's Skinning alone in Deform, the first GPU stage (the shadow casters and the prepass read the deformed vertices, and its time is no other stage's zone); M23's PointShadows in Shadows after SpotShadows; M23's LightClusters at the end of Ambient (after the depth pyramid, before the forward passes read the clusters); M21's Sprites and Lights2D in Sprites; M22's Ui after Text in SceneOverlay (decision 8). Phase 3's passes take the stages their contracts name (`RenderPassDeclaration.h`'s file comment).

### 2. Pass declarations and the one creation entry (R-PASS)

- **`RenderPassDeclaration`** (`Renderer/RenderPassDeclaration.h`) is {Name, Stage, PipelineCount, Programs, StatsRows, Targets}, Targets naming the per-view targets created for the pass (what it produces, the placement rule's targets), not every target it draws into; `ScenePassDeclarations` lists every pass in stats order and in stage order (a pass's stats position is its declaration's position). `GetDeclaredPipelineCount()` is the startup pipeline count, 58: M17's 54 plus the QuadBatcher's 2 and the UpscalePass's 2; `RenderStatsOrder` is every declaration's rows in table order, 25 rows (24 at the contract; the W18.1 integration appended `Letterbox`, decision 6), which replaces the literal of `SceneRenderer.cpp:1232` and §8.3's list. The table is data in the header, implemented by the contract, and changing it is a frozen-header change reviewed by the contract owner. A declaration without rows creates pipelines its users record under their own rows (the QuadBatcher, declared in the Sprites stage, M21's first user) or is a startup computation (the DFG LUT); one without pipelines records with another's (Wireframe and Overlays share the debug lines' two pipelines, Labels the Text pipelines, Letterbox the Upscale's Color pipeline). The mesh pipelines are declared by the passes that record them: DepthNormal 12, ForwardOpaque 6, ForwardTransparent 3, Overdraw 3.
- **`SceneRendererPipelines::CreatePipelines()`** creates every declared pass (its pipelines, binding layouts, samplers and startup resources) through the set's `PipelineFactory`, aside from the current ones, and replaces them only when every creation succeeds; the lazily created debug-view pipelines are dropped, and `GetGeneration()` (1 after `Create`) increases. On any error nothing changes. Startup (`Create`), the shader reload (decision 3) and the device-loss recovery (C18.3) call it, and nothing else creates the set's pipelines. A renderer whose binding sets were made for an earlier generation clears its binding-set caches and material sets before its next `Render`. A `Gpu` error is out-of-memory: fatal at startup (§8.14 item 7), returned by a reload.
- **`StartupPipelineCount`** becomes `GetDeclaredPipelineCount()` once the wave integrates F's two passes into `CreatePipelines` (decision 12); "Pipelines: count matches the expected total" then expects 58, a count that grows with two new passes, not a weakened test.

### 3. Shader hot reload (R-SHR)

- **Explicit, in the editor only.** `shaders.reload` and the editor's menu item Tools > Reload shaders (a Tools menu the integration added to the editor's menu bar) run one action: `SceneRendererPipelines::ReloadShaders()`, which begins a reload of the factory's `ShaderLibrary` (`BeginReload`: the next `Get` or `GetReflection` of each variant reads its compiled files again), runs `CreatePipelines`, and ends the reload keeping what it read (`EndReload(true)`) or restoring the earlier variants (`EndReload(false)`), returning the error with the context "while reloading the shaders; the previous pipelines stay in use". Every reflection pointer handed out stays valid either way.
- **It compiles nothing and starts no process.** The developer rebuilds the `Shaders` project or runs `CompileShaders.py`, then reloads; the editor reads the build output its `shaders://` mount already holds (§8.12). Starting slangc from the editor would amend §13.2's process allowlist, which phase 1's ledger gives to M19, and an editor that watched the output directory could reload a half-written build; an explicit reload has neither problem.
- **Scope.** The scene renderer's pass list, shared by every view and by the screenshot capture; a material debug view's lazily created pipelines specialize the forward variants `CreatePipelines` read, so they take the reloaded shaders when they are next created. ImGui's per-format pipelines and the Runtime's blit are not reloaded: the reload never reads their variants, so the library keeps the shaders it cached for them before the reload, also for a pipeline created after it (a change to them needs a restart). Exported games never reload shaders.
- **The method** (`EditorCore/Automation/ShaderMethods.h`): `shaders.reload {}` returns `{pipelineCount, generation}`; Unsupported without a renderer (the editor injects `AutomationServerSpecification::ReloadShaders` only with a `GraphicsDevice`); the reload's errors pass through unchanged. Not a tool, not in the Runtime or the launcher, no dry run, not a batch op, not `Mutates`; timeout 120 s.
- **Parity.** The menu item and the method share the action; the method has its C++ round trip and its Python test (decision 13).

### 4. The GPU-zone hook

`RenderRecordingContext::BeginStage(RenderStage, nvrhi::ICommandList*)` and `EndStage()` (`Renderer/RenderStats.h`) bracket every stage of every `Render`, in order, whether or not it records a pass. With a command list (none for Prepare) and a profiler, a stage is one GPU zone named `Stage:<RenderStageToString>` that its pass scopes nest in (depth 1), always with a debug marker; every stage is also a CPU zone of that name in the process profiler. A stage is never a stats row (no pass is named `Stage:...`, and `PublishGpu` matches rows by name). Stages do not nest; `BeginPass` and `EndPass` stay valid outside a stage. The hook is what keeps streams B and C out of stream A's files (plan §6 "M18"): stream C implements it in `RenderStats.cpp` and adds R-GPUCRASH's lifetime instrumentation behind it; stream A only calls it.

### 5. The output stage's settings (R-2D-PIXEL)

`RenderOutputSettings` (`RenderSnapshot.h`, the snapshot's `Output`): `SceneWidth` and `SceneHeight` (a 0 component takes the view's), `PixelArt` (decision 7) and `LetterboxColor` (linear, encoded like a clear colour). The scene resolution sizes every scene-resolution target and the camera's viewport. The default renders exactly as before (decision 1's zero diff). M18 sets the settings from tests only, at scale 3 (plan §6 "M18"); M21's PixelPerfectCamera sets them from the scene through render extraction.

### 6. The view-to-scene mapping and the integer upscale

- **The mapping** (`ComputeRenderOutputMapping`, `RenderOutputMapping`): Scale is the largest integer s >= 1 with s times the scene fitting the view in both axes, or 1 when the scene does not fit (then the scene image is centred and cropped, with negative offsets); the offsets are floor((view - Scale * scene) / 2), rounded toward negative infinity and not by C++'s truncating division, so an odd leftover pixel goes to the right or bottom letterbox and an odd surplus pixel of a cropped scene is cropped from the left or top (a 161-pixel scene in a 100-pixel view has offset -31). Integer arithmetic only. `MapViewPixelToScenePixel` gives the scene pixel a view pixel shows, or none in the letterbox; `MapViewPointToScenePoint` maps a point exactly, without clamping (decision 8). They live in `RenderSnapshot.h`, which Scene may include, so a UI system in Scene or Session can use them.
- **The upscale** (`Renderer/UpscalePass.h`, the Output stage): clears the view-resolution OutputColor to the letterbox colour and draws the scene image into the scaled rectangle with `texelFetch` at floor((pixel - offset) / Scale), so each scene pixel becomes exactly a Scale x Scale block; when an editor overlay needs depth, it writes the scene depth into OutputDepth the same way (cleared to 0, the far plane). Two pipelines, program `Upscale`. Recorded only for a mapping that is not the identity. Its viewport is the scaled scene rectangle, which a cropped scene's negative offsets place partly outside the view; its scissor is that rectangle intersected with the view, [0, ViewWidth) x [0, ViewHeight), because Vulkan refuses a negative scissor offset (VUID-vkCmdSetScissor-x-00595) and NVRHI passes `nvrhi::Rect` through unclamped.
- **Consumers.** `SceneRenderer::GetFinalTexture` is the view-resolution image (OutputColor, or the scene's LDR target at the identity); `GetOutputMapping` and `ViewportCapture::GetLastOutputMapping` report the mapping; `RequestPick` takes view pixels and reads `EntityId` (scene resolution) at the mapped scene pixel, and a letterbox pixel picks nothing (the background's result); `viewport.pick` takes its CPU ray from `ComputeOutputPixelRayInterval` (`RenderSnapshot.h`), which maps the screenshot's pixel to a scene pixel exactly once before `ComputeViewPixelRayInterval`, which takes scene pixels, and gives no ray, so no hit, for a letterbox pixel; it sets the camera's `ViewportWidth` and `ViewportHeight` to the mapping's scene resolution itself, as the renderer's Prepare stage does, because a snapshot's camera comes with the request's view size (render extraction), so the CPU ray and the GPU pick agree at every mapping (amended by the W18.1 review). In M18 no editor snapshot carries a non-identity `Output`, so the helper's CPU case freezes the mapping `viewport.pick` will see from M21's PixelPerfectCamera.
- **The editor overlays** draw at the view's resolution into `ViewOverlayFramebuffer`, so lines keep their one-pixel width and labels their pixel size, and end clipped to the scaled scene rectangle as the upscale's viewport and scissor (that rectangle, the scissor clipped to the view) would place and clip them. **Amended by the W18.1 integration and review:** the overlay passes (`DebugRenderer`, `TextRenderer`, `SelectionPass`) set their own full-framebuffer viewport, and a scissor in `TextRenderInputs` would change the font and text pipeline, M19's single-owner file in phase 1 (ADR 0028's ledger), so the stage gets the same result in two steps of its own. It draws the overlays over the whole view with the overlay camera (`ViewOverlayConstants`: the scene camera whose projection is followed by the clip-space transform that maps the scene's clip volume onto the scaled scene rectangle, with the view's size as viewport), so inside the rectangle every overlay lands on the pixels that rectangle's viewport would give it; then, at a mapping with a letterbox, its last row, `Letterbox` (`UpscalePass::RecordLetterbox`, recorded with the Upscale's Color pipeline from a 1 x 1 `LetterboxTexel` it clears to the letterbox colour as the upscale clears `OutputColor`, one draw per letterbox bar with the bar as its scissor), gives the bars exactly the values the upscale left in them. So an overlay that crosses the scene camera's frustum (a grid or debug line, a label, the wireframe, the selection composite's dilated outline, which no draw scissor would have clipped) never reaches the letterbox. Test: "OutputStage: at scale 3 the editor overlays are clipped to the scene image and the letterbox keeps its colour" (`OutputStageTests.cpp`, GPU).

### 7. The 2D colour path

A snapshot with `RenderOutputSettings::PixelArt` runs the post chain fixed, like a debug view's but with the OETF: the Linear tonemapper at exposure 1 (2^0), the sRGB OETF, no blue-noise dither, no bloom and no FXAA, whatever `Post` says, so a texel drawn into SceneColor reaches the output unchanged (8-bit sRGB to linear half-float and back is exact). GTAO keeps following `Post`, since it shades meshes only. The plan's text names the tonemapper, the exposure, the dither and FXAA; bloom is off too, because a bloom of any intensity changes texels and the path's purpose is that they reach the output unchanged. A later milestone that wants bloom in pixel-art mode adds an opt-in; M26 keeps TAA off in it (plan §4.1). Stream A implements the path in its HdrPost, Tonemap and LdrPost stages.

### 8. The UI coordinate space and the reserved slot

UI is laid out and hit-tested in scene pixels: x right, y down, origin at the scene image's top-left corner, the scene resolution's size. A pointer's view position maps through `MapViewPointToScenePoint`, a point of the letterbox mapping outside the scene. M22's Ui pass takes the slot after Text in the SceneOverlay stage, so UI is part of the game's image, upscaled with it and under the editor overlays; it draws with the QuadBatcher's Ldr pipeline into the scene overlay's framebuffer (decision 10).

### 9. The allocator parameter (R-ARENA)

- **`std::pmr`.** The snapshot's own containers that grow with the scene (`Meshes`, `Lights`, `Texts`, `PickTable`, `Icons`) are `std::pmr::vector`s, and `DebugDrawList` keeps its commands in one; `RenderSnapshot(std::pmr::memory_resource*)` and `DebugDrawList(std::pmr::memory_resource*)` build them over a resource, and `DebugDrawList::GetMemoryResource` reports it. The default constructors and every copy use `std::pmr::get_default_resource()`, the global heap, so nothing changes until a producer passes a resource; assignment keeps the target's resource. Nested containers (a mesh item's `Materials`, a text's string, the annotations' label list) keep the default allocator: switching them would change every producer's string handling for a measurement, and the measurement counts them. `SelectedEntities` stays a `std::vector` too: it holds the host's handful of selected UUIDs and gains nothing from a per-frame arena, and switching it would change test lines of `ScreenshotMethodsTests.cpp`, M19's file in phase 1 (ADR 0028's ledger: `ScreenshotMethods`), which the ledger does not allow. A polymorphic resource rather than a template parameter keeps `RenderSnapshot` one type for every producer, test and host; `<memory_resource>` is in the standard library that `RenderSnapshot.h` may include, and macOS's deployment target (14.0) provides it.
- **Measured, then decided.** Stream C measures a per-frame `std::pmr::monotonic_buffer_resource` arena for the snapshot and the debug draw lists in W18.1 (extraction gaining the resource it builds into, in C's files); W18.2 adopts it if a short perf capture on a quiet machine shows a gain of at least 2 % of the frame's CPU time, and otherwise closes Backlog's row with the numbers (plan §6 "M18"). The parameter stays either way, so a later milestone can measure again without a frozen-header change. (Amended by the milestone review: W18.2's stream C2 left no commit, and W18.1's measurement decides it: the arena is not adopted, decision 51.)
- **Compile adaptations.** The contract adapts the code that relied on `std::vector` there: `AppendRenderAnnotations` copies the icons with the snapshot's resource (`Scene/RenderAnnotations.cpp`), the renderer copies the pick table into its own `std::vector` (`SceneRenderer.cpp`), and two test lines name the `std::pmr` type (the pick table in `RenderViewContractTests.cpp`, the meshes in `ColliderDebugDrawTests.cpp`). No file of another phase-1 milestone changes. Architecture §4.7 and §8.2 say so from this change.

### 10. The QuadBatcher, `QuadVertex.h` and the samplers (R-2D-BATCH)

- **`Renderer/QuadBatcher.h`.** A record draws its `QuadDraw`s (four corners in the batch's space, a texture rectangle, a linear straight-alpha colour, a texture or white, a sampler) in the given order into one growing vertex buffer of `QuadVertex` and a static index buffer, split into draws only where the texture or the sampler changes, under the caller's stats row and binding cache. At most 2^18 quads per record (2^20 vertices); the rest are skipped and counted with one warning; a non-finite quad is skipped. A record may be clipped: `QuadBatchInputs::Scissor`, an optional `nvrhi::Rect` in the framebuffer's pixels, is its draws' scissor, intersected with the framebuffer (a rectangle partly or wholly outside it is valid; an empty intersection draws nothing), so M22's scroll views clip without a frozen-header change in phase 2. Two pipelines, program `Quad`: Hdr (SceneColor with SceneDepth tested `GreaterOrEqual`, linear output) for M21's sprites and M26's particles, and Ldr (the scene overlay's LDR target with SceneDepth, untested, the product encoded with the sRGB OETF as Text encodes its colour) for M22's UI. Straight-alpha blending as Text's.
- **Samplers.** Point (nearest) and Linear (bilinear), both clamp to edge, without mips; `GetSampler` shares them (M21's tilemaps). With Point, an integer-scale quad on pixel corners reproduces its source texels exactly.
- **9-slice.** `ExpandNineSlice` (pure) expands a frame's texel rectangle and its left, top, right and bottom borders over a destination into at most nine patches, row-major: the corners keep their texel size times the border scale, the edges stretch along one axis and the centre along both; borders wider than the destination shrink in proportion and the middle vanishes; zero-area patches are left out; texture coordinates are texel edges, without a half-texel inset. `MakeQuadPixelTransform` maps scene pixels (y down) to clip space.
- **`Resources/Shaders/Shared/QuadVertex.h`.** `QuadVertex {Float3 Position; Float2 TexCoord; Float4 Color;}` (36 bytes, offsets 0, 12, 20; Quad.slang's vertex input follows it member by member) and `QuadConstants {Float4x4 Transform;}` (the push constants, 64 bytes).

### 11. `profiler.capture` and the GPU merge (R-PROF)

- **The merge.** `RenderStatsHistory::PublishGpu` also submits an available frame's samples (stages and passes) to the process profiler (`Profiler::SubmitGpuZones`), each at its frame's recording start on the profiler's clock plus its `StartMilliseconds`, with its depth, once per frame. Stream C adds the frame's CPU anchor to `GpuTimingFrame` (its files).
- **The method** (`EditorCore/Automation/ProfilerMethods.h`): `profiler.capture {frames?: 1-600, default 60}`, a pending method polled once per frame, records the profiler's clock at its start, lets `frames` frames and then the frames in flight pass, and writes `Profiler::ExportChromeTrace` of every zone since its start to the server's output directory, returning `{path, frames, cpuZones, gpuZones, droppedZones}`. Without a renderer the trace holds CPU zones only (not an error). **The rings bound a capture**: each thread keeps its last `ZonesPerThread` zones (65,536) and overwrites the oldest, and a busy editor frame records about a hundred zones on the main thread, so 600 frames can overflow a ring. A zone of the capture that a ring overwrote is missing from the trace: the capture counts those zones per thread from the zones each recorded since its start, reports their sum as `droppedZones`, writes it into the trace's top-level `"otherData"` (Chrome's metadata object) and logs one warning, so a truncated trace is never read as complete; the caller asks for fewer frames. Stream C adds what the count needs to `Core/Profiler` (its files). Editor only: the Runtime's automation tests exported games, and `Perf.py`, the program's measurement tool, drives the editor. Not a tool, not in the launcher, no dry run, not a batch op, not `Mutates` (its file goes where screenshots go); timeout 120 s; a disconnect cancels it and writes nothing. Timings are diagnostic: no test outcome depends on their values.

### 14. The SpriteSheet asset (R-2D-SHEET)

- **The type.** `AssetType::SpriteSheet`, value 11 on M18's branch (phase 1's ledger: the name is M18's, values follow the landing order, and M18 adds phase 1's only type), spelled `SpriteSheet` in `.meta` files, the script API (`Field.Asset("SpriteSheet")`) and automation; `ReadCookedArtifact` accepts it. There is no placeholder sheet (`GetPlaceholderHandle` stays null for it): a consumer whose sheet is missing or failed reports it and draws nothing (M21).
- **The file.** `.spritesheet`, format `SpriteSheet` version 1, canonical JSON like every authored file (§6), every field written (Architecture §6.9 from stream S):

```json
{
	"Format": "SpriteSheet", "Version": 1, "Texture": "4a1c0e7d2b9f3a65",
	"Frames": [
		{ "Name": "Idle0", "Rect": { "X": 0, "Y": 0, "Width": 8, "Height": 8 }, "Pivot": [0.5, 1], "Borders": { "Left": 0, "Top": 0, "Right": 0, "Bottom": 0 } },
		{ "Name": "Panel", "Rect": { "X": 0, "Y": 8, "Width": 12, "Height": 6 }, "Pivot": [0.5, 0.5], "Borders": { "Left": 3, "Top": 2, "Right": 4, "Bottom": 1 } }
	],
	"Clips": [ { "Name": "Idle", "Frames": [ { "Frame": 0, "Ticks": 8 }, { "Frame": 1, "Ticks": 12 } ], "Loop": true } ]
}
```

- **The data** (`Asset/SpriteSheetData.h`). `SpriteSheetData` is both the loaded asset and the registry struct `SpriteSheet`, as `MaterialData` is `Material`, so `asset.create`, `asset.getProperties`, `asset.setProperties` and later the inspector edit it through the registry; its parts are the structs `SpriteFrame` (Name, Rect, Pivot, Borders), `SpriteRect` (X, Y, Width, Height), `SpriteBorders` (Left, Top, Right, Bottom), `SpriteClip` (Name, Frames, Loop) and `SpriteClipFrame` (Frame, Ticks). The registry has no integer vectors, so rectangles and borders are structs of `UInt32` fields.
- **Conventions** (the header's file comment, which every consumer follows): a frame's identity is its index in `Frames` (clips, M21's sprites and tilemaps, M22's images and M26's particles refer to frames by index; a frame's Name is optional and only labels it); a clip's identity is its Name (scripts play clips by name); texel space is x right, y down, from the texture's first row, in whole texels, and `Borders` are the 9-slice insets the QuadBatcher's `NineSliceSource` takes (decision 10); a Pivot is the point of the frame at its sprite's position as a fraction of the frame's size from its top-left corner, (0.5, 0.5) by default; a clip step shows its frame for `Ticks` fixed-step ticks (sprite animation is driven by ticks, plan §7 item 8); a null Texture is valid, its frames sampling opaque white (QuadDraw's null texture), so frames also give sizes and pivots to plain coloured quads.
- **`Loop`.** The plan names frames, pivots, borders and clips with tick durations. `Loop` (default true: start again after the last step; false: hold the last frame) is added because M21's SpriteAnimator needs it, and adding it in phase 2 would be a version 2 with a migration that re-canonicalizes every sheet.
- **The texture's size is not part of the sheet.** The importer never reads the texture, so a sheet imports and loads without it and a texture edit changes nothing a sheet cooks: the dependency graph reimports the sheet as the texture's dependent (its `Import.Dependencies`, decision 17), to the same bytes, so no reload follows (amended by the W18.1 integration: the contract said a texture edit does not reimport its sheets, which the ledger's dependency rule makes untrue; `SpriteSheetData.h` says so). Reading the texture would only work for a project texture (`ImportContext::ReadDependency` reads inside the source's own root, so never a built-in texture) and would make every texture edit change its sheets' cooked bytes. A frame that extends past its texture samples the clamped edge texels (the QuadBatcher's samplers clamp to edge); `asset.create` and `asset.setProperties` refuse such a frame when the texture is registered and loads (decision 18).

### 15. The sheet's rules and limits

- **`ValidateSpriteSheet`** checks, with one located issue per violation (`/Frames/3/Borders`, `/Clips/0/Name`, `/Clips/1/Frames/2/Frame`): at most `MaxSpriteSheetFrames` (65,536, so a frame index fits in 16 bits for M21's tilemaps) frames and `MaxSpriteSheetClips` (1,024) clips; each frame's Rect at least 1 x 1 texels and ending at most at `MaxTextureDimension` (16,384, the largest texture `TextureImporter` accepts) in both axes, its Pivot finite and in [0, 1] (a pivot outside the frame can be allowed later by widening the range, which breaks nothing), its Borders inside its Rect (Left + Right <= Width, Top + Bottom <= Height), its Name at most `MaxSpriteSheetNameBytes` (128) bytes of valid UTF-8 without control characters and unique among the frames when not empty (byte-wise); each clip's Name likewise but not empty and unique among the clips, 1 to `MaxSpriteClipFrames` (1,024) steps, every step's Frame an index of `Frames` and its Ticks 1 to `MaxSpriteFrameTicks` (65,535).
- **One rule set, every reader.** The registry carries the per-field ranges and runs the rules across fields as Validate hooks, with Generate hooks that keep the registry suite's random objects valid (the SoundEffect pattern, ADR 0015); the document reader, the cooked reader, `asset.create` and `asset.setProperties` all end in `ValidateSpriteSheet`.

### 16. The cooked format

- **A binary payload**, FormatVersion 1, little-endian, strings as a `uint32` byte count and their UTF-8 bytes (`BinaryWriter::WriteString`): the texture handle (`uint64`, 0 for none), the frame and clip counts, each frame {Name, X, Y, Width, Height, Pivot as two `f32`, Left, Top, Right, Bottom}, each clip {Name, `uint8` Loop, the step count, each step {Frame, Ticks}}, in the sheet's order (the header documents it byte for byte). Not canonical JSON like a material's: a sheet may hold 65,536 frames, which the binary form keeps at about a quarter of the JSON's size and loads without the type registry, and its reader is fuzzed like the other binary payloads.
- **Deterministic.** No padding, no pointers, floats as their bits, authored order. "SpriteSheetData: the cooked payload is the documented little-endian layout" builds the expected bytes independently of the serializer, and the unit suite runs in Debug and Release, so a sheet cooks to the same bytes in both editors' configurations (Roadmap M18's acceptance).
- **Never trusted.** The reader checks every count against its limit and against the bytes left before it allocates, refuses a Loop byte other than 0 or 1 and trailing bytes (Parse), then runs `ValidateSpriteSheet` (Validation). Its fuzz target is `SpriteSheetData` (`Tests/Data/Fuzz/Targets.json`, added by the contract), mutating both the document and the cooked form: the first tenth of 1,000 in the Debug unit run, 1,000 in Release, and 1,000,000 in phase 1's hardened gate (M20's, ADR 0028 decision 12).

### 19. The FileDialog API (E-DIALOGS)

- **Two layers.**
  - **Platform** (`Engine/Platform/NativeFileDialog.h`): the request and answer types both of the editor's dialogs share (`FileDialogKind` OpenFile or PickFolder, `FileDialogRequest`, `FileDialogState`, `FileDialogOutcome`), `ValidateFileDialogRequest`, and `NativeFileDialog` (`Open`, `Poll`, `Cancel`). Windows: nativefiledialog-extended's `IFileDialog` backend (`Platform/Windows/NativeFileDialogWindows.cpp`, decision 21). Linux and macOS: `Platform/Posix/NativeFileDialogPosix.cpp` validates the request, then refuses with `ErrorCode::Unsupported` naming the platform, as `ResourcePatcher::Patch` does there (Windows backends only, ADR 0028 decision 10). The contract writes that file in full: its behaviour is final, and it is never compiled here.
  - **Editor** (`Editor/FileDialog.h`): `FileDialog`, one dialog per site (`Open`, `Draw`, `Cancel`, `IsOpen`, `IsNative`), the injectable backend `FileDialogBackend` (a function that starts an `IFileDialogSession` for a request), `MakeNativeFileDialogBackend(owner)` over `NativeFileDialog`, and `EditorPanelContext::FileDialogs`, which `EditorApp` sets only in a windowed editor.
- **A thread per native dialog.** `NativeFileDialog::Open` starts the dialog on a thread of its own; the dialog's thread initializes the single-threaded COM apartment `IFileDialog` needs (`NFD_Init`), which the main thread cannot give once miniaudio has made it multithreaded (ADR 0015). Open waits for that initialization's result, within a bounded 10 s, and returns before the dialog is answered: an apartment that cannot be created fails Open with Io, so the `FileDialog` falls back to the ImGui picker at once instead of reporting the error at the dialog's site; a thread that reports nothing within the bound is told not to show the dialog and left to end on its own, and Open fails the same way. The main thread polls once per frame (`FileDialog::Draw`) and keeps running frames, so the automation server, the busy watchdog and a play session carry on while the user picks. With an owner window (the editor's main window), the system disables it while the dialog shows. `Cancel` closes the dialog as the user's cancel would and waits for its thread within a bounded 10 s. Its window may not exist yet (a `Cancel` right after `Open`, as the windowed child's after a few polls): `Cancel` marks the dialog cancelled, so a thread that has not shown it does not, and until the thread ends it keeps closing the dialog's window whenever that window exists (on Windows from the main thread, posting the close again), all within the one bound. A dialog that does not close in that time (a shell extension hung inside it) is left to end with the process and the failure is logged as an error. Destroying a dialog cancels it.
- **The ImGui picker is the fallback.** `FileDialog` shows a request in its `FolderPicker` when there is no backend (headless editors and tests), when the backend is Unsupported (Linux and macOS) and when the backend's dialog cannot start (with a warning). The picker learns to filter by several extensions, so it serves exactly the requests the native dialog does.
- **Requests and answers.** `ValidateFileDialogRequest` states the rules (a 1- to 256-byte UTF-8 title without control characters; an absolute initial folder; for OpenFile up to 64 unique lower-case extensions such as `.eproj` or `.test.luau`, matched ignoring ASCII case, and a filter label; for PickFolder neither); `FileDialog::Open` also needs the initial folder to be a readable directory (NotFound or Io, as the picker does now, so the launcher keeps walking up to an existing ancestor). An accepted path is revalidated as the picker revalidates its own (canonical, existing, a directory for PickFolder, a regular file with one of the extensions for OpenFile), and returned once; a refused path or the dialog's own error is returned as an error and closes the native dialog; a cancelled dialog returns nothing and closes.
- **The sites.** The launcher's location (PickFolder), the launcher's project file (OpenFile with `.eproj`, label "Projects") and the content browser's import source (OpenFile without a filter, as now: `asset.import` refuses a file without an importer). Each site cancels its dialog when its UI goes away, as it does now, which closes a native one. Every later editor tool that picks a path uses `FileDialog` (ADR 0028's ledger: the FileDialog API is M18's file in phase 1 and M24's in phase 3).

### 26. The recreation registry

- **One registry per device.** `GraphicsDevice::GetRecreation()` (`Graphics/GpuRecreation.h`) holds the owners of the device's GPU objects. An owner registers once, when it is created, with `GpuRecreationHooks {Name, Release, Recreate}` and keeps the returned `GpuRecreationRegistration` as a member, which unregisters it when the owner is destroyed; the registration moves with its owner.
- **Order.** `ReleaseAll` calls `Release` last registered first, `RecreateAll` calls `Recreate` first registered first: an owner registers after the owners it uses (a renderer after its pipeline set and its cache, the editor's views after the ImGui renderer), so it is released before them and created again after them, which is §8.14 item 4's teardown order (the plan's "tear down in §8.14 item 4's order").
- **Release** drops every GPU object the owner holds, every handle, raw pointer or key into one (an `ImTextureID`), and forgets what it recorded for a submission that will never complete (a pending pick, a frame slot's submission). It runs on a lost device, possibly in the middle of a frame that the loss ended, so it calls nothing on the device but the release of handles, never waits and tolerates a half-recorded frame (an open command list, an ImGui frame begun). It cannot fail.
- **Recreate** creates again what the owner created eagerly (pipelines, the size-dependent targets, a swapchain); what it creates lazily is created by its next use. Its error fails the recovery (decision 29). It may be empty.
- **Neither hook registers or unregisters** an owner (asserted).
- **Why hooks and not an interface.** An owner registers lambdas over its own private members, so no frozen header gains a base class, an owner with several parts (the Application's frame) registers one set of hooks, and the three places of the placement rule (decision 27) register inside their own `Create`. Why a registry in the device and the device recovered in place: every engine object holds `GraphicsDevice&`, so a new `GraphicsDevice` object would leave every back-reference dangling; the device object survives the recovery (decision 29) and every reference to it stays valid.

### 27. The placement rule

A GPU object lives in one of three places, each of which is one registered owner, or its owner registers explicitly (plan §3.4: "new GPU objects live in `SceneRendererPipelines`, `SceneRenderer::State` through `RenderTargetPool`, or `GpuResourceCache`, and anything else registers explicitly"):

1. **`SceneRendererPipelines`**: the pipelines, binding layouts, samplers and startup resources of a pass declared in `ScenePassDeclarations` (decision 2). The set registers in `Create` ("SceneRendererPipelines"); its Release (`ReleaseGpuObjects`) resets every member of its state but its back-references, its registration and its generation, and its Recreate is `CreatePipelines` ("Release and Recreate through `CreatePipelines`"), which creates every declared pass again and increases the generation, so every renderer of the set clears its binding sets before its next `Render` (decision 2). The lazily created debug-view pipelines are created again on first use.
2. **`SceneRenderer::State`**: a renderer's per-view objects: a target acquired from the state's `RenderTargetPool`, or held in its `Targets` and `OutputTargets`, a per-view buffer, a binding or material set. Each renderer registers in `Create` ("SceneRenderer"); its `ReleaseGpuObjects` resets every member of its state but its back-references, its registration, its size, its stats history, its once-per-renderer log flags and its counters (the pool's targets go through `RenderTargetPool::ReleaseAll`, a pending render is forgotten and every pending pick resolves as cancelled through the view generation), and its `RecreateGpuObjects` creates the per-view buffers and the targets of its size again, as `Create` does; everything else is created by its next `Render`.
3. **`GpuResourceCache`**: the asset mirrors. The cache registers in its constructor ("GpuResourceCache"); its `ReleaseGpuObjects` drops every mirror, keeping its material generation counter (no `GpuMaterial::Generation` is reused, so no renderer finds a binding set from before the loss) and its upload-failure count; it has no Recreate: the next `Get` uploads each mirror again from the asset manager's loaded data (the plan's "re-mirror from cooked bytes").
4. **Anything else** registers explicitly with its own hooks (decision 33 lists M18's).

"Resets every member but" is the rule that makes the placement work for later milestones: a pass that M21, M22, M23 or M26 declares and adds to the set's state, and a per-view object it adds to a renderer's state, are released without code of their own, and created again by `CreatePipelines` or by the renderer's next `Render`. A later owner outside the three places (M24's platform windows, a host's new GPU user) registers explicitly. The check that nothing escaped is the recovery's (decision 29, its release step): after every owner released, no tracked object may be alive, so a missed owner fails its loss test (decision 32) with the live counts instead of crashing after a real loss (plan §8 risk 3). Every later GPU owner has one loss test through the shared helper (plan §7 item 3).

### 28. Detecting a loss, and the lost device

- **Every surface reports and returns.** `GraphicsDevice::ReportDeviceLost(context)` replaces `RaiseDeviceLost` at every site that can return: `ExecuteCommandLists` after the flag ("executeCommandLists"), `GetCompletedSubmissionID` ("vkGetSemaphoreCounterValue"), `WaitForSubmission`'s device-lost slice ("<context>: waiting for GPU submission <id>"), `WaitForIdle` ("vkDeviceWaitIdle"), the swapchain's acquire and present, the frame-boundary catch ("Vulkan error at the frame boundary: <what>") and the automation methods' handler ("Vulkan error in automation method '<method>': <what>"). The first report of a loss records its `GpuDeviceLossReport` (the context, `DescribeDeviceFault()` while the device is really lost, whether it was injected) and logs one Warn line; later reports of the same loss change nothing. A loss whose flag alone is set (NVRHI's "Device Removed!" outside a submission, a test hook) is reported at the frame boundary ("the frame boundary").
- **The `vk::SystemError` rule.** The frame-boundary catch and `MakeVulkanSystemErrorHandler` take `VK_ERROR_DEVICE_LOST`, and any Vulkan error thrown while the device `IsLost` (NVRHI's calls on a lost device fail with other codes too, out-of-memory among them, which are the loss's consequences), to `ReportDeviceLost`; every other Vulkan error still ends the process through `RaiseVulkanError`. The catch still never resumes the frame. In an automation method the handler answers the request with Gpu ("the GPU device was lost during '<method>'; it is recovered at the next frame boundary") and the server keeps serving: the Dispatcher's `SystemErrorHandler` returns the request's error, `std::optional<Error>`, instead of `void` (implemented by the contract with the existing handlers returning `std::nullopt`, so nothing changes until W18.3).
- **A lost device does no GPU work.** The creation wrappers fail with Gpu without calling NVRHI; `ExecuteCommandLists` submits nothing and returns `GetLastSubmissionID`, while NVRHI records the lists as executed with `vkQueueSubmit` answered on the host, so a closed list drops the recording through which it keeps itself alive; `GetCompletedSubmissionID` returns `GetLastSubmissionID` (nothing in flight will run, so pools and caches may release what they hold); `WaitForSubmission`, `FramePacer::BeginFrame` and `WaitForIdle` return at once; `RunGarbageCollection` arms nothing and never asks a really lost device what completed: it waits for idle through the C entry point (finite on a lost device), retires NVRHI's command lists as completed with the queue's timeline answered on the host, then sweeps the tracker and collects the host images, the release `Recover` runs too (amended by the W18.2 and W18.3 integration: at the contract it did nothing and left everything to the recovery, decision 39); `Readback::ReadTexture` fails with Gpu. **A Gpu error on a lost device is the loss, never out-of-memory**: every caller that turns a Gpu error into `FatalError(OutOfMemory)` (the Application's frame objects and `ImGuiLayer::Render`, `FramePacer`'s constructor, the editor's `UpdateHostServices` and startup checks, the Runtime's startup checks, a debug view's first pipelines in `SceneRenderer::Render`) checks `IsLost` first and leaves the frame to the recovery.
- **The frame goes on without the GPU.** The events, the queue drain, the safe point, the fixed steps, the update and the audio run whatever the device's state; the Application renders nothing on a lost device (no `BeginFrame` wait, no `OnRender` or `OnImGuiRender`, no submission or `Present`, as for a frame the swapchain skips). Methods that need the GPU (the screenshots, thumbnails, `shaders.reload`, `profiler.capture`'s GPU zones) fail with Gpu until the recovery; a client retries after the `GpuDeviceLost` event.
- **Warn, not Error.** A recovered loss is not an error of the run: its own lines are Warn, so `--expect-no-errors` runs (the export smoke test, the exported-runtime tests) still pass; the device's messages count as before, so a real loss's "Device Removed!" (Error) still fails `--expect-no-gpu-errors`, while an injected loss reports no GPU message. A failed recovery ends the process at Critical as every fatal error does.

### 29. The recovery sequence

At the frame boundary, never in the middle of a frame (the plan's "autosave first in the editor; tear down in §8.14 item 4's order; score devices again; re-mirror from cooked bytes; recreate pipelines"):

1. **The frame boundary.** After every frame, finished or ended by the frame-boundary catch, `FrameLoop::RunFrame` calls `IFrameLoopClient::OnFrameDeviceLost` when the context's device `IsLost`, so the next frame starts on a recovered device.
2. **The autosave first.** `Application::OnFrameDeviceLost` reports a loss whose flag alone was set, then calls `OnDeviceLost(report)`: the editor publishes and writes its autosave there, CPU only, as its fatal hook does; the Runtime does nothing. The play session is not touched.
3. **`EngineContext::RecoverGraphics`** calls `GraphicsDevice::Recover` with the window the Graphics step presented to (none headless):
   1. Release: `GetRecreation().ReleaseAll()`; then the device's own objects: the `HostImageUpload`'s host images and the references of NVRHI's in-flight command lists, retired as completed (a lost device runs nothing more; the retirement must not query a really lost device, risk 8); then its garbage collection and the tracker's `Sweep`. A tracked object still alive is held by an owner outside the placement rule: InvalidState naming the live counts, where the tracker counts.
   2. Teardown in §8.14 item 4's order: the NVRHI device, the `VkDevice`, the debug messenger, the `VkInstance` (a driver reset may change what the loader enumerates, so the instance is created again too).
   3. `Create`'s steps again with the stored `GraphicsSpecification`, scoring the devices again (`SelectDevice`, with the same GPU override; the plan's "score devices again"), so `GetInfo` is filled again (an owner that reads it, such as `BloomPass`'s format, reads it again in its Recreate: `CreatePipelines` does) and the `HostImageUpload` is created again. When the creation fails, the device waits `GraphicsSpecification::ResetWaitMilliseconds` for the operating system's reset and tries once more (`MaxDeviceCreationAttempts` 2); under an injected unrecoverable loss both attempts fail without calling Vulkan and without the wait. A creation that fails both times is the loss continuing: the device stays lost (`IsLost`, its report kept) without Vulkan objects. Once the device exists again, `GpuDiagnostics::ClearDeviceLost`: one order in `GraphicsDevice.h`, `GpuDiagnostics.h` and here, so `IsLost` is true from the first report until the device was created again.
   4. Recreate: `GetRecreation().RecreateAll()`.
   5. A loss reported after the clear, during step 4, is a second loss and fails the recovery; the device is lost again.
   6. `GetRecoveryCount` grows by one, `GetLossReport` becomes empty, one Warn line names the recovered loss.
4. **Success.** The Application appends one `EngineEventType::GpuDeviceLost` event (decision 31) and calls `OnDeviceRecovered(report)` (the editor asks for a fresh UI frame); the next frame renders. The device object, every reference to it, the shader library, the pipeline factory, the GPU message counts and the tracker's created and destroyed counts carry on, so the checks of a device's whole life cover both devices.
5. **Failure.** The Application calls `GraphicsDevice::RaiseDeviceLost` with the report and the recovery's error: `FatalError(DeviceLost)`, whose editor hook writes the autosave again, a crash report carrying the fault description, exit code 4. After a failed `Recover` the device may only be destroyed (a test destroys it; a process ends): when it is lost (a failed creation, a second loss) its destruction releases what exists and waits for nothing, and when it is not (an owner's Recreate failed on the recreated device) it is destroyed as a healthy device.

- **The reset wait** is `GraphicsSpecification::ResetWaitMilliseconds`, 15,000 ms by default (`DefaultGpuResetWaitMilliseconds`): a Windows TDR takes about 2 s plus the driver's recovery, a Linux kernel GPU hang check up to about 10 s (plan §6 "M18"). C18.4 makes it also the hang's second bounded wait and decides how a user sets it (the plan: "it is a setting"; ADR 0028's ledger allows a recorded upgrade only if it becomes a project setting): decisions 40 and 41, the hang's reset wait and the engine option `--gpu-reset-wait`. A recovery waits it only after its first creation failed, so a loss that recovers at once never waits.
- **Determinism.** The simulation never waits for the recovery and never sees it: a replay with a loss injected mid-play keeps every checkpoint hash (Roadmap M18's acceptance), since the steps of the lost frame and of the next ones run as they would have. Captures after the recovery are byte-identical to captures before it: the same snapshot on the same device class renders the same image (§8.3), and a recovered device is created with the same specification.

### 30. The injections

- **`--gpu-inject-fault=device-lost`** (`GpuFault::DeviceLost`): the device is reported lost at the first submission after the device's first `RunGarbageCollection` (`GpuDiagnostics::OnGarbageCollected` arms it), once per process: the arming and the strike are kept apart from the device-lost flag (`GpuDiagnostics::m_InjectedFaultArming`, an armed and a struck bit), so `ClearDeviceLost` never re-arms the fault; the frame boundary recovers it and the process keeps running. At the base it struck the first submission, the DFG LUT of the startup, which decision 25 keeps fatal (a loss before the first frame); arming it at the first garbage collection, which only an application's frame or a test calls, lets it strike the second frame, after the startup objects exist and one frame rendered, so the process test exercises the recovery. The recovered device is never lost again by the same fault.
- **`--gpu-inject-fault=device-lost-unrecoverable`** (`GpuFault::DeviceLostUnrecoverable`, appended): the same loss, and `GpuDiagnostics::ShouldFailDeviceRecreation`, so every device creation of the recovery fails without calling Vulkan and without the reset wait, and the recovery ends in `FatalError(DeviceLost)` after its one retry: the editor's autosave, a crash report and exit code 4. It keeps the fatal path tested: the tests that proved "`device-lost` exits 4" now prove it with this spelling (decision 35).

- **In process**, a test injects with `GpuDiagnostics::SetDeviceLost(true)` (or `InjectUnrecoverableDeviceLoss`) and reports with `ReportDeviceLost`, as the shared helper does (decision 32). ### 31. The `GpuDeviceLost` event and the counter

- **The event** is `EngineEventType::GpuDeviceLost` (value 11, appended), the plan's "`gpu.deviceLost`" spelled as every `EngineEventType` enumerator, so `events.read {types: ["GpuDeviceLost"]}` reads it. The Application appends one after each successful recovery, with `Message` the loss's report ("<context>: device lost; <fault description>") and no `Tick` (the Application does not know a play session's tick; the event's sequence number orders it among `PlayStateChanged` and the others). A failed recovery ends the process, so it never appears.
- **The counter** is `GraphicsDevice::GetRecoveryCount()`, reported by `stats.get` as `deviceLosses` (`StatsGetResult::DeviceLosses`, a `uint32`): the editor's `ReadHostStatistics` and the Runtime's statistics fill it, 0 with renderer none.
- **Registered by W18.3's integration.** The contract adds the enumerator with its name and the field; registering them (the `EngineEventType` registry entry in `Automation/Methods/AutomationTypes.cpp`, the `deviceLosses` field in `StatsMethods.cpp`) changes the generated references and "RegisterMethods: the automation enums are registered with canonical names"'s count (11 to 12), so stream E registers them with the regenerated references (`GenerateDocs.py`) and Architecture §4.9's and §13.5's lists, and `GenerateDocs.py --check` passes on the contract tree.

### 32. The shared loss-test helper

`Tests/Source/Support/DeviceLossTest.h`: `Test::RunDeviceLossTest(gpu, {Owners, Use})` runs one owner's (or a group's) loss test on a `HeadlessGpuFixture` device, whose validation and synchronization validation are on and whose end checks every message and live count of the device's whole life. It uses the owners once and records the tracker's live counts after a garbage collection, injects and reports a loss, uses them on the lost device (which must return, an error or not, without a GPU message), recovers the device (`Recover(nullptr)`), uses them again and records the counts again, and fails, naming what differed, unless every named owner was registered before and after, the output is byte-identical, the live counts are back at their pre-loss values and the recovery count grew by one (Roadmap M18's "tracker counts return to their pre-loss values; synchronization validation stays clean"). Every GPU owner of M18 and every later one that does not present (plan §7 item 3: sprites, 2D lights, UI, world canvases, tilemaps, instancing, point shadows, clusters, BC uploads, skinning, particles, probes, temporal history, terrain) has one loss test through it.

The helper's device is headless and recovers with `Recover(nullptr)`, so it cannot reach what only a presenting device does: the swapchain and its surface released before the old `VkInstance` is destroyed and created again on the new one, `Recover(window)` scoring presentation support again, and the acquire and present paths' loss reports (decision 28). The owners that present, the Application frame's swapchain and later M24's platform windows, prove their loss in a windowed child instead, as "Application: a windowed run recovers its swapchain after an injected loss and presents the next frame without a GPU message" (decision 36) does: a windowed application with `ExpectNoGpuErrors` loses its device during a frame and presents the frames after the recovery.

### 37. R-GPUCRASH's instrumentation (W18.1 stream C)

Backlog §6's intermittent GPU-suite crash on a driver-created thread (ADR 0015 decision 27, ADR 0025 decision 18) gets the instrumentation the plan gives W18.1 (plan §3.4, stream C: the lifetime log and the debug-heap step); W18.2 runs the step 20 times overnight under the GPU lock (`--debug-heap-runs 20`), and the row stays open with that evidence unless the crash reproduces (ADR 0028 decision 9 holds the driver). Recorded by the W18.1 integration, which the documents citing this record (AGENTS.md, Architecture §2.3, §8.14, §15.1 T6 and §15.8, Backlog §6, `Hardening.py`, `CI.py`, `Test.py`) refer to.

- **The lifetime log** (`Graphics/GpuResourceTracker.h`). A tracker built with a log directory (the device's own tracker reads it from the environment variable `ENGINE_GPU_LIFETIME_LOG`, `GpuLifetimeLogVariable`; unset or empty: off) appends one line per event to `<directory>/GpuLifetime-<process id>.log`, a file every tracker of the process shares: `<sequence> <microseconds since the Unix epoch> tracker=<tracker address> <event> [<type> <object address> native=<handle>]`, with the events `open pid=<process id>`, `create` (Track), `destroy` (a Sweep released the object; logged before the release), `host-create` and `host-destroy` (RecordCreated and RecordDestroyed, without an object), `leak` (each object ReleaseAll gives up) and `close` (the tracker's destruction). The sequence counts the tracker's lines from 1; `native` is the Vulkan handle of the object's type (`VkImage`, `VkBuffer`, `VkSampler`, `VkShaderModule`, `VkDescriptorSetLayout`, `VkDescriptorSet`, `VkPipeline`; 0 where the type has none: an input layout, a framebuffer, a command list, a query). Each line is flushed to the operating system before the call returns, so a crash leaves every earlier event in the file. A directory that cannot be created or a file that cannot be opened logs one warning and leaves the log off; the tracker counts as before either way. Not in Dist, where the tracker counts nothing.
- **The debug heap** (`Scripts/Lib/debugheap.py`; `Test.py --debug-heap`, for the unit, gpu and golden suites, Windows only). Each Tests process is created under a debugger of the child alone (`DEBUG_ONLY_THIS_PROCESS`), so the loader gives every heap of the process, the graphics driver's included, the debug heap's block-tail checks, freed-block fills and parameter validation (`NtGlobalFlag` 0x70; the child's environment never holds `_NO_DEBUG_HEAP`), and the launcher detaches at the loader's initial breakpoint (`DebugActiveProcessStop`), so the child runs without a debugger (its crash handler reports a crash as it always does) and keeps the flags for its whole life. Nothing outside the child changes: no Image File Execution Options, no gflags, no system setting. Its JUnit reports are `Gpu-<Config>-Vulkan<cap>-DebugHeap.xml` (and the unit and golden suites' `-DebugHeap` names), so they never replace a plain run's.
- **The hardening step** (`Hardening.py --steps debug-heap`, after `asan-feature` and before `fuzz` as phase 1's ledger places it; `CI.py --milestone`'s hardening stage runs it, so phase 1's hardened gate, M20's, is its first gate run). Each of its `--debug-heap-runs N` runs (default 1) is `Test.py --suite gpu --config Debug --junit --debug-heap` under both API caps, each Tests run bounded by half of the step's 7,200 s, with `ENGINE_GPU_LIFETIME_LOG` set to the run's own `bin/TestResults/DebugHeap/Run<k>/GpuLifetime/`, and the run's JUnit reports copied beside it; the two reports are deleted before each run, so a run whose Tests runs wrote none (killed by the timeout, crashed before their report) never gets an earlier run's copy (amended by the W18.1 review). The crash reports and minidumps of the run's Tests runs, which `Test.py` leaves below each Tests run's own user data (never emptied, so the step deletes their folders before each run), are copied into `Run<k>/Crashes/` the same way, and the run's detail and outcome name the copies (amended before M18's gate, the milestone review's "Before the gate"). Every run runs whatever the earlier ones did, so a reproduction keeps the evidence of all of them; the step fails naming each failed run, and a missing Debug build (exit code 3) stops it. It holds the GPU lock like the gpu suite. Elsewhere than Windows it is skipped, naming why (ADR 0028 decision 10).
- **Tests.** "GpuResourceTracker: the lifetime log brackets each tracker's events in the process's file", "GpuResourceTracker: ENGINE_GPU_LIFETIME_LOG turns the lifetime log on in its directory", "GpuResourceTracker: a lifetime log it cannot write warns once and leaves the log off, and the tracker counts" (the W18.1 review) and "GpuResourceTracker: the lifetime log names each object's creation, destruction and leak with its native handle" (GPU), in `GpuResourceTrackerTests.cpp`; `test_hardening.DebugHeapTests` (a child on the debug heap has its flags and no debugger; the step's runs, their lifetime logs and each run's own reports; Test.py's option and its refusals).

Test: "SceneRenderer: a pick pending at the release resolves as cancelled, and no ticket is reused after the recovery", which failed on both checks without the change. `SceneRenderer::Create` creates its per-view buffers and targets through `RecreateGpuObjects`, so the creation and the recovery share one path.
- **Lost-device paths of the passes and the ImGui renderer** (C; Architecture §8.5 and §8.11). A debug view whose first pipelines fail with Gpu on a lost device returns the loss from `Render` instead of `FatalError(OutOfMemory)`, and an ImGui texture whose creation fails on a lost device stays `WantCreate` without an Error, each with a regression test; `SceneRenderer.h`'s comments of `Render` and `EnsureDebugView` (a frozen header's documentation only) name that error, and InvalidState with an assert between the set's release and `CreatePipelines`. The `EnvironmentBaker` keeps its `PipelineFactory` as a back-reference for its Recreate and has its loss test (`EnvironmentBakerTests.cpp`). The frozen ImGui loss test renders two settling frames first (ImGui sizes a new window over its first two frames), and the capture loss test captures 6 times per use (twice `DefaultKeepFrames`), collecting garbage after each, so the pool and the profiler settle before the live counts are compared.
- **The hosts** (D; Architecture §4.1 and §4.13). `ImGuiLayer::EndInterruptedFrame` (public) ends a UI frame the loss interrupted, from the Application frame's release; the frame's command list that a loss left open is closed and executed so NVRHI retires it; rendered frames keep growing indices across a recovery (the next multiple of the frames in flight; `RenderContext.h`'s comment), so renderers keep their GPU timing history; the editor's host viewports and thumbnails remove their ImGui texture keys in their release, and thumbnail uploads wait while the device is lost.
- **The loss tests through the helper** (decision 32). `GpuResourceCache` is one of the owners of the renderer's and the capture's loss tests, and `ShaderLibrary` of the pipeline set's ("SceneRendererPipelines: a device loss creates every declared pass again and draws the same images" names it, by the integration); their own device-loss cases (`GpuResourceCacheTests.cpp`, `ShaderLibraryTests.cpp`) stay as the contract wrote them.
- **Coverage** (E; decision 36). Besides the two frozen tests, `test_device_loss.py` has `test_startup_device_loss_fault_is_recovered_once_and_reported` (an editor with `--gpu-inject-fault=device-lost`) and `test_exported_game_recovers_a_device_loss_with_the_same_image` (the exported Runtime's automation reports the event and `deviceLosses`, and its game view equals a run without the loss); `events.read`'s description names the recovered losses (generated references).
- **The mid-play test is deterministic** (the review; decision 36). `test_mid_play_device_loss_keeps_every_replay_checkpoint_hash` records a lockstep play (`input.record`, then `play.step` 120 ticks), loses the device there between two steps (`debug.deviceLost`, then a bounded wait for the `GpuDeviceLost` event), steps the other 480 ticks and stops the recording, so the replay file holds checkpoints recorded before and after the loss; `input.replay {verify: true, strictHash: true}` without a loss must then pass every checkpoint and the final hash. The loss lands at a fixed tick whatever the machine's speed, so no wall-clock exception is needed (the integration's version raced `debug.deviceLost` against a replay that a per-tick cost slowed down, and AGENTS.md, CodeStyle §14 and the review checklist no longer list it).
- **Recordings that an error unwinds** (the review; Architecture §8.14 item 10). NVRHI's calls throw a `vk::SystemError` out of the middle of a recording on a really lost device (an upload's first write asks the queue's timeline what completed), and an opened list that is never executed keeps itself and what it recorded alive, which the recovery's live check refuses: every first-party list that records outside the application frame's records through `Graphics/CommandListRecording.h`'s `CommandListRecording`, which executes it, also after a failure of what it recorded, and whose destructor closes it and retires it without submitting it while an exception unwinds the recording (`GraphicsDevice::RetireCommandList`, the host-answered submission of the lost path; a recording that ends otherwise without `Execute` is asserted). - **A throw out of the recovery** (the review; decisions 25 and 29 step 5). A `vk::SystemError` thrown out of `OnFrameDeviceLost` (an owner's `Recreate` whose NVRHI call meets the recreated device lost again) fails the recovery: `FrameLoop` ends the process through `RaiseDeviceLost` for a loss or any error on the lost device, and `RaiseVulkanError` otherwise, instead of reporting it and recovering again at the next boundary; the registry's loops clear their running flag however they end. - **The other review fixes**, each with a test that fails without it: a click queued for the frame whose submission lost the device is dropped instead of asking for a pick the lost device cannot create, which `EditorApp` logged at Error ("EditorHostViewports: a click queued in the frame that lost the device is dropped without a failure"). ### 40. The hang's reset wait (R-HANG)

- **A second bounded wait.** `WaitForSubmission` keeps its 10 s budget and adds the reset wait: when the budget's slices have all timed out, it logs one Warn line naming the context, the submission and the reset wait, then waits up to `GetGpuResetWaitSliceCount(ResetWaitMilliseconds)` more slices of the same 100 ms (15,000 ms is 150 slices), each of which also reports DeviceLost when the device-lost flag is set (NVRHI's "Device Removed!" from another thread's report). `RunGpuWaitWithReset` (`FramePacer.h`) is its pure state machine, `RunBoundedGpuWait` run for the budget and again for the reset wait, unit-tested by counting slices as the budget is: no outcome depends on a clock.
- **What the reset wait ends in.** DeviceLost: the driver or the operating system reset the hung GPU (a Windows TDR, a Linux kernel GPU reset), which every waiter sees as `VK_ERROR_DEVICE_LOST`: the wait reports the loss (`ReportDeviceLost("<context>: GPU submission <id> did not complete within the 10 s budget, and the device was reset during the reset wait")`) and returns, and the frame boundary recovers it like any loss (decision 29: the editor's autosave first, the play session going on). Signaled: the GPU was slow, not hung: one Warn line, and the wait returns. Failed: as before (`GetFatalErrorKind`). Hang, no loss report after the reset wait: `FatalError(GpuHang)` naming both waits, as before: Vulkan cannot cancel submitted work, a device with pending work cannot be destroyed, and a second device on the same GPU would queue behind it (plan §3.8); the editor's fatal hook writes its autosave, which `project.open {recover}` restores.
- **Every bounded wait has it**: the frame pacer's, a read-back's (captures, thumbnails) and the environment baker's. `WaitForIdle` stays `vkDeviceWaitIdle`, unbounded, for shutdown, a swapchain's recreation and the recovery (§8.1), and no recovery waits through a bounded wait, so a hang is never met during a recovery.
- **Bounded time.** A hang now holds the main thread for the budget and the reset wait, 25 s by default; the automation server's I/O thread answers Busy meanwhile, as for any main thread that has not pumped for 5 s (§13.2's watchdog), and a hang longer than both stays fatal by design (plan §8 risk 4). The reset wait is tested only by injection on this machine (decision 46): a real reset is never provoked.
- **Determinism**: a reset hang is a loss, which the simulation never waits for (decision 29).

### 41. The reset wait's setting: an engine command-line option

- **One setting for both reset waits.** `GraphicsSpecification::ResetWaitMilliseconds` is the hang's reset wait (decision 40) and the recovery's wait before its one retry (decision 29): both wait for the same reset of the same GPU.
- **`--gpu-reset-wait <milliseconds>`**, from 0 to `MaxGpuResetWaitMilliseconds` (120,000, two minutes: a Windows TdrDelay raised to about 110 s still fits, and a hang that no reset ends still ends the process in bounded time), default `DefaultGpuResetWaitMilliseconds` (15,000: a Windows TDR takes about 2 s plus the driver's recovery, a Linux kernel GPU hang check up to about 10 s; plan §6 "M18"). 0 waits neither: a hang is fatal when its budget is spent, M5's behaviour, and the recovery retries at once. `ApplyEngineCommandLine` refuses any other value with InvalidArgument naming the option (`CommandLine::GetUnsigned`).
- **An engine setting, not a project setting.** The reset window belongs to the machine that runs the process (its operating system, its driver, a TDR delay its user raised), not to a game: a project setting would give every machine a game's value, and would need a recorded upgrade of every audited project, which phase 1's ledger allows M18 only for this and which nothing else in M18 needs. An editor preference would not reach an exported game.
- **Every configuration, Dist included.** Unlike the GPU test options it serves players: an exported game on a machine with a longer reset window is started with it. §13.9's list of the options Dist honours gains it. Its table entry and parsing are outside the non-Dist block, shared by every configuration, so the Debug and Release unit runs cover the code a Dist build compiles.
- **Not an editor capability.** No method sets or reports it, and the editor shows it nowhere: it is a process's start option, like `--log-level`.
- **Implemented by this contract**, live: the option, its range and its refusal; passed, it already sets the recovery's retry wait (decision 29), and the hang's reset wait from stream A.

### 42. Out-of-memory after startup (R-OOM)

- **Startup stays fatal.** Out-of-memory creating the device, the swapchain, the startup pipelines and targets or a host's startup objects (the Application's `CheckRenderingObject`, the editor's `CheckStartupGpuObject`, the Runtime's `CheckStartupCreation`, the Runtime's first blit) is `FatalError(OutOfMemory)`: nothing has rendered, and there is nothing to fall back to (plan §3.8). So is out-of-memory in a recovery's `Recreate`, a second startup (decision 25).
- **After startup, a request trims and retries once.** A request that creates GPU objects after startup (decision 44 lists them) is one attempt. When it fails with an out-of-memory creation (`IsGpuOutOfMemory`, `Graphics/GpuOutOfMemory.h`: a Gpu error while the device is not lost; on a lost device a Gpu error is the loss, decision 28), the engine logs one Warn line, trims what can be rebuilt (`GraphicsDevice::TrimUnused`, decision 43) and runs the attempt once more (`RetryAfterTrim`). A second out-of-memory failure fails only the request, with `ErrorCode::OutOfMemory` (decision 45), and the process goes on.
- **A request's own classification.** A request whose creations can also fail with Gpu for a reason that is not out-of-memory passes `RetryAfterTrim` a classification that narrows `IsGpuOutOfMemory`, so that failure stays Gpu, untrimmed, as before C18.4. The swapchain's recreation is the one in M18 (the review): `Utils::MakeSwapchainCallError` maps every `VkResult` to Gpu, but only `VK_ERROR_OUT_OF_HOST_MEMORY`, `VK_ERROR_OUT_OF_DEVICE_MEMORY` (the injected `oom-swapchain` included) and a failed image wrapping are out-of-memory; `VK_ERROR_INITIALIZATION_FAILED`, `VK_ERROR_NATIVE_WINDOW_IN_USE_KHR` and a failed surface query or creation stay `FatalError(Gpu)` for the Application, with a crash report (`Swapchain.h`; test "Swapchain: a recreation that fails otherwise still returns Gpu, without a trim").
- **Atomic requests.** An attempt creates its new objects aside and replaces the current ones only when every creation succeeded, as `CreatePipelines` does (decision 2): a failed attempt leaves its owner as it was, its retry starts from the same state, and a request that failed keeps the previous objects (plan §3.8: "a resize keeps the previous swapchain and targets").
- **At most one trim per `RetryAfterTrim` call.** A request inside another (a capture's resize) that failed alone returns OutOfMemory, which is not a Gpu error, so the outer request returns it without trimming for it again; `RetryAfterTrim` returns success, every other error and a lost device's Gpu error at once, without a trim. An inner request that succeeded after its own trim does not keep the outer one from trimming for a later failure of its own (the review: the rule is per call, not per request tree).
- **Never inside the registry.** While the device's recreation registry runs (`GpuRecreationRegistry::IsRunning`: a recovery's Release or Recreate), `RetryAfterTrim` runs its attempt once and returns its result unchanged, without a trim (the review): out-of-memory in a recovery's Recreate stays the recovery's failure, as the first bullet says, instead of tripping the registry's not-reentrant assert in `GraphicsDevice::TrimUnused`.
- **Between recordings.** A trim never runs while a command list records with an object an owner may drop, nor while a caller holds a reference an owner handed out (a `GpuResourceCache` mirror reference): `SceneRenderer::Resize` and a debug view's first pipelines are created before a `Render` records or uploads anything, a capture retries after its first attempt was submitted and retired, and a view's per-frame `Render` is trimmed for after its frame (decision 44). A command list still open with other views' recordings is covered by no wait, and NVRHI does not reference a texture used only by a clear, while in Dist no tracker reference delays a destruction, so dropping the last handle destroys the image at once (ADR 0009 decision 31; the review). The trim is safe there only by the order and the rule of decision 43: `GraphicsDevice::TrimUnused` waits for the device's last submission before any hook drops a handle, and a pool keeps every target acquired since its last `EndFrame`, the only targets such a list can use without NVRHI referencing them; what else an open list uses (the framebuffers, binding sets and buffers of its draws, the destinations of its writes and copies) NVRHI keeps alive. With that, the trim may run between two views of one frame.
- **Never shrunk.** A request is never served with a smaller target, another size or another debug view than it asked for: headless must equal windowed (plan §3.8). A failed one reports OutOfMemory.
- **Unchanged.** Asset uploads keep their placeholder and diagnostic (§8.14 item 7). The shader reload returns its Gpu error and keeps the previous pipelines (decision 3), as it does any error.
- **Still fatal after startup**, each with its reason: out-of-memory results of the swapchain's acquire and present (no creation to retry, `SwapchainAction::OutOfMemory`), and `ImGuiLayer::Render`'s out-of-memory in the application frame's UI draw (a geometry buffer or binding set; the frame has no request to fail alone, and a frame without its UI would be a blank editor window). Neither is in the plan's list ("render targets, pipelines and the swapchain ... at a resize, a new view size, a capture or a debug-view switch"). Nor (added by the milestone review) is a Vulkan out-of-memory error (`vk::OutOfDeviceMemoryError`, `vk::OutOfHostMemoryError`) that vulkan.hpp throws out of NVRHI's internals on a healthy device, at the frame boundary or in an automation method: `RaiseVulkanError` ends the process with `FatalError(OutOfMemory)` (Architecture §4.6 item 2), because the throw unwinds the frame or the method in the middle of a recording, with no request boundary to trim and retry at; only a loss, or any Vulkan error on a lost device, is recovered there (decision 28).

### 43. `TrimUnused`

- **The hook.** `GpuRecreationHooks::TrimUnused` (optional, `Graphics/GpuRecreation.h`) drops what its owner holds that it can create again on demand and that nothing uses now; it runs on a healthy device, between recordings, possibly inside its owner's own request (a renderer's `Resize` trims before its retry), so it drops only what that request does not use and leaves the owner usable; it keeps what a command list that is still open may record into without NVRHI referencing it (a pool's targets acquired since its last `EndFrame`); it calls nothing on the device but the release of handles and `GetCompletedSubmissionID` (which does not wait), never waits and cannot fail, and it may not register or unregister an owner.
- **The calls.** `GpuRecreationRegistry::TrimUnused()` runs every owner's hook, last registered first (an owner trims before the owners it uses, as Release runs), skipping owners without one, and returns how many trimmed. `GraphicsDevice::TrimUnused()` first waits (bounded, `WaitForSubmission`) for the device's last submission, so every submission that may have used what the owners are about to drop has completed before any handle drops, in every configuration (the review: dropping first was the use-after-free of ADR 0009 decision 31 in Dist, where no tracker reference delays the destruction to the Sweep after the wait); a hang there is a hang, and a loss there the loss, after which it does nothing more. Then it runs the registry's hooks, then collects the garbage as `RunGarbageCollection` does (NVRHI's garbage collection, which destroys what retired command lists alone still referenced, the tracker's Sweep, the host images' collection) without arming an injected fault. It logs nothing itself: the request's Warn line names the trim, so a host that trims after every failing frame never logs every frame. It does nothing on a lost device (the recovery releases everything).
- **Why a hook of the recreation registry.** Every owner of GPU objects registers there already (the placement rule, decision 27), so the trim reaches every pool and cache, later milestones' included, without a second registry: the plan's "owners of new pools and targets" (§4.1) set `TrimUnused` in the registration they already make.
- **M18's trimmers.** Each `SceneRenderer`'s registration trims its `RenderTargetPool`: `RenderTargetPool::TrimUnused` releases every free target whose last submission has completed, whatever its age (`keepFrames` does not apply), and keeps the acquired ones and every target acquired since its last `EndFrame` (`PendingSubmission`), free or not, which an open command list may still clear without NVRHI referencing it (the plan's "unused pooled targets"; ADR 0009 decision 31, the rule the pool's header and its regression test "RenderTargetPool: a free target is released only after the submissions that used it completed" already state). After the device's wait every target an `EndFrame` stamped has completed, so there it releases every such free target. `GpuResourceCache::TrimUnused` releases what `CollectStale()` releases (the mirrors of replaced versions) and the mirrors of assets the asset manager no longer has loaded (`GetState` Unloaded), placeholders and built-in assets excepted, and keeps the mirror of a loaded, current asset, which the next `Render` would upload again at once (the plan's "unreferenced uploads"); like `CollectStale` it ends the validity of the references `Get` handed out. `ViewportCapture`'s registration trims the targets of its private renderer (`SceneRenderer::ReleaseTargets`, then the renderer's own registration trims its pool, which runs after it): a capture keeps no image on display, so the last capture's full-size targets, usually the largest memory that can be rebuilt, never outlive a trim between captures (the review); inside a capture its own requests' trims leave them to it, and the capture releases them itself before it resizes its renderer to another size (`ReleaseTargets` also releases the pool's free targets whose submissions completed), so a capture at a new size does not hold the old size's targets while it creates the new ones: their memory goes with their last reference, at once in Dist and, where the resource tracker counts, at the next garbage collection, which the resize's own trim runs when it needs the memory. The device collects what retired command lists held, NVRHI's upload and scratch chunks of retired lists included. The plan's "readback staging" holds nothing between requests (Context), so no trim hook is needed for it.

### 44. The requests, and what a failed one keeps

- **The hosts.** Every host site that turned a Gpu error after startup into `FatalError(OutOfMemory)` fails only its request instead (Context): the editor's `CaptureView`, thumbnails, `CaptureEditorUi`, `OnRender` and `UpdateHostServices`, the Runtime's game-view resize and blit recording. The startup checks stay fatal (decision 42). Every host site classifies through `Graphics/GpuOutOfMemory.h`'s `IsGpuOutOfMemory`, the one out-of-memory rule: the host-private copies `Utils::IsGpuOutOfMemory` in `EditorApp.cpp` (which takes a nullable device) and `RuntimeApp.cpp` are removed (the review), the startup checks included.
- **Persistent out-of-memory degrades, it does not end.** Under a device that has no memory left (`oom-rendertarget-persistent`), the first trim drops the free pooled targets, so later renders that need them fail too: the editor and the Runtime keep running, answer automation and save, and render what they can. That is the request rule's consequence, and the injected persistent faults test exactly it (decision 46).
- **An accepted limit: a black window whose size never changes** (the review). After a swapchain recreation failed twice (its retry after the trim included), the window presents nothing until a recreation succeeds, and the plan's retry trigger is the next resize, which a minimize and restore also is. A window that is neither resized nor minimized (a fullscreen game whose alt-tab does not minimize it, a fixed-size window its user leaves alone) stays black for the rest of the run, while the game simulates, answers input and automation, and saves. A retry on a timer was not taken: no frame is presented to pace it, so a count of skipped frames would retry many times a second in a loop that no longer waits for the presentation engine. The request's Warn line reports the failure, and Backlog §6 records the limit.

### 45. `ErrorCode::OutOfMemory`

- **The code.** `ErrorCode::OutOfMemory` is appended (the 19th code), named "OutOfMemory": a request after startup ran out of GPU memory again after its trim and retry, and only it failed. It is never a `FatalError` and never Gpu, which stays a GPU failure (and, on a lost device, the loss, which a client retries after the `GpuDeviceLost` event): a client tells "repeat it later or smaller" from both.
- **On the wire.** `ToRpcErrorCode` answers it with Internal (−32000), as Gpu and Io: no JSON-RPC code is added, so the protocol's code set and `CurrentProtocolVersion` stay as they are (phase 1's ledger names no protocol bump), and `error.data.errorCode` "OutOfMemory" names it (§13.3). The game-debugging skill's tables say how to react.
- **Never persisted**: the protocol carries names, never values; append-only like every engine enum, so a sibling that also appends an `ErrorCode` appends its own after main's at its merge, by the same append-only rule phase 1's ledger gives its appended lists. `ErrorCode` is not a ledger entry (ADR 0028's ledger and plan §4.2 name the settings sections, the validation codes and the BuiltinComponents categories, §4.4 the registries), and no sibling appends one at C18.4; a sibling that does at its merge appends after main's, as above.
- **Where it comes from**: `RetryAfterTrim`, so `SceneRenderer::Resize` and `Render`'s partial error, `ViewportCapture::Capture`, `CaptureImGuiScreenshot`, `Swapchain::AcquireNextImage` and the frame's resize (decision 44). Scripts never see GPU errors.
- **Implemented by this contract** (inert: nothing returns it before W18.4): the enumerator, its name, `ErrorCodeFromString` and `ToRpcErrorCode`.

### 46. The injections

| Spelling | `GpuFault` | Strikes | Proves |

- **The arming.** All but `hang` arm at the device's first garbage collection, the end of an application's first frame, like the device-lost faults (decision 30), through `GpuDiagnostics::ArmInjectedFault` (`OnGarbageCollected` calls it): an application's startup never meets them, so a process test reaches its requests. The once spellings strike once per process (the struck bit of `m_InjectedFaultArming`, which `ClearDeviceLost` leaves alone and which a process's one fault shares), so neither a retried request nor a recovered device is struck again. `oom-texture`, `oom-specialization` and `hang` keep striking from the start, as since M5.
- **The startup.** With the environment variable `ENGINE_GPU_FAULT_AT_STARTUP=1` (`GpuFaultAtStartupVariable`, non-Dist, read by `GraphicsDevice::Create` like `ENGINE_VULKAN_LOADER`) the device arms them at its creation instead, so the same spellings prove that out-of-memory at startup stays fatal (exit code 4, a crash report, the editor's fatal hook; Roadmap M18's acceptance), and the device-lost spellings that a loss before the first frame does (decision 25). An environment variable rather than more spellings: the Roadmap names the spellings, and the arming point is orthogonal to the fault, as `ENGINE_GPU` is to `--gpu`.
- **Where they strike.** `GpuDiagnostics::TakeBoundedWaitFault` (`WaitForSubmission`, once at the start of each wait; `HasBoundedWaitFault` lets `FramePacer::BeginFrame` wait instead of polling its event query when a fault would strike), `TakeRenderTargetCreationFault` (`GraphicsDevice::CreateTexture` of a render-target or storage texture; staging and sampled-only textures are not render targets), `TakePipelineCreationFault` (`CreateGraphicsPipeline`, `CreateComputePipeline`) and `TakeSwapchainCreationFault` (the swapchain's creation). An injected creation failure is a Gpu error "... out of device memory (injected by --gpu-inject-fault=<spelling>)" without a call to NVRHI or Vulkan, like `oom-texture`'s. The contract implements `GpuDiagnostics`' side, live and unit-tested; the waits and creations take the faults from W18.4's streams, so on the contract tree a C18.4 spelling is accepted and strikes nothing.
- **`oom-specialization` (M9)** fails every specialization from the start, which makes the first debug view a renderer asks for run out of memory. From W18.4 that is a debug-view switch that fails alone (decision 44), no longer `FatalError(OutOfMemory)`, so "SceneRenderer: an out-of-memory debug-view pipeline is fatal" (M9-F39) and its death child "Renderer/DebugViewPipelineOutOfMemory" are retargeted by stream B to "SceneRenderer: under oom-specialization a debug view renders the previous mode and returns OutOfMemory" (decision 49), as C18.3 retargeted the fatal-loss tests (decision 35).
- Test hooks, as every `GpuFault`: non-Dist (`GpuTestHooksEnabled`), never persisted, values appended, the option's text and refusal naming every spelling.

### 50. The W18.4 integration record

W18.4's streams A to C were integrated as one wave; beyond what decisions 40 to 49 freeze, they and the integration changed or added:

- **The hang's reset wait** (A; Architecture §8.1). `WaitForSubmission` takes its injected fault once, at its start (`TakeBoundedWaitFault`), logs one Warn line when its reset wait starts and names both waits in `FatalError(GpuHang)`. `FramePacer::BeginFrame` waits through `WaitForSubmission` instead of polling its slot's event query while `HasBoundedWaitFault` holds, so `hang-reset` strikes the first frame wait after the arming even when the GPU has finished ("FramePacer: under hang-reset the first frame wait after the arming is reset even when the GPU finished", which failed without it); "FramePacer: without a reset wait an injected hang reset ends the wait with GpuHang" covers `--gpu-reset-wait 0`, whose `FatalError(GpuHang)` says that the reset wait is off instead of naming a reset wait that did not run (the review).
- **`GpuDiagnostics::ShouldInjectHang` is removed** (the integration). After stream A no production code called it: `HasBoundedWaitFault` and `TakeBoundedWaitFault` are the one query of a wait's injected fault, and a second predicate that answers false under `hang-reset` would let a later wait check the wrong one. `GpuDiagnosticsTests.cpp` checks `HasBoundedWaitFault` and `TakeBoundedWaitFault` where it checked it.

## Risks

1. **The split is the riskiest change of the milestone** (plan §8 risk 1): the zero diff is checked against all 25 goldens and every GPU test.
2. **The output stage** (plan §8 risk 5) moves picking, rays and captures to the mapping; the scale-3 cases freeze it before M21 builds on it.
3. **A reload between frames.** `ReloadShaders` replaces pipelines that recorded command lists still reference; NVRHI's reference counting keeps them alive until those lists retire (§8.14 item 2), and the editor reloads between frames.
4. **The `std::pmr` change touches files outside the renderer**, in Scene and its tests, none of another phase-1 milestone (decision 9: `SelectedEntities` stays a `std::vector`); each adaptation keeps values and behaviour.
5. **A native dialog on its own thread.** A dialog owned by the main thread's window joins the two threads' input; the main thread pumps its events every frame, and `Cancel`'s bounded wait keeps a hung shell extension from hanging the editor's shutdown. Only the windowed child shows a real dialog; the Linux and macOS refusal is never compiled.
6. **A frame past its texture.** Only `asset.create` and `asset.setProperties` refuse one (decision 14); a hand-edited file samples clamped edge texels, which M21's sprites may report.
7. **The sheet's inspector waits for M21** (decision 18): until then sheets are edited through automation or in their files.
8. **A real loss is never tested here** (plan §8 risk 4). The injected losses leave the Vulkan device alive, so `Recover`'s release on a really lost device (NVRHI's in-flight command lists retired without querying the device, `vkDeviceWaitIdle` and the timeline query answering `VK_ERROR_DEVICE_LOST`) runs only on a real TDR; stream A writes that path not to rely on the device answering, and the wave's review checks it.
9. **A missed owner** crashes only after a real loss (plan §8 risk 3); the tracker check turns it into a failed loss test (decisions 27 and 32), and every later GPU owner has one.
10. **A frame ended by the frame-boundary catch** leaves an ImGui frame begun or a command list open; each owner's Release tolerates it (decision 26), and D's frame release ends an open ImGui frame.
11. **The hosts' device-loss sites move** in phase 1: M19 adds the headless game view and M20 multiple open scenes to `EditorApp` and `EditorHostViewports` after merging main, adapting their registrations (ADR 0028's ledger).
12. **Reset windows vary** (plan §8 risk 4): a hang that no reset ends within the budget and the reset wait stays fatal by design, and the reset wait is tested only by injection on this machine; `--gpu-reset-wait` lets a machine with a longer window wait longer.
13. **A trim inside its own request** (decision 43): a hook that dropped what the request in progress uses would turn a recovered request into a crash; the hooks drop only what nothing holds (free pooled targets an `EndFrame` stamped, stale and unloaded mirrors, a capture's targets between captures), the device waits for its last submission before any hook runs, and the trim runs only between recordings (decision 42). A texture used only by a clear in a list still open is the case no wait covers and NVRHI does not reference (ADR 0009 decision 31): the pools keep every target acquired since their last `EndFrame`, and the regression tests "RenderTargetPool: a free target is released only after the submissions that used it completed" and "RenderTargetPool: TrimUnused keeps a free target whose last submission has not completed" hold the rule.
14. **Persistent out-of-memory degrades the hosts** (decision 44): after the first trim the views may render nothing new, which the injected persistent faults show; the hosts must keep answering automation and saving, which the process and automation tests check.
