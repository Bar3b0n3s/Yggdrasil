# 0012 — M7 contract decisions

- **Status:** proposed by the M7 contract task; the streams implemented against decisions 1 to 20, and the integration task (the contract owner) added decisions 21 to 26 for what changed after the freeze (Roadmap rule 3). The amendments listed at the end are for the docs owner.
- **Date:** 2026-10-07
- **Context:** The M7 contract task (Roadmap rule 3) froze the public interface of the walking skeleton: the play session (`Engine/Source/Engine/Session/`), render extraction and the render snapshot, the scene renderer subset, the Runtime and its manifest, the exporter v0, the play, input and export methods, the shared automation context of `Engine/Automation/Methods`, the M7 parts of `App/`, and the game and camera views of `viewport.screenshot` that ADR 0009 decision 33 deferred to M7. It wrote every Roadmap M7 acceptance test as a skipped skeleton (C++ and Python). Writing complete headers exposed places where the Architecture and Roadmap are silent or cannot be followed literally; `AGENTS.md` ("Deviations") requires a record of each.

## Decisions

### 1. Files and module placement

- **`Session` (layer 5, ≤4):** `PlaySession.h|.cpp` (the session, its step order, run state, entity cap, state hash and game view) and `PlayInput.h|.cpp` (the game's input: the tick-stamped queue, injected actions, the latched views). The Architecture's `Session` row also names the replay player, which arrives with M13/M15.
- **`Scene`:** `RenderExtraction.h|.cpp`. It includes `Renderer/RenderSnapshot.h`, one of the two Renderer headers Scene may include (§3 rule 3, the existing `SnapshotHeaders` lint rule).
- **`Renderer`:** `RenderSnapshot.h` (data, frozen) with `RenderSnapshot.cpp` (the reverse-Z projection, decision 6), `SceneRenderer.h|.cpp`, `BlitPass.h|.cpp` (§8.3 pass 15, decision 7) and the M7 members of `ViewportCapture.h` (decision 9). Pass helpers go under `Renderer/Private/`.
- **`Automation/Methods` (layer 5, may include `Session`):** `AutomationTypes.h|.cpp` (the shared reflected types, moved from EditorCore, decision 12), `AutomationMethodContext.h|.cpp`, `PlayMethods.h|.cpp`, `InputMethods.h|.cpp`, `ScreenshotMethods.h|.cpp` (`viewport.screenshot`'s params and result, moved from EditorCore), `RegisterSharedMethods.h|.cpp` and `RuntimeAutomationServer.h|.cpp`.
- **`Project`:** `GameManifest.h|.cpp`. The manifest embeds the project's `WindowSettings` and `SimulationSettings`, so it sits with them at layer 2, where the exporter (EditorCore) and the Runtime both reach it.
- **EditorCore:** `Play/EditorPlayController.h|.cpp`, `Export/Exporter.h|.cpp` and `Automation/ExportMethods.h|.cpp` (`project.export` in its own file, as `ScreenshotMethods` is for the screenshot theme).
- **Python:** `Tests/Automation/{test_play,test_input,test_export,test_runtime,test_game_view}.py` and the shared helper module `Tests/Automation/tiny_game.py`.
- No premake, `Dependencies.lua` or `ModuleRules.json` change was needed: new files are picked up by the globs, and every new include edge was already allowed.

### 2. The play session

- **One creation path.** `PlaySession::Create(specification, sceneDocument)` loads a canonical scene document strictly (`LoadOptions` Strict) into a new runtime scene; `CreateFromScene` is `SceneSerializer::ToJson(editScene)` then `Create` (§5.6). The Runtime passes the cooked `SceneData` of its start scene (§14.3). Nothing else creates a session's scene.
- **Seeds (§4.8, §4.12).** `PlaySessionSpecification::Seed` is the session seed: `ComputeSessionSeed(Project Simulation.Seed, Scene.Seed)` = their XOR (implemented by the contract), or `play.start`'s seed. It seeds the session's `UUIDGenerator::CreateDeterministic` (runtime spawns: `Hash64(seed, counter)`) and its `Random` stream.
- **State hash (§5.1, §13.7).** XXH64 over the scene's minified canonical serialization, then the tick, the `Random` state and the generator's draw count as little-endian 64-bit values. §5.1 names only the scene (plus physics velocities at runtime); the session also hashes the simulation state that has not reached the scene yet (a random draw, a spawn counter), so a divergence shows at once. M11 appends the physics state. Reported as 16 lowercase hex digits (§4.8).
- **Entity cap (§5.7).** `CheckEntityCapacity(additional)` and `CreateEntity` fail with InvalidState `entity limit <N> reached`. A document over the cap does not start a session. Automation's play-scene creations (`entity.create`, `entity.duplicate` with `target: "play"`) check it through `Utils::CheckPlayEntityCapacity` (EditorCore `Automation/Private/MethodSupport`, wired by the contract); `prefab.instantiate` only targets the edit scene. Scripts check it in M13.
- **The specification copies the project's settings whole** (`PlaySessionSpecification::Project`, a `ProjectSettings`: the editor's open project, the Runtime's Game.pak `Metadata` `Project` document), so a session never reads the project again and the systems of M11–M13 find their settings (Physics, Scripting) without a change to the frozen struct. M7 reads `Simulation.FixedHz` (checked against [1, `MaxFixedHz`]), `Simulation.MaxStepsPerFrame` (the editor's loop, decision 3), `Simulation.MaxEntities` (checked ≥ 1) and `Input.Actions`; `GetProjectSettings` returns the copy.

### 3. The step order, its observer, and the frame loop

- `PlaySessionPhase` lists §5.7's phases in order, the empty M11–M13 hooks included (`StartFlush`, `FixedUpdate`, `Tasks`, `PhysicsPreStep`, `PhysicsStep`, `PhysicsPostStep`, `FrameStartFlush`, `Update`, `LateUpdate`, `AudioUpdate`). Step 5 is two phases, `PreStepTransformUpdate` then `PhysicsPreStep`, so "TransformSystem::Update before PreStep" is observable.
- **`IPlaySessionObserver`** is the instrumentation of "PlaySession: step order matches the documented sequence": the session calls it at the start of every phase it runs. It may change the scene in `FixedUpdate`, `Update`, `LateUpdate` and `PhysicsStep`, standing in for scripts and physics, which is how the interpolation acceptance test is written before M11 and M13. Production code installs none. Simulate mode skips the script and audio phases entirely.
- **Driving.** `FixedStep()` is one tick of the fixed step, `FrameUpdate(frame)` the frame phase, `Tick()` both with Alpha 1 (lockstep, ManualClock). The hosts' frame hooks call `AdvanceLoopStep`/`AdvanceLoopFrame`, which do nothing while the session is paused or in lockstep; `play.step` calls `Tick` itself. The session counts its own ticks from 0, independent of the frame loop's scheduler.
- **Frame-loop controls** (ADR 0008 decision 3 reserved them for M7): `FrameLoop::SetTimeScale` (`Advance(delta, scale)`; a ManualClock frame runs `StepExactly(1)` at a scale of exactly 1 and `Advance(FixedDelta, scale)` otherwise; `DeltaTime` scaled), `FrameLoop::SetThrottleSuspended` and `FrameLoop::SetLoopConfig`. `Application::SetFrameTimeScale`, `SetFrameThrottleSuspended` and `SetFrameLoopConfig` forward to them; the editor and the Runtime apply the session's time scale and its stepping flag at their safe point (wired by the contract in `EditorApp::OnSafePoint`).
- **The project's FixedHz in the editor.** The editor's loop is built from its own `ApplicationSpecification::Loop` (60 Hz), but a session ticks at the project's `Simulation.FixedHz`, one tick per loop step; without more, a 30 Hz project would play at twice real time with 1/60 s frame deltas. So `EditorPlayController::GetFrameLoopConfig` returns the session's `FixedHz` and `MaxStepsPerFrame` (with the default `MaxFrameDelta`) while a session exists and nullopt in Edit mode, and `EditorApp::OnSafePoint` applies it (or its own `Loop`) with `SetFrameLoopConfig` every frame. An unchanged config changes nothing; a new one rebuilds the scheduler (empty accumulator, the loop's tick back to 0; the session counts its own ticks), replaces a ManualClock with one of the new delta and paces the headless throttle at the new rate. The Runtime needs none of this: its loop comes from the manifest's `Simulation` at startup.
- **Headless throttle (§4.2).** Headless editors and runtimes stay throttled to FixedHz frames per second (ADR 0008 decision 4), so headless play without lockstep runs in real time. While a `play.step` runs (`PlaySession::IsStepping`), the throttle is suspended, so lockstep advances as fast as the machine allows; an idle lockstep editor keeps the FixedHz pace instead of spinning a core. Nothing advances in an idle lockstep frame, so this is §4.2's "time advances only on request".
- **Wall-clock exception.** `test_headless_play_without_lockstep_is_throttled` measures ticks against wall-clock time. It asserts only an upper bound with a generous margin (at most twice real time plus one second's ticks) and that the session advanced, so a slow machine passes the same way. It plays a project at 10 Hz, so a session running at the editor's 60 Hz instead of the project's rate fails it too. It joins the two exceptions `AGENTS.md` and `Docs/ReviewChecklist.md` list (both updated in this change). The C++ test of a suspended throttle measures nothing: it runs 10,000 frames of one-second slots, which only an implementation that still waits cannot finish before the suite's timeout.

### 4. Game input

- **Everything goes through one queue.** `PlayInput::Queue(tick, event)` stamps every game input with the tick whose step 1 applies it (§5.7): `play.step` input (offsets from the call's first tick), `input.inject` (offsets from the next tick), real devices (`QueueDeviceEvent`, the next tick), and `Test.Inject*` and replays in M13–M15. A recording (§13.6) therefore sees exactly what the simulation saw. The price: with a SystemClock, the frame view of a frame that runs no step sees new device input only at the next tick; M13 may revisit that with an ADR if a game needs it.
- **Actions are first-class.** §6.7's `Action {Name, State | Value}` cannot be mapped onto bindings exactly (an axis value of 0.35 has no key, and a gamepad axis goes through the dead zone), so `PlayInput` keeps injected action states next to the `InputActionMap` and combines them: down when a binding is down or it was injected down, a press when either reports one, the larger-magnitude axis value. `Platform/Input` is unchanged.
- **Events.** `PlayInputEvent` mirrors §6.7's event set. Four enumerators carry the suffix `Input` (`KeyInput`, ...), because GCC's `-Wshadow` reports an enumerator named like a type; the registry names (`InputEventType`) are the documented spellings. `Tap` is Down on its tick and Up on the next.
- **Observability.** `play.state` reports `input`: the last tick's step view as `PlayInputSummary` (device controls by binding name, actions as `Action.<Name>`, sorted; non-zero axes). This is what "test_tap_event_seen_once_pressed_and_released" observes before scripts exist, and it tells an agent whether its input landed.
- **`input.inject {events, releaseAll?}`** validates every event before it queues any; the result names the tick the offsets count from. Lockstep sessions accept it from the owner only.
- **Deferred:** `input.record` and `input.replay` need the replay format's Luau expectations and the cooked replay player (M13, M14); they are not registered in M7.

### 5. Render interpolation (§5.2)

- **Step 0 reads current matrices.** The interpolation snapshot runs `TransformSystem::Update` first when the scene's revision changed since the session's last update, then copies `WorldTransform` into `PreviousWorldTransform` for every entity and clears `InterpolationResetTag`. Without the refresh, a write at the safe point (automation's `entity.update {target: "play"}`) would be snapshotted stale and then interpolated, which §5.2 forbids for automation writes.
- **Frame-phase writes are detected, not reported.** `FrameTransformUpdate` compares every entity's new world matrix with its value at the end of the last fixed step (or the last frame phase) and tags the ones that changed. The comparison covers descendants (their world matrices change with their parent's) and every writer outside the fixed steps (scripts in `OnUpdate`/`OnLateUpdate`, the editor gizmo, automation at the safe point) without hooks in each write path. A write that changes nothing is not tagged and needs no tag.
- **Teleports** call `PlaySession::MarkTeleported` (`RigidBody:Teleport` in M11, `Transform:Teleport` in M13). **New entities** have no `PreviousWorldTransform` yet and render at their current pose; entities that became effectively enabled since the last snapshot are tagged.
- Render extraction (stream B) honours a tag on the entity or an ancestor and never interpolates edit scenes. `ComputeRenderedWorldMatrix` and `TransformSystem::GetRenderPosition`/`GetRenderRotation` (scripts' `Transform.RenderPosition`) implement one rule: the same start pose and tags, the translation always lerped, and the current rotation and scale when the previous matrix does not decompose. They read the current pose from `WorldTransformComponent` and from a fresh chain walk respectively, which are equal wherever the session extracts.
- **Views between ticks.** A screenshot of the play scene is taken at the safe point, after writes the next frame phase has not seen (a paused or lockstep session, `entity.update {target: "play"}` earlier in the same pump). `PlaySession::ExtractView(request)` therefore first runs the step-0 refresh and the comparison of `FrameTransformUpdate` (tagging what changed outside the fixed steps), then extracts at `GetViewAlpha()`: 1 while paused, in lockstep or after `Tick()`, otherwise the last `FrameUpdate`'s alpha. `viewport.screenshot` of the play scene and the Runtime's `--screenshot-at` both use it; only runtime-only components change, so the state hash does not.

### 6. The render snapshot

- `RenderSnapshot.h` is frozen with the members M7 extracts: camera, meshes, lights (all three kinds), the environment and post-process settings, the extraction's alpha. M8 adds texts and the debug draw list, M9 the pick table and view flags, as reviewed additions; no member changes meaning.
- It cannot include Scene, so it has its own enums (`RenderProjection`, `RenderTonemapper`, `RenderSsaoQuality`, `RenderLightType`); extraction maps the component enums. §8.2's `EnvironmentData Environment` is `RenderEnvironment` (the name `EnvironmentData` is the cooked environment's, ADR 0010 decision 9).
- `ComputeReverseZProjection` (§8.3's two mappings) is implemented in `Renderer/RenderSnapshot.cpp` with `Core/DetMath`, so Scene's extraction, which is in the simulation path's lint scope, may call it, and the matrix is identical in every configuration.

### 7. The scene renderer, M7 subset

- Passes 1 (Prepare), 4 (depth/normal prepass: `SceneDepth`, `SceneNormals`; no `EntityId` until M9), 7 (forward opaque) and 11 (tonemap + encode), as `SceneRenderer.h` documents.
- **Lighting:** Lambert with the snapshot's first directional light, plus the constant ambient `RenderEnvironment::FallbackColor * Intensity` (§5.3: "ambient when no map"), times the material's `BaseColor` factor. Maps, PBR and IBL arrive with M8. Every material renders opaque.
- **Tonemap:** Linear (clamp) after `2^ExposureEV`, sRGB OETF in the shader, whatever `Tonemap` says; the others, dither, bloom and FXAA are M8's. Cameras clear to `ClearColor` in both clear modes until the skybox (M8).
- **One pipeline set per device.** `SceneRendererPipelines` creates every pipeline of the pass list once at startup; every `SceneRenderer` (the host's game view, the `ViewportCapture` of screenshots, the M10 scene viewport) takes it in `Create` and owns only its targets and per-view buffers, and `ViewportCapture::CreateForScenes` takes the host's set. So startup creates each pipeline once and §8.5's tested pipeline count does not depend on the number of views; splitting pipelines from targets later would have changed the frozen `Create` signatures.
- **`BlitPass`** presents `LdrColor` into a framebuffer of another format (the swapchain's BGRA8, an RGBA8 target): the Runtime's game view (Dist has no ImGui) and the editor's game view under its UI until the viewport panels (M10). Its pipeline is created for one format at startup (§8.12).
- The Slang programs and their `Shared/*.h` structs (`DrawConstants`, the light and tonemap constants) are stream B's internal design, not frozen: no other stream uses them. `Shaders.LayoutsMatchReflection` gains `SceneRendererPipelines::GetLayoutDescriptions` and `BlitPass::GetLayoutDescription`.

### 8. Run states, lockstep and play.step

- **States** use the names `_meta.playState`, `session.info`'s `playState` and `PlayStateChanged` have had since M4: Edit (editor without a session), Play, Simulate (running, by mode), Paused (`mode` tells which). One vocabulary on the wire: play.state's `state` equals `playState`, and one mapping produces it (`GetPlayRunState` and `PlayRunStateToString` in `PlayMethods.h`, used by play.state, session.info, both hosts' `_meta` and `PlayStateChanged`, whose event `Utils::MakePlayStateChangedEvent` builds for every host). **Lockstep** belongs to the client that started it (`play.start {lockstep: true}`); other clients' `play.stop`, `play.pause`, `play.resume`, `play.step`, `play.setTimeScale` and `input.inject` fail with InvalidState naming the owner (§13.6). Edits of the play scene stay open to every client: lockstep owns time, not the scene; an observer debugging the session needs them, and a recording (M13) is invalidated by them anyway. The owner's `play.pause` leaves lockstep and pauses, like its disconnect (§13.2); `play.resume` of a lockstep session is InvalidState. Without lockstep, `play.step` needs a paused session (§5.6 "Step(n)") and leaves it paused; on a running session it is InvalidState. One `play.step` runs at a time per session. A pending `play.step` holds no session pointer: each `Poll` resolves the session again and checks its serial (`PlaySession::GetSerial`, decision 21), and one that ended meanwhile (the owner's `play.stop`, the M10 UI's Stop, another client's `play.stop` of a paused session) resolves the step with Cancelled naming the ticks run.
- **`play.step`** runs as many ticks per frame as fit `PlayStepFrameBudget` (50 ms of the host's wall clock, `AutomationMethodContext::GetWallClockTime`, which tests script), at least one. `render`: `every` (one tick per frame, each extracted), `last` (default: budgeted ticks, only the last extracted), `none`. The result carries `tick`, `stateHash`, `ticks`, `frames` (host frames spanned) and `rendered` (extractions), which is how "test_lockstep_600_ticks_render_last" and "test_play_step_respects_frame_budget_and_reports_state_hash" observe the policy without a GPU: extraction is part of the frame phase (§5.7 step 4) and runs with `--renderer none` too. The split across frames never changes results. The budget test steps 200,000 ticks, which no machine fits in one 50 ms frame (that would take under 250 ns per tick), and asserts 2 ≤ `frames` < `ticks`, so an implementation that ignores the budget or never uses it fails; the replay of the same total in one call must reach the same hash. The exact split is tested in C++ against a scripted wall clock ("PlayMethods: a play.step frame runs the ticks that fit 50 ms of the host's wall clock, and at least one").
- **`play.state`** works in Edit (state `Edit`), so agents poll it freely. `play.start`, `play.pause`, `play.resume` and `play.setTimeScale` return the state after the call; `play.stop` the final tick and hash.
- **Disconnect.** `AutomationServer::State::RemoveClient` cancels the client's pending operations first (a running `play.step` clears its stepping flag and re-enables extraction), then calls `EditorPlayController::OnClientDisconnected`, which releases the client's lockstep and pauses play (wired by the contract). The Runtime's server does the same.
- **Time scale:** `play.setTimeScale` and `play.start {timeScale}` accept 0 to `PlaySession::MaxTimeScale` (100).

### 9. Screenshots: the game and camera views

- **Views.** `view: "game"` renders the target scene through its primary camera: the play scene while playing, else the edit scene, because §13.11 step 7 looks at the game view while building. Without a primary camera it is InvalidState naming `SCENE_NO_PRIMARY_CAMERA`. `view: "scene"` renders the target scene through the editor's scene-view camera. `camera: <EntityRef>` renders through that camera entity of the view's target scene (InvalidParams at `/camera` without a `CameraComponent`, NotFound when it names nothing).
- **Wire additions** (the M5 wire contract otherwise unchanged): the param `target` (§13.4, as every read method has it) and the result members `target` and `camera` (the `EntitySummary` of the camera entity; empty for the scene view), so an agent learns which scene and camera it is looking at.
- **The scene-view camera** is `EditorContext::GetSceneViewCamera`: until the editor camera and `viewport.camera` (M10), a fixed default `ExplicitRenderCamera` (perspective 60°, at (0, 3, 10) looking at the origin). The Runtime has no scene view: `view: "scene"` is Unsupported there at `/view` (§13.5: the Runtime serves the game view).
- **Interpolation alpha** of a screenshot of the play scene is `PlaySession::GetViewAlpha` (decision 5); 1 for the edit scene. `ScreenshotMethods.h` and `RenderSnapshot.h` state the same rule.
- **Capture path.** The handler extracts the view for the requested size (`PlaySession::ExtractView` for the play scene, `ExtractRenderSnapshot` for the edit scene) and the host renders it (`AutomationMethodContext::CaptureView`; the editor's `ScreenshotCaptures::View` over `ViewportCapture::Capture(request, snapshot)`). `ViewportCapture::CreateForScenes` owns a `SceneRenderer` over the host's `SceneRendererPipelines`. The headers that name `ViewportScreenshotRequest` in a capture signature (`AutomationMethodContext.h`, `RuntimeAutomationServer.h`, EditorCore's `ScreenshotMethods.h`) forward-declare it, so NVRHI stays out of the automation headers. The M5 members (`Create` with the triangle, `Capture(request)`, `ScreenshotCaptures::Viewport`) stay until stream B moves their last callers (the Triangle golden then draws `TrianglePass` directly) and removes them in the same change.
- `debugView` (M8) and `annotate` (M9) stay refused at their pointer.
- **"test_exported_screenshot_matches_editor"** compares the editor's game view after `play.step {ticks: 60}` in lockstep with the exported game's `--screenshot-at 60:<path>` at the manifest window's size, using Testing/ImageCompare's rule with the golden thresholds of §15.4 (the Python helper `compare_png` calls a C++ entry point stream B chooses).

### 10. The manifest, the paks and the Runtime

- **Game.json** (`GameManifest.h`, frozen): §14.1's keys in its order; `Window` is the full `WindowSettings` (with `Resizable`, which §14.1's example omits); `Paks` lists exactly Engine.pak then Game.pak (any other count is Validation at `/Paks`), paths relative to the manifest; hashes and the start scene as 16 hex digits; strict reading with located errors. `"Testing": true` is Unsupported at `/Testing` until testing exports (M15), in reading and writing, because the M7 Runtime would ignore it. `GameManifestSerializer` needs no `EngineContext`, because the Runtime's factory reads the manifest before the `ProcessContext` exists (the application name decides the user-data folder, §4.4).
- **Engine.pak mount.** `EngineContextSpecification::EnginePak` (and `ApplicationSpecification::EnginePak`) opens the pak, verifies its TOC and mounts it read-only as `engine://` in the EngineResources step, before the Graphics step, whose `ShaderLibrary` root is then `engine://Shaders` in every configuration: an exported Debug or Release game renders with its pak's SPIR-V, never the build machine's shader directory. `GetEnginePak` hands the opened pak to the `RuntimeAssetManager`. This completes ADR 0009 decision 4's "M7 mounts in this step".
- **Game.pak** is mounted as `project://` by `RuntimeApp`; its TOC `Metadata` is `{"Project": <the canonical ProjectSettings document>}` (§14.1: the project settings ride in the TOC). Engine.pak's `Metadata` is `{"Configuration", "EngineVersion"}`.
- **The Runtime's options** (`RuntimeApp.h`, none in Dist): `--manifest <path>` (the C++ tests run the built Runtime against test games without copying it), `--screenshot-at <T>:<path>` (after the frame in which the tick reaches T; the session's `ExtractView`), `--automation[=<port>]` and `--paused` (the session starts paused at tick 0). `play.start` is editor-only, so without `--paused` a client of an exported build could only pause at a tick that depends on the wall clock; with it, `play.step` advances the paused session from tick 0 and its state hashes match the editor's lockstep session tick for tick, which `test_runtime_automation_subset` checks. A missing or invalid manifest is `ExitCode::InitFailed` (3) naming the file; a pak whose hash differs from the manifest likewise.
- Until stream C lands, the Runtime keeps its M6 behaviour (the contract changed only declarations), so the existing Runtime tests stay green; stream C moves them onto test games (`--manifest`). `RuntimeApp::OnInitialize`, `OnShutdown` and `CreateRuntimeApp` keep their M6 bodies behind `ENGINE_CONTRACT_STUB();`, so the strict gate fails until stream C replaces them.
- The Runtime's non-Dist F1 overlay (§8.3 pass 15, §14.4) is not part of M7.

### 11. The editor's play mode

- `EditorPlayController` (owned by `EditorContext`, `GetPlay()`) holds at most one session. `Start` refuses while a session runs, without a scene, and while the asset manager has error diagnostics (§7.2: the failure policy blocks play and export); it maps `PlayStartOptions` and the project's settings to the specification.
- **Play-scene edits are transient** (§13.4): a `SceneEdit` whose changes touch the play scene records no command and reports `undoIndex` 0; stream A implements this in the edit machinery (`Commands/SceneEdit`), not per method.
- **Reloads** are deferred while a session is in lockstep (§7.5 race rule 4, `EditorAssetManager` deferral) and applied at `Stop`; a reload during ordinary play marks the session modified, whatever started it: `EditorAssetManager::SetReloadListener` reports every published reload (external changes, the editor's own writes of a source or its `.meta`, `Reimport`), and `EditorContext` marks the session.
- `MetaState` reports `GetPlayStateName` and `GetTick` (wired by the contract in `AutomationServer::GetMetaState`).

### 12. The shared automation context and the Runtime subset

- **`AutomationMethodContext`** (ADR 0008 decision 26) is the intermediate base of `EditorMethodContext` and the Runtime's context. The contract implemented its `IsHostType` and the editor's overrides of its services (`EditorMethodContext.cpp`: target scenes with the play scene, the play controller, client names, the event log, the view capture, the scene-view camera, output files).
- **Moved by the contract:** the shared reflected types (`SceneTarget`, `EntitySummary`, `AssetSummary`, `NoParams`, `ToAutomationCounter` and the engine enums automation reports, now registered by `RegisterAutomationSharedTypes`; EditorCore's `RegisterAutomationCommonTypes` adds only `CommandOrigin`) and `viewport.screenshot`'s params and result. Their names and wire format are unchanged.
- **Moved by stream C (amends ADR 0008 decision 26, which gave the move to the contract):** the handlers of the M4–M6 Runtime-subset methods (`session.*`, `rpc.discover`, `scene.tree|query|get`, `entity.get`, `entity.bounds`, `log.read`, `events.read`), with their params, results and registration, unchanged in wire format. Moving working handlers is implementation, not interface: done by the contract it would have replaced working code with stubs. Stream C adds the pure virtual functions those handlers need to `AutomationMethodContext` as reviewed additions; nothing frozen changes.
- **`RuntimeAutomationServer`** hosts the protocol on the Runtime's session: the shared methods (`RegisterSharedMethods(methods, AutomationHost::Runtime)`), its session file under `<UserData>/<Name>/Automation/Sessions`, output files under `user://Automation/Out/`. It is compiled into Engine but created by the Runtime only under `#if !defined(ENGINE_DIST)`, so the Dist map file lists none of its symbols (§14.4, M15's check).

### 13. Registration and the catalogue

- As in M6 (ADR 0010 decision 20), the new methods' `Register*MethodTypes` and `Register*Methods` are stubs that register nothing, so the method catalogue, `Tools/MCP/catalog.json` and the coverage gate are unchanged until each stream registers its methods with its tests. The contract wired every call already: `RegisterSharedMethodTypes` and `RegisterSharedMethods` (input, play, viewport.screenshot) from `RegisterEditorMethodTypes`/`RegisterEditorMethods`, and `RegisterExportMethodTypes`/`RegisterExportMethods`. So streams A, B and D register without touching `RegisterMethods.cpp`.
- When stream B registers the shared `viewport.screenshot`, it removes the EditorCore registration in the same change (one name, one registration).
- `Tools/MCP/catalog.json` is generated: each stream regenerates it in its branch for its own tests, and the integration task regenerates it after the merge; a conflict in it is resolved by regenerating, never by hand.

### 14. The exporter v0 and project.export

- **Package and steps** as `Exporter.h` documents (§14.1, §14.2). Engine.pak holds every `.spv` and `.refl.json` of the target configuration's shader directory as plain files under `Shaders/` (the `PipelineFactory` checks layouts against the reflection at run time, §8.12, so the reflection ships too), and the `File` built-ins of `EngineAssets.json` from the engine cooked cache; the two HDRIs are left out with a warning until M8 bakes them (Roadmap: "no environments yet"). Procedural built-ins are generated by the Runtime's asset manager.
- **Pending and cancellable.** `project.export` is a pending operation polled once per frame; validation and cooking spread over frames, the smoke test's process is polled, and a disconnect cancels and removes the staging directory. It is `Mutates` (read-only editors refuse it), not dry-runnable (§13.4), not batchable.
- **Where.** Output under `<Project>/Build/` (§13.2), by default `Build/<Platform>-<Config>/<Name>` (§14.1); `outDir` must lie under `Build/`. The binaries come from `ExportSpecification::BinaryRoot` (`AutomationServerSpecification::ExportBinaryRoot`, `<repo>/bin`, wired by the contract in `EditorApp`). A missing Runtime is NotFound naming the `Build.py` command (§14.2 step 4).

### 22. Choices the streams made where the contract was silent (accepted)

### 24. The M6 remote CI backlog (ADR 0011)

The M6 merge's GitHub Actions run failed on Linux; the integration fixed each failure at its root. They are verified locally with MSVC and clang-cl only; the Linux jobs confirm them.

- **Clang 19 with libstdc++:** `std::optional<State::Project>::emplace()` in `EditorAssetManager::OpenProject` found no viable overload: a class nested in another has its default member initializers parsed only when the enclosing class is complete (CWG 1397), so libstdc++'s constructibility check, instantiated inside `State`, saw it as not default-constructible. The struct is now `OpenProjectState` at namespace scope. The same compile error failed Lint's naming check, which compiles every file with clang-tidy.
- **GCC 14 `-Wchanges-meaning`:** `RuntimeAssetManager`'s `LoadSlot` declared `AssetRef<Asset> Asset`, a member that changes the meaning of the type name `Asset` inside the class; the member is `Loaded`.
- **GCC 14 Release `-Wmaybe-uninitialized`:** in `AssetEditCommand::CreateForWrites`, a `std::optional<Buffer>` moved out of a `Result` into a local and then into the edit made GCC's -O2 flow analysis report the vector as possibly uninitialized. `ReadAssetFileState` now fills the edit's `Before` in place (`Status`, `std::optional::emplace`), so no optional buffer is moved.

### 25. Remaining known limits

- A cancelled `play.step` (its client disconnected, or its session ended) leaves the input it queued for ticks it never ran in the session's queue. This is deliberate: dropping it could leave a key it pressed held down forever, since its release may be among the dropped events; `input.inject {releaseAll: true}` clears the controls explicitly.
- Play-scene edits append no `EntityCreated` or `ComponentChanged` events: an event does not say which scene it is about. M10's play-mode UI decides whether events gain a scene.
- In Debug builds, every `SceneEdit` during play also snapshots every play-scene entity for the untouched-entity check (§12.3), so edits of large play scenes cost more in Debug.
- The walking-skeleton renderer ignores emissive colour and every texture, so the Error material is flat magenta only where it is lit.
- The Dist `Runtime.map` lists ImGui symbols, because `Application.cpp` references `ImGuiLayer` unconditionally; it lists no `AutomationServer` or `AssetPipeline` symbol. M15's map check must handle it.
- `Editor --export` waits for M15 (decision 14).
- GCC 14, Clang 19 with libstdc++ and Apple Clang build only in GitHub Actions; locally the integration ran MSVC and clang-cl (Release and Dist).
- **§8.2 frame structure, two deviations.** `SceneRenderer` creates its pass targets (`SceneDepth`, `SceneNormals`, `SceneColor`, `LdrColor`) itself instead of taking them from `RenderTargetPool`, and wraps each pass in a debug marker but no per-pass `GpuProfileScope`: the host times the whole view (`Viewport` in the editor, `GameView` in the Runtime, both through `RenderContext::Profiler`). The M7 pass list has four fixed-size targets per view and nothing reads per-pass timings yet; M8 takes the pass targets from `RenderTargetPool` when the HDR chain gains transient targets (SSAO, bloom), and M9 adds the per-pass scopes with `stats.get`'s per-pass detail. Both change `SceneRenderer`'s frozen interface (a pool and a profiler to record with) and wait for those milestones' contracts.
- **§5.6's benchmark gate** (1,000 entities copy in under 30 ms, Release) is not measured by a test in M7: a wall-clock gate on shared CI runners would need a recorded exception and still be noisy. The integration measured it on the reference machine: in Release, `play.start` of a 1,000-entity scene took 36 ms round trip (median of five) and `play.state` of the same session 20 ms (both compute the state hash and wait for the editor's frame), so the copy takes about 15 ms, inside the gate. M15's perf capture (`bin/TestResults/Perf.json`, Roadmap M15) takes it over as a tracked number.
- **The Runtime's out-of-memory paths at render time** (`SceneRenderer::Resize`, a `BlitPass` binding set) end the process through `FatalError(OutOfMemory)` as the editor's do (§8.14 item 7).
