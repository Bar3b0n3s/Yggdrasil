The facts this contract relies on, checked in the code at the base:

1. **Contact events go to body owners only.** PostStep collapses the step's records into body-pair events and sorts them by `(Low, High, Type)` (`Engine/Source/Engine/Scene/PhysicsSystem.cpp:1582`); each event is delivered to the two body owners, and a compound's child collider appears only as `contact.Collider` or `contact.OtherCollider`. Bricks gathered into one static compound therefore never receive their own `OnCollisionEnter` (ADR 0026 P01; the skills' pattern gives each brick its own `Static` body instead).
2. **Speculative contacts start pairs (P03 reproduces).** The world's listener appends an Added record for every manifold Jolt reports in `OnContactAdded` (`Engine/Source/Engine/Physics/Private/PhysicsContactListener.cpp:76-100`), and `OnContactPersisted` only counts the step's contacts (`:102-106`). Jolt reports a manifold as soon as two shapes are within its speculative contact distance (2 cm), still apart. The contract's reproduction, "PhysicsSystem: a speculative contact that never touches reports no enter" (decision 3), run forced against the base with `--no-skip`: a 0.25 m ball gliding along +X at 3 m/s without gravity, 1 cm above a static brick's top face, crosses the brick with its height and velocity unchanged (the case's position and velocity checks pass), yet the ball and the brick both receive `OnCollisionEnter` and the ball `OnCollisionExit` (the three event checks fail). The opposite case, "PhysicsSystem: a speculative contact that bounces a body enters at the step it bounces" (a ball at 30 m/s with restitution 1 against a static wall), passes at the base: its enter arrives at the step its velocity reverses. The fix must keep that: Jolt's solver applies a speculative contact's impulse and restitution when the pair would meet within the step, before the shapes touch, so "touching" cannot mean a non-negative penetration depth alone (decision 3).
3. **An entity pending destruction is unreadable at once.** `Scene::FindEntityByID` returns an invalid handle for an entity marked by `DestroyEntity` (`Engine/Source/Engine/Scene/Scene.h:108-111`), and `ScriptProxy::ValidateEntity` refuses every access, reads included, with `entity <id> was destroyed` (`Engine/Source/Engine/Scripting/ScriptProxy.cpp:52-61`), so a partner's collision callback that reads an entity destroyed earlier in the same dispatch raises a script error (ADR 0026 P02).
4. **`Scene.Load` reloads the saved file, and there is no `Scene.Reload`.** `Scene.Load` (`Engine/Source/Engine/Scripting/Bindings/Scene.cpp:250-264`) loads the scene asset through the asset manager (`Engine/Source/Engine/Session/PlaySession.cpp:365-389`), the saved file: in editor play, `Scene.Load` of the scene being played drops the unsaved edits the play copy holds. A second request in one frame is refused with InvalidState (`PlaySession.cpp:368`). No binding names the current scene (ADR 0026 P06).
5. **The Play copy keeps no document.** `PlaySession::CreateFromScene` serializes the edit scene to an in-memory document and creates the session from it (`Engine/Source/Engine/Session/PlaySession.h:335-337`); the document is dropped afterwards. The 1,000-entity copy's 30 ms budget is tracked warning-only by `Perf.py`'s `playCopy/1000Entities` (Architecture §5.6, ADR 0024 decision 16).
6. **Prefab members cannot be deleted.** `PrefabOverrideKind` has no deletion (`Engine/Source/Engine/Scene/Components/PrefabInstanceComponent.h:16-22`), so the setup's E-PFB-REFUSE refuses it: `Utils::FindKeptPrefabMember` (`Editor/Source/EditorCore/Private/PrefabInstances.h:31-43`) in `entity.destroy` (`Editor/Source/EditorCore/Automation/EntityMethods.cpp:403`) and `prefab.create {replaceWithInstance}` (`Editor/Source/EditorCore/Automation/PrefabMethods.cpp:196`), and the hierarchy panel shows its hint.
7. **Nested instances are flattened.** A prefab document never holds a `Prefab` or `PrefabLink` component (`Engine/Source/Engine/Scene/Prefab.h:27-28`; `PREFAB_NESTED_INSTANCE`, `Engine/Source/Engine/Scene/LoadReport.h:113-115`), `Prefab::CreateFromEntity` drops them, and "PrefabInstantiator: an instance of a prefab created from nested instances is one flat instance" pins that. An override names its target by one prefab-local id (`PrefabOverride::PrefabEntityID`, `PrefabInstanceComponent.h:40-47`); `prefab.apply {instance}` writes every override of an instance root into its own prefab (`Editor/Source/EditorCore/Automation/PrefabMethods.h:74-89`).
8. **The demos.** Breakout's `Main.scene` (`Tests/Projects/Breakout`) holds 50 Brick instances (50 `Prefab` and 50 `PrefabLink` components; 40 of the instances carry overrides, a `MeshRenderer.Materials` and a `Script.Fields` override each), and every brick has its own `Static` body, so no scripted collider is gathered into a compound. Tetris's saved scene holds no Prefab, PrefabLink or physics component, but its `Game` script instantiates `Cell.prefab` at run time (`Projects/Tetris/Assets/Scripts/Game.luau:267` and `:277`, about 212 instances at tick 0), and the state hash serializes those instances (decision 11; this fact first said that Tetris holds none of them, counting only its saved scene). M19's native test game holds none of them (ADR 0028's ledger).

The facts C20.2 relies on, checked in the code at the base:

9. **One open scene.** `EditorContext` holds one scene, one history and one selection (`Editor/Source/EditorCore/EditorContext.h:486-493` at the base: `m_Scene`, `m_History`, `m_Selection`); `SetScene` replaces the scene and clears the history; the revision is the scene's own revision plus a base that `SetScene` advances, so a revision never repeats across scenes opened one after another. ## Decisions

### 1. What the contract freezes, and what it implements

The contract freezes the C++ declarations under "Frozen interfaces", the data formats under "Data formats" and the registry entries of decision 10; this record's decisions state the behaviour each stream implements.

- **C++ stubs:** every new function's first statement is `ENGINE_CONTRACT_STUB();`. A stub returning `Result` or `Status` returns `Unsupported` naming this record and its stream; any other returns an empty or neutral value (ADR 0024 decision 1).
- **Implemented by the contract** (behaviour-neutral, so the tree behaves as at the base): the new data members `ContactEvent::Touching` (default true, what every record is today) and `PhysicsEvent::SelfBody` (set to `Self` for every event the system delivers, all of them body events; its default, the null UUID, also means a body event, so `IsColliderEvent()` is `SelfBody.IsValid() && SelfBody != Self` and the order key takes `Self` for a null `SelfBody`, and an event built without it, by a test or a later milestone's dispatch, stays a body event); `ContactEventKind::Touched` (no listener appends one yet); `GetPhysicsEventOrderKey` and `PhysicsEventOrderKey`, with a live test; `PlaySessionSpecification::SceneAsset` (no host sets it yet); the test fixture's `IScriptHost` members (`Tests/Source/Support/ScriptTestFixture`, which records the requests as it records `RequestSceneLoad`'s); the header comments that state the frozen behaviour.
- **Acceptance tests:** final names, marked `doctest::skip(true)` (C++) or `unittest.skip` with a "contract stub" reason (Python); each body fails when forced against the contract tree (checked with `--no-skip` for the C++ cases), because it calls a stub or asserts behaviour that does not exist yet. Tests that pass against the contract tree are live (decision 3's bounce case, the order key's case).
- **No registry change:** no registered enum value, component field, method param or result field, script binding or validation code changes in the contract, so no generated reference changes (`GenerateDocs.py --check` passes on the contract tree). The registry entries below are frozen by their names and texts here and land with their streams, together with the regenerated references, the format migration and fixtures, and their FeatureTest coverage (Roadmap rule 5).
- **No existing behaviour changes.** The tests of the setup's refusal (decision 7) stay live until stream A1 replaces them.
- **Counts:** 10 stubs (`ScriptProxy::ValidateEntityForRead`; `PlaySession::State::RequestSceneReload` and `GetCurrentScene`, `PlaySession::GetCurrentSceneAsset`, the edit evaluation host's `GetCurrentScene`; `PrefabInstantiator::GetInstanceLevels`, `GetOverridePath` and the level overload of `ApplyOverrides`; `EditorContext::GetNestingPrefabs` and `CreateNestingPrefabUpdateCommand`), 16 skipped C++ cases and 2 skipped Python cases (listed under "Acceptance"), and 2 live cases.

### 2. Collider events and the canonical event order key (W-P01; stream P1)

P01's default (each brick its own body) stays valid; the fix routes callbacks to scripted child colliders.

- **Scripted gathered collider:** an entity whose Script component names a script (Script version 1: a valid `Script` handle; version 2: a slot with a script, decision 17) and whose solid colliders are gathered into another owner's body: a `RigidBody`'s compound or an implicit static body's tree (§5.3 "Gathering"); it is listed in the body's `PhysicsBodyEntry::Colliders` and is not the body's owner. An entity whose Script component names no script creates no instance, so events for it would reach nobody, and it is not one. `PhysicsSystem` reads the component alone, never the script or a VM, so the acceptance cases attach a component naming a script handle and simulate without scripts (`PlayMode::Simulate`). A collider pair takes its body pair's kind: a solid gathered collider whose body meets a partner's sensor gets `OnTriggerEnter` and `OnTriggerExit` of its own, and its other events are collision events (W20.1 integration, stream P1; this sentence first said that collider events are collision events only, which a solid piece of a moving body entering a sensor disproves).
- **Collider pairs:** keyed by (the collider entity, the partner's body owner, kind). A collider pair counts only the contacts of the collider's own sub-shapes and follows every rule of a body pair (§9.4 and `PhysicsSystem.h`: the pending enter of decision 3, dormant contacts, rebuilds and unconfirmed pairs, the drop rules, synthesized exits at the destroy and disable flushes of either side, the repeating flush), with the collider entity as its only receiving side.
- **The callback:** `PhysicsEvent::Self` is the collider entity, `SelfBody` its body's owner, `Other` the partner's body owner; the contact names the collider itself (`Collider`) and the partner's collider of the first sub-shape pair (`OtherCollider`), with that contact's point, its normal from the collider's body towards the partner, and its relative speed. The partner and the body's owner keep their body-pair events unchanged.
- **Leaving and joining:** a collider that stops being a scripted gathered collider (its Script component removed or naming no script any more, a `RigidBody` of its own, moved out of the body) ends its collider pairs at the next PostStep with synthesized exits to it. One that becomes a scripted gathered collider while its body touches a partner enters at the next PostStep (stream P1).
- **An accepted limit** (stream P1; `Docs/Backlog.md` §6): Jolt's manifold reduction, on for every body, merges a body pair's contacts whose normals agree into the manifold of the first sub-shape pair. A body resting flat across two coplanar pieces, or a ball on the seam between two bricks, therefore gives only one piece its collider event; pieces whose contacts face different ways each get theirs. A piece with its own `Static` body still gets one event per piece at a seam.
- **The canonical order key** (`PhysicsEventOrderKey`, `GetPhysicsEventOrderKey`): pair events are sorted by `(Low, High, Type, Collider)`, Low and High the pair's body owners (the lower UUID first) and `Collider` the null UUID for a body event and the receiving collider for a collider event. A body event delivers to Low, then High; a collider event its one callback to `Collider`. A body event's key has the null collider, so body events keep today's relative order, and a scene without scripted gathered colliders dispatches exactly as before: Breakout's events and hashes are unchanged by P01 (fact 8).
- **The phase-1 interface for later milestones** (plan §4.1): M21's 2D world and tilemap collision and M26's height fields dispatch `PhysicsEvent`s sorted by this key.

### 3. The pending enter (W-P03; stream P1)

P03 reproduces (fact 2), so the enter is deferred until real contact (plan §6 "M20").

- **Touching.** A sub-shape contact touches when Jolt reports its manifold with a penetration depth of at least 0, or when Jolt's solver acts on it within the collision step: its relative normal speed (`ContactEvent::RelativeNormalSpeed`, positive when approaching) times the collision step's delta time (the world step's delta over its collision steps, §9.3) is at least its separation (the negated penetration depth). That is Jolt's own test for applying a speculative contact's impulse and restitution, so the bounce case of fact 2 still enters at its step. Sensors and character contacts: Jolt adds sensor contacts only for overlapping shapes, and `CharacterContactListener` reports the contacts a character's update collided with, so both are recorded touching; stream P1 confirms each with a test and records the outcome here. Outcome (stream P1): confirmed for both. "PhysicsWorld: a sensor's contacts are added only for overlapping shapes and recorded touching" (Jolt's narrow phase gives a sensor pair a maximum separation of 0) and "CharacterController: a character records the contacts it collides with, touching, and not a wall it walks past" (CharacterVirtual calls its listener only from HandleContact and for a supporting contact within its collision tolerance, never for a contact merely within its predictive distance); the listener records every sensor contact touching whatever its depth.
- **The world** (`Physics/ContactBuffer.h`, `Physics/Private/PhysicsContactListener`): an Added record carries `Touching`. The listener remembers each contact added without touching and appends one `Touched` record, from `OnContactPersisted`, at the first collision step in which it touches, with that step's point, normal, relative speed and collider indices; a contact removed first never gets one.
- **The system** (`Scene/PhysicsSystem`): a contact added without touching counts for its pair's lifetime but cannot start it. A pair enters at the first PostStep after which it holds a touching contact (an Added record that touches, or a Touched record), its enter's contact being the first of its touching contacts of that step by today's total order. A pair whose contacts all end before one touched reports nothing, neither enter nor exit. An entered pair exits when its last contact, touching or not, ends. Collider pairs (decision 2) follow the same rule.
- **No PlaySessionPhase.** The rule runs inside `PhysicsPostStep`; M20 takes no PlaySessionPhase name. ADR 0028's ledger reserved a position for "M20's pending enter, if P03 reproduces"; the ledger amendment that records "none" is the lead's documentation change on main (requested in the contract's result).
- **Hashes.** The world's state is unchanged (events never feed back into the simulation), so the 200-body pile's committed hash stands; scripts that see fewer or later enters change state, so Breakout's Opening replay is re-recorded in M20's one re-record (plan §3.4 step 4, "P03 if it reproduced").

### 4. Reading an entity pending destruction (W-P02; stream P2)

An entity pending destruction stays readable until the flush; writes to it are refused (plan §6 "M20").

- **Pending destruction:** an entity of the current scene generation marked by `Scene.Destroy` (or below a destroyed ancestor) earlier in the tick, until the flush that destroys it (§5.7 step 8 and the frame-phase flush).
- **Reads accepted:** every member the binding layer registers as not mutating (`ScriptMemberOptions::Mutates` false) and every proxy field read validate the entity with `ScriptProxy::ValidateEntityForRead`, which accepts it: its components' fields (the Transform fast paths included), `Name`, `ID`, its tags, `GetComponent` and the shortcuts (returning proxies whose reads work), and its place in the hierarchy.
- **Writes refused:** every mutating member keeps `ValidateEntity`, which refuses it with the unchanged NotFound `entity <id> was destroyed`.
- **Unchanged:** `IsValid()` is false at once (§5.7); the scene's searches (`Scene.Find*`, `GetEntityCount`) leave it out; the systems' own state is not covered (the physics body and character functions and the audio calls keep refusing it, since its body and voices are leaving); after the flush every access is NotFound as before. Automation never sees one, since it runs at safe points after the flushes.
- **Where:** stream P2 adds the scene-side read access it needs (for example a read-only lookup that accepts a pending entity) and lists any public `Scene` declaration it adds for the contract owner's review.

### 5. `Scene.Reload` and `Scene.GetCurrent` (W-P06; stream P2)

- **`Scene.Reload(parameters: {[string]: any}?) -> ()`** (a mutating member): requests, like `Scene.Load`, the replacement of the current scene at the end of the frame, by the document the current scene started from: the session's start document while the current scene is the one the session started with (in editor play the play copy: the edit scene as it was at Play, unsaved edits included; a suite's or replay's scene; the Runtime's start scene), otherwise the document of the scene asset the last `Scene.Load` loaded, as it was then. Given parameters replace the load parameters; without them the current scene's (`Scene.GetLoadParameters()`) are kept. Everything else is `Scene.Load`'s: a fresh VM, the session's clock, tick, random stream and replay owner preserved (§5.6).
- **`Scene.GetCurrent() -> AssetRef?`** (not mutating): the scene asset the current scene came from: `PlaySessionSpecification::SceneAsset` (the editor's open scene or `play.start`'s scene, a suite's or replay's scene, the Runtime's start scene) until a `Scene.Load`, then the asset it named; `Scene.Reload` keeps it; nil for a scene without an asset (an editor scene never saved).
- **One transition per frame:** the first request of a frame wins; a second `Scene.Load` or `Scene.Reload` in the same frame raises the located InvalidState `The session already has a pending scene transition or stop`, as a second `Scene.Load` does today.
- **Hosts** set `PlaySessionSpecification::SceneAsset`: `EditorPlayController` (the open scene's asset, or `play.start {scene}`'s), the FeatureTest runner (each suite's and replay's scene), the Runtime (its start scene). An edit evaluation's host (`script.eval` on the edit scene) refuses `Scene.Reload` as read-only, like `Scene.Load`, and answers `Scene.GetCurrent` with the open scene's asset.
- **Determinism:** the start document is the document the session was created from, so a replay that reloads replays identically in all four runs.

### 6. The faster Play copy (S-PLAYCOPY; stream P3)

- **One copy path:** the serializer (§1.3 principle 3); the registry-based copy is won't-do (ADR 0028 decision 2). Stream P3 speeds up `CreateFromScene`'s `ToJson` and `FromJson` without changing their output: the runtime scene, its state hash, every load diagnostic and the document are identical, which tests compare on the FeatureTest scenes and on a 1,000-entity scene.
- **The start document stays:** `CreateFromScene`'s document is kept as the session's start document (shared and immutable), which `Scene.Reload` reloads (decision 5). This is why W-P06 and S-PLAYCOPY share a wave (plan §3.4).
- **Measured, never gated:** P3 records `Perf.py`'s `playCopy/1000Entities` before and after its change in this record; the 30 ms budget stays warning-only, and a copy over budget keeps the warning (plan §6). No unit test times anything (AGENTS.md "Deterministic").
- **The measurement (stream P3; Release, on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5070 Ti Laptop GPU, device class `nvidia-61x`):** `Perf.py`'s `playCopy/1000Entities` had a median of 156.66 ms and 22.44 ms in two captures before the change, and 14.67 ms and -18.05 ms in two after it. The metric subtracts two `play.start` round trips of about 0.75 s whose spread from cycle to cycle reaches hundreds of milliseconds, so five cycles cannot resolve a copy of about 10 ms: a capture over the budget is that noise, not the copy, and keeps its warning. The copy itself, timed in process on the same 1,000 entities (`Transform` and `MeshRenderer`, as `Perf.py` generates them; the fastest of 40 runs, in a scratch harness that is not committed): `ToJson` went from 4.3 ms to 2.7 ms, the strict `FromJson` into a runtime scene from 7.4 ms to 4.3 ms, and `CreateFromScene` less an empty scene's from 12.9 ms to 8.6 ms, well under the budget. The largest part left is `StructuralValidator::Validate`'s copy of every entity object (about 1.7 ms), in stream A2's file.

### 7. Deleting prefab members: RemoveEntity (E-PFB-DEL; streams A1 and B)

RemoveEntity turns the setup's refusal into a recorded deletion (ADR 0028 decision 16).

- **The override:** `PrefabOverrideKind::RemoveEntity`, appended after `EntityKey`: `{Path: <the removed entity's path>, Kind: RemoveEntity, Component: "", Field: "", Value: null}`.
- **Recorded by the diff,** as every override is (§5.5): `ComputeOverrides` and `RefreshOverrides` record one RemoveEntity for each prefab entity absent from the instance whose prefab parent is present (the top of each removed subtree; the instance root itself cannot be removed this way: deleting it deletes the instance). The removed entity's prefab descendants are removed with it and get no override of their own; overrides that targeted them disappear from the diff.
- **Applied:** `Instantiate`'s overrides, `UpdateInstance` and the nested rebuilds build neither the removed entity nor its prefab descendants; `prefab.revert` clears a RemoveEntity like any override, and the entity comes back with its derived id. A RemoveEntity whose entity the prefab no longer has is stale and dropped with `PREFAB_STALE_OVERRIDE`. `ApplyOverrides` removes the entity and its descendants from the prefab's document; references to them elsewhere in the prefab stay as they are and name no entity, as any reference to a deleted entity does.
- **The edit scene:** `entity.destroy` (alone, in a dry run and as an `edit.batch` op), the editor's Delete and `prefab.create {replaceWithInstance}` of a member no longer refuse: the member is destroyed, and the commit's override refresh records the RemoveEntity on its outermost instance root, in the same undo step. With `replaceWithInstance`, the new instance's root keeps the member's id and is a user child of the outer instance; a later revert of that RemoveEntity makes the outer instance's update fail with today's InvalidState for a derived id used outside the instance, which names both. `Utils::FindKeptPrefabMember` and the hierarchy panel's refusal go.
- **Unchanged:** user children, instance roots and the transient play scene, where scripts' `Scene.Destroy` destroys members as today and records nothing.
- **The refusal's tests are rewritten, not deleted:** "EntityMethods: entity.destroy refuses a prefab member that is not its instance root in the edit scene", "SceneHierarchyPanel: Delete of a prefab member is refused with its hint and keeps the member", "PrefabMethods: prefab.create replaceWithInstance refuses a member of another instance" and the Python `test_prefab.PrefabTests.test_destroying_a_prefab_member_is_refused_with_a_hint` and `test_replacing_a_prefab_member_with_an_instance_is_refused` pin the behaviour ADR 0028 decision 16 scheduled for replacement. Stream A1 rewrites each into its recorded-deletion counterpart, which the contract's skipped cases name: "EntityMethods: entity.destroy of a prefab member records a RemoveEntity override in one undo step", "SceneHierarchyPanel: Delete of a prefab member records its deletion in one undo step", "PrefabMethods: prefab.create replaceWithInstance of a member records its deletion and keeps its id" and the Python `test_destroying_a_prefab_member_records_its_deletion`, which covers both Python refusal tests. A1 keeps their other checks (user children, the instance root with its members, the play scene, dry runs, batches, one undo step); the protection they gave, that a member deleted from an instance never comes back silently, is kept by "PrefabInstantiator: a deleted prefab member stays deleted after an update".

### 8. Live nested prefabs (E-PFB-NEST; stream A2)

- **Documents.** A prefab document holds instances of other prefabs exactly as a scene holds them: expanded, with their `Prefab` and `PrefabLink` components and their overrides, each instance outermost within that document. The prefab's root is never an instance root or a member itself (`PREFAB_NESTED_INSTANCE`, now that defect only: a `Prefab` or `PrefabLink` component on the prefab's root entity; repair drops it). Instantiation never loads another prefab: the outer document is self-contained, so the Runtime instantiates it as today.
- **Ids.** An instance nested in a document is an instance of its prefab at its root's id. A nested root's id derives at the level that encloses it, `Hash64(enclosing instance root, its id in the enclosing prefab)`, and its members' ids derive from it, `Hash64(nested root, their id in its prefab)`, recursively. So the nested instance in a scene is exactly an instance of its prefab, which unpacking or re-nesting keeps addressable; existing instances, whose prefabs nest nothing, keep every id.
- **Links.** Every `PrefabLink` keeps naming the entity's own innermost instance (`InstanceRoot`; a root, nested or not, names itself) and its id in that instance's prefab (`PrefabEntityID`). The additive key `OuterPrefabEntityID` (default null, written on every link) records, on a nested instance root only, its id in the prefab of the instance that encloses it; it is null on members, on outermost instance roots and on user-child instances (an instance a user placed under a member, which belongs to no prefab). The enclosing instance root is the nearest ancestor instance root `R` for which `Hash64(R, OuterPrefabEntityID)` is the nested root's id. Without the key, a nested instance and a user-child instance under a member look the same, and the nested root's place in the outer prefab could not be read from the scene alone.
- **Overrides (PrefabInstance version 2).** Only an instance outermost in its document records overrides, each targeting an entity by its path (`PrefabOverride::Path`, decision 10's format): for an entity of the outermost instance itself, `[its id in the outermost prefab]`; for an entity of a nested instance, the `OuterPrefabEntityID` of each nested root from the outermost level inwards, then the entity's own `PrefabEntityID` unless it is that nested root. `fold(Hash64, outermost root, path)` is the entity's id for every entity but the outermost root. A nested root's `Overrides` stay empty: one with overrides is `SCENE_INCONSISTENT_PREFAB_LINK` (repair drops them). Sorted by (Path, compared element by element, a prefix first; Component; Kind; Field).
- **Updates.** An instance is rebuilt only from its outermost root, from the outer prefab's document plus the outermost root's overrides; its nested instances follow it. `UpdateInstance`, `Revert`, `ComputeOverrides`, `RefreshOverrides` and `ApplyOverrides` called on a nested root refuse with InvalidArgument naming its outermost root.
- **Inner edits propagate.** When a prefab gets a new version (`prefab.apply`, `asset.setImportSettings`, `asset.reimport`), the editor rewrites every prefab asset that nests it, directly or through others, in nesting order (each after every prefab it nests; ties in asset path order), rebuilding each document's nested instances from the current versions and keeping the overrides that document records for them (`EditorContext::GetNestingPrefabs`, `CreateNestingPrefabUpdateCommand`), then rebuilds every outermost instance in the open scene whose prefab changed or was rewritten (`CreatePrefabUpdateCommand`), as one undo step. External changes keep raising `SceneChangedOnDisk`; `scene.open` rebuilds a scene's instances from the current prefab versions as today.
- **Cycles are refused.** No editor action adds a nested instance to an existing prefab (`prefab.apply` adds no user children, and `prefab.create` makes a new asset), so a nesting cycle comes only from files. The nesting graph is read from the registry's import dependencies: the prefab importer lists every prefab a document nests in `Import.Dependencies` (M19's rule that every importer fills them, ADR 0028's ledger). A prefab update whose propagation meets a cycle is refused with Validation `PREFAB_CYCLE`, one issue per prefab on it, naming their paths, and changes nothing; `project.validate` reports `PREFAB_CYCLE` for each prefab on a cycle.
- **`prefab.create` keeps nested instances live.** Every instance wholly inside the subtree (its root strictly below `entity`) stays live in the new document, outermost there (`OuterPrefabEntityID` cleared, its overrides recomputed against its prefab, so the overrides its enclosing instance recorded for it move onto its own root). The instance of `entity` itself, and every instance whose root is outside the subtree, are unpacked in the copy, as `prefab.unpack` leaves them. With `replaceWithInstance`, the entity is replaced by an instance of the new prefab keeping its root id, as today; its nested instances get ids derived from it.
- **`prefab.unpack` unpacks one level.** On an outermost instance root: its own links go, and each instance nested directly in it becomes outermost (`OuterPrefabEntityID` cleared, its overrides recomputed against its prefab, so it looks exactly as before). On a nested root: InvalidState with the hint to unpack its outermost instance first.
- **`prefab.revert` on a nested root** removes the outermost root's overrides whose paths lie within the nested instance; `prefab.revert {overrides}` keys match `Path` (decision 10).
- **The flat-instance test is rewritten:** "PrefabInstantiator: an instance of a prefab created from nested instances is one flat instance" pins the flattening this decision replaces; stream A2 rewrites it into "Prefab: CreateFromEntity keeps a nested instance live" (skipped in the contract), keeping its id and override checks.

### 9. The `prefab.apply` level (E-PFB-NEST; streams A2 and B)

The level is the user's pick, defaulting to the outermost (plan §6).

- **`prefab.apply {instance, level?}`:** `instance` is an instance root, outermost or nested. The overrides applied are those of `instance`'s outermost instance that target `instance` or entities below it in the instance (all of them for an outermost root). `level`, an entity reference, names the instance whose prefab receives them: one of `PrefabInstantiator::GetInstanceLevels(instance)`, from the outermost root down to `instance` itself; absent, the outermost.
- **The write:** `PrefabInstantiator::ApplyOverrides(instance, level, levelPrefab, options)` re-bases each override's path onto `level`'s prefab; an override of an instance nested in that prefab becomes that nested instance's own override there; a RemoveEntity removes the entity from that document. Then the nesting prefabs are rewritten and the instances rebuilt as decision 8 says, all in one undo step, as `prefab.apply` records today. Overrides the other levels record stay where they are: applying to an inner prefab an override that an enclosing prefab overrides too leaves the enclosing value in force, and the scene's override stays recorded.
- **The level root's own overrides.** An override whose path ends at `level` itself (it targets the level root) is re-based to `[the id of levelPrefab's root]`, except the level root's Name, Transform and Parent. Those are its placement in the prefab that encloses it, implicit at its own level as every instance root's are (§5.5), so they are never written into the level's prefab and stay recorded on the outermost root; the level root's Active, Tags and component overrides are written there like any member's. So moving or renaming a Lamp nested in a Room and applying at the Lamp level never moves or renames every Lamp. "PrefabMethods: an applied edit of a nested prefab reaches the prefab nesting it and its instances" checks it.
- **The result:** `prefab` (the level's prefab asset), `updatedInstances` (the outermost instances rebuilt in the open scene, this one's included), `updatedPrefabs` (new: the nesting prefab assets rewritten, in their order) and `undoIndex`.
- **Errors:** InvalidArgument when `instance` is not an instance root, or `level` is not one of its levels (the hint lists them by path); NotFound for a missing prefab asset (`PREFAB_MISSING_ASSET`); Validation `PREFAB_CYCLE` (decision 8). Not dry-runnable, as now.
- **The editor:** the inspector's Apply offers the levels, outermost first and selected by default; its `InspectorPanel` change goes through W20.2's stream M-C, which owns that file for the milestone (plan §3.4).

### 13. Open scenes in the editor (E-SCENES; stream S-A)

The editor keeps several scenes open, one of them active; tabs are editor-only, and additive runtime scenes are not built (plan §6 "M20"): the Runtime, `PlaySession` and `Scene.Load` keep one scene per session.

- **The open scenes.** Up to `MaxOpenScenes` (16) open scenes in tab order, one of them active: the tab the editor shows, the scene play mode plays and the scene the UI edits. A seventeenth is refused with InvalidState. The bound keeps autosave's fatal path on fixed slots (decision 15). Each open scene has an `OpenSceneId`, assigned when it opens (`SetScene`, `AddScene`, a recovery) and never reused for the editor's lifetime, so an id taken before a scene closed or was replaced names nothing afterwards. The id is 32 bits wide everywhere (`EditorCore/OpenSceneId.h`, `SceneTargetReference::Id`, and the uint32 `id` of every result), so no result narrows it; its counter never wraps, an editor that has opened 2^32 - 1 scenes failing the next open as a verified programmer error (`ENGINE_VERIFY`), as the dispatcher's request sequence does.
- **One document per open scene.** Each open scene has its own scene, path, dirty flag, SceneChangedOnDisk flag, undo history, revision, document epoch and selection, which survive switching tabs.
- **The addressed scene.** The members of `EditorContext` that name "the open scene" address one scene: `GetScene`, `HasScene`, `GetScenePath`, `IsSceneDirty`, `MarkSceneSaved`, `IsSceneChangedOnDisk`, `GetRevision`, `GetSceneDocumentEpoch`, `GetHistory`, `Execute`'s history, `CloseScene()`, the selection and the prefab update command. That scene is the active scene, unless an `EditorSceneTargetScope` is open, and then the scope's scene. `GetScene()` therefore stays the active-scene accessor for every caller outside a targeted request (plan §3.4), and a targeted request's handlers and commands address the target without passing it on (decision 16). A scope does not nest. Inside one, only the scope's own scene can be activated or closed, because `scene.activate`'s and `scene.close`'s target is their subject; a closed scope scene leaves the scope addressing no scene until it ends.
- **Revisions never repeat across the open scenes.** One editor-wide counter, which every mutation of any open scene advances as a mutation of the one open scene advances it before M20, gives each open scene its revision at its last change. A revision names one state of one scene, so an `ifRevision` taken in one scene never matches another (ADR 0008 decision 28 kept), and with one open scene the values are exactly those before M20.
- **Commands that change no scene** (`project.setSettings`, the asset commands) are recorded in the addressed scene's history, as they are recorded in the one history before M20. With no open scene they go to the editor's scene-less history, which the next scene opened replaces, as opening a scene clears the history today.
- **The member functions** (frozen below): `GetOpenScenes`, `GetActiveSceneId`, `GetAddressedSceneId`, `FindOpenScene`, `FindScene`, `DescribeOpenScene`, `IsAnySceneDirty`, `AddScene` (keepOpen: append at the end of the tab order and activate), `ActivateScene`, `CloseScene(id)` (the next tab becomes active, the previous one when the closed tab was the last). `SetScene` replaces the addressed scene in its tab position with a new id and activates it; it opens the first scene when none is open. `ActivateScene` and `CloseScene` are refused inside a transaction, a dry run or a target scope of another scene, and while a test run owns the editor.
- **Events.** `SceneOpened` is appended for every scene that opens, keepOpen included. Two values are appended to the registry enum `EngineEventType`, `SceneActivated` and `SceneClosed`, each with the scene's path (decision 22).
- **Play.** Play mode plays the active scene. Activating another tab, or closing any tab, the played scene's included, while playing is allowed and leaves the session running, as `scene.open`'s replacement of the played scene during play does today. A played scene closed or replaced leaves the session belonging to no open scene (decision 16's play rules). While the played scene's tab is active, the editor shows the play scene as today. Another tab shows its edit scene, and the Game view keeps showing the session. The play selection belongs to the played scene, and Stop carries it back into that scene's edit selection, or drops it when that scene is no longer open.
- **Dry runs** copy the addressed scene only and leave the other open scenes alone (§13.4's sandbox, decision 15's propagation excluded).

### 14. The scene methods (E-SCENES; stream S-D)

- **`scene.list {}`**: the open scenes in tab order, each as `OpenSceneEntry {id, scene: SceneSummary, active, changedOnDisk}`. `id` is the open-scene id as a uint32.
- **`scene.activate {scene}`**: the scene target is required (`MethodSpecification::RequiresSceneTarget`, set on this method alone), and absent it is InvalidParams naming `scene`, which its schemas list as required. The scene it names becomes active, and the result reports it.
- **`scene.close {scene?, save?, discardChanges?}`**: closes the addressed scene. A dirty scene needs exactly one of `save` and `discardChanges`, as `scene.open` does, and both is InvalidArgument; `save` of a scene never saved is InvalidState (`scene.save {path}` first). The result is `{closed: SceneSummary, file, activeId}`: the closed scene as it was, the file `save` wrote, and the now active scene's id (0 when none is open).
- **`scene.new` and `scene.open {keepOpen}`.** With `keepOpen: true` the new or loaded scene opens as an additional scene at the end of the tab order and becomes active. Every open scene stays open, so `save` and `discardChanges` are not needed; giving either is InvalidArgument. Without `keepOpen` the scene replaces the active scene in its tab position, the other open scenes untouched, so a transcript without it behaves as before. A path that is already open, active or not, needs `reload`, as the open scene's own path needs it today (InvalidState naming it, with the hint to use `scene.activate`). `reload` re-reads that open scene in its tab position, needs `save` or `discardChanges` when it is dirty, and makes it active. Both results gain `id`.
- **`scene.save {scene?, path?}`** saves the addressed scene. Saving to the path of another open scene is InvalidState naming it.
- **Flags.** The three new methods are tools (`scene_list`, `scene_activate`, `scene_close`). None of them `Mutates`: they change what the editor holds open, and `close`'s `save` writes through `EditorContext`, as `scene.open`'s does. None is `AllowedInBatch`, because `activate` and `close` change the scene a batch addresses, and none runs in the Runtime.
- **The deny-mutations discard rule.** Discarding unsaved changes writes no file and runs no command, so no write boundary sees it, and the server refuses it under the deny-mutations policy (`EditorAutomationPolicy`, `AutomationServer`'s `DiscardsUnsavedScene`) with PermissionDenied, as it refuses `scene.open {discardChanges}` and `session.shutdown {force}` today. With several open scenes the rule reads the scene each request would discard, not the active scene alone:
  - `scene.close {discardChanges}` when the scene it closes, its target or the active scene, is dirty;
  - `scene.open {discardChanges}` when the scene it replaces (the active one) is dirty, and `scene.open {reload, discardChanges}` when the open scene it reloads, active or not, is dirty;
  - `session.shutdown {force}` without `save` when any open scene is dirty (`IsAnySceneDirty`).

  The check runs in `AdmitRequest` after the target's scope opens (decision 16), so it reads the targeted scene. "SceneTarget: deny-mutations refuses discarding the unsaved changes of any open scene" checks the three requests on a dirty tab that is not active.
- **The methods that act on every open scene** (decision 16's inventory):
  - `project.save` writes every dirty open scene with a path, in tab order (`savedFiles` lists them), after checking that none is dirty and never saved (InvalidState naming it, nothing written).
  - `session.shutdown {save}` does the same, and without `save` or `force` it refuses naming every dirty open scene.
  - `project.upgrade` refuses while any open scene is dirty, and reloads every open scene whose file it rewrote.
  - `project.export` warns, as today, about an open scene with unsaved changes, now about every open scene with a path (it packages the saved files), and `test.run` refuses, as today, an unsaved open scene that a selected suite plays, now among every open scene.
  - `project.refreshAssets` raises SceneChangedOnDisk for each open scene whose file or prefabs changed.
  - `project.open {recover}` opens every recovered scene (decision 15).
- **The methods that report or show the active scene**: `project.info`, `session.info` and `editor.state` report it as their open scene, with unchanged result shapes; `scene.list` lists them all. The same holds for `editor.screenshot`, `viewport.frame` and `viewport.pick`, whose views show the active scene.

### 15. Prefab updates, autosave and recovery across open scenes (E-SCENES; streams S-B and S-A)

- **A prefab update reaches every open scene** (stream S-B, after W20.1's integration, plan §3.4). Any command, its undo or its redo, may change a Prefab asset: `prefab.apply`, `asset.setImportSettings`, `asset.reimport`, the nesting rewrites of decision 8, the inspector's asset edits. When it does, the addressed scene's instances are rebuilt in the command, as today. Every other open scene that instantiates a changed prefab, directly or through nesting, gets its outermost instances rebuilt as one "Update Prefab Instances" entry of its own history, in tab order. So a prefab update rebuilds every open scene while undo stays isolated per scene: undoing that entry restores that scene's instances, which its next commit records as overrides. Undoing the asset command restores the file, and the other open scenes follow it again through new entries. Results report the addressed scene as today (`updatedInstances`). Dry runs rebuild only the addressed scene's copy.
- **External changes.** SceneChangedOnDisk is raised and cleared per open scene, and `scene.open {reload}` adopts it per scene.
- **Autosave is isolated per open scene** (stream S-A; `Autosave.h`'s M20 paragraph). `Autosave` and the editor host's autosave, recovery and close files are M18's in phase 1 (ADR 0028's ledger, whose row has M20 adapt them after merging main): S-A changes them on M20's branch before the merge, which the ledger amendment of decision 22 records, and M20 reconciles them with M18's device-loss changes at its merge (decision 23).
  - `Publish` keeps one immutable snapshot per dirty open scene, at most `MaxOpenScenes`, each keyed by its scene document (its `OpenSceneId`, which a replacement or a recovery renews, plus its path), and publishes them together, in tab order, in one of the fixed publication slots, so the fatal path's slots stay fixed and bounded. (W20.2 amended this bullet, which C20.2 froze as one snapshot slot per open scene: `Autosave.h`'s M20 paragraph states the design as built.)
  - One generation holds one scene, as today. `Save`, and `WriteFatalSnapshot` from the claimed publication, write one generation per published dirty open scene in tab order and publish the manifest once, last.
  - Retention keeps `RetainedGenerations` per scene. An explicit save discards only the saved scene's generations, and the generation and manifest formats are unchanged.
- **Recovery.**
  - `FindRecoveries` offers the newest recoverable generation of every scene, in manifest order, and `FindRecovery` keeps offering the newest overall. A scene whose newest generation is stale (Conflict: the project or the scene's file changed after it was captured, as for a tab closed with its changes discarded) has no offer and is named in a warning, and the other scenes are still offered; the conflict is the answer only when no scene has an offer, as it is `FindRecovery`'s.
  - `Recover` installs a scene as the open scene of its source, replacing it when it is open and otherwise adding it. `OpenRecoveredProject(project, recoveries)` preflights every offer, then opens them all, dirty with empty histories, the first active.
  - `project.open {recover: true}` and the editor's recovery prompt (`EditorHostRecovery`) recover every offered scene. The close prompt (`EditorHostClose`) lists every dirty open scene.
- **Device loss** (Roadmap M20 acceptance "an injected device loss with two open scenes autosaves both and recovers both views"): the fatal path writes every dirty open scene, which W20.2 tests ("Autosave: a fatal snapshot writes every dirty open scene"). The recovery of both views is M18's in-process recovery, which M20 adapts and tests at its merge (decision 23).

### 16. The scene target and its inventory (E-SCENES; streams S-D and S-B)

- **The member.** `"scene"` is a reserved request member, like `dryRun`: a scene path (a string, project-relative or `project://`, as `scene.list` reports it) or an open scene's id (an integer from 1 to 2^32 - 1, the only way to name a scene never saved). It is accepted only by methods with `MethodSpecification::SupportsSceneTarget`, whose `PrepareParams` moves it into `RequestOptions::Scene`; anything else, an id out of that range included, is InvalidParams at `/scene`. Absent, the request addresses the active scene, so transcripts without targets behave exactly as before (Roadmap M20 acceptance). `MethodSpecification::RequiresSceneTarget` (only `scene.activate`, decision 14) makes it required: `PrepareParams` answers its absence with InvalidParams naming `scene`, as for a missing `RequiredParams` member, and the schemas list it in `required`. A params struct of a flagged method never declares `scene` (asserted at registration). The two methods that declare a `scene` member of their own, a scene file (`play.start`, `input.record`; fact 10), are not flagged and keep it. Every other method without the flag refuses `scene` as an unknown member. The schemas, `rpc.discover` and the MCP catalogue list the member for flagged methods, as they list `dryRun`.
- **Resolution.** `EditorMethodContext::ResolveSceneTarget` resolves the member against the open scenes: a path through `ResolveProjectPath` (".scene") and `FindOpenScene`, an id through `FindScene`. A malformed path is InvalidArgument and a path or id that names no open scene is NotFound, both located at `/scene`, the NotFound hint listing the open scenes. Without a target and with no open scene, the result is InvalidState "no scene open".
- **Where it applies.** The scope covers the request's whole stretch on the main thread, in the editor's `IMethodHost` hooks (`AutomationServer`; `Dispatcher.h`'s lifecycle and the `IMethodHost` interface are unchanged):
  - `AdmitRequest` of a flagged method whose request names a scene resolves the target (`ResolveSceneTarget`; its error is the call's response) and opens an `EditorSceneTargetScope` on it first, after the test-run admission and before the read-only and deny-mutations checks (decision 14's discard rule reads the target), the `ifRevision` check (compared with the addressed scene's revision) and the dry-run sandbox (a copy of the addressed scene, decision 13). The scope stays open through `EnterInvocation`, the handler and `LeaveInvocation`.
  - `FinishRequest` closes the dry-run sandbox, records the response's `"_meta"` state (`MetaState`: the addressed scene's revision, dirty flag, undo label and `sceneChangedOnDisk`, with the play state and tick) while the scope is still open, then closes the scope. `GetMetaState` answers with that record: every `FinishRequest` replaces it, and the next request's start (the dispatcher's `RequestObserver` Started event, which precedes its admission and lookup) clears it, so a response refused before admission reports the live state. ### 17. Script slots (S-MULTI; streams M-B and M-A)

Script migrates to an ordered list, so there is one way to attach scripts (plan §6 "M20").

- **The component (Script version 2).** `ScriptComponent { std::vector<ScriptSlot> Scripts; }` with `ScriptSlot { TypedAssetHandle<AssetType::Script> Script; std::map<std::string, VariantValue> Fields; int32_t ExecutionOrder = 0; }` (the registry struct `ScriptSlot`). A slot is identified by its index in the list. Each slot holds its own script, its own field overrides, resolved against its own script's schema, and its own `ExecutionOrder`. The same script may fill two slots. A slot without a script creates no instance, as an empty `Script` does today, and an empty list is valid.
- **The migration from version 1** wraps the one script as the only slot: `{"Script": h, "Fields": f, "ExecutionOrder": n}` becomes `{"Scripts": [{"Script": h, "Fields": f, "ExecutionOrder": n}]}`, with absent members staying absent inside the slot. In the same upgrade, whatever the Prefab component's version, every PrefabOverride whose `Component` is `Script` follows it. A Field override of `Script`, `Fields` or `ExecutionOrder` names slot 0 (`Scripts/0/<member>`, decision 19), and an AddComponent override's value becomes a one-slot list. The golden fixture is `Tests/Data/Formats/Scene/ScriptV1.scene` with `ScriptV1.upgraded.scene`.
- **No compatibility shim.** Params that give the Script component its version 1 shape (`{"Script": {"Script": ...}}`) are refused as any unknown field is, with a hint naming `Scripts`. Every scaffold, skill, test, fixture and template that attaches a script is rewritten (streams M-B and M-D). The demos change through M20's one recorded upgrade and re-record (decision 11).
- **Instances.** Every slot with a script is its own instance, keyed (entity UUID, slot), with its own table (`{Entity = <Entity>, <resolved fields>}`, unchanged), tasks, held physics events and fault state (`ScriptInstanceInfo::Slot`, `ScriptTaskOwner::Slot`).
  - **Order.** Callbacks run in (ExecutionOrder, canonical entity order, slot) order wherever §5.7 and §11.3 say (ExecutionOrder, canonical entity order). It is identical in all four runs, because the slot is data (Roadmap M20 acceptance).
  - **Entity callbacks.** The entity-addressed callbacks (`OnEnable`, `OnDisable`, the physics callbacks, decision 2's collider events included) reach every started slot of the entity in slot order. Each slot holds or discards its own events, and `OnDestroy` runs for one entity's slots in reverse slot order, children first as before.
  - **Faults.** A fault disables only its own instance and cancels only its tasks. The entity's other slots keep running (Roadmap M20 acceptance "a fault stays in its instance").
  - **List writes.** A write of the Script component's list, from automation, the inspector or a script's component proxy, keeps an instance whose slot index still holds the same script asset. It destroys and creates the others, as removing and adding the component would.
  - **Hot reload** patches every instance of a reloaded behaviour, whatever its slot.
- **Errors** carry the faulting instance's slot (`ScriptError::Slot`). `ScriptErrorToJson` writes `"slot"` after `"entity"` for an error with an entity, and `script.errors` entries gain `slot`. The deduplication key is unchanged.
- **Physics.** A scripted gathered collider (decision 2) is an entity whose Script component names a script: at version 2, a slot with a script, decision 2's definition carried over (an empty list, or slots without scripts, name none).
- **Elsewhere.** `scene.tree`'s text names every slot's script (`Script(Scripts/Aim.luau, Scripts/Fire.luau)`). The validation codes are unchanged, their pointers naming the slot (`/Components/Script/Scripts/1/Fields/Torque`). `entity.update`'s Fields-only patch of a slot borrows that slot's Script handle (`CompleteParameterOwners`, slot by index).

### 18. The script bindings, bubbling and `script.eval` (S-MULTI; stream M-A)

- **`Entity:GetScript(name: string?) -> any`** returns the instance of the first slot, in slot order, whose behaviour's `Script.Define` name is `name`; without `name`, the first slot's instance, which is today's behaviour for one script. It returns nil when there is no such slot or that slot has no live instance.
- **`Entity:GetScripts() -> {any}`** returns the live instances of the entity's slots, in slot order. Both are reads, not mutations.
- **Bubbling is defined per behaviour.** M20 adds no bubbling callback; M22's UI events are the first (plan §6 "M22"), and this rule is the interface they build on. A bubbling event visits its target entity, then each ancestor. At each entity, every behaviour that defines the callback receives it in slot order. Propagation to the next ancestor stops after an entity where any behaviour's callback returned true, and the other behaviours of that entity still receive it. Non-bubbling callbacks ignore return values, so no slot can keep another from receiving one.
- **`script.eval {entity, script?}`** binds `self` to the instance of the slot whose behaviour is named `script` (the first such slot), or of the entity's first slot without `script`. A `script` that names no slot of the entity is NotFound at `/script`. `ScriptEngine::Evaluate` and `AutomationMethodContext::EvalInEdit` gain the behaviour name, an empty name meaning the first slot; stream M-A changes their signatures with the registry entry. Replay predicates (`ExecuteBytecode`) keep addressing the first slot.

### 19. Per-slot prefab overrides (S-MULTI; streams M-B and M-C)

- **The target.** A Field override of the Script component names one slot's member: `Field` is `Scripts/<slot>/<member>`, where `<slot>` is the slot's index in the prefab entity's list, a decimal number without leading zeros, and `<member>` is `Script`, `Fields` or `ExecutionOrder` (`PrefabInstantiator::ParseScriptSlotField`, `MakeScriptSlotField`). Its `Path` names the entity as every override's does (decision 10).
- **The diff.** When the member's list has as many slots as the prefab entity's, the diff records one override per differing slot member. A slot's `Fields` is recorded as today's key patch (RFC 7386, the changed keys only), so the prefab's edits to the slot's other keys and to the other slots still reach the instance. When the lengths differ, because a slot was added to or removed from the instance, the whole list is one Field override `Scripts`, as for any list field, and no slot override is recorded beside it.
- **Applying.** A slot override applies to the prefab entity's slot at that index. One whose slot the prefab entity no longer has is dropped with `PREFAB_STALE_OVERRIDE`. A `Scripts` override replaces the list. `ApplyOverrides` writes slot overrides back into the prefab's slot, and `prefab.revert {overrides}` keys match `Field` as written.
- **Value schemas** (the override resolver of `CoreRegistration.cpp`): `Scripts/<n>/Script` resolves to the slot's `Script` field (an AssetRef to a Behaviour), `Scripts/<n>/Fields` to the component-resolved free-form value that `Fields` has today, `Scripts/<n>/ExecutionOrder` to an int32, and `Scripts` to the list's schema.
- **A limit, accepted and recorded in the Backlog by the integration.** A slot override follows its index. A prefab edit that reorders or inserts slots before an overridden slot moves the override to whichever slot now has that index; appending slots, the inspector's default, keeps every index. The inspector's override list (stream M-C) names each slot override by its slot and script.

### 20. Tabs and the editor shell (E-SCENES; stream S-C)

- **The tab bar.** A tab bar above the scene viewport lists the open scenes in tab order, showing each scene's name with a dirty marker and the active tab highlighted. Clicking a tab activates it. Each tab's close button closes it, with the save, discard or cancel prompt for a dirty scene. The New and Open scene commands gain "open in a new tab" (`keepOpen`). Every action is submitted as its automation method (`scene.activate`, `scene.close`, `scene.new` and `scene.open {keepOpen}`) through `EditorActions`, so the tabs need no parity exemption (Roadmap rule 5).
- **The panels.** The hierarchy, inspector, scene viewport, undo history and console show the active scene, as they show the open scene today, and need no new interface: `GetScene()` is the active scene outside a request. Closing the editor with dirty scenes lists every one in its prompt (decision 15).
- **Goldens.** The editor-UI goldens change with the tab bar. M20 regenerates them on phase 1's merged tree, which ADR 0028 decision 9 already gives it, and names the regeneration in this record.

## Data formats

- **Scene and prefab documents, `Prefab` component version 2** (decision 10):

  ```json
  "Prefab": {
  	"Prefab": "11d04163305d2e9f",
  	"Overrides": [
  		{ "Path": ["55425a047af78945"], "Kind": "Field", "Component": "MeshRenderer", "Field": "Materials", "Value": ["18c27cc27d24eda2"] },
  		{ "Path": ["4c1f0a9e2b7d6e31", "00000000000b0002"], "Kind": "RemoveEntity", "Component": "", "Field": "", "Value": null }
  	]
  }
  ```

- **`PrefabLink` (version 1, one additive key):** `{ "PrefabEntityID": "...", "InstanceRoot": "...", "OuterPrefabEntityID": null }`; on a nested instance root `OuterPrefabEntityID` holds its id in the enclosing prefab.
- **Prefab documents** may hold `Prefab` and `PrefabLink` components on any entity but the root (decision 8).
- **`Script` component version 2** (decision 17), with a per-slot override in an instance's `Prefab` component (decision 19):

  ```json
  "Script": {
  	"Scripts": [
  		{ "Script": "5a1d0c77e3b14f20", "Fields": { "Torque": 10 }, "ExecutionOrder": 0 },
  		{ "Script": "7c2e9b0d41a6f835", "Fields": { "Count": 3 }, "ExecutionOrder": 5 }
  	]
  }
  ```

  ```json
  { "Path": ["55425a047af78945"], "Kind": "Field", "Component": "Script", "Field": "Scripts/1/Fields", "Value": { "Count": 7 } }
  ```

- **The scene target** (decision 16), a reserved member of a request's params: `{"entity": "/Board", "scene": "Assets/Scenes/Level2.scene"}` or `{"entity": "/Board", "scene": 3}`.

## Risks

1. **Pending enter at the margin.** The touching test mirrors Jolt's solver from the values the listener sees; a contact exactly at the threshold could be classified differently from Jolt's own decision. Both computations are deterministic, so the four runs agree; the bounce case and the glide case pin the two sides, and P1 adds a case at Jolt's restitution threshold.
2. **A collider event changes callbacks of existing content.** Only scenes with scripted gathered colliders gain events; FeatureTest's Physics scaffold is regenerated, and Breakout has none (fact 8).
3. **Reads of pending entities.** A binding registered as not mutating that in fact mutates would now act on a pending entity; P2 audits every non-mutating member against the binding layer's mutation check (`PrepareHostMutation`).
4. **Nested prefab propagation rewrites assets.** A propagation writes several prefab files in one undo step; undo restores each, and the provenance recorder attributes each write to the method (§13.12).
5. **PrefabInstance version 2 touches every scene with an instance:** Breakout, FeatureTest and the fixtures, through the migration, its golden fixture and the one recorded upgrade, and the state hash of every run that instantiates a prefab at run time (Tetris's replays, decision 11), through the one re-record.
6. **Stream A2 is the milestone's largest** (2 sessions after A1); B builds against the frozen `PrefabInstantiator` and `EditorContext` functions and the registry entries, so it does not wait for A2's internals.
7. **The addressed scene is implicit.** The target scope changes what `GetScene()` returns for a request's duration, so code that caches a scene reference across requests, or holds one while the active scene changes, would address the wrong scene. The scope is opened only by the server, from a request's admission to its `FinishRequest`, never across a frame (a flagged method is never pending), `ActivateScene` of another scene is refused inside it, and the inventory and the targeted cases cover every flagged method.
8. **Per-scene histories and shared assets.** An asset command recorded in one scene's history changes files every scene sees; decision 15's propagation keeps the open scenes' instances in step on execute, undo and redo, but undoing one scene's "Update Prefab Instances" entry leaves that scene's instances as overrides by design.
9. **Script version 2 touches every file with a script** (Breakout's 52 components and 40 Script overrides, Tetris's one component, FeatureTest, the fixtures, the templates and every test that builds a `ScriptComponent`), and every replay of those scenes changes hash: the migration's golden fixture, M-B's mechanical slot-0 first checkpoint and the one recorded upgrade and re-record bound it (decision 11).
10. **Index-keyed slot overrides** follow a slot's index (decision 19's accepted limit); the inspector appends slots, and the per-slot case pins the stale rule.
11. **Autosave's fatal path holds more slots.** Up to 16 published snapshots instead of one; the slots stay fixed and bounded, and the fatal write publishes the manifest once, last, so an interrupted write leaves the previous manifest whole.
