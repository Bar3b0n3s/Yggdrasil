## Context

The facts this record relies on, each checked in the code at `bb51e110`:

1. **The inner body is an ordinary Kinematic body on the Moving kind.** `CharacterController::Create` creates it through `PhysicsWorld::CreateBody` with `MotionType = Kinematic` on the character's layer and collision group, and `CreateBody` gives every non-sensor, non-static body the object kind `Moving` (`PhysicsWorld.cpp`, `kind = ... Moving`). `State::PlaceInnerBody` teleports it to the character's padded base after every update and `SetPose` (`PhysicsWorld::SetPose(..., false)`), so its velocity stays zero and Jolt resolves its overlap with a Dynamic body as a contact with an infinite mass. The kind rule `PhysicsObjectKindsCollide` pairs Moving with Static, Moving and Sensor, so nothing keeps that pair out of the solver.
2. **The object kind has a free value.** An object layer is `(layerIndex << 2) | kind` in Jolt's 16-bit `ObjectLayer` (`PhysicsLayers.h`), kinds Static 0, Moving 1 and Sensor 2; `PhysicsLayerTable::ShouldCollide` treats the fourth value, 3, as unknown and refuses it (`IsKnownObjectKind`). The kind decides the broad-phase layer (`GetPhysicsBroadPhaseLayer`) and the pair rule; the queries filter by the project layer and, for sensors, by kind (`PhysicsWorldQueries.cpp`'s `QueryObjectLayerFilter`); nothing else reads the kind.
3. **The character's own collision uses the world's pair filter.** `CharacterController::Update` passes `system.GetDefaultLayerFilter(MakePhysicsObjectLayer(layer, Moving))` to `ExtendedUpdate`, so a character collides with what a Moving body on its layer would, other characters' inner bodies included: characters block each other through their inner bodies. Its body filter leaves out its own inner body and its collision group.
4. **`CharacterVirtual` pushes Dynamic bodies itself.** In Jolt 5.6.0 (`Vendor/JoltPhysics`, `CharacterVirtual::HandleContact`), when the character's move hits a Dynamic body whose contact has `mCanReceiveImpulses`, it applies an impulse that brings the body's velocity along the contact normal to 0.9 times the character's relative velocity (plus a penetration term), clamped to `mMaxStrength * deltaTime` (`CharacterVirtualSettings::mMaxStrength`, 100 N by default; the engine never sets it), with its downward part removed. `mMass` (the component's `Mass`, 70 kg by default) is used only for the weight Jolt puts on the body the character stands on at the end of `Update` (`Mass * |n . g| / |g| * g * deltaTime` at the ground's contact point, which the engine caps through a shortened gravity at `MaxGroundImpulse`, 1e10 N s).
5. **A Dynamic body the character touches moves it.** `CharacterContactSettings::mCanPushCharacter` is on by default (the recorder sets nothing), so a Dynamic body's contact velocity enters the character's constraints; and the ground velocity the controller reads (`UpdateGroundVelocity`, then `GetGroundVelocity`) is the ground body's velocity at the character, which `Update` adds, while grounded, as the character's vertical velocity (`groundVertical + desiredVertical`). A light body under the character that moves up gives the character that upward velocity: the launch of brief 11.
6. **`ExtendedUpdate` climbs any steep contact.** Its walk-stairs step (`CharacterVirtual::CanWalkStairs` and `WalkStairs`) runs when the update moved the character less far than it asked and a steep contact holds it back, whatever that contact's body is, so a character climbs a Dynamic shard below `StepHeight`. Jolt documents `ExtendedUpdate` as "an example of how these functions could be combined"; `CanWalkStairs`, `WalkStairs`, `StickToFloor`, `GetActiveContacts`, `IsSlopeTooSteep` and the ground getters are public.
7. **The character's contact listener.** `CharacterContactRecorder` (`Physics/Private`) refuses sensors in `OnContactValidate` and records `OnContactAdded` and `OnContactRemoved`; Jolt calls those callbacks during the character's update with no body locked, and its own pushes go through the world's no-lock interfaces.
8. **The brief's two effects reproduce on the base, through four mechanisms (a fifth showed later, "Waves").** W34.1's cases, run on `bb51e110`'s Physics code: the Warden walking through fifteen 0.35 kg shards rises to 1.84 m and pushes one shard aside; a character standing on a 0.35 kg plate of restitution 0.8 is launched to 26.5 m at 22.9 m/s (its 80 kg weight, `Mass * g * dt` in one impulse, drives the plate into the floor, the plate bounces, and the character takes the plate's upward velocity as its ground's, fact 5); a character walking at 2 m/s into a 0.35 kg box 0.25 m high climbs it (fact 6); and a box sliding into a standing character stops against its inner body (fact 1). So the fix is not only the inner body's: decision 2's light-body rules close the other three.
9. **No committed replay plays a character.** FeatureTest's 14 committed replays play `Animation`, `Combined2DUi`, `Determinism`, `Graphs`, `Input`, `MultiScript`, `Physics2D`, `PhysicsSemantics`, `Sprites2D`, `Terrain`, `Tilemaps`, `UiTextInput`, `UiWidgets` and `UiWorld` (their headers' `Scene`); FeatureTest's characters are in `Components.scene`, `Physics.scene` and `Scripting.scene`, which suites play and no replay does. No demo (`Projects/Tetris`, `Projects/RollingBall`, `Tests/Projects/Breakout`, `Tests/Projects/NativeTest`; eight committed replays) holds a `CharacterController`.
10. **Button bindings refuse gamepad axes.** `InputActionMap::Create` resolves a Button action's `Bindings` (and an Axis action's `Positive` and `Negative`) through `ResolveButtonBindings`, which refuses every gamepad axis ("'Gamepad.RightTrigger' is a gamepad axis, not a button"). `InputState` keeps each gamepad's axes as values (triggers in [0, 1], 0 at rest, `KeyCodes.h`) and records edges for keys, mouse buttons and gamepad buttons only; `LatchStep` and `LatchFrame` copy the live state and move each phase's pending edges into its view.
11. **Bindings are checked at play only.** `PlayInput::Create` runs `InputActionMap::Create` on the project's `Input.Actions` when a session starts and refuses with the issues under `/Input/Actions` ("N problems in the input action definitions"). `ProjectSettingsCommand::CreateFromPatch` (behind `project.setSettings` and the settings panel) checks the patched settings with `ProjectSerializer::FromJson`, which does not read bindings; `project.validate` has 67 codes, the last M26's `TERRAIN_SPLAT_NOT_RAW`, and `INPUT_UNKNOWN_ACTION` is about script literals naming no action.
12. **The camera ray reads the rendered pose.** `Camera:ScreenToWorldRay` (`Scripting/Bindings/Components.cpp`, `CameraRay`) projects through `GetCameraView`, which takes `TransformSystem::GetRenderPosition` and `GetRenderRotation` (the interpolated pose) and, under a `PixelPerfectCamera`, the snapped view of `ComputePixelPerfectView`; its tangent is `DetMath::Tan` and its view size the host's (`ScriptEnvironment::WindowSize`), fixed in every fixed-view session.

## Decisions

### 1. The inner body meets sensors only (W34.1)

- **A new object kind.** `PhysicsObjectKind::Character = 3` (`PhysicsLayers.h`, M11's frozen header, reviewed as such): the inner body's kind. The kind rule pairs a Character with a Sensor only (`PhysicsObjectKindsCollide`, symmetric), so the solver never resolves a contact between an inner body and a Dynamic, Kinematic, Static or other inner body; the matrix still applies to its pairs with sensors. Its broad-phase layer is Moving, where sensors find it.
- **Set at creation.** `CharacterController::Create` sets the inner body's object layer to `(Layer << 2) | Character` right after `CreateBody` (Jolt's `BodyInterface::SetObjectLayer`), before any step can pair it. `PhysicsWorld::GetLayer` still answers the project layer.
- **What still sees it.** Queries (raycasts, casts, overlaps) filter by project layer and sensor kind only, so they hit it as before; sensors pair with it (they set `mCollideKinematicVsNonDynamic`), so triggers still detect characters; the collision group rule is unchanged.
- **Characters still block each other.** The character's own collision takes `CharacterObjectLayerFilter`: the pair rule of a Moving body on the character's layer, with an inner body (Character kind) met as the Moving body it was, so a character's capsule meets other characters' inner bodies exactly as before M34.
- **What goes.** The world's own contact records between an inner body and a Dynamic body (the second source of a character's collision events with Dynamic bodies, ADR 0031) are gone; the character's own contacts (`FromCharacter`) report those pairs, so a character's `OnCollisionEnter` with a Dynamic body it walks into or stands on is unchanged. Nothing in the solver stands for the character any more, so what stops a Dynamic body moving into a character is the character's own update (decision 3 states which bodies pass through it and when).

### 2. Pushing, and light bodies (W34.1)

- **The push.** A character pushes Dynamic bodies only through `CharacterVirtual`'s own contacts (fact 4), with a strength of `Mass * 9.81` N (`CharacterVirtualSettings::mMaxStrength`; `PushAcceleration` in `CharacterController.cpp`): the force of its weight under the Earth's gravity, whatever the world's gravity, so a character pushes a body lighter than it along any ground whose friction coefficient is at most 1. No push-strength field is exposed: `Mass` already says "how hard the character pushes dynamic bodies" (`CharacterControllerDescription`), so `CharacterController` stays version 1 with its fields and no scene's canonical form or hash changes (the ledger's CharacterController row allowed a field; `Docs/Backlog.md` §6 records the limit).
- **Light bodies.** A Dynamic body is light for a character when its mass is below the character's `Mass` (`IsLighterThanCharacter`, from Jolt's inverse mass; a body whose translation is locked has none and is never light). A light body never moves the character:
  - it cannot push it: the recorder turns `mCanPushCharacter` off for its contact in `OnContactAdded` and `OnContactPersisted`, so its velocity and penetration never move the character, while the character still pushes it;
  - it never lifts it: Jolt slides the character along a contact's plane, which for a light body's surface facing upwards (a shard's sloped top, a box's edge below the capsule's middle) carries the character up onto it, so the recorder's `OnContactSolve` meets such a contact as a vertical wall (the character's motion into the body along the normal's horizontal part stops, its vertical motion stays); a surface facing straight up or downwards keeps Jolt's answer, which never lifts the character, and so does the body the character stands on (its walkable ground of the last update, `OnGround`): the rule stops a character getting onto a light body by sliding, never moving across the one it is on, so a character walks up a tilted light plank it stands on (review finding 1). A velocity the wall leaves still moving into the contact's plane, a fall onto a steep face whose horizontal part is gone, then loses that part along the normal, so it slides along the face: Jolt would otherwise meet the same contact again at once with nowhere to go, and the character would hang on the face (review finding 2); this never lifts the character, since the result's vertical part lies between the fall's and zero;
  - it gives it no velocity as its ground: `Update` takes the ground velocity as zero when the ground is a light body (fact 5), so a shard the character stands on never launches it;
  - it is never a stair: `Update` runs `ExtendedUpdate` with its walk-stairs step off and then the same step itself (`State::WalkStairs`, `ExtendedUpdate`'s rule and settings) with light bodies left out of the contacts that may be climbed, so the character pushes a light body below `StepHeight` instead of climbing it; a body at least as heavy as the character is a stair as before;
  - it carries at most its own weight: `Update` gives Jolt no gravity (whose only use is the weight on the ground, fact 4) and puts the weight on a Dynamic ground itself after the step (`State::PushGround`, Jolt's impulse at the ground's contact point), at most the body's own weight (`min(Mass, m)`) and capped at `MaxGroundImpulse`, so a character standing on a shard never drives it into the floor.
- **What is unchanged.** Static and Kinematic grounds and stairs, a Dynamic body at least as heavy as the character (it pushes the character, carries its velocity and full weight, and is climbed), `InheritGroundVelocity` on everything but a light ground, the jump, the fall, the speed clamp, the range rule and the contacts the character records.
- **The test case of World Tree's walk-through.** "CharacterController: a character walking at 6.5 m/s through fifteen 0.35 kg Dynamic shards on the floor stays below 0.1 m over 60 ticks, and no shard goes below -0.05 m: the shards are pushed aside along the ground" (Roadmap M34's acceptance, verbatim but for the ASCII minus sign) plays the brief's numbers: World Tree's Warden (1.8 m, radius 0.35 m, 80 kg, step 0.35 m) and fifteen irregular convex shards of 0.35 kg.

### 3. Determinism, replays and what changes for games (W34.1)

- **Deterministic.** Every rule above runs on the main thread in the character's update, from the world's state and the character's description, through the world's no-lock interfaces (fact 7), so a run gives the same state hash in Debug, Release and Dist; no hash block, phase or input changes (the ledger's execution-order row: PhysicsPreStep's character update and the contacts PhysicsStep resolves change in place).
- **The replays it moves.** It changes the state of every run in which a character touches a Dynamic body or another body touches a character's inner body. No committed replay plays a character (fact 9), and the tests say so: `Scaffold.py` on W34.1's tree recorded FeatureTest's 14 replays again byte for byte as committed (a moved replay would have changed its hashes), and no demo project holds a `CharacterController` (their scenes, prefabs and scripts, searched on the base), so the demos' eight replays cannot move; the determinism and games suites, which verify every committed replay at its hash, run once at the batch gate on the merged tree (the lead's GPU rules of 2026-11-16: no builder runs them before it). So M34 records no replay again and needs no bridge window; a FeatureTest replay another phase-5 milestone records with a character touching a Dynamic body is recorded again by `Scaffold.py` at the later lander's merge (the ledger). World Tree's own replays (its Arena 1 tour) are the World Tree session's to record again (brief 11).
- **What a game sees.** A character kicks light debris aside instead of climbing it or being launched by it, and pushes heavier Dynamic bodies with `Mass * 9.81` N. What a Dynamic body moving into a character does (review finding 3; `Docs/Backlog.md` §6 records it):
  - **A light body** is stopped by the character's own push: the character's update meets a Dynamic body approaching within its predictive contact distance (0.1 m) and pushes it back with at most `Mass * 9.81` N for the tick, which the light body never returns. It passes into the capsule only when it outruns that look-ahead within one tick or is driven harder than the push (a velocity set every tick, a joint, a heavier body behind it). "CharacterController: a light Dynamic body thrown at a standing character is stopped by its push" pins it.
  - **A light platform never lifts or carries a character.** It cannot push the character, so one driven up under it rises through it ("CharacterController: a light Dynamic platform rising under a character passes up through it"), and its velocity is never the character's ground velocity, so a moving light platform carries no character, with `InheritGroundVelocity` or without. A platform that carries characters is Kinematic, or Dynamic and at least as heavy as they are.
  - **A heavy body** (at least the character's `Mass`) is no longer stopped by an inner body of infinite mass: it shoves the character through the character's own contacts, and overlaps the capsule only when the character cannot give way, pinned against static geometry or under the body (the character's upward push on a body coming down on it is capped at `Mass * 9.81` N). This is what ADR 0042's M34 bullet and brief 11's "Wanted" 1 ask for; stopping heavy bodies at a character would need a solver presence of finite mass, new scope (`Docs/Backlog.md` §6).
  - A Dynamic projectile that must hit a character is a trigger or a cast, which still see the inner body.
- **Run modes and coverage.** The rules are Physics', in every run mode (editor play, the suites, replays, Release and Dist exports). FeatureTest: the `CharacterDebris` scene and its suite (`Assets/Tests/CharacterDebris/CharacterDebris.test.luau`, suite `Physics.CharacterDebris`, run in the Editor, Release and Dist modes), written by M34's scaffold `CharacterDebris.jsonl` (IdSeed 3101): a character walks through fifteen light shards on the floor and stays on it while they are pushed aside, a heavy crate below the step is climbed, and a light one is pushed. It adds no component, field, enum value, setting, format, script API or method, so it changes no coverage gate's list.

### 5. Triggers as buttons (W34.2)

- **In the input state.** `InputState` keeps each trigger's button level (`GamepadState::TriggersDown`, by `GamepadAxis` index): an axis event of `LeftTrigger` or `RightTrigger` sets the level down when the value rises to `GamepadTriggerPressThreshold` (0.5) and up only when it falls below `GamepadTriggerReleaseThreshold` (0.4), and each change records `Pressed` or `Released` edges in both phases' pending changes (`GamepadTriggersPressed`, `GamepadTriggersReleased`), latched by `LatchStep` and `LatchFrame` like a gamepad button's; a `Disconnected` event releases a held trigger with a `Released` edge. Queries: `IsGamepadTriggerDown`, `WasGamepadTriggerPressed`, `WasGamepadTriggerReleased` (false for a stick axis). The thresholds are engine constants (`InputState.h`), not an `InputAction` setting, so no project file changes and no upgrade runs (the ledger's settings row left the choice).
- **In the action map.** A trigger (`Gamepad.LeftTrigger`, `Gamepad.RightTrigger`) is a button binding wherever button bindings go: a Button action's `Bindings` and an Axis action's `Positive` and `Negative` (one rule, `ResolveButtonBindings`); a stick axis there is still refused ("'Gamepad.LeftY' is a stick axis, not a button"). `IsDown`, `WasPressed`, `WasReleased` and `GetAxis` read a trigger over every connected gamepad, as for gamepad buttons.
- **Why triggers only.** A stick has two directions, so one threshold on its signed value would make only up or right a button; ADR 0042's "a gamepad axis" is made exact as the two triggers, the axes whose rest is 0 and whose range is one-sided, which is what brief 10 asks for. A stick direction as a button waits for a game that needs it (`Docs/Backlog.md` §6).
- **Determinism.** The level is input state, outside the state hash as all input state is; it depends only on the sequence of axis values injected, which replays, `input.inject`, `play.step` and `Test.InjectGamepad` carry, so a replay reproduces it. The frame preview (`PlayInput`, ADR 0033 decision 12) takes the applied tick's trigger levels as well as its values (`TrackedDevices::TakeLevels`: a trigger whose level differs goes through the far end of its range first), since a level is not a function of the value alone.
- **Ledger.** No phase is added (ApplyInput and LatchFrame latch it, the ledger's execution-order row); `Session/PlayInputEventCodec.cpp` is unchanged (no event's shape changes).

### 6. Bindings checked when set (W34.2)

- **One check.** `ValidateInputActions(const InputSettings&) -> Status` (`Session/PlayInput.h`, beside `ValidatePlayInputEvent`; a change to M7's frozen header, reviewed as one) is the check `PlayInput::Create` makes when `play.start` builds a session: `InputActionMap::Create`'s problems as Validation, located at `/Input/Actions`, one issue per problem at `/Input/Actions/<Name>/<Field>[/<i>]`. `PlayInput::Create` uses it, so the three callers can never disagree.
- **`project.setSettings` from automation.** `ProjectSettingsCommand::CreateFromPatch` runs it on the patched settings when the patch has an `Input` member. With `InputActionCheck::Refuse`, which `project.setSettings` passes for an agent's request (`EditorContext::GetCommandOrigin` is `Agent`: every request the automation server serves, a dry run and an `edit.batch` op included) and the validator's fixes pass, it refuses the patch with its error, each issue located under `/patch` (`/patch/Input/Actions/Attack/Bindings/1`); no command is made, so nothing is written, recorded or undoable. The method's description (the MCP tool's and `catalog.json`'s), `ProjectMethods.h` and Architecture §13.5's row say so (review finding 8). A patch that does not write `Input` never fails on actions the project already holds: `project.validate` reports those (decision 7), and refusing an unrelated change (a window title) for them would block every other fix.
- **The editor's own settings paths** (review finding 7). The Project Settings panel submits `project.setSettings` through `EditorActions`, as the user (origin `User`), and the inspector's settings path (`ReflectedEditController`) is the user's gesture; both pass `InputActionCheck::Warn`, so such a patch commits as one undoable command with each problem among the command's warnings (at its pointer, no code; `project.setSettings`' `warnings` put `/patch` before it). The reflected list's "Add element" appends an empty binding and commits at once, which the refusal would have turned down, leaving the panel no way to add a binding; now the binding commits empty and the user fills it in. The panel's Input section shows each problem of its draft's actions (`ValidateInputActions` again, so a problem shows until it is fixed, whoever fixed it) under the value it concerns, in a warning colour (`ReflectedDrawerContext::Notes`, a new optional member of the Editor's drawer context). A project that holds the problem meanwhile is what `INPUT_INVALID_ACTION` and `play.start`'s refusal already report. Brief 10 asks that an agent's invalid binding be refused, and it still is; a person editing in the panel sees the problem at once instead. Tests: "ProjectMethods: project.setSettings from the editor's panel commits a binding added empty, naming its problem in warnings, and the binding is filled in next", "ReflectedEditController: a binding added empty to an input action commits, and the next edit fills it in" and "ProjectSettingsPanel: an input binding added empty commits and shows its problem beside it until it is filled in".
- **Where the check lives.** In EditorCore and Session, not in `Project/ProjectSettings.cpp`'s validator: that validator runs when a project loads, and a project holding an invalid binding must still open so `project.validate` can report it (the ledger's row left the choice). The Ui settings' navigation lists, which `PlaySession.cpp` parses at `play.start` with rules of their own, are not part of this check (`Docs/Backlog.md` §6), so `PlaySession.cpp` is unchanged.

### 8. `Camera:ScreenToWorldRayFrom` (W34.2)

- **Signature.** `Camera:ScreenToWorldRayFrom(x: number, y: number, position: vector, rotation: Quat) -> (vector, vector)`, appended to the Camera type's methods in `Bindings/Components.cpp` after `WorldToScreen` (the ledger's script-API row). It is `ScreenToWorldRay` (fact 12) with the pose given instead of the rendered one: the ray through the centre of view pixel (x, y), origin top-left, its origin and unit direction; the camera's projection, `DetMath` tangent and clip planes and the session's view size as `ScreenToWorldRay`'s; under a `PixelPerfectCamera` the given position snapped and the reference view and output mapping applied as for the rendered pose.
- **Errors.** As `ScreenToWorldRay`'s (a zero viewport, a pixel outside the view), and its arguments' own: the position is a vector, whose every component `Lua::Check` requires finite (review finding 9: the method adds no check of its own), and the rotation a `Quat` (nonzero and finite by its type), normalized as `RigidBody:MoveKinematic`'s is.
- **Phases and determinism.** Callable wherever `ScreenToWorldRay` is, the fixed step included; it reads only its arguments, the camera's fields, its entity's `PixelPerfectCamera` and the session's view size, which every fixed-view session keeps fixed (suites, replays, recordings, lockstep), so gameplay that aims with it from a pose it keeps in the fixed step is deterministic and independent of the frame rate. `ScreenToWorldRay` is unchanged.

## Risks

- **Characters versus Dynamic bodies change for every game** (ADR 0042's risk): a game whose characters relied on the inner body's infinite push or on Dynamic bodies stopping at a character behaves differently. No committed replay has a character (decision 3); World Tree records its own again; `Docs/Backlog.md` §6 states what is left.
- **The stair step is the controller's copy of `ExtendedUpdate`'s.** A Jolt update that changes `ExtendedUpdate`'s step would not reach the controller; `VENDOR.md`'s update procedure re-reads it, and the step and floor cases ("a character climbs a step below StepHeight but not a higher one", the light and heavy stair case) pin the behaviour.
- **The light-body threshold is a mass comparison.** A Dynamic body just lighter than the character is pushed, never climbed, and never moves the character; one just heavier is climbed and moves it. The threshold is the character's own `Mass`, which a game tunes per character.
