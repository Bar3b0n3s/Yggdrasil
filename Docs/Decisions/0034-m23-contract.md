## Context

M23 lands last in phase 2 and closes it with phase 2's hardened gate, `CI.py --milestone` on the merged tree of M21, M22 and M23 (ADR 0028 decision 12). Its eleven items run in three waves (Roadmap M23, plan §3.5):

- **W23.1 (C23.1):** R-INST (instancing), R-PLS (point-light shadows), R-CLC (clustered light culling), R-TEXC-BC (BC compression and the `Compression` setting) and A-OPUS (Ogg Opus import); streams A, B, C, X and O.
- **W23.2 (C23.1, the same contract):** C-MESH2 (mesh version 2 and the second vertex binding, with Material version 2), A-EXR (EXR environments) and R-TEXC-HDR (BC6H environment cubes); streams Y (after X), M (after A is integrated), D1 (instancing and lights coverage) and F (the demos' Material upgrade and the textured goldens).
- **W23.3 (C23.3):** C-SKEL, C-MORPH and C-ANIM-BLEND.

M23 owns, in phase 2 (ADR 0028's ledger): the format versions of `MeshData` and `GltfImporter`, the material file, `MaterialData` and `MaterialImporter`, `TextureData` and `TextureImporter`, `EnvironmentData` and `EnvironmentImporter`, `AudioClipData` and `AudioImporter`, PointLight version 2, the new Skeleton and AnimationClip formats and AssetType values 14 and 15 (C23.3), `EngineVersionString` 0.3.0 at its landing; the FeatureTest IdSeeds 2100 to 2199; the Skinning, PointShadows and LightClusters passes at their ledger places; `SceneRenderer.cpp`'s Prepare, Deform, Shadows, Ambient and Forward stage functions and `RenderPrepare`; the 3D path's shared shader headers and `Scene.slang`; `RenderSnapshot.h`'s existing members; `GpuResourceCache`; the mesh and light paths of `RenderExtraction.cpp`, `EntityBounds.cpp` and `RenderAnnotations.cpp`; `Vendor/miniaudio`; the vendored CMP_Core, tinyexr, libogg, libopus and opusfile; and the reviewed regenerations of every golden its compression changes, at the integration of each wave that changes them and once more on phase 2's merged tree at its landing (the ledger as amended on 2026-10-29; decision 17).

The facts this contract relies on, checked in the code at the base:

1. **One draw per submesh.** Every mesh pass records one draw per culled submesh draw with the per-draw push constants `DrawConstants {World, EntityId, Flags}` (`Resources/Shaders/Shared/DrawConstants.h`, `Renderer/Private/SceneRendererState.h`'s `SceneRendererDraw`); opaque draws sort by `(Pipeline, Material, Mesh, DrawIndex)` (`Renderer/RenderPrepare.h`), so two submeshes of one mesh interleave by snapshot order.
2. **Every pixel shades every light.** `CullLights` keeps at most `MaxVisibleLights` (256) lights, and `Scene.slang`'s `ShadeForward` loops over all `ViewConstants::LightCount` of them (`Passes/Scene.slang:315`), sampling the directional cascades and the spot atlas there. `LightData::CastShadows` exists; "points cast no shadows" (`RenderSnapshot.h`), and the `PointLight` component (version 1: `Color`, `Intensity`, `Range`, `SourceRadius`) has no `CastShadows`. No audited project holds a `PointLight` (the ledger).
3. **`ShadowConstants`** is 1424 bytes with `Counts.w` unused padding (`Shared/ShadowConstants.h`). The device already enables `imageCubeArray` (`Graphics/GraphicsDevice.cpp`).
4. **M18's pass list** declares 58 startup pipelines and 25 stats rows and reserves the Deform stage and the places of PointShadows (after SpotShadows) and LightClusters (the end of Ambient) (`Renderer/RenderPassDeclaration.h`, `RenderStage.h`; ADR 0029 decision 1). `stats.get` lists only the rows a frame recorded.
5. **Mesh format version 1** is 48-byte vertices (position, normal, tangent, one UV set); the glTF importer warns `ASSET_UNSUPPORTED_UV_SET` for a texture bound to `TEXCOORD_1` and `ASSET_VERTEX_COLORS_IGNORED` for `COLOR_0` (`AssetPipeline/Importers/GltfImporter.cpp`). Material version 1 has five maps on one UV set with one transform.
6. **Textures** are RGBA8 or R8 only, imported with `TextureImportSettings {Usage, GenerateMips}`; the device does not enable `textureCompressionBC`. **Environments** are RGBA16F cubes; `.exr` is claimed only to be refused. **Audio** decodes WAV, FLAC, MP3 and Ogg Vorbis through miniaudio, and refuses Ogg Opus (`Audio/AudioDecoder.h`).
7. **Process-level state** is listed in Architecture §3 rule 5 and initialized by `ProcessContext`'s steps (`App/ProcessContext.h`).

### 1. What the contract freezes, implements and stubs

### 2. Instancing (R-INST; stream A)

- **Automatic batching.** The Prepare stage writes one instance record per submesh draw of the view and splits each pass's draws, in the order the pass records them, into maximal runs of adjacent draws with equal `MeshInstanceKey {Pipeline, Material, Mesh, Submesh, VertexStream}` (`GroupMeshInstances`, `Renderer/RenderPrepare.h`); each run is one instanced draw whose instances are its draws in order. Recording a run instanced draws exactly what one draw per element draws, in the same order (Vulkan rasterizes instances in order), which is why an instanced render equals the per-draw render byte for byte.
- **The opaque order** gains the submesh before the snapshot order, `(Pipeline, Material, Mesh, Submesh, DrawIndex)`, so equal draws are adjacent: `OpaqueSortKey::Submesh` and `SortOpaqueDraws` are implemented by the contract ("RenderPrepare: opaque draws of one mesh and material sort by submesh before snapshot order", live), and stream A fills the key from the draw's submesh in the Prepare stage (until then every key's is 0, so the contract changes no order). The instancing case draws two-submesh quads and pins the instanced count, 8 forward draws of 48, which fails unless the Prepare stage fills it. The transparent order is unchanged (only adjacent equal draws batch, so back to front holds).
- **The vertex source:** `MeshInstanceKey::VertexStream` is 0 for the mesh asset's own vertex buffers, every draw until C23.3; C23.3's Deform stage gives each deformed draw (a skinned or morphed entity's, whose deformed vertices are its own) a value of its own, so deformed draws of one mesh and material never share an instanced draw, and C23.3 changes no frozen member of this contract.
- **The passes that batch:** every pass that draws mesh submeshes: the DepthNormal prepass (and its picking form), ForwardOpaque, ForwardTransparent, the Overdraw view and the material and data debug views (their forward specializations), the shadow casters (every cascade, spot tile and point face) and the selection mask. Each pass's `RenderPassStats::DrawCalls` then counts its instanced draws, so stream A owns the existing real-render `DrawCalls` pins that batching changes, "DebugViews: overdraw counts accepted fragments before depth rejection" among them (its `Triangles` pin is unchanged).
- **The instance record** (`Shared/InstanceRecord.h`, 144 bytes, std430): `World`, `PreviousWorld` (decision 8), `EntityId`, `Flags` (the `DrawFlag` bits). The per-view `Instances` structured buffer, `SceneRenderer::State::InstanceBuffer` (the placement rule's place 2), is `t10` of descriptor set 0 of the Scene program and a binding of the Shadow and SelectionMask programs' own layouts (every program that draws meshes reads it), grown to the view's instance count and written once per view with the other per-view constants (`RecordViewSetup`).
- **`DrawConstants` version 2** (stream A): `{uint32_t FirstInstance; uint32_t Padding0; UInt2 Padding1;}`, 16 bytes; `World`, `EntityId` and `Flags` move into the record. The vertex shaders read `Instances[Draw.FirstInstance + instance]` with the instance index counted from 0 within the draw (the draw's own first instance stays 0), and keep computing the clip position with `MultiplyExact` from the record's `World`, so the prepass and the forward pass stay bit-identical (ADR 0013 decision 25's limit).
- **The reference path:** `RenderQualitySettings::Instancing` false records every submesh draw as its own draw of one instance, through the same shaders and records. "SceneRenderer: an instanced render is byte-identical to the per-draw render" compares the two and checks that the instanced render records exactly one ForwardOpaque draw call per pipeline variant, material and submesh (8 of the 48 submesh draws). `RenderPassStats::DrawCalls` counts draw calls; `SceneRenderStats::MeshDraws` keeps counting submesh draws (instances).
- **The 24 mesh pipelines stay:** the record is a structured buffer, not a vertex binding; only the binding layout of set 0 grows.

### 3. Point-light shadows (R-PLS; stream B)

- **PointLight version 2** (the ledger's reservation): `CastShadows` (bool, false) between `Range` and `SourceRadius`, as `SpotLight` orders its fields; the migration from version 1 writes `"CastShadows": false` and keeps everything else (the fixture pair `PointLightV1.scene` and `PointLightV1.upgraded.scene`). Render extraction copies it to `LightData::CastShadows`.
- **The plan** (`Renderer/PointShadowMaps.h`): the visible point lights with `CastShadows`, in `CullLights`' importance order, the first `MaxPointShadowLights` (4) of them; the rest are lit unshadowed and counted in `Dropped` (`RENDER_POINT_SHADOW_BUDGET`, a Warn line once per renderer through `SceneRenderer::State::PointShadowLimitLogged`, which the release keeps like `SpotLimitLogged`, and a validation warning, decision 18); a light that cannot be shadowed (a non-finite position, a range not above twice its near plane) is counted in `Rejected` and never costs another its shadow.
- **The target:** `PointShadowMaps`, a cube-array depth texture of `6 x MaxPointShadowLights` layers in the Shadows declaration's depth format, faces of `GetPointShadowFaceSize(Quality.ShadowMapSize)` texels (a quarter of the shadow map size, at least 128), with one framebuffer per layer, in `SceneRendererTargets` (place 2), created while a point light is planned. Light `i`'s faces are the layers `6 i` to `6 i + 5`, in Vulkan's cube face order.
- **The pass:** PointShadows (the Shadows stage, after SpotShadows; its own stats row) draws the casters of each face's frustum with ShadowPass's caster pipelines and one `ShadowCasterConstants` per face (the spot shadows' bias defaults: `DepthBias` and `NormalBias` 1). It declares no pipeline: the Shadow program "extends" to it (the ledger's "unless it extends Shadow").
- **The face projection** (`ComputePointShadowFaceViewProjection`): a 90-degree perspective reverse-Z projection with depth 1 at the near plane (`max(SourceRadius, 0.05)`, at most `Range / 2`) and 0 at `Range`, looking down the face's major axis with the up vector of Vulkan's cube face table, so a cube sampler reads, for a direction from the light, the face and texel the pass wrote: the texel (column, row) of a face of N texels that Vulkan's face selection table gives a direction (`Test::CubeTexelDirection`) lands at NDC x = 2 (column + 0.5) / N - 1 and y = 1 - 2 (row + 0.5) / N, since NVRHI's flipped viewport puts NDC +Y on row 0. The face case checks the axis, the depths and two off-axis texels per face, so a mirrored, rotated or swapped face fails it.
- **Sampling:** `t12` of set 0 is `PointShadowMaps` as a `TextureCubeArray<float>` with the shadow comparison sampler (`s1`). `SamplePointShadow` (`Common/Shadows.slang`) takes the direction from the light to the normal-offset receiver, the reference depth of the face its major axis selects, and a fixed, deterministic percentage-closer kernel in the face's tangent plane whose radius grows with `SourceRadius` up to `DepthSoftness.w`; no temporal or random term (plan §7 item 9). `ShadowConstants::Points[i]` (`Data formats`) carries what it reads.
- **Stats:** `RenderStats` gains `ShadowedPointLights` and `DroppedPointShadows`, reported by `stats.get` (decision 18).
- **The oracle and the golden:** the CPU oracle of point-shadow visibility, `Test::IsPointLightVisible(receiver, light, occluders)` with `Test::ReferenceTriangle` in `Tests/Source/Support/RenderReference` (a receiver is lit when the segment to the light crosses no occluder triangle; implemented by the contract, with "RenderReference: a point light is hidden exactly where the segment to it crosses an occluder"), backs "SceneRenderer: a shadow-casting point light leaves the occluded floor unlit, as the CPU oracle predicts": an asymmetric scene (the light off the camera's axis, the occluder off the light's, the shadow across the -Z and +X faces) whose every floor pixel with a uniform oracle answer over its 5 x 5 neighbourhood must be black where the oracle hides the light and equal the render without `CastShadows` elsewhere. The golden scaffold `GoldenPointShadows.jsonl` (IdSeed 2104) adds the point-shadow golden.

### 4. The light loop and its owner (stream C)

- **Stream C owns the forward light loop.** It moves the loop of `ShadeForward` (`Scene.slang`) into `Resources/Shaders/Common/LightLoop.slang`, which `Scene.slang` includes after its resource declarations (as it includes `Common/Shadows.slang`): one function shades the direct light of the pixel's lights, iterating the set bits of its cluster's masks in ascending order (decision 5), or every light of the list in the brute-force mode, and multiplies each light's `ShadeLight` by its visibility.
- **The visibility** of one light (`GetLightVisibility`, `Common/Shadows.slang`) is stream B's for points; the directional and spot branches move there unchanged. Stream B lists its point branch for C; the wave's integration joins them. Stream A changes only the vertex shaders; stream X (W23.1) only the normal map's z reconstruction (decision 10) and stream M (W23.2) the rest of the surface evaluation (UV sets and the vertex colour).
- **For later milestones:** M26's probes add their indirect term after the loop, outside it (plan §4.1: "light loop" is M23's frozen interface).

### 5. Clustered light culling (R-CLC; stream C)

- **The grid** (`Shared/LightClusterConstants.h`, `Renderer/LightClusters.h`): 64 x 64-pixel tiles of the scene resolution (partial at the right and bottom edges) times 24 slices of view depth, exponential between the camera's near and far clip (the last slice open beyond), for both projections; `LightClusterGrid`, `ComputeLightClusterGrid` and `GetLightClusterIndex` state it on the CPU, and the shaders compute the same index.
- **Ordered bitmasks, no atomics** (plan §6 "M23"): each cluster holds `LightClusterWordCount` (8) words, one bit per entry of the light list. The LightClusters pass (the end of the Ambient stage, after the GTAO denoise; its own stats row) dispatches one thread per cluster and word; each thread tests its 32 lights against its cluster's view-space bounds, conservatively (a margin covers the shaders' slice computation), and is the only writer of its word. Directional lights are in every cluster. The masks are `t11` of set 0 for the forward passes (the pass's `u0`), `SceneRenderer::State::LightClusterBuffer`; the grid's constants are `b3` of set 0, `LightClusterConstantsBuffer` (place 2). No depth-bounds tightening from the depth pyramid in M23: a cluster's depth range is its slice's.
- **Equal to brute force, exactly:** the forward loop shades the cluster's lights in ascending list order, and a light outside a pixel's cluster contributes exactly zero there (the windowed falloff is 0 beyond `Range`, a spot's cone factor 0 outside its outer cone, and `ShadeLight` returns `float3(0)` for a zero attenuation), and adding an exact zero leaves an IEEE sum unchanged. So the clustered image equals the brute-force image byte for byte: "SceneRenderer: clustered lighting equals the brute-force light loop exactly on 256 lights". The brute-force mode (`LightClusterConstants::Mode` `LightClusterModeBruteForce`) runs when `RenderQualitySettings::ClusteredLights` is false, and the LightClusters pass is then not recorded.
- **The pass:** `LightClusterPass` (`Renderer/LightClusterPass.{h,cpp}`, stream C), one compute pipeline of the program `LightClusters` (`Passes/LightClusters.slang`, sorted into `Shaders.json`), in `SceneRendererPipelines::State` (place 1, created by `CreatePipelines`); stream C sets the declaration's `PipelineCount` to 1 and its `Programs` to `{LightClusters}`, which makes the startup count 59 (`static_assert` and "Pipelines: Create makes exactly the declared startup pipelines").
- **The LightCount debug view** (the plan's "light-count view"): `RenderDebugView::LightCount`, appended after `Overdraw` (value 9, `RenderDebugViewCount` 10), a data view showing each pixel's cluster's light count through `GetLightCountDebugColor`'s palette (0 black, 1 to 2 blue, 3 to 4 cyan, 5 to 8 green, 9 to 16 yellow, 17 to 32 orange, more red). `viewport.screenshot` and `viewport.setOptions` parse their `debugView` through `RenderDebugViewCount`'s names, and the editor's debug-view menu enumerates them, but both parameters' descriptions list the views by name (`Engine/Automation/Methods/ScreenshotMethods.cpp`, `EditorCore/Automation/ViewportMethods.cpp`), and they feed `Docs/Reference` and `Tools/MCP/catalog.json`. **The ledger amendment** (the lead, 2026-10-29; `ScreenshotMethods` is otherwise single-owner "none" in phase 2): stream C rewords both descriptions to name `RenderDebugView`'s generated enum instead of listing the views, so the LightCount view and later views need no edit of the descriptions, only their entry in that enum's registration (`ScreenshotMethods.cpp`'s `RegisterViewportScreenshotMethodTypes`, which stream C added; decision 20), which "ScreenshotMethods: the registered RenderDebugView enum holds every debug view under the name debugView parses" keeps in step with `RenderDebugViewCount` and the parser's names, and the data views' encoding sentence ("stores each value v as round(255 v)") names LightCount's palette as its exception; the generated references change with stream C.

### 7. The recreation protocol (every new GPU owner)

Every new GPU object follows ADR 0029 decision 27's placement rule, so none registers on its own:

- **Place 2 (each `SceneRenderer`):** `InstanceBuffer` (A), `LightClusterBuffer` and `LightClusterConstantsBuffer` (C), `PointShadowMaps` with its framebuffers (B). The contract added these members to `SceneRendererState.h`, so the release, which replaces the whole state, drops them without code, and the next `Render` creates them again.
- **Place 1 (`SceneRendererPipelines`):** `LightClusterPass` (C), created again by `CreatePipelines`.
- **Place 3 (`GpuResourceCache`):** the attribute buffers and the shared default attribute stream (M), BC textures and the fallback's decompressed textures (X), BC6H environment cubes (Y).
- **Loss tests** through `Test::RunDeviceLossTest`: "SceneRenderer: a device loss recreates the instances, the clusters and the point shadows and renders the same image" (A, B and C, un-skipped at W23.1's integration, which joins their parts), and stream X's "GpuResourceCache: a device loss drops the BC and fallback mirrors, and the next Get uploads them again" (decision 15). Stream B keeps `PointShadowLimitLogged` across the release (`SceneRendererRecovery.cpp`'s kept members): "SceneRenderer: the point-shadow budget warning stays once per renderer across a release and recreation" (W23.1's review).

- No member is added after `Output`, where M21 and M22 append theirs.

### 9. Mesh version 2 and the second vertex binding (C-MESH2; stream M)

- **`MeshData` version 2** (`Data formats`): the version 1 payload with a `Streams` word and a reserved word after the four counts, and the optional sections after the indices, in bit order: the attribute stream (`MeshVertexAttributes`, 24 bytes: `TexCoord1`, `Color`), then C23.3's skin and morph sections. A version 2 reader refuses the `Skin` and `Morph` bits until C23.3 defines their sections. Cooked meshes are cache artifacts, rebuilt by `GltfImporter` version 2, so no reader keeps version 1.
- **`GltfImporter` version 2** reads `TEXCOORD_1` (float, normalized unsigned byte or short) and `COLOR_0` (vec3 or vec4, float or normalized unsigned byte or short; vec3 takes alpha 1) into the attribute stream; `ASSET_UNSUPPORTED_UV_SET` now names only `TEXCOORD_2` and up, and `ASSET_VERTEX_COLORS_IGNORED` is no longer produced (decision 18). A primitive without either attribute gets no stream.
- **The second binding:** every mesh-drawing pipeline (the Scene program's 24 and the debug views', the Shadow casters, the selection mask) takes vertex binding 1, stride 24: `TEXCOORD1` at location 4 (`RG32_FLOAT`, offset 0) and `COLOR` at location 5 (`RGBA32_FLOAT`, offset 8), because a Mask material's alpha test reads the base colour's UV set and the vertex colour's alpha. `GpuMesh::AttributeBuffer` binds the mesh's stream, or the cache's one shared default stream (`TexCoord1` (0, 0), opaque white), grown to the largest vertex count of a mesh without attributes and bound at offset 0, since all its entries are equal. The 24 mesh pipelines stay.
- **The shader:** the surface multiplies the base colour (RGBA) by the vertex colour, always, as glTF defines it, and samples each map with its UV set (decision 10). A mesh without the stream draws as before within rounding: the interpolated opaque white times the base colour moves a few pixels by 1/255 (section "Review (W23.2)", item 4).

### 10. Material version 2 (stream M; the demos' upgrade, stream F)

- **The format:** registry enum `TextureCoordinateSet {UV0, UV1}` and five fields, each after its map: `BaseColorUVSet`, `MetallicRoughnessUVSet`, `NormalUVSet`, `OcclusionUVSet`, `EmissiveUVSet` (UV0 by default). `MaterialData::CurrentVersion`, `FormatVersion` (cooked) and `MaterialImporter::Version` become 2. A version 1 file loads with every set UV0 (an absent key keeps its default) and saves as version 2: the fixture pair `MaterialV1.material` and `MaterialV1.upgraded.material`. One `UVScale` and `UVOffset` still transform every map.
- **The vertex colour has no field:** it always multiplies the base colour (decision 9).
- **`MaterialConstants::Flags`:** `MaterialFlag<Map>UV1` from the UV sets (stream M), and `MaterialFlagNormalMapTwoChannel` when the bound normal map's `TextureFormat` is `BC5Unorm` (decision 13), on a device with or without BC (the fallback's RGBA8 mirror of a BC5 texture holds (x, y, 0, 255)); the shader then reconstructs z as `sqrt(saturate(1 - x² - y²))`. The two-channel flag and its z reconstruction are stream X's, in W23.1, since X's Auto makes normal maps BC5 there: `GpuResourceCache::GetMaterial`'s flag (by function with M's material path) and `Scene.slang`'s normal-map read (by function with M's surface evaluation).
- **The flags follow the textures:** the material mirror rebuilds its constants only for a new material version, and a texture change alone (a placeholder replaced by the imported texture, a new version, a `Compression` change) keeps them (`GpuResourceCache.cpp`'s texture-change path). So the mirror writes new constants (a new buffer and a new `Generation`) whenever the flags its resolved textures imply differ from `GpuMaterial::Flags`, the flags its constants hold (implemented by the contract; stream X compares them): "GpuResourceCache: a material's two-channel normal flag follows its normal map's format when only the texture changes" (GPU, stream X).
- **glTF:** version 2's material sub-assets take each `textureInfo.texCoord` (0 or 1; 2 and up keep the warning and drop the map). `MaterialImporter` version 2 fills `Import.Dependencies` with its textures (the ledger's importer-dependency rule).
- **The demos' recorded upgrade** (stream F in W23.2, redone by the lander on the merged tree): `Editor --headless --project <P> --upgrade` rewrites every `.material` of the audited projects canonically as version 2. Materials are referenced by handle and are not in the scene serialization, so every state hash is kept (the games suite and the provenance audit prove it). The kept golden scenes' materials and `GltfFixture.scene`'s canonical upgrade, planned for the recorded regeneration of `Golden.jsonl` at M23's landing, were brought forward to W23.2's integration by the lead, through FeatureTest's own recorded upgrade (decisions 17 and 21).

### 12. Determinism and the gate

- **Simulation:** nothing in C23.1 touches the simulation or the state hash: no component the audited projects hold changes (PointLight is not held), and materials are not hashed.
- **GPU images:** no atomic decides a sum or an order (the cluster masks have one writer per word; instancing keeps the draw order); the point-shadow kernel is fixed (plan §7 item 9).
- **Encoders:** byte-identical across the configurations of one compiler, never compared across compilers (plan §7 item 12; decision 13's check).
- **The gate:** the contract checkpoint runs the Debug build, CMP_Core's clang-cl Release and Dist builds in the portability workspace, `Lint.py --allow-contract-stubs`, `Format.py --check`, `CheckBuildConfig.py` on the workspace and on `FastMathCMPCore`, the touched unit cases in Debug, the TextureCompression cases in Release too (the determinism check), each skipped unit case forced with `--no-skip`, and the GPU cases its shared-struct change touches; never `PreCommit.py` or `CI.py`. M23's milestone end is phase 2's hardened gate (ADR 0028 decision 12).

### 13. BC compression through CMP_Core (R-TEXC-BC; vendored by the contract, stream X)

- **The pin:** Compressonator `V4.6.0` (commit `095a143ad8cfbf9ed5afa4ea63c735bfd0fd04dd`, 2026-10-19, released 2026-10-20), whose `cmp_core`, `applications/_libs/cmp_math` and `license` trees are identical to `V4.5.52`'s (2024-01-31); MIT. `Vendor/CMP_Core/VENDOR.md` records the 34 kept files with their SHA-256, the removed files and the build. The release's source archive is the whole repository (about 740 MB); the per-file hashes are the pin.
- **The build:** one static library of upstream's `CMP_Core` sources and its three SIMD libraries' sources; precise floating point without contraction (`PRECISE_VENDOR_PROJECTS`); under clang-cl, `-msse4.2` for every source, because under `_M_X64` the kernels' shared `cmp_floor` (`bcn_common_api.h`) and `core_simd_sse.cpp` use SSE4.1 intrinsics, which MSVC compiles without an option and Clang refuses without the target feature (the review's blocker; the engine needs SSE4.2 already, for Jolt); no defines (the `CMP_` family stays empty in every project, checked by `VENDOR_ABI_FAMILIES` and by Lint's `banned-abi-macro`); `Asset/TextureCompression.cpp` alone includes `cmp_core.h` (`ModuleRules.json`: Asset's private third party); every executable links it (`UseCMPCore()`, in `UseRuntimeDependencies()` too), since the Runtime's decode fallback uses it. It compiles without warnings in Debug and Release with MSVC, and in Release and Dist with clang-cl (the vendored code's warnings tolerated, as the portability stage tolerates them).
- **Process-level state:** CMP_Core selects the BC1 endpoint search's SIMD variant from the CPU on its first BC1 block and builds the BC7 ramp tables on first use, unsynchronized. `InitializeTextureCodecs` (`ProcessContext`'s `TextureCodecs` step, after Physics, its place in `ProcessContextStep`'s order) turns the SIMD selection off and builds the tables once, before any import worker exists, so every compression runs the scalar paths, whatever the CPU, and no two threads write that state (Architecture §3 rule 5 and §4.1 name it).
- **The codec** (`Asset/TextureCompression.h`, implemented by the contract): `CompressTextureBlocks` and `DecompressTextureBlocks` over whole levels for BC1, BC4, BC5, BC6H and BC7, the partial blocks padded by repeating the last column and row; fixed settings (quality 1.0 for BC1, BC4 and BC5, 0.1 for BC6H and BC7, BC6H unsigned); each call creates its own codec options.
- **The determinism check** (the adoption's condition, ADR 0028 decision 7, plan §6 "M23"): "TextureCompression: the fixture set compresses to the committed bytes in every configuration" compresses the six committed PNG textures of `Tests/Data/Assets/Textures` and four procedural RGBA8 levels (gradients with an alpha ramp, checkers with transparent columns, seeded noise, a 37 x 23 level) with BC1, BC4, BC5 and BC7, and three procedural RGBA16F levels (an HDR sky with a 3000-nit sun, seeded noise over six orders of magnitude, a 13 x 9 ramp) with BC6H: 30,800 bytes of blocks whose XXH64 is `0x74F5A212DFCCACAB` in Debug and in Release (MSVC 14.51). It passed in both.
- **BC3 failed the check, and BC2 and BC3 are left out.** The first fixture set held BC3 too: its digest differed between Debug and Release, in one block, the second block of `GreyAlpha.png` (Debug `...7def7def55555555`, Release `...7def000000000000`, the same colour encoded two ways). The cause is a read of uninitialized memory in CMP_Core: `CompressBlockBC1_RGBA_Internal` (`cmp_core/shaders/bcn_common_kernel.h`), BC3's colour fit, collects the block's texels at or above its alpha threshold (128, which BC3's options cannot change) into `dwBlk` and then sorts `dwBlk, dwBlk + 15` whatever their count, so a block with transparent texels sorts uninitialized stack entries. BC1 takes another path (`m_bUseAlpha` off) and passed. **Decision:** CMP_Core is adopted for the codecs that pass the check: BC1, BC4, BC5, BC6H and BC7. The plan's fallback ("if CMP_Core fails the check, bc7enc_rdo covers BC1–BC5 and BC7, and R-TEXC-HDR goes to the owner") is read per codec: BC7 holds every RGBA level at BC3's 16 bytes a block, so dropping BC2 and BC3 loses nothing the `Compression` rule uses, no fallback library is vendored, and R-TEXC-HDR stays in M23. Backlog §6 records the limit. **The lead accepted the per-codec reading** (2026-10-29, a ledger amendment); Risk 1 is the fallback should the owner overrule it.
- **`TextureFormat`, persisted (append only), frozen here, added by stream X:** `BC1Unorm = 4`, `BC1Srgb = 5`, `BC4Unorm = 6`, `BC5Unorm = 7`, `BC7Unorm = 8`, `BC7Srgb = 9` (`TextureFormatToString` spells them so); `GetTextureBytesPerPixel` is 0 for them and `GetTextureBlockCodec(TextureFormat) -> std::optional<TextureBlockCodec>` names their codec. BC6H is for environment cubes only (decision 14).
- **`TextureData` version 2** (`Data formats`) and **`TextureImporter` version 2:** `TextureImportSettings::Compression` (`TextureCompression {Auto, None}`, appended after `GenerateMips`, so an earlier `.meta` reads as Auto). Auto compresses every level of the mip chain (made in RGBA8 as today): Color as BC7Srgb; Linear as BC7Unorm, whatever its texels; NormalMap as BC5Unorm (x and y; decision 10's flag reconstructs z). None keeps version 1's output. A grey Linear texture is BC7 too, never BC4: BC4 samples as (r, 0, 0, 1) on the GPU and decodes so in the fallback (decision 15), and a Linear texture may be a metallic-roughness map, which `Scene.slang` reads from G (roughness) and B (metallic), so it must keep G = B = R as its None import does (the review). BC1 and BC4 are formats the payload, the upload and the fallback handle but Auto never chooses (BC1 halves BC7's size at a visible loss of colour quality; BC4 suits only data read from R); a later `Compression` value or usage may choose them. The glTF importer's texture sub-assets compress by their usage.
- **What is never compressed:** the built-in procedural textures and font atlases (generated, never imported); sprite, tile and UI art takes None (the ledger): M18's FeatureTest sprite-sheet textures get `Compression: None` in their scaffolds (stream F), and the lander applies None to M21's and M22's textures, scaffolds and creation paths on the merged tree. **Every sprite sheet's texture** (the lead, 2026-10-29): M23, phase 2's last lander, adapts its siblings' code on the merged tree, and its landing makes the sprite-sheet creation and editing paths there (M21's) set their texture's `Compression` to None, with a test, so a texture a user's sprite sheet references never cooks as BC; the game-building skill's texture row (stream F) says so.
- **The editor's texture thumbnails** (`Editor/Private/EditorHostThumbnails.cpp`, stream X, the lead's decision): built on the CPU from the texture's first level, which for a BC format holds blocks (`GetTextureBytesPerPixel` is 0), they decode a BC level with `DecompressTextureBlocks` first, BC4 and BC5 expanded as the cache's fallback expands them: "EditorHostThumbnails: a BC7 texture's preview shows its decoded texels" (unit).
- **The existing TextureImporter cases** pin version 1's RGBA8 output: stream X passes `Compression = None` to those that test decoding and mips, keeping each, and adds the Auto cases.
- **The pak:** BC data is stored when deflate saves less than an eighth (M19's rule, unchanged; the pak format is single-owner "none" in phase 2).

### 15. The decode fallback for a device without BC (stream X)

- **The device:** `GraphicsDevice` reads `VkPhysicalDeviceFeatures::textureCompressionBC`, enables it, and sets `GraphicsDeviceInfo::TextureCompressionBC` unless `GraphicsSpecification::DisableTextureCompressionBC` (no command-line option, like `DisableDepthClamp`); the device report lists the path.
- **The cache:** a BC texture on a device with BC uploads its blocks (`BC1_UNORM`, `BC1_UNORM_SRGB`, `BC4_UNORM`, `BC5_UNORM`, `BC7_UNORM`, `BC7_UNORM_SRGB`, `BC6H_UFLOAT` for cubes). Without BC, `DecompressTextureBlocks` decodes each level, and the mirror is `RGBA8_UNORM` (BC5, BC7Unorm, BC1Unorm), `SRGBA8_UNORM` (the sRGB formats), `R8_UNORM` (BC4) or, for BC6H cubes, `RGBA16_FLOAT`; one Info line per cache names the path. The mirror is never the placeholder for that reason.
- **Tests:** "GpuResourceCache: a device without BC support uploads a BC texture through the decode fallback" (GPU; a device made without the feature through `HeadlessGpuOptions::DisableTextureCompressionBC`), stream Y's "GpuResourceCache: a BC6H environment uploads as BC6H with BC support and through the decode fallback without it" (GPU: every face of a BC6H skybox decodes to `DecompressTextureBlocks`' texels and close to its source), and the loss test of decision 7. CMP_Core's decoders take any bytes without a fault ("TextureCompression: any block bytes decompress without a fault", live), and decode close to their source, BC6H's channels within a small relative error ("TextureCompression: decompressed levels keep their size and stay close to their source", live).

### 22. C23.3: what the contract freezes, implements and stubs, and the Animation module

C23.3 is W23.3's contract (plan §3.5 "M23": C-SKEL, C-MORPH and C-ANIM-BLEND): skeletal and morph animation with script-driven blending, root motion and animation events, without an animation state-machine graph (decision 26). It freezes the declarations under "Frozen interfaces (C23.3)", the formats under "Data formats (C23.3)" and the entries of decision 34; decisions 23 to 33 state the behaviour each stream implements.

### 24. The Skeleton and AnimationClip formats (version 1) and their fuzz targets (stream A)

### 26. Sampling, script-driven blending and root motion (the Animation module; stream B)

- **A layer's time** (`AdvanceAnimationTime`): advanced by the fixed delta times the layer's Speed; a looping layer wraps (time minus whole durations, computed in double), counting the ends it passes, at most `MaxAnimationWrapsPerStep` (8): the count saturates in double before its conversion, so even a clip whose cycle count would exceed 2^32 converts safely, and the layer's time stays exact while the events and root motion of the cycles beyond are dropped; another holds at its clip's end, `Ended` set in the step that reaches it.
- **Events** (`CollectAnimationEvents`): an event at e fires when the layer's time passes it, PreviousTime <= e < Time; a looping step that passes its end fires the rest of the cycle up to and including the clip's Duration, every event once for each whole cycle between, then the start of the next ([0, Time)), so an event at Duration fires at each end a looping layer passes, before the one at 0, as a held clip that reaches its end fires it. Equal times fire in clip order. A zero step fires nothing. At most (Wraps + 1) times the clip's events; the AnimationSystem delivers at most `MaxAnimationEventsPerStep` (256) per Animator a step (decision 29).
- **A pose** (`AnimationPose.h`): the Skeleton's rest pose; then each layer in order whose clip loads and whose Weight is above 0, the clip sampled at its time over the rest pose (`SampleAnimationClip`: glTF's interpolation, a Linear rotation by `SlerpQuaternion` along the shorter arc through DetMath's ACos and Sin, a CubicSpline rotation normalized) and blended over the result by the layer's weight (`BlendAnimationPose`: translation and scale linearly, rotation by normalized linear interpolation along the shorter arc; weight 1 copies exactly). Only the paths a layer's clip keys blend (`AnimationPose::Keyed`), so an upper-body clip over a walk leaves the legs walking. Morph channels blend the same way, by their Target path, over a base the AnimationSystem (and `PoseAnimator`) seeds: for each Target a loaded layer's clip keys, that entity's SkinnedMesh weights resolved against its mesh's defaults (`ResolveMorphWeights`, decision 27), so a morph layer at weight 0.5 moves half way from the authored weights to the clip's, as a joint blends from its rest pose (zeros where no SkinnedMesh or loaded mesh gives a base).
- **Script-driven, no state machine** (C-ANIM-BLEND; plan §6 "M24": "an animation state-machine graph is not built"): the layers are component data that scripts set (decision 31), and crossfades are scripts tweening weights.
- **Matrices:** `ComputeJointModelTransforms` (parent * local, parents first, in the Animator entity's space) and `ComputeSkinMatrices` (JointModel * InverseBind).
- **Root motion** (`RootMotion.h`): the planar motion of the skeleton's `RootMotionJoint` (its model translation's X and Z and its model rotation's twist about +Y, the swing-twist decomposition, sqrt only; the static joints above it hold their rest pose, so it is the joint's own keyed motion carried into the Animator entity's space), from the step's previous time to its time across the counted wraps; Apply and Extract hold it out of the pose (X, Z and the twist held at the clip's time-0 values, written back into the joint's local transform through its parent's model transform, so the mesh stays at its entity) and blend the layers' deltas as poses blend: `BlendRootMotion` takes the layer's keyed bits of the root-motion joint and blends only the paths its clip keys, so a layer whose clip keys neither contributes nothing (a turn layer keying only the rotation leaves the walk's translation whole).
- **Determinism** (lead note 2): the module uses only IEEE-exact operations, sqrt and DetMath, and is in the banned-API step's scope. **The frozen acceptance:** "AnimationPose: a sampled pose hashes to its committed digest in every configuration": the digest (`ComputeAnimationPoseDigest`, XXH64 of the joint model matrices and the morph weights) of a fixed two-layer pose, which stream B commits from Debug and confirms in Release; FeatureTest's Animation suite checks the digest of a sampled scene pose through `Test.GetPoseDigest` in the Editor, Release and Dist runs (decision 33), so Debug, Release and Dist give one value (Roadmap M23: "a sampled pose hashes equal in all three configurations").

### 27. Deformation: the Skinning pass, palettes and skinned previous positions (stream C)

- **One rule for morph weights** (C23.3's review): `ResolveMorphWeights` (`Asset/MeshDeform.h`, implemented by the contract with the live case "MeshDeform: a missing morph weight takes its target's default, and an extra one is dropped") gives one weight per morph target, the given weight where there is one and the target's `DefaultWeight` where there is none, an extra one dropped. Render extraction loads no mesh, so the defaults travel with the GPU mesh (`MeshDeformBounds::DefaultWeights`, in `GpuMesh::DeformBounds`): the Prepare stage resolves each item's `MorphWeights` against them before it culls the item and before the Skinning pass (whose `SkinningPassInputs::MorphWeights` holds exactly `MorphTargetCount` resolved weights), `ComputeDeformedBounds` and `DeformMeshVertices` resolve the same way, and so do EntityBounds and the wireframe. So the CPU reference, the pass and a SkinnedMesh added by hand with no weights agree, and a mesh without a SkinnedMesh draws at its targets' defaults (glTF's mesh weights). Weights are any finite value: a SkinnedMesh's own are in [-1, 1], but a clip's sampled weights may leave it (a cubic spline overshoots), so `ComputeDeformedBounds` widens each joint's box further by (|w| - 1) times the target's reach for every weight beyond [-1, 1].
- **The CPU reference** (`Asset/MeshDeform.h`, in Asset because Renderer and Scene both include it and neither includes Animation): morph first (position, normal and tangent deltas weighted in target order), then skin (the four influences' palette matrices blended by weight; position by the blended matrix, normal and tangent by its upper 3 x 3 then normalized; tangent sign and texture coordinates kept). Normals are not transformed by the inverse transpose, so a joint with a non-uniform scale shades approximately (an accepted limit, Backlog §6). `ComputeMeshDeformBounds` and `ComputeDeformedBounds` give conservative bounds (each joint's influenced-vertex box widened by the morph reach and carried by its palette matrix's eight corners; a skinned vertex is a convex combination of those points), with which the Prepare stage culls a deformed item and EntityBounds bounds it.
- **The snapshot** (`MeshDrawItem`, an existing member, as the ledger lets M23 change it): `JointPalette`, one matrix per joint of the driving Animator's pose, from the mesh's bind space into the item's local space at the rendered poses (inverse of the item's rendered world matrix, times the Animator entity's, times the joint's skin matrix), and `MorphWeights` (the SkinnedMesh's, each replaced by the pose's blended channel's weight at its index; possibly fewer than the mesh's targets, resolved by the renderer). An item is deformed when its mesh has a skin and the palette covers it, or has morph targets and a non-zero resolved weight; otherwise it draws the mesh's own vertices. A palette shorter than the skin draws the bind pose, logged once per renderer (`RenderSkinMismatchCode`, "RENDER_SKIN_MISMATCH", a log code only: the validation code of the cause is `ANIMATION_SKELETON_MISMATCH`, decision 30); `SceneRenderer::State::SkinMismatchLogged` is kept across a device loss's release and recreation with the other once-per-renderer flags (`SceneRendererRecovery.cpp`'s kept members, stream C; "SceneRenderer: the skin mismatch warning stays once per renderer across a release and recreation"). No member is added after `Output`, where M21 and M22 append theirs.
- **The pass** (`Renderer/SkinningPass.h`): Skinning, alone in the Deform stage (the declaration after Prepare; stats row "Skinning", after Prepare: 28 rows on this branch, the ledger's 31 on phase 2's merged tree), one compute pipeline of the program `Skinning` (`Passes/Skinning.slang`, sorted into `Shaders.json` by stream C), which stream C adds to the declaration with its `PipelineCount` 1 (60 startup pipelines). One dispatch per deformed item, one thread per vertex, each the only writer of its outputs (no atomic, plan §7 item 9), all recorded by one `Record` in one pass scope (W23.3's integration). Its output is a vertex buffer of `MeshVertex` layout, which every pass binds as vertex binding 0 instead of the mesh's (the attribute stream, binding 1, stays the mesh's), so **the 24 mesh pipelines stay** and no vertex layout changes (Roadmap M23).
- **Instancing:** each deformed item's draws carry a `VertexStream` of their own (`SceneRendererDraw::VertexStream`, `MeshInstanceKey::VertexStream`, decision 2's frozen member), unique per renderer, so a deformed draw never shares an instanced draw with another item's: skinned items are never instanced together, each having its own pose (two skinned items of one mesh and material are two forward draws, where the same two items at rest are one instanced draw).
- **Skinned previous positions** (plan §4.1: M26's frozen input for TAA's motion vectors): each dispatch first copies its vertex's position of the previous deformation into the item's previous positions (on its first deformation, the new position), so after a render they hold the deformed positions of the render before. M23 writes them and reads nothing; M26 reads them.
- **The GPU objects and the recreation protocol** (ADR 0029 decision 27's placement rule; lead note 2): each deformed item's vertices, previous positions, palette and morph weights are its SceneRenderer's (place 2, `SceneRenderer::State::DeformedMeshes`, keyed by entity and mesh, released at the end of a render that did not use them; the key is what carries the previous positions from one render to the next, so each deformed item of a snapshot has its own Entity: render extraction gives every item its entity, and a snapshot built by hand gives each deformed item one, as the GPU cases do), so a loss's release drops them and the next render deforms afresh; the Skinning pipeline is the pipeline set's (place 1, `SceneRendererPipelines::State::Skinning`, created by `CreatePipelines`); the meshes' skin and morph buffers are the cache's (place 3, `GpuMesh::SkinBuffer` and `MorphBuffer`). The loss test "SceneRenderer: a device loss recreates the deformed vertices, palettes and previous positions and renders the same image" goes through `Test::RunDeviceLossTest`.
- **"The deform equals the CPU reference"** (Roadmap M23): "SkinningPass: the deform equals the CPU reference, and the previous positions hold the last deformation" reads the pass's output back (`Test::ReadBufferBytes`, a test helper of stream C's) and compares it with `DeformMeshVertices` within 1e-4 per component (fused multiply-adds and the GPU's square roots), the previous positions exactly.
- **The wireframe overlay** (C23.3's review): `AppendWireframeOverlay` (`Renderer/EditorOverlay.cpp`, stream C) draws a deformed item's edges from `DeformMeshVertices` with its palette and resolved weights, culled by `ComputeDeformedBounds`, so the scene view's wireframe lies over a posed character, never its bind pose ("Wireframe: a deformed item's edges follow its palette and morph weights, culled by its deformed bounds").

### 29. The AnimationSystem phase, the sampled pose and scenes without animation (stream B)

- **The phase** (ADR 0028's phase-2 execution order): `PlaySessionPhase::AnimationSystem`, after NativeStep (and after M21's SpriteAnimation on the merged tree) and right before PreStepTransformUpdate, in Play and Simulate (a system, not a script). It runs after the tick's gameplay code, which starts clips and sets weights, so root motion reaches physics through the transform update, and a clip started in a contact callback or `OnDestroy` advances from the next tick. The live case "PlaySessionPhase: AnimationSystem has its enumerator name and follows NativeStep, before PreStepTransformUpdate" checks the order, never an adjacency a sibling's phase takes; stream B enters the phase and adds it to `PlaySessionTests.cpp`'s phase lists at its place.
- **The step** (`Scene/AnimationSystem.h`'s file comment), two passes, as the physics system steps and then dispatches: first, per Animator in canonical order (skipping an entity pending destruction or effectively disabled), the layers' times advance and are written back through the scene, the events they pass are collected (at most `MaxAnimationEventsPerStep`, 256, per Animator a step, the rest dropped with one Warn line naming the entity, once per entity), the pose is sampled and blended and written to the entity's runtime `AnimationPoseComponent` (joint model and skin matrices, morph weights, root motion, tick), and with Apply the Transform moves by the blended delta; then, after every Animator was stepped, the collected events reach the listener in (canonical entity, layer, firing) order. So every callback reads every Animator's new pose, what a callback changes (a clip started, an Animator destroyed, disabled or created) takes effect from the next tick whatever the entities' order, an event whose entity an earlier callback destroyed or disabled is dropped (as physics drops a callback whose Self is gone), and a callback's `Scene.CreateEntity` never invalidates the walk.
- **The session** creates the AnimationSystem right after the PhysicsSystem (and again at a scene transition, for the new scene), with its asset manager (`PlaySession::GetAnimation`), which may be null (W23.3's integration: a session without an asset manager gets a system that loads nothing, so nothing advances or is posed, and `GetAnimation` is never null). Its listener is not part of its specification, because the session creates its ScriptEngine after it and replaces that engine at a transition: `AnimationSystem::SetEventListener` sets the session's ScriptEngine wherever `PhysicsSystem::SetEventListener` sets it (`PrepareScripts`, after it creates the engine at the start, a `Scene.Load` or a `Scene.Reload`) and clears it wherever that one is cleared (the transition and `~PlaySession`, before `Scripts.reset()`), so events reach scripts from the session's first tick and after every transition, and never a destroyed engine ("ScriptAnimation: OnAnimationEvent reaches the session's scripts from its start and after Scene.Reload"); Simulate sets none.
- **The sampled pose is runtime state:** `AnimationPoseComponent` follows `RuntimeComponents.h`'s rules (never registered, serialized, hashed or seen by scripts and automation; no revision change), and is a pure function of the Animator's fields, the assets and the tick. Render extraction reads it for the palettes (decision 27), the joint query for scripts (`FindAnimatorJointWorldTransform`), Test.GetPoseDigest, and EntityBounds.
- **No hash block:** the state hash gains nothing (ADR 0028's ledger: "Sprite and skeletal animation keep their playback state in component data, which the serialization covers"): an Animator's times, a moved Transform and every script consequence of an event are in the scene's serialization.
- **Scenes without the new components keep their state hash** (lead note 2; M22's C22.2 review's lesson): a scene without an Animator or a SkinnedMesh gains no runtime component, writes nothing and hashes exactly as before: no stored state, no hash block, no phase work beyond two empty views (Animator and SkinnedMesh; W23.3's review). ## Data formats

- **`PointLight` version 2** (decision 3):

  ```json
  "PointLight": { "Color": [1, 0.5, 0.25], "Intensity": 20, "Range": 12, "CastShadows": false, "SourceRadius": 0.1 }
  ```

- **`MeshData` version 2** (the cooked payload, little-endian):

  ```
  uint32 VertexCount; uint32 IndexCount; uint32 SubmeshCount; uint32 SlotCount;
  uint32 Streams (MeshStreams bits: 1 Attributes; 2 Skin and 4 Morph reserved for C23.3, refused until then); uint32 Reserved (0; C23.3's);
  f32x3 BoundsMin; f32x3 BoundsMax;
  SubmeshCount x { uint32 IndexOffset; uint32 IndexCount; uint32 MaterialSlot; f32x3 Min; f32x3 Max; }
  SlotCount x { uint32 NameLength; UTF-8 Name; uint64 DefaultMaterial (0 = none); }
  VertexCount x MeshVertex (48 bytes); IndexCount x uint32
  [Streams & Attributes] VertexCount x MeshVertexAttributes { f32x2 TexCoord1; f32x4 Color; } (24 bytes)
  [Streams & Skin, Streams & Morph] C23.3's sections, in bit order
  ```

- **The material file, version 2** (decision 10), each UV set after its map:

  ```json
  { "Format": "Material", "Version": 2, "BaseColor": [1, 1, 1, 1], "BaseColorMap": "7e57000000000001", "BaseColorUVSet": "UV0",
    "Metallic": 1, "Roughness": 0.35, "MetallicRoughnessMap": "7e57000000000002", "MetallicRoughnessUVSet": "UV0",
    "NormalMap": "7e57000000000003", "NormalUVSet": "UV0", "NormalScale": 0.8, "OcclusionMap": "7e57000000000002", "OcclusionUVSet": "UV1",
    "OcclusionStrength": 0.6, "Emissive": [1, 0.5, 0], "EmissiveStrength": 4, "EmissiveMap": null, "EmissiveUVSet": "UV0", "AlphaMode": "Mask",
    "AlphaCutoff": 0.3, "DoubleSided": true, "UVScale": [2, 2], "UVOffset": [0.5, 0] }
  ```

- **`TextureData` version 2:** version 1's layout with the BC formats (decision 13); a BC level's size is `ComputeTextureBlockLevelSize`, its width and height any of 1 to 16384 (the mip rule unchanged), its texels the blocks row by row.
- **`EnvironmentData` version 2:** version 1's layout with a format byte and three reserved zero bytes after each cube's mip count (`uint32 FaceSize; uint32 MipCount; uint8 Format; uint8 Reserved[3]`), and a BC6H cube's levels as each face's blocks in face order.
- **`AudioClipData` version 2:** version 1's layout; `Encoding` 5 is Opus, holding the original Ogg Opus file.
- **`.meta` settings:** `TextureImportSettings {Usage, GenerateMips, Compression}`, `EnvironmentImportSettings {ClampLuminance, MaxLuminance, Compression}`; an absent `Compression` reads as Auto.

## Risks

1. **The per-codec reading of the fallback rule** (decision 13), which the lead accepted. Should the owner overrule it and read the rule as all-or-nothing, the codec moves to bc7enc_rdo for BC1 to BC7 and R-TEXC-HDR goes to the owner; `TextureCompression.h` is the only interface the importers and the cache call, so the swap stays inside its implementation and the committed digest.
2. **Six libraries in one milestone** (plan §8 risk 17): CMP_Core is in and checked; tinyexr, libogg, libopus and opusfile are pinned with their configurations stated. Their GCC, Clang and Apple Clang builds are never compiled (ADR 0028 decision 10).
3. **Opus determinism rests on the fixed-point build.** A float build or run-time CPU dispatch would make the samples depend on the configuration or the CPU; the digest test in Debug and Release and FeatureTest's Dist run catch it.
4. **Conservative clusters at slice boundaries.** The forward shader and the LightClusters pass compute a pixel's slice with the same formula on the same device; a margin in the cluster test absorbs the difference between a pixel's depth and its slice's bounds. A light missing from its cluster would break the byte equality, which the 256-light case checks.
5. **Instancing reorders the opaque pass.** The submesh joins the opaque order before the snapshot order; coplanar surfaces of one mesh and material drawn in another order could change a golden. The golden suite at W23.1's integration shows any change, which is then a reviewed regeneration named here.
6. **Three streams in the light loop and the set-0 layout.** Decision 4 gives the loop to C and the visibility to B, and the layout is shared by function; the integration joins the listed parts.
7. **The built-in environments as BC6H** change every golden lit by them; they are regenerated at W23.2's integration, and at the landing only where the merge changes them again (decision 17).
8. **The existing TextureImporter cases** pin RGBA8; stream X keeps each by passing None, so their coverage of decoding and mips stays.

## Data formats (C23.3)

- **Scene documents** (components version 1, in `BuiltinComponents` order after Script and after M21's and M22's categories on the merged tree):

  ```json
  "Animator": { "Skeleton": "5ce1e70000000001", "Layers": [
  	{ "Clip": "a41c000000000001", "Time": 0.25, "Speed": 1, "Weight": 1, "Loop": true },
  	{ "Clip": "a41c000000000002", "Time": 0, "Speed": 1.5, "Weight": 0.5, "Loop": false } ],
  	"Playing": true, "RootMotion": "Apply" },
  "SkinnedMesh": { "MorphWeights": [0.5, 0] }
  ```

- **`SkeletonData` version 1** (the cooked payload, little-endian, a string as a uint32 byte count then UTF-8):

  ```
  uint32 JointCount; uint32 RootMotionJoint;
  JointCount x { string Name; int32 Parent; f32x3 Translation; f32x4 Rotation (x, y, z, w); f32x3 Scale; f32x16 InverseBind (column-major) }
  ```

- **`AnimationClipData` version 1** (Duration 0 or in [`MinAnimationClipDuration`, a day]):

  ```
  f32 Duration; uint32 ChannelCount; uint32 EventCount;
  ChannelCount x { string Target; uint8 Path (Translation 0, Rotation 1, Scale 2, Weights 3); uint8 Interpolation (Step 0, Linear 1, CubicSpline 2);
                   uint16 Reserved (0); uint32 WeightCount; uint32 KeyCount; KeyCount x f32 Time; KeyCount x GetAnimationKeyFloatCount f32 Value }
  EventCount x { string Name; f32 Time; }
  ```

- **`MeshData` version 2's sections C23.1 reserved** (after the attribute stream, in bit order):

  ```
  [Streams & Skin]  uint32 SkinJointCount; VertexCount x MeshSkinVertex { u16x4 Joints; f32x4 Weights; } (24 bytes)
  [Streams & Morph] uint32 TargetCount; TargetCount x { string Name; f32 DefaultWeight; VertexCount x MeshMorphDelta { f32x3 Position; f32x3 Normal; f32x3 Tangent; } (36 bytes) }
  ```

- **The glTF `.meta` settings** (version 3, stream A): `GltfImportSettings {Scale, GenerateMissingTangents, ImportMaterials, MergeMeshes, AnimationEvents}`, an absent `AnimationEvents` reading as none:

  ```json
  "Settings": { "Scale": 1, "GenerateMissingTangents": true, "ImportMaterials": true, "MergeMeshes": false,
  	"AnimationEvents": [ { "Animation": "animation:0:Walk", "Name": "Footstep", "Time": 0.25 } ] }
  ```

- **The wire format of `animation.preview`** (decision 32): `{"entity": "/Hero", "time": 0.5, "playing": false}` returns `{"entity": {"id": ..., "name": "Hero", "path": "/Hero"}, "previewing": true, "time": 0.5, "playing": false, "duration": 1.2}`; `{"entity": "/Hero", "stop": true}` returns `"previewing": false`.

**The lead's decision on skins that share an Animator** (the open item of C23.3's fixes, 2026-11-10; binding): when two or more glTF skins resolve to the same Animator entity (common when an exporter gives each skinned mesh its own skin over shared joints), the importer merges them into one Skeleton asset: the union of their joints in canonical node order; each skinned mesh keeps its own inverse bind matrices, mapped into the merged skeleton's joint indices; one Animator drives every SkinnedMesh of that skeleton. Skins that resolve to different Animator entities stay separate skeletons. As built (`GltfImporter.h`'s version 3 paragraph, which states the rule):

- **The Skeleton** is keyed by the first of its skins (the lowest skin index, `skin:<index>[:<name>]`), its joints the union of the skins' joints and the static joints between them and the Animator's node, in canonical order; its `RootMotionJoint` is the first skin's first root joint.
- **Each mesh keeps its own inverse binds within the frozen formats.** A Skeleton has one inverse bind per joint and a mesh none (`MeshData` and the palette path are frozen), so the importer moves each skinned mesh into the Skeleton's bind space instead: a joint's inverse bind is the first listing skin's, a later skin that shares a joint with the earlier ones is carried into their bind space by its first shared joint's correction (the Skeleton's inverse bind's inverse times its own; the joints it adds take the same correction), and each vertex of a skinned mesh variant is moved by its heaviest joint's correction (the skin's inverse bind left-multiplied by the Skeleton's inverse), with its normal, tangent and morph deltas. When the skins share one bind pose (each skin's inverse binds are the joints' inverse bind transforms times its own mesh's placement, which is what exporters write) every correction of a skin is one matrix, so each mesh deforms exactly as its own skin deforms it in every pose ("GltfImporter: skins of one Animator merge into one Skeleton, and each mesh keeps its own inverse bind matrices": skin 1's mesh is authored one unit off and comes back by its inverse binds, and the case checks every weighted influence in a posed Skeleton against the mesh's own inverse binds). Skins bound in different poses of the shared joints give a vertex whose influences disagree its heaviest joint's correction, approximately: a limit for the Backlog (open item 3). No interface outside stream A's files changes.
