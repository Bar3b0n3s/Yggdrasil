## Context

World Tree's art pass needs per-entity overrides on a mesh's materials: hit flashes, tinted elites, breathing emission, scrolling lava, dissolving deaths and walls that fade while they hide the player (brief 5 and its addition). ADR 0042 decision 3 fixed the shape: a component of its own, the values in the instance record, simulation time plumbed into extraction, dissolve through the Mask variants with a built-in noise texture, and a dithered fade in the opaque path. The facts this record relies on, each checked in the code at `bb51e110`:

1. **The instance record** (`Resources/Shaders/Shared/InstanceRecord.h`) is 144 bytes: `World`, `PreviousWorld`, `EntityId`, `Flags` and two padding words, the std430 element of the per-view `Instances` buffer (t10 of set 0), read by the Scene, Shadow and SelectionMask programs' vertex shaders; the Terrain program declares it without reading it. Every pass writes its records with `MakeMeshInstanceRecord` (`Renderer/RenderPrepare.h`): the Prepare stage's camera draws, `ShadowPass::PrepareCasters` and `SelectionPass::PrepareDraws`. "Shaders: SharedStructsMatchReflection" compares it with the reflection member by member.
2. **Instanced draws** split by `MeshInstanceKey` (pipeline variant, material, mesh, submesh, vertex stream); the records are adjacent per run, so items that differ only in their records share a draw.
3. **The Mask variants** (the `ALPHA_MASK` permutation) of the prepass, the forward opaque pass and the shadow casters clip with `SV_Coverage` (no demote feature); the variant follows the material's `AlphaMode` (`Utils::GetForwardVariant`, `GetPrepassVariant`, `ShadowPass::PrepareCasters`), and the forward pass's `GreaterOrEqual` test against the prepass depth keeps exactly the prepass's surface because both evaluate the same coverage on bit-identical positions. Blend materials draw in the transparent pass and cast no shadow.
4. **The Scene program's material UV transform** is `uv * UVScale + UVOffset` (`GetMapTexCoord`), every map sampled with `AnisoWrap` (repeat); `MaterialConstants` carries `AlphaMode`.
5. **Set 0's registers** run to t14 (`ReflectionProbeCubesRegister`); the forward view layout is shared with the terrain's forward pipeline (`GetForwardViewLayout`, `TerrainPass`), and `ValidatePipelineLayout` requires every layout item to be declared by some entry of the pipeline's program.
6. **Render extraction** (`Scene/RenderExtraction.h`) has no time: `RenderExtractionRequest` carries the camera, size, `Alpha`, flags and quality. The play session builds its requests in `ExtractGameView` and `ExtractView` (`Session/PlaySession.cpp`), every other host (`FeatureTestRunner`, screenshots, the editor's views, the Runtime) reaching a play scene through `ExtractView`; `Tick` is the next tick, and `Time.GetTime` reads `Tick × FixedDelta`.
7. **Built-in textures** take 0x0181 to 0x018b of the textures block (0x0181 to 0x01c0); procedural ones are generated in integer arithmetic and served by both asset managers without a file; `TextureFormat::R8Unorm` exists (the blue noise).
8. **Colours are limited to [0, 1]** (ADR 0006); HDR emission comes from a strength (`MaterialData::EmissiveStrength`).
9. **Categories** end with Terrain: twelve categories, 52 components.

## Decisions

### 1. The component

`MaterialParameters` (`Scene/Components/MaterialParametersComponent.h`), version 1, in the category **MaterialParameters** (`Scene/Registration/MaterialParametersRegistration.cpp`, `RegisterMaterialParametersComponents`, appended after Terrain, the ledger's categories row), requiring `MeshRenderer`, flags the defaults (serializable, script-visible, editor-visible, removable). Its fields, in order:

| Field | Type | Default | Range | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Tint` | colour (vec3) | (1, 1, 1) | [0, 1] each | multiplies the base colour's RGB |
| `Emissive` | colour (vec3) | (1, 1, 1) | [0, 1] each | the added glow's colour |
| `EmissiveStrength` | float | 0 | [0, 1000] | the added emission is `Emissive` × this |
| `UVOffset` | vec2 | (0, 0) | [−1000, 1000] | added to every map's texture coordinates after the material's UV transform |
| `UVScroll` | vec2 | (0, 0) | [−1000, 1000], unit 1/s | texture units per second of simulation time, added to `UVOffset` |
| `DissolveAmount` | float | 0 | [0, 1] | the share of the surface dissolved |
| `DissolveEdgeColor` | colour (vec3) | (1, 0.5, 0.1) | [0, 1] each | the edge glow's colour |
| `DissolveEdgeStrength` | float | 2 | [0, 1000] | the edge's emission is `DissolveEdgeColor` × this |
| `DissolveEdgeWidth` | float | 0.05 | [0, 1] | the share of the noise above the amount that glows |
| `DissolveNoiseScale` | float | 1 | [0.01, 100] | the noise's repeats per unit of the first texture coordinates |
| `Opacity` | float | 1 | [0, 1] | the fade |

The defaults are neutral: they draw exactly as no component (decision 3). The fields are ordinary component data: serialized, hashed, edited by `entity.*` and the inspector, read and written by scripts through the generic proxy (`entity.MaterialParameters.Tint`), and animatable by graphs (`Get`/`Set.MaterialParameters.<Field>`). No migration, no validation code (the requirement is `COMPONENT_MISSING_REQUIREMENT`'s, the ranges the registry's).

**Departures from ADR 0042 decision 3, each with its reason.** The decision names "a tint, an emissive colour and strength, a texture-coordinate offset, a scroll velocity, an amount with an edge colour and width, an opacity". This record adds `DissolveEdgeStrength` (an edge colour limited to [0, 1] by ADR 0006 could not glow above the base without a strength, as the material's `EmissiveStrength` shows) and `DissolveNoiseScale` (one noise tiling cannot suit a pebble and a giant). `Tint` is RGB: the fade has its own field, and an alpha tint would only duplicate `Opacity` on Blend materials. `Emissive` defaults to white with `EmissiveStrength` 0, so a hit flash animates one number.

### 2. What each parameter does

Applied on top of every material of the item's submeshes, in the Scene program's fragment shaders (decision 5):
- **Tint** multiplies the base colour's RGB after the material's factor, its map and the vertex colour, so the albedo, the diffuse colour and a metal's F0 follow it (the prepass's `SceneRoughness` specular inputs too); the alpha is untouched.
- **Emission** (`Emissive` × `EmissiveStrength`) adds to the material's emission, in the same units.
- **The offset** (`UVOffset` + `UVScroll` × simulation time, decision 3) adds to every map's coordinates (both UV sets, all five maps) after the material's own `UVScale` and `UVOffset`, so a whole unit repeats the texture, in a Mask caster's alpha test too, so a scrolling cut-out's shadow holes move with it; it does not move the dissolve noise, which stays on the surface.
- **Dissolve** clips every fragment whose noise (decision 6), sampled at the mesh's first texture coordinates × `DissolveNoiseScale`, lies below `DissolveAmount` (and every fragment at 1); `DissolveEdgeColor` × `DissolveEdgeStrength` is added to the emission where the noise lies below `DissolveAmount` + `DissolveEdgeWidth` while `DissolveAmount` is above 0. The shadow casters clip the same fragments, so a dissolving entity's shadow dissolves with it.
- **Fade** (`Opacity` below 1) clips an Opaque or Mask material's fragments by a 4 × 4 ordered (Bayer) dither: a fragment is clipped when `Opacity` is below its pixel's threshold, so the share 1 − `Opacity` of the pixels opens (screen-door transparency). The threshold is the Bayer index plus one half over 16, moved by the frame's phase in TAA's 8-frame jitter cycle in whole eighths and wrapped into (0, 1): `frac((index + 0.5) / 16 + phase / 8)`, the phase `floor((Jitter.x + 0.5) × 8)` (`ViewConstants::Jitter`), the frame's index in the cycle, since Halton (2, 3)'s base-2 coordinate puts each of its eight frames in a different eighth. Over the cycle a pixel's threshold visits eight evenly spaced values, so every pixel is covered in floor or ceil of `Opacity` × 8 of the frames and TAA resolves a uniform fade; without TAA the jitter is 0 (phase 4) and the dither stands still. (W31.1 first moved the 4 × 4 pattern by `floor((Jitter + 0.5) × 4)`, whose eight offsets all have an odd sum, so its 2 × 2 level never moved and TAA kept a fixed pattern: the review's finding 1.) The faded entity keeps its depth where its pixels remain, its place in the opaque sort and its whole shadow (the casters ignore the fade), and never moves to the transparent list.
- **Blend materials** stay in the transparent pass: `Opacity` multiplies their alpha (a true fade, as they blend already), and their dissolved fragments take alpha 0, so they blend nothing; the edge still glows.
- **Skinned meshes** take their parameters like any item: a deformed item's draws read the same record.

### 3. Extraction and simulation time

- **The snapshot.** `MeshDrawItem::Parameters` (`Renderer/RenderSnapshot.h`'s `MeshMaterialParameters`: `Tint`, `Emissive`, `UVOffset`, `DissolveAmount`, `DissolveEdge`, `DissolveEdgeWidth`, `DissolveNoiseScale`, `Opacity`, with `IsNeutral()`), neutral by default.
- **The request.** `RenderExtractionRequest::SimulationTime` (double, seconds, finite and ≥ 0, else `InvalidArgument`), after `Alpha`.
- **The resolution.** `ResolveMaterialParameters(component, time)` (`Scene/RenderExtraction.h`, pure, IEEE-exact operations only): `Tint` and `Opacity` as they are, `Emissive` × `EmissiveStrength`, `UVOffset` + `UVScroll` × `time` in double precision wrapped into [0, 1) per component (`value − floor(value)`, and 0 for a result that rounds to 1 as a float), so an hour of play keeps the offset exact; while `DissolveAmount` is above 0 the edge (`DissolveEdgeColor` × `DissolveEdgeStrength`), its width and the noise scale, otherwise their neutral values. So the component's defaults resolve to the neutral parameters at any time, and an entity whose component holds them draws as one without it.
- **The session's time** (the ledger's "C31.1 states what an edit-time request passes"). The play session passes its rendered time, `(Tick − 1 + Alpha) × FixedDelta` (0 before its first tick): the time between its last two simulated states that the frame's alpha interpolates, equal to `Time.GetTime()`'s `Tick × FixedDelta` at alpha 1 (every lockstep frame, suite, replay and FeatureTest screenshot). Its game view (`ExtractGameView`) and every view it extracts between ticks (`ExtractView`: screenshots, the editor's views of a play scene, `FeatureTestRunner`, the Runtime) take it, so a scroll moves smoothly with the interpolated poses at any frame rate and stands still while the session is paused. This departs from the ledger's wording ("the session's tick times the fixed step", without the alpha) for smoothness at frame rates above the tick rate, the brief's "scrolls smoothly in play"; at alpha 1 the two agree. Every edit-time request (the editor's views of the edit scene, thumbnails, `lighting.bake`, screenshots and picks of the edit scene) passes 0: edit-time images show `UVOffset` alone and never move.
- **The world's clock** (batch 1's landing, with M35's world pause, ADR 0050; ADR 0042's execution-order rule: a system a sibling adds is classified at the later lander's merge). The scroll is the world's: the rendered time is the world's clock's, `(WorldTick − 1 + Alpha) × FixedDelta`, `WorldTick × FixedDelta` at any alpha while the last fixed step held the world (its poses stand still, and the alpha a free-running frame passes would otherwise move it), and 0 before the world's first tick (`PlaySession::State::GetRenderTime`). In a session that never paused the world's clock is the session's, so nothing above changes there; behind a world pause a scrolling surface holds with the bodies and particles, and after the resume it scrolls on from where it stood, every world tick counted once ("PlaySession: a world pause holds a scroll at the world's clock at any alpha, and the resume scrolls on from there").
- **Presentation only.** The scroll's time is the request's; nothing writes the component, so the state hash sees only the authored fields.

### 4. The instance record (208 bytes)

`InstanceRecord` grows from 144 to 208 bytes (std430, every `Float4x4` and `Float3` at a 16-byte boundary, the `Float2` at 8): `World` (0), `PreviousWorld` (64), `EntityId` (128), `Flags` (132), `Opacity` (136), `DissolveAmount` (140), `Tint` (144), `DissolveEdgeWidth` (156), `Emissive` (160), `DissolveNoiseScale` (172), `DissolveEdge` (176), `Padding0` (188), `UVOffset` (192), `Padding1` and `Padding2` (200, 204). `MakeMeshInstanceRecord` copies the item's parameters and sets **`DrawFlagMaterialParameters`** (8, `Shared/DrawConstants.h`) when they are not neutral; neutral parameters leave the record's members at their neutral defaults and the flag clear. The fragment shaders read the parameters only for an instance with the flag (decision 5), so an instance without it computes exactly what it did before M31. The keys of the opaque sort and of the instanced draws are unchanged: 200 instances with 200 tints are one instanced draw per pass, as 200 untinted ones are.

### 5. Pipelines and shaders

- **The variant choice.** `SelectMeshAlphaMode(material, parameters, shadowCaster)` (`Renderer/RenderPrepare.h`, pure): a Blend or Mask material keeps its mode; an Opaque material takes **Mask** while its item dissolves (`DissolveAmount` > 0) and, in the camera's passes only, while it fades (`Opacity` < 1). `SceneRenderer::State::PrepareDraws` takes the forward and prepass variants from it, `ShadowPass::PrepareCasters` its caster variant (binding the Opaque material's constants and base colour map as a Mask caster's). So no pass, pipeline or program is added: DepthNormal's 12, ForwardOpaque's 6 and Shadows' 6 pipelines stay, and the startup count stays 73 (the ledger's render-pass section).
- **The Mask variants test a material's alpha only for a Mask material** (`Material.AlphaMode == MaterialAlphaModeMask`), since an Opaque material now draws with them for its parameters; for a Mask material the test is the one it was.
- **Scene.slang** (M31's regions, the ledger's Scene.slang row): `VertexOutput` gains the record's index (`nointerpolation uint Instance`), set by `TransformVertex` for `VSMain`, `VSPrepass` and `VSPicking` (their positions unchanged); `EvaluateSurface` adds the offset to every map's coordinates (`GetSurfaceTexCoord`) and applies the tint, the emission, the edge and a Blend material's alpha under the flag; `GetCoverage` adds the dissolve's and the fade's clip under the flag in the Mask variants, evaluated before a Mask material's per-pixel alpha test and returned once, with the noise sampled at level 0 (`SampleLevel`), so no implicit derivative takes part and every pass reads the same noise (the review's finding 3). `PSPrepass`, `PSForward` and `PSPicking` evaluate the same coverage, so the picking prepass picks through a dissolved or faded entity's holes. `VSOverdraw` and `PSOverdraw` are unchanged.
- **Shadow.slang** (M31's addition to M29's file): the Mask variants carry the record's index to the fragment shader, sample a Mask material's alpha at the instance's `UVOffset` as the camera's passes do (the review's finding 2) and clip a dissolving instance's fragments by the same noise test, the noise at level 0; the Opaque variants are unchanged.
- **Selection.slang** reads only the record's `World` (its stride follows the shared header): the selection outline ignores the parameters, so a faded or dissolving mesh is outlined whole (decision 7).

### 6. The dissolve noise and its binding

- **The texture.** `engine://Textures/DissolveNoise` (`BuiltinAssetHandles::DissolveNoiseTexture`, **0x018c**, the ledger's built-in handles row; `BuiltinTexture::DissolveNoise`; `GenerateBuiltinDissolveNoiseTexture`), procedural, never compressed, a Procedural entry of `Resources/EngineAssets.json` (sorted by handle) and of `GetProceduralBuiltinEntries`: 128 × 128 `R8Unorm`, one level (the dissolve samples without mips, so a distant surface keeps its pattern rather than a grey mean), three octaves of tiling value noise (lattices of 4, 8 and 16 cells a side, each wrapping, weighted 4, 2 and 1, an integer hash per lattice point and a fixed-point smoothstep blend), equalized by rank (ties by texel index) so that its 16,384 texels take the values 1 to 254 in equal shares (64 or 65 each): a dissolve of amount a removes about the share a of a surface, a linear blend of texels never reaches 0 or 1, so 0 removes nothing and 1 everything. Integer arithmetic only, so its bytes are identical in every configuration. Not a reader of external input: no fuzz target (the ledger's fuzz row).
- **The binding.** `t16` (`Utils::DissolveNoiseRegister`, the next register after main's, the ledger's ForwardPipelines row: `t15` on M31's branch, `t16` since batch 1's landing, where M29's `PointShadowMapsHalf` had taken `t15`) in the prepass and forward set-0 layouts and in the Shadow program's Mask set-0 layout, sampled with `s2 AnisoWrap`. The Terrain program declares it unread beside its `Instances` declaration, since it shares the forward set (ADR 0042's Terrain.slang row as corrected on main `77d42dd0`: "M31 one unread declaration of its noise texture beside the Instances buffer's").
- **What is bound.** The Prepare stage binds the noise's mirror (`GpuResourceCache::GetTexture`; a procedural built-in, which every asset manager serves without a file) only while an item of the snapshot dissolves; otherwise, and when the mirror is a placeholder or missing, it binds the renderer's neutral white R8 texture (`SceneRendererPipelines::WhiteAo`): unread without a dissolve, so a snapshot without one never asks the asset manager for the noise; with a dissolve and a placeholder or missing mirror, only an amount of 1 dissolves anything. `ShadowRenderInputs::DissolveNoise` hands the shadow pass the same texture; null binds the shadow pass's own 1 × 1 white `R8_UNORM` texture (`ShadowPass::State::NeutralNoise`, created with the pass's pipelines, so it is recreated with them after a device loss), under which only an amount of 1 dissolves anything. The Runtime needs no pak entry: procedural built-ins are served without a file.

### 7. What does not take the parameters

The selection outline (decision 5), the Overdraw data view (it counts the material's own fragments), terrains, sprites, tilemaps, particles, text and the UI. These are accepted limits (`Docs/Backlog.md` §6). The picking prepass takes them (decision 5).

### 8. The script and automation surface

No namespace, method, MCP tool, validation code, setting or `stats.get` row: the component's fields go through the generic component proxies (§11.4), `entity.*`, `component.schema` and the inspector, and instanced draws already show in the passes' draw counts (the ledger's script-API, methods and `stats.get` rows). The generated references (`Docs/Reference/*`, `Resources/Scripting/Engine.d.luau`, `Tools/MCP/catalog.json`) gain the component. Parity: `Tests/Automation/test_material_parameters.py` drives the component through the entity methods (create, read, merge-patch update, undo, the ranges and the requirement refused) and, in a rendering editor, shows 200 tinted cubes drawing in one `ForwardOpaque` draw through `stats.get`.

### 10. Determinism

The component is component data: serialized and hashed like any component's, absent from every existing scene, prefab and replay, so no committed hash moves and no demo changes. No state-hash block, no phase (the ledger's execution order: "M31 takes none"). The scroll's offset, the noise and the dither are presentation, computed from the request and the record; the noise's bytes are integer-generated. A scene without the component draws through exactly the shaders' pre-M31 expressions (decision 4's flag), so every existing golden and FeatureTest's byte-compared probe bakes stay as they were.

## Risks

- **Byte identity without the component.** Growing the record and adding instructions must not move a pixel of an existing scene: the parameters are read only under `DrawFlagMaterialParameters`, the coverage of a Mask material is the test it was, and the goldens and FeatureTest's byte-compared probe bakes are the check.
- **The fade's dither under post-processing.** GTAO and SSR read the dithered prepass depth and normals, so a faded wall's ambient occlusion and reflections are noisy until TAA resolves them over its 8-frame cycle, and stay noisy without TAA; accepted (Backlog §6).
- **M29's static shadow cache** (the review's finding 4). M29 keys each cached point-light layer group and the spot atlas on a caster signature (world, mesh, submesh, both variants, a Mask caster's material handle and generation, the deformation), which holds no instance record. After the batch landing a dissolving entity takes the Mask variant once (hashed), but as its `DissolveAmount` rises nothing in the signature would change, so its point and spot shadows would freeze at the first dissolving frame.
