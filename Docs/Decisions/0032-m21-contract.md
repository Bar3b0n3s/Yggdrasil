The facts this contract relies on, checked in the code at the base:

1. **The Sprites stage exists and records nothing.** `SceneRenderer::State::RecordSpritesStage` (`Engine/Source/Engine/Renderer/SceneRenderer.cpp:1407`) is M18's reserved stage between Forward and HdrPost; the QuadBatcher's declaration sits in it with its two pipelines and no stats row (`Renderer/RenderPassDeclaration.h`), and its Hdr pipeline draws into SceneColor depth-tested `GreaterOrEqual` against SceneDepth without writing depth (`Renderer/QuadBatcher.h`). The 2D colour path (`RenderOutputSettings::PixelArt`) leaves a texel drawn into SceneColor unchanged (ADR 0029 decision 7).
2. **The output stage is driven by the snapshot.** `RenderOutputSettings` (`Renderer/RenderSnapshot.h`) sets the scene resolution, the pixel-art colour path and the letterbox; M18 set it from tests only and said "M21's PixelPerfectCamera sets it from the scene through render extraction" (ADR 0029 decision 5). Render extraction is a pure function of the scene and the request, with no asset access (`Scene/RenderExtraction.h`).
3. **The SpriteSheet asset carries clips but nothing plays them.** `SpriteSheetData` (`Asset/SpriteSheetData.h`) holds frames (texel rectangles, pivots, 9-slice borders) and clips (`{Frame, Ticks}` steps with `Loop`); frames are identified by index and clips by name; there is no placeholder sheet. M18's inspector shows nothing for a sheet (Backlog §2's row, scheduled for M21 W21.1; ADR 0029 decision 18).
4. **Plane locks are Jolt's.** `PhysicsDofs` (`Physics/PhysicsTypes.h:65`) maps bit for bit to Jolt's `EAllowedDOFs` (`Physics/PhysicsWorld.cpp:46-49`), and `BodyDescription::AllowedDofs` acts on Dynamic bodies. The shape variant (`Physics/PhysicsShape.h`, frozen by M11) has boxes, spheres, capsules, convex hulls, meshes and shared shapes: a circle is a sphere and a box or polygon an extruded box or hull, with no change to it.
5. **Events have one canonical key.** `PhysicsEventOrderKey` (`Scene/PhysicsSystem.h`, ADR 0031 decision 2) sorts pair events by (Low, High, Type, Collider), and its comment already names M21's 2D world as a user. The ScriptEngine is the 3D system's `IPhysicsEventListener` and delivers each event to every slot of the receiving entity in slot order (ADR 0031 decision 17).
6. **The state hash has room after the native block.** `PlaySession::ComputeStateHash` (`Session/PlaySession.cpp`) hashes the scene's canonical text, the tick, the Random state and the id draws, then `PhysicsSystem::AppendStateHash` (empty without bodies), then M19's native block (empty without a module).
7. **The editor picks meshes on the GPU.** The scene view's click selection reads the picking prepass's EntityId at the clicked scene pixel (`SceneRenderer::RequestPick`), which only meshes write; `viewport.pick` casts a CPU ray against mesh triangles (`Scene/SceneRaycast.h`). A drop of a mesh onto the scene view is an `entity.create` (`Editor/Panels/SceneViewportPanel.cpp`, `AcceptAssetDrop`).
8. **Screenshots build their own extraction request.** `viewport.screenshot` copies the project's rendering quality into its `RenderExtractionRequest` (`Engine/Source/Engine/Automation/Methods/ScreenshotMethods.cpp:195`), a file no phase-2 milestone may change without a ledger amendment (ADR 0028's phase-2 ledger, "Single-owner files"); main granted M21 its one statement there on 2026-10-29 (`5092c00`, decision 16). The editor's scene and game views build theirs in `Editor/Source/Editor/Private/EditorHostViewports.cpp` (`EditorHostViewports::Extract`), M21's own file in the ledger, and pass it unchanged to `PlaySession::ExtractView` while playing.
9. **Components are registered in categories.** `BuiltinComponents` (`Scene/Components/BuiltinComponents.h`) lists Core, Rendering, Physics, Audio and Scripting; the ledger puts a milestone's new components in categories of its own after Scripting. No audited project (the demos, FeatureTest's kept files) holds a 2D component.

## Decisions

### 1. What the contract freezes, implements and stubs

The contract freezes the C++ declarations under "Frozen interfaces", the data formats under "Data formats" and the registry and ledger entries of decision 16; this record's decisions state the behaviour each stream implements.

- **C++ stubs:** every new function whose behaviour a stream implements starts with `ENGINE_CONTRACT_STUB();`. A stub returning `Result` or `Status` returns `Unsupported` naming this record, its decision and its stream; any other returns an empty or neutral value (ADR 0024 decision 1). Nothing calls a stub outside its skipped tests: no host creates the 2D systems, enters the new phases or registers the new methods, so the tree behaves as at the base.
- **Implemented by the contract** (inert unless used): the seven 2D components, registered (decision 2); `SpriteItem` and `RenderSnapshot::Sprites` with the snapshot's memory resource; the `Sprites` pass declaration and stats row; `BuiltinTexture`'s four sprite values with their names and `BuiltinAssetHandles`' four handles; the four `PlaySessionPhase` values with their names; `PlaySession::GetPhysics2D` (null until stream P2 creates the system) and `IScriptHost::GetPhysics2D` (a default returning null, which the play session's and the script test fixture's overrides replace; decision 10); `RenderExtractionRequest::SortingLayers`; the unregistered `SortingLayersSettings` and `Physics2DSettings` members of `ProjectSettings`; `EditorViewportOptions::Mode2D`; the `physics2d.*` wire types and their registration functions, written but not called; the validation-code constants; the accessors and constructors of the new classes.
- **Acceptance tests:** final names, marked `doctest::skip(true)` (C++) or `unittest.skip` with a "contract stub" reason (Python); each fails when forced against the contract tree (checked with `--no-skip`), because it calls a stub or asserts behaviour that does not exist yet. The tests of what the contract implements are live (decision 18).
- **Counts:** 89 stubs (`Physics2DWorld` and its four free functions 39, `Physics2DSystem` 31, `SpriteAnimationSystem` and `AdvanceSpriteAnimator` 4, `SpriteGeometry` 2, `ScriptEngine`'s 2D listener 2, the `physics2d.*` handlers 2, `EditorCamera` 2, `SpriteDrop` 2, and one each for `GenerateBuiltinSpriteTexture`, `ComputePixelPerfectView`, `PickSprite`, `FindSpriteSceneIssues` and `ValidateScenePhysics2D`; listed under "Frozen interfaces"), 36 skipped C++ cases and 6 skipped Python cases (listed under "Acceptance (W21.1)"), and 5 new live cases besides the existing cases the registration and the pass declaration extend (decision 18).

### 3. Sprites: the SpriteRenderer component and the sprite geometry (R-2D-SPRITE; stream A)

- **SpriteRenderer** (`Scene/Components/SpriteRendererComponent.h`): `{Sheet, Frame, Texture, Color, FlipX, FlipY, PixelsPerUnit, SortingLayer, OrderInLayer, Lit}`, requires Transform. The image is frame `Frame` of `Sheet`, or with a null `Sheet` the whole of `Texture` pivoted at its centre, or with neither the built-in white square (decision 7), as a null Text font is the default font.
- **What is missing, one rule** for the Sprites stage, picking (decision 14), bounds (`EntityBounds`) and the editor, stated in `SpriteRendererComponent.h`, `SpriteItem`, `SpritePicking.h` and `EntityBounds.h`: a texture that is missing or fails to load, `Texture` or the sheet's own, is replaced by its placeholder, the Missing checker (64 x 64 texels), shown whole over the sprite's quad: the frame's quad with a sheet (its texel rectangle and pivot are the sheet's, whatever the texture), the placeholder's own 64 x 64 texels centred with `Texture`. That quad is drawn, picked and bounded. A sheet that is missing or fails to load (sheets have no placeholder, fact 3) and a frame index the sheet lacks (`SPRITE_FRAME_OUT_OF_RANGE`) give no quad: the sprite draws nothing, is never picked and adds no bounds.
- **The size is texels over PixelsPerUnit,** in the entity's local units (default 16, so a 16-texel built-in shape is one unit), and the entity's scale applies. A sheet carries no pixels-per-unit (its format is frozen at version 1), and a sprite size in units would let a sprite and its camera disagree; the PixelPerfectCamera carries the same field (decision 6), so equal values map one texel to one scene pixel.
- **`Lit`** is C21.2's flag (sprites stay unlit by default and opt in to 2D lights, plan §6 "M21"), registered now so the component never needs a migration for it; it has no effect until W21.2.
- **The geometry** (`Asset/SpriteGeometry.h`): `GetSpriteFrameGeometry` (the frame's texel rectangle and pivot, or the whole texture centred) and `ComputeSpriteLocalQuad` (the corners in the entity's local XY plane, +Y up while texels run down, the pivot at the origin, flips mirroring about it, the texture rectangle as texel edges). The Sprites stage, sprite bounds (`EntityBounds.cpp`) and 2D picking (decision 14) use it, so what is drawn is what is bounded and picked. It lives in Asset, beside `SpriteSheetData`, because both Renderer and Scene include Asset and neither includes the other (§3).

### 4. Sorting, the snapshot and the Sprites pass (R-2D-SPRITE, R-2D-SORT; stream A)

- **`SpriteItem`** (`Renderer/RenderSnapshot.h`): `{World, Sheet, Frame, Texture, Color, FlipX, FlipY, PixelsPerUnit, SortingLayer, OrderInLayer, Lit, Entity}`; `RenderSnapshot::Sprites` follows `Output`, as the ledger places phase 2's members, and allocates from the snapshot's memory resource. Extraction never touches assets (fact 2): the renderer resolves the frame and the texture through its AssetManager and GpuResourceCache, as it resolves meshes.
- **Draw order:** extraction lists every effectively enabled SpriteRenderer, sorted stably by (sorting layer index, OrderInLayer) from canonical order, so ties keep canonical order and the order is deterministic. The renderer draws the vector in its order, back to front, without writing depth (painter's order), depth-tested against the opaque meshes (fact 1). A distance-based order is not used: an orthographic 2D scene has equal depths, and a script that wants y-sorting sets OrderInLayer.
- **Sorting layers** are names in the project's new settings section `SortingLayers` (`SortingLayersSettings::Layers`: 1 to 32 unique non-empty names, the first `Default`, as `PhysicsSettings.Layers`), appended after Testing (ADR 0028's ledger). A sprite's layer is its name's index there; an undeclared name sorts as layer 0 and is `SPRITE_UNKNOWN_SORTING_LAYER`. Hosts pass the list in `RenderExtractionRequest::SortingLayers`, as they copy the rendering quality: the play session's game view, the FeatureTest runner, the Runtime, the editor's thumbnails and `viewport.screenshot` (stream A; the one statement in `ScreenshotMethods.cpp` that main's ledger amendment grants, decision 16), and the editor's scene and game views in edit and in play (`EditorHostViewports::Extract`, stream D, which also passes the list in the `SpritePickRequest` of its click selection, as `viewport.pick` does in stream A's `ViewportPickMethods.cpp`). "ScreenshotMethods: viewport.screenshot extracts sprites in the project's sorting layers" and "EditorHostViewports: scene and game extraction carry the project's sorting layers" prove those two hosts with two sprites whose layer order contradicts both their OrderInLayer and their canonical order, as `test_the_2d_mode_looks_down_z_and_picks_the_topmost_sprite` proves `viewport.pick`; the settings observation test ("ProjectSettings: every AllSettings.eproj value is observed by its consumer", gate 8) observes the section in the play session's own extraction once stream A registers it.
- **The pass:** the declaration `Sprites` (stage Sprites, no pipelines of its own, stats row `Sprites`) follows the QuadBatcher's in `ScenePassDeclarations`, so the stats order gains `Sprites` between `Overdraw` and `Bloom` (26 rows; C21.2's `Lights2D` follows it, 31 at phase 2's end by the ledger). `RecordSpritesStage` turns each item into one QuadBatcher quad in world space (the camera's view-projection as the batch transform, the Point sampler, the frame's texture rectangle, `Color` as the quad colour) and records them with the Hdr pipeline into SceneColor under the `Sprites` row, in a lit view only (the data views and the Overdraw view draw no sprites). The QuadBatcher splits draws where the texture changes, so a sheet's sprites batch.
- **Recovery:** the stage keeps its binding sets in a `SceneRenderer::State` `PassBindingCache` (place 2 of ADR 0029 decision 27), and the textures are GpuResourceCache mirrors (place 3), so the recreation protocol covers the sprites without hooks of their own; "Sprites: a renderer drawing sprites recovers from a device loss with the same image" proves it through the shared helper (plan §7 item 3).

### 5. The SpriteAnimator and the SpriteAnimation phase (R-2D-ANIM; stream B)

- **SpriteAnimator** (`Scene/Components/SpriteAnimatorComponent.h`): `{Clip, Playing, Step, StepTicks}`, requires SpriteRenderer, and plays a clip of the SpriteRenderer's sheet. The playback state is component data, so the scene's serialization and the state hash cover it and a replay reproduces every frame (ADR 0028's ledger, "State-hash blocks").
- **The phase** `PlaySessionPhase::SpriteAnimation` runs after NativeStep and before PreStepTransformUpdate (ADR 0028's phase-2 execution order), in Play and Simulate alike, since it is a system and not a script (`Scene/SpriteAnimationSystem.h`). For every effectively enabled entity with both components, in canonical order, a playing animator whose clip the sheet has advances one tick (`AdvanceSpriteAnimator`): the current step (clamped to the clip's last) has its frame written to `SpriteRenderer.Frame`, then the step's tick count advances, and when it reaches or passes the step's Ticks the next step begins; past the last step a looping clip restarts and a held one stops on its last frame and clears `Playing`. `Step` and `StepTicks` are fields, which `entity.update`, a scene file or a `Clip` written without `SpriteAnimator:Play` can leave from another clip, so a `StepTicks` at or past the step's Ticks shows that step for one tick, then moves on. So a step shows its frame for exactly its Ticks ticks, and a clip started in `OnFixedUpdate` shows its first step at that tick, while one started in a contact callback or `OnDestroy` advances from the next tick (the ledger's rule).
- **An unknown clip** (or a sheet missing or failing to load) changes nothing and is `SPRITE_UNKNOWN_CLIP`, logged once per entity and clip name at run time.
- **No speed field:** clips state their timing in ticks (fact 3), so a speed would make a fractional tick count; a script that wants another speed plays another clip.

### 6. The PixelPerfectCamera (R-2D-CAM; stream C)

- **PixelPerfectCamera** (`Scene/Components/PixelPerfectCameraComponent.h`): `{ReferenceWidth, ReferenceHeight, PixelsPerUnit, LetterboxColor}`, requires Camera.
- **It drives M18's output stage** through render extraction and changes no output-stage code (ADR 0028's ledger): for the Primary and Entity camera sources whose entity holds one (never the editor's Explicit camera), the snapshot's `Output` is `{ReferenceWidth, ReferenceHeight, PixelArt: true, LetterboxColor}`, the projection is orthographic with half height `ReferenceHeight / (2 PixelsPerUnit)` at the reference aspect whatever the Camera's own projection, and the camera's position is snapped so the view's left and bottom edges lie on the pixel grid (`ComputePixelPerfectView`, `Scene/PixelPerfectCamera.h`). The integer scale is the output mapping's, the largest that fits the view (ADR 0029 decision 6), never a field: "reference resolution and integer scale" are the component's resolution and the mapping's scale.
- **Pointers and rays** in view coordinates go through the output mapping first (`MapViewPointToScenePoint`), so `Camera:ScreenToWorldRay`, `WorldToScreen` and the game view's pointer agree with the image under a PixelPerfectCamera; stream C changes the Camera proxy's methods (`Bindings/Components.cpp`) by that rule.

### 7. The built-in sprite textures (stream B)

The four procedural textures of the ledger's handles, `engine://Textures/SpriteSquare` (0x0187), `SpriteRounded` (0x0188), `SpriteBevel` (0x0189) and `SpriteCircle` (0x018a), are `BuiltinTexture` values generated by `GenerateBuiltinSpriteTexture` (`Asset/BuiltinTextures.h`): 16 x 16 RGBA8Srgb white shapes of one level, transparent texels `(255, 255, 255, 0)`, a sprite's `Color` tinting them, defined texel by texel in the enumerators' comments and generated in integer arithmetic, so their bytes are identical in every configuration. They are never compressed (ADR 0028's ledger, "Texture compression"). Stream B adds their catalogue entries (`Resources/EngineAssets.json`, kept sorted by handle, and the compiled-in procedural entries); no CC0 pack is vendored (plan §6 "M21").

### 8. The 2D world on Jolt (R-2D-PHYS; ADR 0028 decision 6; stream P1)

`Physics2DWorld` (`Physics/Physics2DWorld.h`) is the interface ADR 0028 decision 6 names: a second Jolt world (a `PhysicsWorld` it owns), whose Dynamic bodies allow only X and Y translation and Z rotation (`Physics2DDofs`; `Physics2DTranslationDofs` with FixedRotation), whose shapes are the existing variant laid in the XY plane (`MakeBody2DShape`: a circle is a sphere; a box is a box and a polygon the hull of its points, each extruded along Z on both sides by its own half depth, `GetPhysics2DHalfDepth`), and whose API speaks 2D (`Physics2DPose`, planar vectors, a float angular velocity). Every body lies on the plane z = 0 and nothing it is handed moves one off it. No library is added (Box2D is won't-do, ADR 0028 decision 2), the frozen `PhysicsShape.h` does not change, and the 3D `PhysicsWorld` keeps every result: the 2D world is a separate instance.

- **The depth rule.** A box or polygon is extruded by the diagonal of its bounding rectangle after the collider's scale, twice the radius of a circle enclosing it; a circle's half depth is its sphere's radius. Every body lies on z = 0, so two shapes overlap along Z by the sum of their half depths while their overlap in the plane never exceeds the sum of their enclosing radii: the overlap along Z always exceeds the deepest overlap the pair can have in the plane, so Jolt's minimum-penetration axis, the contact normal, lies in the plane however deep the overlap. A fixed depth (0.5 m, the contract's first text) made Z that axis for a pair overlapping by more than 1 m in the plane (a body teleported into a wall, a fast ball one step deep into it), and a normal along the locked Z pushes nothing, so the pair stayed embedded or sank; it also made the mass properties' isotropic floor (`Physics/Private/PhysicsMassProperties.cpp`: a determinant below 10^-3 of the trace cubed) add up to 63% to the inertia about Z of a box under about 6 cm. With the rule the conditioning does not depend on the size, and a box's or hull's inertia about Z, which its depth does not change (m (w^2 + h^2) / 12 for a box), is its 2D one. The depth counts against `PhysicsShape::MaxColliderSize`, so a box or polygon whose bounding rectangle's diagonal exceeds 50 km is refused (the 3D world takes 100 km): an accepted limit (Backlog §6). "Physics2DWorld: a body sunk deep into a box is pushed out in the plane" and "Physics2DWorld: a small body's angular acceleration under a torque follows its 2D inertia" prove it.
- **The plan's risk** ("shapes extruded in Z must not produce out-of-plane forces", §8 risk 6) is met by the locks and the depth rule: Jolt zeroes the locked velocity components every step, so a body's Z and its rotation's X and Y stay exactly zero, and no contact normal leaves the plane. "Physics2DWorld: a dynamic body never leaves its plane" checks the first bit for bit on a pile after 600 steps; the deep-overlap case checks the second.
- **Circles and polygons.** A circle is a sphere, so its inertia is 0.4 m r^2, not a disc's 0.5 m r^2: a torque turns it 25% faster than a 2D engine's circle (the torque case pins it). A polygon is Jolt's convex hull shape, whose corners Jolt rounds by its default convex radius (up to 0.05 m, less on a small hull) for contacts and overlaps, while rays meet the sharp hull and a box's corners stay sharp (`PhysicsShape.cpp`, §9.2 "Seams"); the frozen `PhysicsShape.h` cannot set the radius, so a square PolygonCollider2D lets a box slide off its corner earlier than an equal BoxCollider2D, almost a pixel at 16 pixels per unit: an accepted limit (Backlog §6), stated in `PolygonCollider2DComponent.h`.
- **Queries and seams.** `Overlap` takes an `Overlap2DShape`, a box or a circle, the shapes `Physics2D.OverlapBox` and `OverlapCircle` and `physics2d.overlap` use, laid as a box and a sphere, which `PhysicsWorld::Overlap` accepts; a polygon query would need a hull query in `PhysicsWorld`, which no caller needs. `Body2DDescription::EnhancedInternalEdgeRemoval` passes RigidBody2D's flag (decision 9) to `BodyDescription`, so a body rolling or sliding across the merged boxes of one compound (C21.2's tilemap collider) crosses their seams as a 3D body does (§9.2 "Seams").

### 9. The 2D components and the Physics2DSystem (R-2D-PHYS; stream P2)

- **New components, not new fields** (the contract decides them, preferring new components to new fields on existing ones, plan §7 item 6): RigidBody2D `{Type, Mass, Friction, Restitution, LinearDamping, AngularDamping, GravityFactor, MotionQuality, AllowSleeping, FixedRotation, Layer, MaxLinearVelocity, MaxAngularVelocity, EnhancedInternalEdgeRemoval, InitialLinearVelocity (vec2), InitialAngularVelocity}` (EnhancedInternalEdgeRemoval is RigidBody's, registered now so W21.2's tilemap collider needs no change to a frozen component), BoxCollider2D `{HalfExtents (vec2), Offset (vec2), Angle, IsTrigger}`, CircleCollider2D `{Radius, Offset, IsTrigger}` and PolygonCollider2D `{Points (3 to 64 vec2), Offset, Angle, IsTrigger}`, each requiring Transform, with RigidBody's bounds and its `BodyType` and `MotionQuality` enums. Angles are degrees about +Z (counter-clockwise seen from +Z), angular velocities radians per second, as the 3D components'.
- **One world per entity is a validation error, never a registry exclusion:** an entity with a 2D and a 3D physics component is `PHYSICS2D_MIXED_WORLDS`; it keeps its 3D body exactly as without its 2D components, and its 2D components make no 2D body, so the 3D world is bit-identical (Breakout's replay).
- **The system** (`Scene/Physics2DSystem.h`) is `PhysicsSystem`'s design in the plane, rule for rule (composition with RigidBody2D in RigidBody's place, rebuilds, pairs across rebuilds, kinematic moves, teleports, write-back, M20's pending enter and collider events, the flushes, the diagnostics, queries sorted by distance then UUID). A body's pose is its owner's world X and Y and the twist about Z of its world rotation; an owner whose rotation is not about Z alone is `PHYSICS2D_OFF_PLANE` (Warning) and takes the twist. The write-back keeps the entity's world Z (free for draw depth) and replaces a swing by none.
- **Layers** are the 3D world's (`PhysicsSettings.Layers` and `Collisions`, `Physics.LayerMask`); the 2D world adds the settings section `Physics2D` (`Physics2DSettings::Gravity`, vec2, default (0, -9.81)) after SortingLayers.

### 10. The phases and the session (stream P2; stream B for SpriteAnimation)

- **Phases** (`PlaySessionPhase`, values implemented by the contract): SpriteAnimation (4b, after NativeStep), and Physics2DPreStep, Physics2DStep, Physics2DPostStep (7a to 7c, after PhysicsPostStep and before DestroyFlush), at ADR 0028's fixed positions. They run in Play and Simulate. Physics2DPreStep first runs the transform update when the 3D post-step changed a transform, since a pre-step reads the cached world matrices; 3D callbacks see an unstepped 2D world and 2D callbacks a stepped and written-back 3D world. DestroyFlush and the frame's destroy flush call the 2D system's `FlushDestroyed` after the 3D system's; PostStepTransformUpdate propagates both write-backs. The streams that enter a phase add it to `PlaySessionTests.cpp`'s phase lists at these places (the ledger's rule).
- **The session** creates the `Physics2DSystem` right after the `PhysicsSystem` (its errors fail the start like the 3D system's) and the `SpriteAnimationSystem` with the session's asset manager; `PlaySession::GetPhysics2D` and the play session's `IScriptHost::GetPhysics2D` return it (null until then). `IScriptHost::GetPhysics2D` has a default that returns null, which the edit evaluation host keeps, so `EditorMethodContext.cpp`, a file without an owner in ADR 0028's phase-2 ledger, does not change.
- **The 2D world is created at the first 2D body** (`Physics2DSystem.h`): the system validates its specification at once, but makes its `Physics2DWorld`, a second `PhysicsWorld` with its own temp allocator (16 MB by default) and Jolt's pair and manifold caches, only when the scene's components first make a 2D body (in `Create`, or in the `Physics2DPreStep` that composes it), and keeps it until the session ends; before that it answers as an empty world. So a session without 2D bodies, every 3D game's, costs and behaves exactly as before M21, and the existing "PhysicsSystem: destroying the session destroys every body and the world" keeps counting one world; "Physics2DSystem: the 2D world is created at the first 2D body, so a scene without one holds no second world" proves it.

### 11. The physics2d state-hash block (stream P2)

`PlaySession::ComputeStateHash` appends `Physics2DSystem::AppendStateHash` after M19's native block (ADR 0028's ledger, "State-hash blocks"; M22's ui block follows it): every 2D body in canonical order of its owner, an owner's solid body before its sensor body (an entity with a solid and a trigger 2D collider and no RigidBody2D owns an implicit static and an implicit sensor body; Architecture §5.3's body order, which the 3D block follows), the owner's UUID, its position, angle and velocities as the little-endian bit patterns of their floats and its sleeping flag. ### 14. The 2D editor mode, picking, drag-drop and the sheet inspector (R-2D-EDITOR; streams D and A)

- **The 2D mode** is the scene view's option `EditorViewportOptions::Mode2D` (UI state, never in the scene): the toolbar's 2D toggle and `viewport.setOptions {mode2D}` share it. In it the camera is orthographic looking down -Z (`EditorCamera::Make2D`), navigation is pan and zoom only (`Navigate2D`), the grid lies in the XY plane, and the gizmos (`GizmoOverlay`, M21's file) translate in X and Y, rotate about Z and scale in X and Y. Turning it off keeps the camera, so `viewport.camera` reports what the view shows either way.
- **2D picking** (`Scene/SpritePicking.h`, stream A): sprites draw without writing depth or EntityId (fact 7), so the sprite a pixel shows is the last drawn whose quad covers it. The editor's click selection and `viewport.pick` ask `PickSprite` first, through the same output mapping as the GPU pick, and fall back to the mesh pick; texture alpha is not sampled. A sprite therefore wins over a mesh in front of it, an accepted limit of scenes that mix them, recorded in Backlog §6 with this contract. `scene.raycast` stays the mesh raycast its method describes. In `EditorHostViewports` (M21's file) for the editor, in `ViewportPickMethods.cpp` for automation.
- **Drag-drop:** a SpriteSheet or a Texture dropped onto the scene view creates a sprite where the cursor's ray meets z = 0 (`EditorCore/Viewport/SpriteDrop.h`), through `entity.create`, the drop's whole action, so automation already reaches it (as a mesh drop, fact 7).
- **The sprite sheet inspector** (Backlog §2): `InspectorPanel` shows a selected `.spritesheet`'s texture, frames and clips and edits them through `asset.setProperties`, the action the inspector's edits submit, so it adds no method (ADR 0029 decision 18's parity holds).

## Contract review (C21.1)

One review of the frozen interfaces (ADR 0028 decision 11): the staged contract against `111c1c9`, every new file in full and the context of every hunk, against ADR 0028 decisions 11, 12, 16 and 17 and phase 2's ledger, the plan's §3.5, §4.1 to §4.5, §6 "M21", §7, §8 and R-2D-* rows, and Roadmap M21. The lead chose the route of four fixes (2, 3, 11 and the granted amendment of 2), recorded with them.

**Major:**
- **1. The fixed 1 m extrusion made Z the contact axis of deep overlaps and distorted small bodies' inertia** (`Physics2DWorld.h`). Checked: boxes have no convex radius (`PhysicsShape.cpp`), so Jolt resolves two boxes' overlap along EPA's minimum-penetration axis, which with equal half depths of 0.5 m is Z once they overlap by more than 1 m in the plane (or a circle's centre lies more than 0.5 m inside a box); the locks discard every motion along Z, so the overlap in the plane is never resolved. (The review's further step, that the growing normal impulse then lets friction hold the pair, depends on the pair; the unresolved overlap does not.) The mass properties' isotropic floor (`Physics/Private/PhysicsMassProperties.cpp`) trips for a 4 cm box at a depth of 1 m and adds 63% to its inertia about Z. Fixed: the constant `Physics2DHalfDepth` is replaced by the per-collider rule `GetPhysics2DHalfDepth` (the diagonal of the collider's bounding rectangle; decision 8), which `MakeBody2DShape` and `Overlap`'s query box use; decision 8 and risk 1 are corrected, and the size limit the rule brings is in Backlog §6. New cases: "Physics2DWorld: a body sunk deep into a box is pushed out in the plane" and "Physics2DWorld: a small body's angular acceleration under a torque follows its 2D inertia"; "Physics2DWorld: shapes are laid in the XY plane as the existing shape variant" checks the rule.
- **2. The editor's scene and game views got no sorting layers, and no case checked any host.** Checked: `EditorHostViewports::Extract` builds its own request and passes it unchanged to `PlaySession::ExtractView`, stream D's row said nothing of the layers, stream A's host list left the views out, risk 4 cited a screenshot case that did not exist, and decision 16 still called the granted amendment requested. ## Risks

1. **Out-of-plane motion.** The solver is 3D (plan §8 risk 6); the locks zero the off-plane velocities, the depth rule keeps every contact normal in the plane however deep two shapes overlap (decision 8), the plane test checks positions and rotations bit for bit after 600 steps of a pile and the deep-overlap case the normals, then the Physics2D replay runs in all four runs.
2. **Mixed scenes.** A 2D collider gathered under a 3D body, or the reverse, is not one entity in two worlds; each world's composition looks only at its own components, so the rule is the entity's (decision 9), and the validator reports the mixed entity, never the hierarchy.
3. **Picking order.** CPU picking by draw order can disagree with the image where a mesh occludes a sprite (decision 14's accepted limit); 2D scenes rarely put meshes in front of sprites.
4. **Hosts and sorting layers.** A host that does not copy `SortingLayers.Layers` into its request draws every sprite in the first layer, and nothing else fails. The ledger amendment is granted (decision 16). `viewport.screenshot` and the editor's scene and game views have cases of their own (decision 4), `viewport.pick` has `test_the_2d_mode_looks_down_z_and_picks_the_topmost_sprite`, and the settings observation test observes the play session's own extraction. The rest copy the list in the statement beside the rendering quality, without a case of their own: the FeatureTest runner's and the Runtime's test screenshots (`MakeTestScreenshotRequest`), the Runtime's game view and the editor's thumbnails.
5. **The registered components before their systems.** From the contract on, a scene may hold 2D components that nothing simulates or draws yet; no audited project holds one, and the FeatureTest coverage that would read them lands with E1 after the systems.
6. **Phase lists.** Entering the new phases changes `PlaySessionTests.cpp`'s expected lists; streams B and P2 each add their own phases there at the ledger's places, and the integration checks the merged lists.
