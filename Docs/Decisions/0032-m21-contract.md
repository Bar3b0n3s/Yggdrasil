## Decisions

### 1. What the contract freezes, implements and stubs

The contract freezes the C++ declarations under "Frozen interfaces", the data formats under "Data formats" and the registry and ledger entries of decision 16; this record's decisions state the behaviour each stream implements.

- **C++ stubs:** every new function whose behaviour a stream implements starts with `ENGINE_CONTRACT_STUB();`. ### 3. Sprites: the SpriteRenderer component and the sprite geometry (R-2D-SPRITE; stream A)

- **SpriteRenderer** (`Scene/Components/SpriteRendererComponent.h`): `{Sheet, Frame, Texture, Color, FlipX, FlipY, PixelsPerUnit, SortingLayer, OrderInLayer, Lit}`, requires Transform. The image is frame `Frame` of `Sheet`, or with a null `Sheet` the whole of `Texture` pivoted at its centre, or with neither the built-in white square (decision 7), as a null Text font is the default font.
- **What is missing, one rule** for the Sprites stage, picking (decision 14), bounds (`EntityBounds`) and the editor, stated in `SpriteRendererComponent.h`, `SpriteItem`, `SpritePicking.h` and `EntityBounds.h`: a texture that is missing or fails to load, `Texture` or the sheet's own, is replaced by its placeholder, the Missing checker (64 x 64 texels), shown whole over the sprite's quad: the frame's quad with a sheet (its texel rectangle and pivot are the sheet's, whatever the texture), the placeholder's own 64 x 64 texels centred with `Texture`. That quad is drawn, picked and bounded. A sheet that is missing or fails to load (sheets have no placeholder, fact 3) and a frame index the sheet lacks (`SPRITE_FRAME_OUT_OF_RANGE`) give no quad: the sprite draws nothing, is never picked and adds no bounds.
- **The size is texels over PixelsPerUnit,** in the entity's local units (default 16, so a 16-texel built-in shape is one unit), and the entity's scale applies. A sheet carries no pixels-per-unit (its format is frozen at version 1), and a sprite size in units would let a sprite and its camera disagree; the PixelPerfectCamera carries the same field (decision 6), so equal values map one texel to one scene pixel.
- **`Lit`** is C21.2's flag (sprites stay unlit by default and opt in to 2D lights, plan §6 "M21"), registered now so the component never needs a migration for it; it has no effect until W21.2.
- **The geometry** (`Asset/SpriteGeometry.h`): `GetSpriteFrameGeometry` (the frame's texel rectangle and pivot, or the whole texture centred) and `ComputeSpriteLocalQuad` (the corners in the entity's local XY plane, +Y up while texels run down, the pivot at the origin, flips mirroring about it, the texture rectangle as texel edges). The Sprites stage, sprite bounds (`EntityBounds.cpp`) and 2D picking (decision 14) use it, so what is drawn is what is bounded and picked. It lives in Asset, beside `SpriteSheetData`, because both Renderer and Scene include Asset and neither includes the other (§3).

### 4. Sorting, the snapshot and the Sprites pass (R-2D-SPRITE, R-2D-SORT; stream A)

- **`SpriteItem`** (`Renderer/RenderSnapshot.h`): `{World, Sheet, Frame, Texture, Color, FlipX, FlipY, PixelsPerUnit, SortingLayer, OrderInLayer, Lit, Entity}`; `RenderSnapshot::Sprites` follows `Output`, as the ledger places phase 2's members, and allocates from the snapshot's memory resource. Extraction never touches assets (fact 2): the renderer resolves the frame and the texture through its AssetManager and GpuResourceCache, as it resolves meshes.
- **Draw order:** extraction lists every effectively enabled SpriteRenderer, sorted stably by (sorting layer index, OrderInLayer) from canonical order, so ties keep canonical order and the order is deterministic. The renderer draws the vector in its order, back to front, without writing depth (painter's order), depth-tested against the opaque meshes (fact 1). A distance-based order is not used: an orthographic 2D scene has equal depths, and a script that wants y-sorting sets OrderInLayer.
- **Sorting layers** are names in the project's new settings section `SortingLayers` (`SortingLayersSettings::Layers`: 1 to 32 unique non-empty names, the first `Default`, as `PhysicsSettings.Layers`), appended after Testing (ADR 0028's ledger). A sprite's layer is its name's index there; an undeclared name sorts as layer 0 and is `SPRITE_UNKNOWN_SORTING_LAYER`. Hosts pass the list in `RenderExtractionRequest::SortingLayers`, as they copy the rendering quality: the play session's game view, the FeatureTest runner, the Runtime, the editor's thumbnails and `viewport.screenshot` (stream A; the one statement in `ScreenshotMethods.cpp` that main's ledger amendment grants, decision 16), and the editor's scene and game views in edit and in play (`EditorHostViewports::Extract`, stream D, which also passes the list in the `SpritePickRequest` of its click selection, as `viewport.pick` does in stream A's `ViewportPickMethods.cpp`). "ScreenshotMethods: viewport.screenshot extracts sprites in the project's sorting layers" and "EditorHostViewports: scene and game extraction carry the project's sorting layers" prove those two hosts with two sprites whose layer order contradicts both their OrderInLayer and their canonical order, as `test_the_2d_mode_looks_down_z_and_picks_the_topmost_sprite` proves `viewport.pick`; the settings observation test ("ProjectSettings: every AllSettings.eproj value is observed by its consumer", gate 8) observes the section in the play session's own extraction once stream A registers it.
- **The pass:** the declaration `Sprites` (stage Sprites, no pipelines of its own, stats row `Sprites`) follows the QuadBatcher's in `ScenePassDeclarations`, so the stats order gains `Sprites` between `Overdraw` and `Bloom` (26 rows; C21.2's `Lights2D` follows it, 31 at phase 2's end by the ledger). `RecordSpritesStage` turns each item into one QuadBatcher quad in world space (the camera's view-projection as the batch transform, the Point sampler, the frame's texture rectangle, `Color` as the quad colour) and records them with the Hdr pipeline into SceneColor under the `Sprites` row, in a lit view only (the data views and the Overdraw view draw no sprites). The QuadBatcher splits draws where the texture changes, so a sheet's sprites batch.
- **Recovery:** the stage keeps its binding sets in a `SceneRenderer::State` `PassBindingCache` (place 2 of ADR 0029 decision 27), and the textures are GpuResourceCache mirrors (place 3), so the recreation protocol covers the sprites without hooks of their own; "Sprites: a renderer drawing sprites recovers from a device loss with the same image" proves it through the shared helper (plan §7 item 3).

### 5. The SpriteAnimator and the SpriteAnimation phase (R-2D-ANIM; stream B)

- **SpriteAnimator** (`Scene/Components/SpriteAnimatorComponent.h`): `{Clip, Playing, Step, StepTicks}`, requires SpriteRenderer, and plays a clip of the SpriteRenderer's sheet. The playback state is component data, so the scene's serialization and the state hash cover it and a replay reproduces every frame (ADR 0028's ledger, "State-hash blocks").
- **The phase** `PlaySessionPhase::SpriteAnimation` runs after NativeStep and before PreStepTransformUpdate (ADR 0028's phase-2 execution order), in Play and Simulate alike, since it is a system and not a script (`Scene/SpriteAnimationSystem.h`). For every effectively enabled entity with both components, in canonical order, a playing animator whose clip the sheet has advances one tick (`AdvanceSpriteAnimator`): the current step (clamped to the clip's last) has its frame written to `SpriteRenderer.Frame`, then the step's tick count advances, and when it reaches or passes the step's Ticks the next step begins; past the last step a looping clip restarts and a held one stops on its last frame and clears `Playing`. `Step` and `StepTicks` are fields, which `entity.update`, a scene file or a `Clip` written without `SpriteAnimator:Play` can leave from another clip, so a `StepTicks` at or past the step's Ticks shows that step for one tick, then moves on. So a step shows its frame for exactly its Ticks ticks, and a clip started in `OnFixedUpdate` shows its first step at that tick, while one started in a contact callback or `OnDestroy` advances from the next tick (the ledger's rule).
- **An unknown clip** (or a sheet missing or failing to load) changes nothing and is `SPRITE_UNKNOWN_CLIP`, logged once per entity and clip name at run time.
- **No speed field:** clips state their timing in ticks (fact 3), so a speed would make a fractional tick count; a script that wants another speed plays another clip.

### 6. The PixelPerfectCamera (R-2D-CAM; stream C)

- **PixelPerfectCamera** (`Scene/Components/PixelPerfectCameraComponent.h`): `{ReferenceWidth, ReferenceHeight, PixelsPerUnit, LetterboxColor}`, requires Camera.
- **It drives M18's output stage** through render extraction and changes no output-stage code (ADR 0028's ledger): for the Primary and Entity camera sources whose entity holds one (never the editor's Explicit camera), the snapshot's `Output` is `{ReferenceWidth, ReferenceHeight, PixelArt: true, LetterboxColor}`, the projection is orthographic with half height `ReferenceHeight / (2 PixelsPerUnit)` at the reference aspect whatever the Camera's own projection, and the camera's position is snapped so the view's left and bottom edges lie on the pixel grid (`ComputePixelPerfectView`, `Scene/PixelPerfectCamera.h`). The integer scale is the output mapping's, the largest that fits the view (ADR 0029 decision 6), never a field: "reference resolution and integer scale" are the component's resolution and the mapping's scale.
- **Pointers and rays** in view coordinates go through the output mapping first (`MapViewPointToScenePoint`), so `Camera:ScreenToWorldRay`, `WorldToScreen` and the game view's pointer agree with the image under a PixelPerfectCamera; stream C changes the Camera proxy's methods (`Bindings/Components.cpp`) by that rule.

### 7. The built-in sprite textures (stream B)

The four procedural textures of the ledger's handles, `engine://Textures/SpriteSquare` (0x0187), `SpriteRounded` (0x0188), `SpriteBevel` (0x0189) and `SpriteCircle` (0x018a), are `BuiltinTexture` values generated by `GenerateBuiltinSpriteTexture` (`Asset/BuiltinTextures.h`): 16 x 16 RGBA8Srgb white shapes of one level, transparent texels `(255, 255, 255, 0)`, a sprite's `Color` tinting them, defined texel by texel in the enumerators' comments and generated in integer arithmetic, so their bytes are identical in every configuration. They are never compressed (ADR 0028's ledger, "Texture compression"). Stream B adds their catalogue entries (`Resources/EngineAssets.json`, kept sorted by handle, and the compiled-in procedural entries); no CC0 pack is vendored (plan §6 "M21").

### 8. The 2D world on Jolt (R-2D-PHYS; ADR 0028 decision 6; stream P1)

`Physics2DWorld` (`Physics/Physics2DWorld.h`) is the interface ADR 0028 decision 6 names: a second Jolt world (a `PhysicsWorld` it owns), whose Dynamic bodies allow only X and Y translation and Z rotation (`Physics2DDofs`; `Physics2DTranslationDofs` with FixedRotation), whose shapes are the existing variant laid in the XY plane (`MakeBody2DShape`: a circle is a sphere; a box is a box and a polygon the hull of its points, each extruded along Z on both sides by its own half depth, `GetPhysics2DHalfDepth`), and whose API speaks 2D (`Physics2DPose`, planar vectors, a float angular velocity). Every body lies on the plane z = 0 and nothing it is handed moves one off it. No library is added (Box2D is won't-do, ADR 0028 decision 2), the frozen `PhysicsShape.h` does not change, and the 3D `PhysicsWorld` keeps every result: the 2D world is a separate instance.

- **The depth rule.** A box or polygon is extruded by the diagonal of its bounding rectangle after the collider's scale, twice the radius of a circle enclosing it; a circle's half depth is its sphere's radius. Every body lies on z = 0, so two shapes overlap along Z by the sum of their half depths while their overlap in the plane never exceeds the sum of their enclosing radii: the overlap along Z always exceeds the deepest overlap the pair can have in the plane, so Jolt's minimum-penetration axis, the contact normal, lies in the plane however deep the overlap. A fixed depth (0.5 m, the contract's first text) made Z that axis for a pair overlapping by more than 1 m in the plane (a body teleported into a wall, a fast ball one step deep into it), and a normal along the locked Z pushes nothing, so the pair stayed embedded or sank; it also made the mass properties' isotropic floor (`Physics/Private/PhysicsMassProperties.cpp`: a determinant below 10^-3 of the trace cubed) add up to 63% to the inertia about Z of a box under about 6 cm. With the rule the conditioning does not depend on the size, and a box's or hull's inertia about Z, which its depth does not change (m (w^2 + h^2) / 12 for a box), is its 2D one. The depth counts against `PhysicsShape::MaxColliderSize`, so a box or polygon whose bounding rectangle's diagonal exceeds 50 km is refused (the 3D world takes 100 km): an accepted limit (Backlog §6). "Physics2DWorld: a body sunk deep into a box is pushed out in the plane" and "Physics2DWorld: a small body's angular acceleration under a torque follows its 2D inertia" prove it.
- **The plan's risk** ("shapes extruded in Z must not produce out-of-plane forces", §8 risk 6) is met by the locks and the depth rule: Jolt zeroes the locked velocity components every step, so a body's Z and its rotation's X and Y stay exactly zero, and no contact normal leaves the plane. "Physics2DWorld: a dynamic body never leaves its plane" checks the first bit for bit on a pile after 600 steps; the deep-overlap case checks the second.
- **Circles and polygons.** A circle is a sphere that turns about Z as a disc (the milestone review, the lead's decision 10): `Physics2DWorld::CreateBody` sets `BodyDescription::SpheresAsDiscs`, so `PhysicsWorld` adds to a Dynamic body's moment about Z a tenth of each sphere's share of its Mass times its radius squared (Jolt shares a compound's mass by volume), making each circle's own moment m r^2 / 2 instead of a sphere's 0.4 m r^2, kept through `SetShape` and added after `PhysicsShape::CheckDynamicBody`'s inertia limit, which it exceeds by at most a quarter, so the validator and the world refuse the same bodies; contacts and the other moments are the sphere's ("Physics2DWorld: a circle turns as a disc, alone, off the origin, in a compound and after a new shape"). A polygon is Jolt's convex hull shape, whose corners Jolt rounds by its default convex radius (up to 0.05 m, less on a small hull) for contacts and overlaps, while rays meet the sharp hull and a box's corners stay sharp (`PhysicsShape.cpp`, §9.2 "Seams"); the frozen `PhysicsShape.h` cannot set the radius, so a square PolygonCollider2D lets a box slide off its corner earlier than an equal BoxCollider2D, almost a pixel at 16 pixels per unit: an accepted limit (Backlog §6), stated in `PolygonCollider2DComponent.h`.
- **Queries and seams.** `Overlap` takes an `Overlap2DShape`, a box or a circle, the shapes `Physics2D.OverlapBox` and `OverlapCircle` and `physics2d.overlap` use, laid as a box and a sphere, which `PhysicsWorld::Overlap` accepts; a polygon query would need a hull query in `PhysicsWorld`, which no caller needs. `Body2DDescription::EnhancedInternalEdgeRemoval` passes RigidBody2D's flag (decision 9) to `BodyDescription`, so a body rolling or sliding across the merged boxes of one compound (C21.2's tilemap collider) crosses their seams as a 3D body does (§9.2 "Seams").

### 9. The 2D components and the Physics2DSystem (R-2D-PHYS; stream P2)

- **New components, not new fields** (the contract decides them, preferring new components to new fields on existing ones, plan §7 item 6): RigidBody2D `{Type, Mass, Friction, Restitution, LinearDamping, AngularDamping, GravityFactor, MotionQuality, AllowSleeping, FixedRotation, Layer, MaxLinearVelocity, MaxAngularVelocity, EnhancedInternalEdgeRemoval, InitialLinearVelocity (vec2), InitialAngularVelocity}` (EnhancedInternalEdgeRemoval is RigidBody's, registered now so W21.2's tilemap collider needs no change to a frozen component), BoxCollider2D `{HalfExtents (vec2), Offset (vec2), Angle, IsTrigger}`, CircleCollider2D `{Radius, Offset, IsTrigger}` and PolygonCollider2D `{Points (3 to 64 vec2), Offset, Angle, IsTrigger}`, each requiring Transform, with RigidBody's bounds and its `BodyType` and `MotionQuality` enums. Angles are degrees about +Z (counter-clockwise seen from +Z), angular velocities radians per second, as the 3D components'.
- **One world per entity is a validation error, never a registry exclusion:** an entity with a 2D and a 3D physics component is `PHYSICS2D_MIXED_WORLDS`; it keeps its 3D body exactly as without its 2D components, and its 2D components make no 2D body, so the 3D world is bit-identical (Breakout's replay).
- **The system** (`Scene/Physics2DSystem.h`) is `PhysicsSystem`'s design in the plane, rule for rule (composition with RigidBody2D in RigidBody's place, rebuilds, pairs across rebuilds, kinematic moves, teleports, write-back, M20's pending enter and collider events, the flushes, the diagnostics, queries sorted by distance then UUID). A body's pose is its owner's world X and Y and the twist about Z of its world rotation; an owner whose rotation is not about Z alone is `PHYSICS2D_OFF_PLANE` (Warning) and takes the twist. The write-back keeps the entity's world Z (free for draw depth) and replaces a swing by none. A body is placed again only when its owner's planar pose changes: the pre-step compares the planar pose of the owner's Transform with the one it had at the body's last creation, placement, kinematic move or write-back, bit for bit, never with the body's own pose (whose angle the round trip through the quaternion may move by an ulp), so a write of the world Z alone moves and wakes no Static, Kinematic or Dynamic body (W21.1's review).
- **Layers** are the 3D world's (`PhysicsSettings.Layers` and `Collisions`, `Physics.LayerMask`); the 2D world adds the settings section `Physics2D` (`Physics2DSettings::Gravity`, vec2, default (0, -9.81)) after SortingLayers.

### 10. The phases and the session (stream P2; stream B for SpriteAnimation)

- **Phases** (`PlaySessionPhase`, values implemented by the contract): SpriteAnimation (4b, after NativeStep), and Physics2DPreStep, Physics2DStep, Physics2DPostStep (7a to 7c, after PhysicsPostStep and before DestroyFlush), at ADR 0028's fixed positions. They run in Play and Simulate. Physics2DPreStep first runs the transform update when the 3D post-step changed a transform, since a pre-step reads the cached world matrices; 3D callbacks see an unstepped 2D world and 2D callbacks a stepped and written-back 3D world. DestroyFlush and the frame's destroy flush call the 2D system's `FlushDestroyed` after the 3D system's; PostStepTransformUpdate propagates both write-backs. The streams that enter a phase add it to `PlaySessionTests.cpp`'s phase lists at these places (the ledger's rule).
- **The session** creates the `Physics2DSystem` right after the `PhysicsSystem` (its errors fail the start like the 3D system's) and the `SpriteAnimationSystem` with the session's asset manager; `PlaySession::GetPhysics2D` and the play session's `IScriptHost::GetPhysics2D` return it (null until then). `IScriptHost::GetPhysics2D` has a default that returns null, which the edit evaluation host keeps, so `EditorMethodContext.cpp`, a file without an owner in ADR 0028's phase-2 ledger, does not change.
- **The 2D world is created at the first 2D body** (`Physics2DSystem.h`): the system validates its specification at once, but makes its `Physics2DWorld`, a second `PhysicsWorld` with its own temp allocator (16 MB by default) and Jolt's pair and manifold caches, only when the scene's components first make a 2D body (in `Create`, or in the `Physics2DPreStep` that composes it), and keeps it until the session ends; before that it answers as an empty world. While there is no world and the scene holds no 2D physics component, the pre-step returns at once whatever else changed (an entity enabled or disabled, a 3D component), so it composes nothing (`Physics2DSystemStats::Compositions`), and `Create` checks the world's limits with the 3D world's own check (`ValidatePhysicsWorldLimits`), so the later creation cannot fail (both from W21.1's review). So a session without 2D bodies, every 3D game's, costs and behaves exactly as before M21, and the existing "PhysicsSystem: destroying the session destroys every body and the world" keeps counting one world; "Physics2DSystem: the 2D world is created at the first 2D body, so a scene without one holds no second world" proves it.

### 14. The 2D editor mode, picking, drag-drop and the sheet inspector (R-2D-EDITOR; streams D and A)

- **The 2D mode** is the scene view's option `EditorViewportOptions::Mode2D` (UI state, never in the scene): the toolbar's 2D toggle and `viewport.setOptions {mode2D}` share it. In it the camera is orthographic looking down -Z (`EditorCamera::Make2D`), navigation is pan and zoom only (`Navigate2D`), the grid lies in the XY plane, and the gizmos (`GizmoOverlay`, M21's file) translate in X and Y, rotate about Z and scale in X and Y. - **2D picking** (`Scene/SpritePicking.h`, stream A): sprites draw without writing depth or EntityId (fact 7), so the sprite a pixel shows is the last drawn whose quad covers it. The editor's click selection and `viewport.pick` ask `PickSprite` first, through the same output mapping as the GPU pick, and fall back to the mesh pick; texture alpha is not sampled. The click selection picks among the sprites of the image that was clicked (each view keeps its displayed snapshot's sprites; `PickSprite`'s overload over a snapshot's sprites), when it requests the GPU pick, so a sprite a playing session moves before that pick completes is still the one selected (W21.1's review). A play scene's revision, which its ticks move (a playing SpriteAnimator writes its sprite's frame through the scene, scripts write proxies), never makes that pick stale: only an edit scene's revision cancels a click, and a shown scene replaced or a session started or stopped cancels the views' clicks (the milestone review, the lead's decision 1). A sprite therefore wins over a mesh in front of it, an accepted limit of scenes that mix them, recorded in Backlog §6 with this contract. `scene.raycast` stays the mesh raycast its method describes. In `EditorHostViewports` (M21's file) for the editor, in `ViewportPickMethods.cpp` for automation.
- **Drag-drop:** a SpriteSheet or a Texture dropped onto the scene view creates a sprite where the cursor's ray meets z = 0 (`EditorCore/Viewport/SpriteDrop.h`), through `entity.create`, the drop's whole action, so automation already reaches it (as a mesh drop, fact 7).

### 20. Light2D (R-2D-LIGHT; stream L)

- **The component** (`Scene/Components/Light2DComponent.h`): `{Type, Color, Intensity, Radius, Falloff, Height}`, requires Transform, Rendering2D after PixelPerfectCamera; `Light2DType` Point or Global. Point lights sit at their entity's rendered world position; Radius and Height are world units, unaffected by the entity's scale and rotation; Intensity multiplies the linear colour (0 to 1000, HDR above 1). A Global light gives every lit pixel of the view the same light, wherever its entity is.
- **What it lights:** only lit sprites and lit tilemaps (`SpriteRenderer.Lit`, `TilemapRenderer.Lit`); unlit sprites, meshes and texts ignore every 2D light, so a scene without a lit item renders exactly as before, and the existing goldens do not change (plan §3.5 "M21"). Lights add up; a lit pixel without any light is black (a scene opts in to darkness by lighting a sprite).
- **No 2D shadows**, a kept limit (plan §6 "M21"; Backlog §6).
- **No simulation state.** A Light2D's data is component data in the scene's canonical text and nothing else; no phase, no block, no system reads it in the fixed step (decision 29).
- **The editor** shows a Light2D with the PointLight icon in its colour (its `Color`, alpha 1) where the view's icons are on (`RenderAnnotations.cpp`, M21's own addition after M23's paths), so the scene view shows where each light is; "RenderAnnotations: a Light2D shows the PointLight icon in its colour when icons are on" proves it (stream L).

### 21. Lit sprites and normal maps (R-2D-LIGHT; stream L)

- **The lit flag's effect:** a lit sprite's texel RGB is multiplied by its `Color`'s RGB and by the light the view's 2D lights give its pixel (decision 22), alpha unchanged; a lit tilemap's tiles likewise, with the flat normal: neither `TilemapRenderer` nor a palette entry has a normal map in Tilemap v1, a kept limit (Backlog §6).
- **Normal maps are optional** (plan §6 "M21"): `SpriteRendererComponent::NormalMap`, a texture imported with `Usage` `NormalMap` (linear), tangent space with +X towards the image's right and +Y towards its top, laid like the image's texture (sampled at the same normalized texture coordinates, so a sheet's normal map has the sheet texture's layout), turned with the sprite's quad (its flips and its entity's rotation). Null is the flat normal, which takes each light whole, so a lit sprite without a normal map is never dimmed by a light's height. An unlit sprite ignores it. Point lights light a normal-mapped pixel by the cosine between its normal and the direction to the light raised `Height` above the plane; Global lights ignore normals.
- **Registration** by stream L (decision 19's reason); the field follows `Lit`, so the component's registered order matches its header and needs no migration (M21 has not landed, so SpriteRenderer v1 is not yet on main).
- **The texture compression rule:** normal maps M21's scaffolds import take `Compression: None` with the other sprite textures (ADR 0028's ledger; decision 31's list), so M23's BC5 never moves a lit sprite's image.

### 22. The Lights2D pass (R-2D-LIGHT; stream L)

- **The light model** is `Renderer/Light2DShading.h`'s, on the GPU and on the CPU alike: a Point light gives Color × Intensity × a × n, where the attenuation a is 1 within Radius × (1 − Falloff) of the light in its plane, the smoothstep t²(3 − 2t) of t = (R − d)/(R F) beyond, and 0 from Radius on (Falloff 0: a hard edge), and n is 1 for the flat normal and max(0, N·L) for a normal N, L the unit vector from the pixel's point to the light raised Height; a Global light gives Color × Intensity. The view's light is the sum, in the snapshot's order (canonical entity order), of its first `MaxVisibleLights2D` (1,024) lights that reach the view; the renderer skips the rest and logs the first skip once per renderer (Backlog §6). A Global light always reaches the view; a Point light reaches it unless its square of side 2 Radius about its position in its plane, projected by the view (its depth held inside the clip range, as the pass draws it), lies wholly outside the view's clip rectangle; so lights off the view never count against the cap. `EvaluateLights2D` takes the lights the view draws, culled to the view by its caller as the renderer culls them, and applies the same cap, so the CPU reference of a view sums exactly the lights its image adds (the contract's review: the header's first text capped the snapshot's lights before culling, the GPU's after). Only square roots, products and sums: deterministic, and the CPU reference of the acceptance ("A lit sprite matches its CPU reference"); "Lights2D: a view draws the first MaxVisibleLights2D lights that reach it, and lights off the view do not count" proves the cap.
- **The recording order inside the Sprites stage** (the ledger leaves it to C21.2): in a Lit view with a camera (not the wireframe), when the view's 2D items include a lit sprite or tilemap: (1) `Lights2DNormals` (RGBA16F, the scene resolution) is cleared to the flat normal and, when a lit sprite has a normal map, the lit items' normals are drawn into it in draw order through the QuadBatcher's `Normals` pipeline, depth-tested against SceneDepth; (2) `Lights2DLight` (RGBA16F, the scene resolution: the PixelPerfectCamera's reference resolution, so "lights accumulate at the reference resolution") is cleared to zero and every light is added into it, one draw each (a Point light's square around its radius in its plane, projected by the view with its depth held inside the clip range, so a light outside the camera's depth range still lights an orthographic view; a Global light's full-screen triangle), with the Lights2D program's one pipeline and additive blending, reading the normals; (3) the sprites and tilemaps draw in their one draw order, unlit runs with the Hdr pipeline and lit runs with `HdrLit`, which multiplies by `Lights2DLight` loaded at the fragment's pixel. Without a lit item nothing of (1) and (2) is recorded, the targets are not created and the `Lights2D` row does not appear: unlit sprites keep their texels and the existing goldens their bytes. Steps (1) and (2) record under the `Lights2D` row, (3) under `Sprites`.
- **The QuadBatcher's two new targets** (`QuadBatcher.h`'s file comment states them): `HdrLit` (Hdr's target, blending and depth test, times the light texture's texel at the fragment's pixel) and `Normals` (into Lights2DNormals with SceneDepth, no blending: where the quad's texel alpha times its colour's alpha is above 0, the world normal of its normal map turned by the quad's tangent frame, which the fragment derives from the screen-space derivatives of its position and texture coordinates, with 1 in w; without a normal map (0, 0, 1) and 0). `QuadBatcher::PipelineCount` becomes 4 and the Quad program gains the two entries; the declarations' startup count becomes 61 (58 + 2 + Lights2D's 1). M22 uses the QuadBatcher as landed (its Hdr and Ldr targets and the scissor are unchanged) and, by ADR 0028's ledger amendment of 2026-11-03 (main `1707cf47`, which the landing merged; ADR 0033 decision 57), appends one depth-tested LDR target, `QuadBatchTarget::LdrDepth`, with its pipeline, after M21's `HdrLit` and `Normals`, changing none of M21's.
- **One normal per pixel:** where lit items overlap, those beneath the topmost lit item take the light computed with its normal (a kept limit, Backlog §6, as in other engines' screen-space 2D lighting).
- **Placement and recovery** (ADR 0029 decisions 26 and 27): the Lights2D pipeline in `SceneRendererPipelines` (place 1, `Renderer/Lights2DPass.{h,cpp}`, created through `CreatePipelines`), the two targets from the renderer's `RenderTargetPool` and its binding caches in `SceneRenderer::State` (place 2), the normal maps as GpuResourceCache mirrors (place 3): no hooks of their own. "Lights2D: a renderer drawing 2D lights recovers from a device loss with the same image" proves it through the shared helper (plan §7 item 3).
- **Programs and shared structs:** the program `Lights2D` (`Passes/Lights2D.slang`, sorted into `Shaders.json`), its push constants in a `Shared/` header of its own (`Shared/Light2DConstants.h`); the lit and normals entries in `Passes/Quad.slang` (the ledger: sprites draw with Quad).
- **Data views:** lights are recorded only where sprites are (the Lit view, not the wireframe).

### 23. The Tilemap asset (R-2D-TILE; stream T1)

- **The type:** `AssetType::Tilemap`, 12 on M21's branch (ADR 0028's ledger: the first of M21 and M22 to land takes 12, the other renumbers its own at its merge and rebuilds its checkouts' cooked caches), spelled `Tilemap` in `.meta` files, the script API (`Field.Asset("Tilemap")`) and automation; no placeholder (a consumer whose tilemap is missing or failed reports it and draws or collides nothing).
- **The file:** `.tilemap`, format `Tilemap` version 1, canonical JSON like every authored file (§6), every field written ("Data formats"): `CellSize` (local units), `ChunkSize` (cells a side, 16 by default, a power of two from 1 to 64), `Palette` (entries `{Sheet, Frame, Solid}`: a SpriteSheet frame and whether the TilemapCollider collides with it) and `Chunks` (`{X, Y, Tiles}`, `Tiles` the chunk's ChunkSize² cell values row by row from its bottom row up, each row left to right). A cell value is 0 for empty or k for `Palette[k − 1]`. Cells are integers, x right and y up (the entity's local axes, unlike texel space); cell (x, y) spans [x CellSize.x, (x + 1) CellSize.x] × [y CellSize.y, (y + 1) CellSize.y].
- **Canonical by rule:** chunks sorted by (Y, X), each once, none empty, so the file and the cooked form have one spelling of each map and `tilemap.paint` adds a chunk at its first tile and removes it with its last (`ApplyTilemapCellEdits`).
- **The rules and limits** (`ValidateTilemap`, every violation a located issue: `/CellSize`, `/Palette/3/Frame`, `/Chunks/2/Tiles/17`, `/Chunks/4`): CellSize's components finite in [0.001, 1000]; ChunkSize a power of two from 1 to 64 (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 or 64: each divides 2²⁰, so the chunk of any cell in [−2²⁰, 2²⁰) holds only such cells, and painting the edge cells (−2²⁰, −2²⁰) and (2²⁰ − 1, 2²⁰ − 1) creates chunks the validator accepts; the contract's review found that a chunk size of 3 made `tilemap.paint` write a file its importer refused); at most 65,535 palette entries (a cell value fits in 16 bits), each Frame below `MaxSpriteSheetFrames`; chunks sorted, unique, each of exactly ChunkSize² values of at most the palette's size and not all 0, its X and Y each in [−2²⁰ / ChunkSize, 2²⁰ / ChunkSize), compared before any product with ChunkSize (so a fuzzed coordinate never overflows); at most 2²² stored cells. The rule holds at import (the importer's `ValidateTilemap`), in `asset.setProperties` and in `tilemap.paint` (both read through `TilemapFromJson`) and in `ApplyTilemapCellEdits`' result. Sheets and their frame counts are not checked (the importer never reads a sheet).
- **The cooked format:** a binary payload, FormatVersion 1, little-endian (`TilemapData.h` documents it byte for byte: the cell size as two `f32`, the chunk size and the palette and chunk counts, each entry {`uint64` sheet, `uint32` frame, `uint8` solid}, each chunk {`int32` X, `int32` Y, ChunkSize² × `uint16`}), deterministic in every configuration; the reader checks every count against its limit and the bytes left before it allocates, refuses a Solid byte other than 0 or 1 and trailing bytes (Parse), then runs `ValidateTilemap`. Its fuzz target is `TilemapData` ("TilemapData: 1,000 seeded mutations of a tilemap file and of its cooked form never crash", `Tests/Data/Fuzz/Targets.json`, sorted by Reader, added by the contract): the first tenth of 1,000 in the Debug unit run, 1,000 in Release, 1,000,000 in phase 2's hardened gate (M23's), and W21.2's targeted unit run of stream T1 runs it (ADR 0028 decision 12).
- **The importer** (`AssetPipeline/Importers/TilemapImporter.h`): id `Tilemap`, version 1, `.tilemap`, no settings, one artifact; strict (an unknown member fails the import); its `Import.Dependencies` are the palette's sheets (`GetTilemapDependencies`, ADR 0028's phase-2 ledger), so M19's pruned export keeps them; a sheet not registered or not a SpriteSheet is no import error. Registered with its source-format row (gate 9 then requires a tilemap in every run mode) and its loader (`LoadCookedTilemap` in `RegisterBuiltinLoaders`), so exported Release and Dist games load tilemaps; `.tilemap` becomes an authored extension (§6: load and save byte-identical; `Scripts/ModuleRules.json`'s `Json` list, the lead's at the integration).
- **The validator:** `project.validate` checks a tilemap's palette sheets as it checks a sprite sheet's texture (`ASSET_MISSING`, `ASSET_TYPE_MISMATCH`), and reports `TILEMAP_FRAME_OUT_OF_RANGE` (decision 30).
- **Authoring:** `asset.create {type: Tilemap, path, values?}` (`AssetCreateType::Tilemap`, appended; absent values make an empty unit-cell map of 16-cell chunks), `asset.getProperties` and `asset.setProperties` (a tilemap's palette, cell size and chunk size, a `ChunkSize` sent without `Chunks` keeping every painted cell, laid in chunks of the new size in canonical order with no empty chunk, W21.2's review; `Chunks` too, replaced whole as RFC 7386 says, though painting is `tilemap.paint`'s), a sheet reference in `Palette` taking every asset reference of §7.1 as a sprite sheet's `Texture` does; the content browser's Create popup gains "Tilemap" after "Sprite sheet" (the ledger's create-list rule); `MakeCanonicalFile` and the upgraded extensions gain `.tilemap`. Painting is decision 26.
- **Textures:** none: a tilemap holds sheet references only, and M21 adds no importer or creation path that makes textures.
- **Kept limit:** no per-cell flips or rotations (Backlog §6).

### 24. TilemapRenderer and chunked rendering through the sprite path (R-2D-TILE; stream T1)

- **The component** (`Scene/Components/TilemapRendererComponent.h`): `{Tilemap, Color, PixelsPerUnit, SortingLayer, OrderInLayer, Lit}`, requires Transform, Rendering2D after Light2D. Rendering only: it makes no 2D body (decision 25, decision 29).
- **A tile is a sprite:** the non-empty cell (x, y) holding `{Sheet, Frame}` draws exactly as a SpriteRenderer with that Sheet and Frame and the component's Color, PixelsPerUnit and Lit, unflipped, whose world matrix is the tilemap's rendered world matrix times the translation to the cell's centre (`GetTilemapCellCenter`), so the frame's pivot sits at the cell's centre; SpriteRendererComponent.h's missing-asset rule applies per tile. So "A tilemap renders byte-identical to the same tiles drawn as sprites" (Roadmap M21's acceptance) holds by construction: "Tilemaps: a tilemap renders byte-identical to the same tiles drawn as sprites" draws a tilemap and the same tiles as sprites at those world matrices and compares the images, with tiles twice their cells' size that overlap across chunks' edges and a chunk outside the view whose tile reaches into it, and shows that chunk-by-chunk order and cell-rectangle bounds would each change the image.
- **The draw order:** the tilemap is one item of the 2D draw order, sorted with the sprites by (sorting layer, OrderInLayer, canonical entity order); `TilemapItem::DrawPosition` places it among `RenderSnapshot::Sprites` (the sprites' vector keeps its meaning). Within a tilemap, tiles draw in its cell order, row by row from the top row (highest y) down, each row left to right, across the whole tilemap and never chunk by chunk: the drawn chunks' tiles are merged into that order, so a tile taller than its cell draws over the row above it and tiles that overlap across a chunk's edge draw as the same tiles drawn as sprites would.
- **Chunked rendering:** the renderer keeps each tilemap's chunk bounds between frames in its `SceneRenderer::State` (`Renderer/Private/TilemapDrawCache.h`), rebuilt when the tilemap's or one of its sheets' asset version or the PixelsPerUnit changes and released by a render that draws no 2D item (the milestone review), and resolves each palette entry's quad, texture rectangle and texture mirror every render, so a texture's hot reload or a device loss never leaves a stale texture behind (stream T1's design, accepted at W21.2's integration in place of the contract's per-chunk quads with their textures; the images and the culling are the same), and draws only the chunks whose bounds meet the view: a chunk's bounds are the union of its tiles' quads in world space (each tile's whole sprite rectangle, whatever its frame's size and pivot, never only its cell), and a chunk meets the view unless they lie wholly outside its clip rectangle, so a tile larger than its cell never pops in or out at the view's edge. Tiles go through the QuadBatcher like sprites (lit tilemaps through `HdrLit`), counted in the `Sprites` row.
- **No quad is lost to the batch's capacity** (the contract's review: a zoomed-out view of a large tilemap would have filled the QuadBatcher's 2^18 quads and dropped every later quad, the sprites drawn after the tilemap included): the QuadBatcher draws a record of more than `MaxQuadsPerRecord` quads in consecutive batches of at most that many, in order, each written into its vertex buffer and drawn before the next is written, every binding set and buffer created before the first command (`QuadBatcher.h`; stream L, M21 owning the QuadBatcher in phase 2). The vertex buffer stays bounded at 2^18 quads, the Sprites stage keeps its one record per run, and nothing is skipped or logged. "QuadBatcher: a record beyond the vertex buffer's capacity draws every quad in order, in further batches" (L) replaces M18's "QuadBatcher: quads beyond the per-record budget are skipped with one warning" (the same quads: the one past the capacity now draws), and "Tilemaps: a sprite drawn after a tilemap of more than MaxQuadsPerRecord visible tiles still draws" (T1 with L, at the integration) proves it end to end. The milestone review (the lead's decision 3) bounds the CPU side too: a view draws at most `SceneRenderer::MaxTileQuadsPerView` (2^19, two batches) tiles, the first in its 2D draw order, the rest counted (`SceneRenderStats::DroppedTiles`) and logged once per renderer while every sprite still draws, and after a render the stage's quad vectors and the QuadBatcher's staging keep room for at most one batch (Backlog §6). No GPU object of its own: the quads use the QuadBatcher's per-record buffer and the sheets' texture mirrors, and "Tilemaps: a renderer drawing a tilemap recovers from a device loss with the same image" proves the recovery through the shared helper (plan §7 item 3).
- **Bounds and picking:** `ComputeEntityWorldBounds` counts a TilemapRenderer with its drawn tiles' quads; `PickSprite` picks a tile as its tilemap's entity in the one draw order, in the scene and, through the new overload over a snapshot's sprites and tilemaps, in the editor's clicked image (`EditorHostViewports`' click selection, M21's file); `viewport.pick` reaches tiles through the scene overload. A tilemap's tiles are tested only in the chunks whose bounds (the rectangle of the chunk's cell centres widened by the palette quads' extents and one cell) hold the point where the ray meets the tilemap's plane, with the result of a test of every tile (the milestone review, the lead's decision 5).

### 25. TilemapCollider (R-2D-TILE; stream T2)

- **The component** (`Scene/Components/TilemapColliderComponent.h`): `{Tilemap, IsTrigger}`, requires Transform, Physics2D after PolygonCollider2D. A null Tilemap takes the entity's TilemapRenderer's (as a MeshCollider without a Mesh takes its MeshRenderer's).
- **Merged boxes per chunk, in canonical order** (plan §6 "M21"): `MergeTilemapChunkBoxes` (`Asset/TilemapCollision.h`) merges each chunk's solid cells (non-empty, a palette entry, Solid) greedily: cells visited row by row from the chunk's bottom row up, left to right; a solid cell in no box starts one that grows right while the next cell is solid and free, then up while a whole row across its width is; boxes never cross a chunk's edge, so editing one chunk changes only its boxes. `MergeTilemapBoxes` lists every chunk's boxes in the tilemap's chunk order. Integer arithmetic only: the same shape in every configuration.
- **One static compound in the 2D world:** the TilemapCollider is a 2D collider of the composition beside the other three (`Scene/Private/Physics2DComposition`: `Physics2DColliderType::Tilemap`, after Polygon in component order), so without a RigidBody2D on its entity or above it its boxes form the implicit static body, one compound; under a RigidBody2D they join that body's compound. Every box is a `BoxShape2D` sub-shape with the collider's user data, so contacts, ray hits, overlaps and `physics2d.bodyInfo` name the tilemap's entity. A body rolling across the boxes' seams sets `RigidBody2D.EnhancedInternalEdgeRemoval` (decision 8, §9.2 "Seams").
- **Assets:** `Physics2DSystemSpecification::Assets` (the session's asset manager, set by `PlaySession`'s `MakePhysics2DSpecification`) loads the tilemaps; `ValidateScenePhysics2D` takes the editor's (project.validate passes it), so edit-time and run-time diagnostics agree; its `assets` parameter is defaulted only so that the contract tree's callers compile unchanged, and stream T2 removes the default when it passes the editor's manager from `ProjectValidator.cpp`, as `ValidateScenePhysics` has none. A tilemap that is missing or fails to load, no asset manager, neither Tilemap nor a TilemapRenderer's, or more than `MaxTilemapColliderBoxes` (65,536) boxes is `PHYSICS_INVALID_SHAPE` on the collider's entity (`Component` TilemapCollider, `Field` Tilemap) and makes no body; a tilemap without a solid cell adds no shape, and a body left without any is not created, without a diagnostic (an authoring state, as a RigidBody2D without colliders).
- **Rebuilds:** as a MeshCollider's mesh in the 3D world, a collider's tilemap getting a new asset version (`AssetManager::GetVersion`: a `tilemap.paint` edit, a reimport) rebuilds its body's shape in place at the next `Physics2DPreStep` (`PhysicsWorld::SetShape`: pose, velocities and pairs kept). So does a change of the `TilemapRenderer.Tilemap` of an entity whose TilemapCollider has no Tilemap (as a MeshRenderer's Mesh does for a MeshCollider without one; the contract's review), which "Physics2DSystem: a tilemap's new version rebuilds its collider's shape in place" switches too; a TilemapRenderer change on an entity without a TilemapCollider composes nothing. A play session in lockstep or a test run sees a paint only after it ends (decision 26), so a replay never meets a rebuild it did not record.
- **One world per entity:** a TilemapCollider counts as a 2D physics component for `PHYSICS2D_MIXED_WORLDS`.
- **The state hash:** a tilemap's body is an ordinary 2D body, hashed by the physics2d block (decision 11) like any other; no block is added (decision 29).

### 26. `tilemap.paint` and the tile brush (R-2D-TILE; stream T3)

- **The method** (`EditorCore/Automation/TilemapMethods.h`): `tilemap.paint {tilemap, cells: [{x, y, tile}]}` → `{tilemap, changed, chunks, undoIndex}`: reads the `.tilemap` source, writes the cells in order (`ApplyTilemapCellEdits`: a later entry of a cell wins, chunks created and removed as needed), and writes the canonical text as one `AssetEditCommand` (one undo step, "[agent] Paint Tilemap"); a call that changes no cell writes nothing (`undoIndex` 0). Mutating, `SupportsDryRun`, `AllowedInBatch`, an MCP tool (`tilemap_paint`), not in the Runtime. 1 to 65,536 cells a call. Errors located at `/tilemap`, `/cells`, `/cells/<i>/x|y|tile` (the header lists them); nothing is written on error. Asset reloads are deferred like `asset.setProperties`' while a lockstep, recording, replay or test session runs. It edits an asset, so it takes no scene target (the scene-target inventory's None).
- **The tile brush** (parity, Roadmap rule 5): in the scene view, with an entity holding a TilemapRenderer whose Tilemap is set selected, the brush's controls (a palette entry picker and an erase toggle) show in the scene view's toolbar; a left-button stroke paints the chosen entry into the cells the cursor crosses (`IntersectTilemapCell` through the view's ray, with the tilemap's rendered world matrix at the displayed image's interpolation alpha, `GetTilemapStrokeCells` between frames), a right-button stroke erases, and each stroke, press to release, is one `tilemap.paint` (`MakeTilemapPaintParams`), one undo step. The controls show only while such an entity is selected, so no editor-UI golden changes. The tilemap asset's inspector section (`InspectorPanel`, M21's file) shows and edits its palette, cell size and chunk size through `asset.setProperties`, the action its edits submit.

### 27. `physics2d.bodyInfo` (stream P3)

`physics2d.bodyInfo {entity}` → `{entity, body, origin, type, isSensor, layer, colliders, position, angle, linearVelocity, angularVelocity, sleeping, boundsMin, boundsMax, contacts, tick}`: `physics.bodyInfo` in the plane (`Physics2DSystem::GetBodyInfo`'s `Physics2DBodyReport`: positions and velocities as planar vectors, the angle in degrees about +Z, the bounds a rectangle, a gathered collider's entity reporting its owner's body, each collider entity once, a tilemap's for all its boxes, contacts as `PhysicsContactSummary` sorted by the partner's id). `origin` takes the registry enum `PhysicsBodyOrigin`, whose descriptions name the 3D components; for a 2D body `RigidBody` is the owner's RigidBody2D, `ImplicitStatic` solid 2D colliders without a RigidBody2D above them and `ImplicitSensor` the trigger 2D colliders of an entity without its own RigidBody2D, which `Physics2DMethods.h` and P3's field description say. Read-only, `AllowedInBatch`, available in the Runtime, not a tool, a play session needed (`InvalidState`, the hint naming `play.start {mode: "simulate"}`); `NotFound` at `/entity` for an entity without a 2D body. Registered by stream P3 in `RegisterPhysics2DMethods` and `RegisterPhysics2DMethodTypes` (fact 7), with its scene-target inventory entry (None) and its Python test.

### 28. The 2D collider debug draw (stream P3)

`BuildCollider2DDebugDraw` (`Scene/ColliderDebugDraw.h`) gives every 2D collider of every body the 2D composition lists one `Lines` record in the plane of its collider entity's world Z: a box's rectangle, a circle as a 32-sided polygon (DetMath), a polygon's convex hull, a tilemap's merged boxes; categories as the 3D colliders' (Static, Kinematic, Dynamic, Sleeping, Trigger, Invalid). `AppendRenderAnnotations` draws them after the 3D colliders when the view shows colliders (`RenderViewFlags::Colliders`: the editor's Colliders option, `viewport.screenshot`'s `colliders` annotation), told awake from asleep by its new `physics2D` argument, the session's `Physics2DSystem`; both hosts pass it for a play scene. `EditorHostViewports.cpp` (M21's file) and `ScreenshotMethods.cpp`, whose one argument, `isPlayScene ? session->GetPhysics2D() : nullptr` beside `&session->GetPhysics()`, is **granted by a ledger amendment** (main `48d68e51`, ADR 0028's phase-2 "Single-owner files" row, 2026-11-02): without it a `viewport.screenshot` of a play scene would draw its 2D colliders as an edit scene's (Dynamic bodies awake and asleep alike, bodies the session refused found by building their shapes). Stream P3 owns both arguments (by function; it implements the overlay). Its cases: "ColliderDebugDraw: with the session's 2D system, sleeping 2D bodies are Sleeping and refused ones Invalid" (the play path: a sleeping body, an awake one and one the session refused at its body limit, with the system and without), "RenderAnnotations: with Colliders, a play scene's 2D colliders follow the 3D ones" (the wiring) and "ScreenshotMethods: a play scene's screenshot draws a sleeping 2D body's collider in the sleeping category" (the screenshot's argument); "ColliderDebugDraw: 2D colliders draw in their plane, a tilemap's merged boxes included" needs T2's boxes and runs at the integration (decision 33).

### 29. Scenes without the new components keep their state hash

The lesson of M22's C22.2 review, applied to M21's own surface: a scene that holds no TilemapCollider makes no new body, loads no tilemap and hashes exactly as before, and neither a Light2D nor a TilemapRenderer is a 2D physics component or simulation state: no PlaySessionPhase, no hash block, no stored state and no input read is added for them (their component data reaches the hash only as every component's does, through the scene's canonical text). A scene with lights and tilemap renderers but no 2D physics component composes nothing, creates no 2D world and appends no physics2d block. `TilemapRendererComponent.h`, `Light2DComponent.h`, `TilemapColliderComponent.h` and `Physics2DSystem.h` say so, and the live guard "Physics2DSystem: lights and tilemap renderers make no 2D body, world or hash block" holds on the contract tree and guards streams L, T1 and T2 (only a TilemapCollider makes a body from a tilemap). The existing replays at their committed hashes (FeatureTest's through the determinism suite, the demos' through the games suite) prove the rest at the integration.

## Risks

1. **Out-of-plane motion.** The solver is 3D (plan §8 risk 6); the locks zero the off-plane velocities, the depth rule keeps every contact normal in the plane however deep two shapes overlap (decision 8), the plane test checks positions and rotations bit for bit after 600 steps of a pile and the deep-overlap case the normals, then the Physics2D replay runs in all four runs.
2. **Mixed scenes.** A 2D collider gathered under a 3D body, or the reverse, is not one entity in two worlds; each world's composition looks only at its own components, so the rule is the entity's (decision 9), and the validator reports the mixed entity, never the hierarchy.
3. **Picking order.** CPU picking by draw order can disagree with the image where a mesh occludes a sprite (decision 14's accepted limit); 2D scenes rarely put meshes in front of sprites.
4. **Hosts and sorting layers.** A host that does not copy `SortingLayers.Layers` into its request draws every sprite in the first layer, and nothing else fails. The ledger amendment is granted (decision 16). `viewport.screenshot` and the editor's scene and game views have cases of their own (decision 4), `viewport.pick` has `test_the_2d_mode_looks_down_z_and_picks_the_topmost_sprite`, and the settings observation test observes the play session's own extraction. The rest copy the list in the statement beside the rendering quality, without a case of their own: the FeatureTest runner's and the Runtime's test screenshots (`MakeTestScreenshotRequest`), the Runtime's game view and the editor's thumbnails.
5. **The registered components before their systems.** From the contract on, a scene may hold 2D components that nothing simulates or draws yet; no audited project holds one, and the FeatureTest coverage that would read them lands with E1 after the systems.
6. **Phase lists.** Entering the new phases changes `PlaySessionTests.cpp`'s expected lists; streams B and P2 each add their own phases there at the ledger's places, and the integration checks the merged lists.
7. **Lit sprites and the QuadBatcher (C21.2).** The QuadBatcher's two new targets change a shared M18 primitive that M22's UI uses as landed: the Hdr and Ldr pipelines, the vertex layout and the scissor stay unchanged, the new members default to what M18's callers mean, and the golden suite at W21.2's integration (every existing golden byte-identical) checks it; a switch over `QuadBatchTarget` that M22 adds before it merges main gains the two cases at that merge (the later lander adapts). M22's `LdrDepth` follows M21's two targets by the ledger amendment of 2026-11-03 (`1707cf47`), changing none of them.
8. **Byte-identical tiles.** A tile draws through the sprite path with the matrix a child sprite at the cell's centre would have (decision 24), so the tilemap's and the sprites' images agree wherever their corners round alike; the acceptance case draws pixel-art tiles at dyadic positions, where the arithmetic is exact, as every pixel-art map is.
9. **One normal per pixel.** Screen-space accumulation lights overlapping lit items with the topmost one's normal (decision 22's kept limit); a game that layers normal-mapped sprites sees it, and the Lights2D golden shows the behaviour that is kept.
10. **The tilemap collider's cost.** A map's boxes are merged per chunk, so a large solid area is several boxes, and `MaxTilemapColliderBoxes` bounds the compound; a body crossing the seams needs `EnhancedInternalEdgeRemoval`, which the Tilemap replay and "Physics2DSystem: a TilemapCollider makes one static compound of its merged boxes in canonical order" exercise.
11. **The screenshot's 2D colliders.** `viewport.screenshot` builds its own annotations, so a play scene's 2D colliders show the session's sleeping state only through the one argument main's ledger amendment grants (`48d68e51`, decision 28); stream P3 adds it with "ScreenshotMethods: a play scene's screenshot draws a sleeping 2D body's collider in the sleeping category", which fails without it, so the screenshot and the editor's views agree.
12. **The QuadBatcher's batches beyond its capacity (C21.2).** Drawing a record of more than `MaxQuadsPerRecord` quads in further batches writes the one vertex buffer again inside a command list, after draws that read it; NVRHI's write tracks the buffer's state, as it does between two records of one frame today, and the capacity case reads back pixels drawn on both sides of the boundary. M22's UI records are far below the capacity, so its images are unchanged.
